# DIG II — THE READER'S HALF OF THE TRANSACTION

*Questions served: 3 (connoisseur), 2 (authority). Strata: Rosenblatt, Iser, Barthes, Gadamer; Fish (one paragraph). Adversary instruments: Adorno (aura), Orne (demand characteristics).*

Access: the only primary opened at coordinates is Iser, *The Reading Process* (1972), read directly, pp. 280–298 [P]. *S/Z* 4–5 and *Pleasure of the Text* came as verbatim quotation inside secondary reproductions ([S], not [P]). ## FINDINGS

**Rosenblatt — the two stances and their switches.** Efferent stance: attention on what is *carried away* after reading — referential, analytic residue [S]. Aesthetic stance: attention on what is *lived through during* the event — qualitative, affective [S]. Neither is a property of the text; both are settings of selective attention, so one text admits either, and the "poem" is the lived event evoked, not the marks — hence three terms [S]. Condition-design data: stance is set by pre-orientation, shaped by purpose, instructional framing ("what information…" pulls efferent; "what did you live through" invites aesthetic), test pressure, and schooled habit; Rosenblatt's polemic is that schooling over-trains the efferent, so students analyze literature efferently and the aesthetic event never fires — the remedy being designed invitation and *delayed analysis* [S].

**Verdict on the third stance (artifact a).** Her framework *implies* an event-ontology (reading as done, the poem as happening) but *forecloses* an enactive stance in the book's sense [I]. The continuum is defined entirely by where attention falls *during* the transaction; anything after, she re-files as efferent residue — so enaction gets no seat: the experiential slot is the aesthetic (terminating in the savoring of the lived-through), the consequential slot is efferent [I]. The contamination warning holds exactly — the aesthetic stance *is* the connoisseur's in finer dress, absorbed attention to one's own responses, which Rosenblatt values as an end in itself [S/I]. A book whose standard is lived capacity needs a term her binary excludes.

**Iser — the participation gap, from the primary [P].** The work is neither text nor reader but their convergence, the *virtual dimension* (284) [P]. The load-bearing split (artifact b) is his own. In "traditional" texts gap-filling is "more or less unconscious," building a consistent world-picture — **completion** (285) [P]. But "modern texts frequently exploit it quite deliberately… so fragmentary that one's attention is almost exclusively occupied with the search for connections… the object… is not to complicate the 'spectrum'… so much as to make us aware of the nature of our own capacity for providing links… the text refers back directly to our own preconceptions" (285) — **participation** [P], attention turned off the world and onto the reader's own apparatus. Iser moralizes completion as the *danger*: consistency-building is illusion, and "if reading were to consist of nothing but an uninterrupted building up of illusions, it would be a suspect, if not downright dangerous, process" (289) [P]. Payload: "efficacy… is brought about by the apparent evocation and subsequent negation of the familiar… an affirmation of our assumptions leads to our own rejection of them, thus… a re-orientation" (295), compelling "a creative examination not only of the text, but also of ourselves" (295) [P].

**Barthes — writerly/readerly and the impossibility report (artifact c).** The goal of literary work is "to make the reader no longer a consumer, but a producer of the text" (*S/Z* 4); the readerly positions the reader as receiver of a fixed reading, "products rather than productions" (*S/Z* 5) [S]. The failure at the center: the writerly is a *value/productivity*, not a producible object; because it can only be written and not spoken about, there is nothing to say of it and it cannot be found as a thing in a bookstore, so *S/Z* turns to the "reactive value," the readerly, and slowly reads a classic *readerly* text, Balzac's *Sarrasine* [S]. **The theorist of the reader-as-producer could exhibit no writerly text and demonstrated his method on a readerly one; the writerly is asymptotic — a direction of evaluation, not an achievable artifact. A project wanting "writerly effects at scale" chases precisely what Barthes located as a limit-case** [S/I].

**Barthes — plaisir/jouissance, mapped and strained.** Plaisir "contents, fills… comes from culture and does not break with it," comfortable reading [S]. Jouissance "imposes a state of loss, discomforts… unsettles the reader's historical, cultural, psychological assumptions" [S]. Map: plaisir ↔ appreciation (connoisseur comfort); jouissance ↔ felt shift (unsettling loss). Where it strains: jouissance is dissolution, "to die," self-oblivion — not built capacity but unbuilt selfhood; asocial, unsayable, erotic, resisting the relational frame [S/I]. A negative, momentary, unprogrammable shift — the opposite of a transmissible practice, and its unsayability is Adorno's aura target exactly.

**Fish — one paragraph, out.** Fish (*Is There a Text in This Class?*, 1980): interpretive strategies precede and constitute the text; meaning is produced by *interpretive communities*, not text or individual — "it is the community that invests a text… with meaning" [S]. If the community constitutes the text, "participation" collapses into socialization into prior strategies, no text is left to participate *with*, and the felt shift becomes a community artifact — the compliance attack in sociological dress. Out.

## DEVICES

- **Participation gap** (Iser) [P]: a blank un-resolvable into a consistent gestalt, redirecting attention onto the reader's preconceptions; guards against connoisseur completion; bounded by "boredom and overstrain" (280); breaks when the reader re-consolidates the disruption into a *deeper* consistency (irony as new gestalt, 294) — participation relapses into completion.
- **Evocation-then-negation** (Iser, 295) [P]: summon a familiar assumption, cancel it, force self-rejection and re-orientation. Cost: no receipt — "the reader cannot know what his participation entails."
- **Stance-induction** (Rosenblatt) [S]: engineer pre-orientation (purpose, question type, delayed analysis) to move the reader efferent↔aesthetic. Cost: a nudge only; stance stays the reader's.
- **Readerly/writerly evaluation** (Barthes) [S]: grade a text by how much of a writer it makes of its reader — evaluating toward a limit it cannot instantiate.
- **Play / transformation into structure** (Gadamer) [S]: the work exists only in the event of being-played by a participant who is "played" in turn. Cost: surrender of mastery, and (Habermas) it can smuggle authority.

## FAILURES (one per figure)

- **Rosenblatt** [S/I]: her own "efferent reading of literature" is a failure report on uptake — decades of transactional pedagogy have not dislodged the efferent default under testing, and the aesthetic stance is more often *reported* than evoked. The classroom is high-demand; "what did you experience" invites the wanted answer (Orne). The record cannot rule whether a reported aesthetic reading is evoked or performed.
- **Iser** [P/S]: attacked on target by Fish — the "implied reader" is a construct the critic fixes, so the reader's freedom is scripted by the text Iser calls co-produced. And he concedes the fatal point: "we do not know what happens to us" (295) [P]. A theory of participation that cannot verify participation.
- **Barthes** [S]: the impossibility report *is* the failure — *S/Z* means to honor the writerly and can only anatomize a readerly text. Jouissance is by definition unsayable and unteachable — transmission failure built into the concept.
- **Gadamer** [S]: attacked by Habermas (below), conceding ground on critique's standpoint; "fusion of horizons" presupposes interlocutors graciously changing one another, obscuring real asymmetries of power.
- **Fish** [S]: his own reductio — if the community constitutes the text, he cannot explain how a reading ever changes a community against its own strategies, i.e. how transformation is possible at all.

**The Habermas stress-test** [S]. Habermas (review of *Truth and Method*, 1967; the ideology-critique exchange) charged that Gadamer's rehabilitation of authority, tradition, and prejudice is conservative and blind to systematically distorted communication — power operates behind the back of dialogue, so understanding needs an emancipatory critical moment (psychoanalysis as depth-hermeneutic) able to criticize tradition from a standpoint the dialogue cannot supply. Gadamer: no Archimedean point outside language and tradition exists; critique is itself situated; authority rightly understood rests on recognition of superior insight, not obedience. Conceded: the hermeneutic includes critique and application, and suspicion has its place — *not* that it escapes the universality of hermeneutics. Unresolved; the record cannot rule.

## REMAINDER

All four are theories of *meaning*; the book's standard is a change in *lived capacity*. The bridge is **absent** in Rosenblatt (she stops at the lived event, re-filing consequences as efferent residue) [S/I]; **named-but-uncrossed** in Iser, who reaches the threshold — "something happens to us" (296), self-examination — then declares the payload unknowable ("we do not know what happens to us"), substituting retrospective criticism for a check [P]; present only as *loss* in Barthes, whose jouissance is dissolution, un-transmittable [S]; **partially built** in Gadamer, where play transforms the player [S] — but stays a hermeneutic gain whose only check, the felt bindingness of a "claim to truth," is exactly what Habermas shows can be manufactured authority. **So question 3's premise — that a text produces enactment — is not established here; the strata establish that texts produce participation-in-meaning and certify nothing past it. To read Iser's participation-gap as "enactment" overstates it: it is self-examination at the level of preconceptions, not altered capacity.** The record cannot rule that the bridge exists.

## FELT-SHIFT AUDIT (artifact e)

Only two of the five carry any in-situ standard, and both sit where demand characteristics are strongest. **Rosenblatt** — weak: the aesthetic transaction is checked by the reader's report of "what was lived through," but this verifies experience-*during*, not change-*after*, and the demand setting *is* the classroom (Orne) [S/I]. **Gadamer** — a standard of sorts: being "pulled up short," the felt bindingness of a "claim to truth" — closest to a felt shift with content, and the exact point the compliance attack bites [S]. The other three have none: **Iser** by his own statement (295), substituting retrospective criticism [P]; **Barthes**, whose jouissance "verifies" only by its unspeakability — unfalsifiable, Adorno's aura [S/I]; **Fish**, who has only community assent.

## THE PLAY RECONSTRUCTION (Gadamer, ≤400 words) [S]

The mode of being of the artwork is *play* (*Spiel*), and the analysis inverts the subject. Play "has primacy over the consciousness of the player"; players do not direct the play but are "caught up in it," given over to a to-and-fro that "eludes the grasp and guidance of an agent's will" yet is full of initiative. The real subject is not the players but *the game itself* — play reaches presentation *through* them without being their product. This grounds the authority problem: the maker who wants to disappear into the event has, here, an ontology where the event has primacy over both maker and participant.

Play becomes art through "transformation into structure" (*Verwandlung ins Gebilde*): the to-and-fro is lifted into a lasting form whose meaning is completed not by the player but by the *spectator*, drawn in — not the connoisseur's disengaged distance but participation in which the watcher is claimed. The being of the work is the event of its being-presented, which needs the reader to complete it.

*Fusion of horizons* (*Horizontverschmelzung*) is the reading version. The text contributes its horizon — a tradition-laden claim, the question to which it stands as answer; the reader contributes prejudices and fore-understanding, a "historically effected consciousness." In the encounter "neither remains unaffected." What belongs to neither party is the fused horizon — the subject-matter (*die Sache*), a "new context of meaning" owned by the dialogue, not either side. This is the strongest second-person account in the strata: meaning as event *between*, not transfer.

Its price is the Habermas fracture: if understanding is completion within tradition, the model has no standpoint from which to tell a binding truth-claim from an inherited authority in its clothes — the participation-model and its authority-laundering warning arrive from the same source.

## SEAT VERDICTS

- **Rosenblatt — stress-test.** Her binary locates the missing third stance by what it cannot hold; her aesthetic-equals-connoisseur equation is a warning, not a support.
- **Iser — load-bearing.** The participation-gap is the strata's most exact mechanism for enactment-over-appreciation, and "we do not know what happens to us" is the honest boundary the felt-shift claim must answer.
- **Barthes — stress-test / precedent-bench.** The writerly-impossibility report is load-bearing against writerly-at-scale; jouissance is a felt-shift precedent that also exposes it to the aura charge.
- **Gadamer — ground for running machinery.** Play and fusion give the participation-model its ontology (the event between); the Habermas concession is the built-in warning about laundered authority.
- **Fish — decline.** He converts participation into compliance, kept only as the reductio to refuse.

## SURPRISED CONVERGENCES

- **[SURPRISED — most valuable] The loss-convergence.** Iser quotes Shaw — "You have learnt something. That always feels at first as if you had lost something" (296) [P] — and Barthes independently defines the transformative reading (jouissance) as "a state of loss" [S]. Two hostile strata locate genuine change at the site of *loss*, not gain — rhyming, unshown, with a felt-shift standard that is disruption rather than confirmation. Not the object of my search, which was the completion/participation distinction.
- **[SURPRISED] Iser makes connoisseur-completion the *danger*** — "a suspect, if not downright dangerous, process" (289) [P] — rhyming unshown with question 3's worry that beautiful prose breeds collectors.
- **[SURPRISED — adversary-relevant] The strata reproduce the compliance attack from inside**: every framework reaching transformation disables its own verification in the same breath (Iser explicitly, Barthes by unsayability, Gadamer via a bindingness Habermas shows is distortable) — the theories of participation cannot tell participation from its performance. Not sought.
- **[SOUGHT] Gadamer's "the game plays the players"** ↔ the participant does the transforming, the maker disappears into the event. **[SOUGHT] Rosenblatt's stance-induction** ↔ the facilitator setting conditions rather than delivering content.
