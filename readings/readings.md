## What this folder is

Every claim in this vault that cites a primary work cites it by the work's own locators — a page, a numbered footnote, a lettered section — and the trace behind that citation is a reading record in `archive/`. Until now the record named a retrieval address and the vault kept nothing else, which meant that re-checking a quotation meant re-fetching a paper, and a rotted link made a quotation uncheckable. This folder closes that gap by keeping the file each reading was made from.

**These are research copies retained in a private repository, and they are not the vault's to redistribute.** The repository is private and owned by one person. If it is ever opened to anyone else, or made public, this folder is the part that has to be reconsidered first, because retaining a copy of a paper you have read is ordinary scholarly practice and republishing one is not. Nothing here is quoted in the vault beyond what the reading records already quote, and no file here is a source in the vault's sense: `sources/` holds the author's own corpus, `archive/` holds the records, and this folder holds the outside texts those records read.

## What is missing, and why

Two completed readings have no file here, and both gaps are recorded rather than quietly left.

**Katz 1978, "Language, Epistemology, and Mysticism."** No copy could be obtained in any downloadable form. The copy Trinity College once published has been taken down; its snapshot sits behind a host this session's network policy refuses; the copy in the Internet Archive's lending collection opens only to a borrower with a login. The essay was read instead through a scan of the printed pages published at `yumpu.com` (document 15134537), which exposes its text page by page and cannot be downloaded whole. The consequence is stated in the reading record and holds until a paper copy is found: any single sentence quoted from that essay in the manuscript should be checked against print before it prints.

**Erhard and Jensen 2010, revised 2020, "Creating Leaders."** The paper is freely readable on SSRN at abstract 1681682, and every automated route to the file returns a refusal — SSRN's own delivery endpoint, the Harvard Business School working-paper mirror, and the Harvard repository all answer with a block rather than the document. A person with a browser can download it in one click, which is the shortest route to closing this row.

## How this folder stays current

A completed reading produces two things from now on: its record in `archive/`, and its file here with a row in the table above stating the work, the retrieval address, and the retrieval date. Where the file cannot be got, the row moves to the section above and says what was tried. The reading queue's own entries carry the same information for readings not yet done, which is the observation this folder half-answers: an address says where to read and says nothing about whether reading is possible (`ops/observations/the reading queue records addresses without recording whether they are reachable.md`).
