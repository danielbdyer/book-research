# The reframe conversation, mapped: how the books function, and the system they need

*This document reads the whole of `reframe-conversation-full-2026-08-13.md`, the 19,087-line transcript of the author's conversation with a language model on the evening of 2026-08-12, read end to end by one reader. It overlays three earlier analyses of that transcript: the exploration (`reframe-exploration-2026-08-13.md`), the harvest (`reframe-harvest-2026-08-13.md`), and the reconciliation register (`reframe-reconciliation-register-2026-08-13.md`), together with the archive record of the conversation (`archive/encounter — the reframe conversation (2026-08-13).md`). No other file in the repository was read for it. Reconciling it with the vault as the vault now stands is the next phase.*

*The author set the task on 2026-09-25. It has three parts: map the whole conversation; clarify it against its intent and against how the books will actually function; and design the system it points toward, as "an enumerable surface that would be a legible profile of the definable need of the content of the book itself." He framed the mapping itself as one pole of a swing "between the two poles of collapse and oneness." That framing applies the conversation's own founding observation to the work on the book: two poles, neither "fundamentally less than the other" (line 156). Numbers in parentheses are transcript line numbers. Everything here stands at the lowest standing the system below defines, surfaced material, which only the author raises.*

- **Part I** describes the conversation as a whole: what it was for, its shape, where it turned, and who said what.

### Six drifts to flag

1. **Flourishing is put back at the center.** The reweave's title, "The Ecology of Flourishing" (7642), returns to a frame the author had called "a very informationally... biased outcome" (800), and one the cartography had just demoted: "Flourishing need not be the doctrine. It can be the horizon" (7373).
2. **The author's own case is called evidence.** The model calls the author's experience "evidence for the thesis" (5519; 12178) and says "The medium becomes evidence" (10413). The author's stance points rather than proves: "I don't claim a straight lineage there at all" (186); "it's really just like another set of fingers pointing to the moon" (224). The transcript's own better word is exemplar: "your own durable perspective then becomes an exemplar, not a doctrine" (3322).
3. **The late architectures tilt toward restraint.** They state at length what facilitation must not do: appropriate, colonize, supply meaning. They underweight two things the author said. His facilitation stance includes "guiding, directing, moving... having a... certain agency or telos" (214), and the aim is positive: a reader whose own reality is enlivened (770).
4. **"Concept" carries too much.** The cartography's examples of a concept (5884–5899) include concerns (sovereignty, recognition), a tradition's vocabulary (spanda, vimarśa, relevance realization, felt sense), a practice (dialogue), and a method (polyphony). That overload is why each projection redrew the entity model.
5. **The ecology is placed on top.** The hierarchy puts ECOLOGY above everything (17241), while the conversation's own rule makes the book "another locus" (2602) and applies non-sovereignty to "your ecology" and "the book" as well (6089–6097).
6. **Technology grows toward the subject.** The content map itself warns that AI "shouldn't become *the book about AI*" (18575).

### Where this reading departs from the earlier analyses

The three earlier analyses read the variation among the projections as a reason to hold all of them at arm's length. The register calls the model's instability "the single most important fact here" and concludes that "the most faithful use of it is the least-architected" (register, opening note). The exploration holds the whole apparatus as "candidate wardrobe over the author's lived seed" (exploration, Pass 2, §0). At the author's direction (2026-09-25), this reading departs in three ways:

- The variation is read as the conversation's own durability test applied to itself. The seven projections are seven standpoints on one material, and their invariants are the durable content. Nothing is discarded, and the whole transcript stands as valid material.
- A design is measured not by how little structure it adds but by whether each of its parts serves a function of the books (Part VI).
- The earlier split between the author's turns (primary material) and the model's turns (works consulted) is kept as a record of how the thinking moved, and it is extended by a standing ladder (Part VII). Every sentence of the transcript enters as surfaced material, and only the author raises anything higher.

The earlier analyses also tied the reframe to the pace of the manuscript. This document does not, because the transcript itself places the research inside the writing: "The process is not an inconvenience preceding the artifact. The process is part of the artifact's meaning" (12471–12473).

The aim and the constraint need each other. The constraint says what the books must not do: take the reader's place as the author of what the reader recognizes. The aim says what the books are for. The cartography states both at once in its twelfth axiom, "Relationship can increase sovereignty" (3828). A book that kept only the constraint would be safe and would change nothing. The late architectures lean that way (drift 3 above), and the author's own turns correct them.

## IV. The founding insight, and the books' native form of thought

### The insight, stated precisely

At 7:11 PM the author asked for the names of "those two pieces where you're identifying with yourself and kind of saying I'm not one with the universe and reality and then where you're actively participating in that" (134). Two minutes later he gave the insight that, by his account, "spawned the whole thing": "neither of those is actually looked at as being fundamentally less than the other. It's just like that is the fundamental ebb and flow of life" (156). At 7:15 he placed the freedom in a third move, made from outside both poles: "popping out of that bubble, observing that that is a bubble that contains the two parts, is the most interesting flavor of all of what Kashmir Shaivism kind of points towards to for me" (166).

The transcript names the poles in two ways. In the voice half, the model offered "contraction and expansion, like sankocha and" (139), and the author completed the pair with vimarśa (144). In the text half, the model pairs saṃkoca with vikāsa, contraction with expansion (8222). It treats vimarśa as reflexive awareness: the move by which consciousness takes on a standpoint "and through reflexive awareness comes to recognize something about the nature of that limitation and of itself" (2997).

Read together, the clearest shape the transcript supports is this:
- the two parts inside the bubble are contraction and expansion;
- seeing the bubble is the reflexive move, vimarśa;
- the movement between the two parts is the pulse, spanda;
- what happens when the bubble is seen is recognition, pratyabhijñā, "Recognition of what one already is" (2161).

Whether the tradition's own texts pair the terms this way is a question for the texts. The transcript does not settle it, and the model says it is not trying to: it speaks "deliberately without pretending to settle the Śaiva technical vocabulary here" (2988).

For the books, the insight fixes the core move. The books do not move the reader from contraction toward expansion, because that would rank the poles. They invite the reader to see both as movements of the reader's own awareness. The cartography's axiom "contraction is information, not failure" (391) states the same commitment as a design rule.

### The pair as the books' native form

When the model put the insight as "from I'm this to I'm also that" (151), the author called it "The this and that thing" (156). His phrase names the form of nearly all the conceptual content that follows, and that content comes in pairs of two kinds:

- **A distinction** separates two things a reader confuses: "Participation is not sovereignty. Influence is not authorship. Information is not recognition" (6945–6949).
- **A polarity** holds together two goods a reader tends to trade against each other, each of which has a characteristic collapse: "pluralism without relativistic collapse, synthesis without homogenization, sovereignty without isolation, relationship without enmeshment... agency without control, and emergence without chaos" (10111–10121). The reweave says of this list that it "may be closer to the actual 'thesis' than any single statement" (10123).

The chapter outlines confirm the form, because most of their chapter titles name a pair: "Influence Without Ownership" (15231), "Knowing About vs. Knowing Through" (15350), "Awareness Is Not Attention" (15392), "The Difference Between Acceptance and Resignation" (16302), "Witnessing Without Dissociation" (16316), "Boundaries Without Withdrawal" (16456), "Facilitate, Don't Force" (16682). The founding insight's third move applies to a polarity: the reader sees both poles as their own movement, "a bubble that contains the two parts." For this reason the system in Part VII takes the pair, in place of the overloaded "concept," as the unit of the books' conceptual content.

### The false peak, answered

At 7:29 the author named a worry the conversation never returned to: "there's many many ways to bake... the acceptance of that invitation very human. And so there's sort of like this false peak that I see is very possible" (286). He raised it two minutes after saying "the book can't verify that you've done the work" (266). The word "peak" appears nowhere else in the transcript.

The unranked poles answer the worry by construction. A book that raises expansion above contraction invites the reader to chase the high and take it for arrival. A book that lets the reader see both poles as their own movement leaves no high point to mistake for arrival. The chapter outlines end the same way: "You do not graduate from self-facilitation" (16743); the practitioner becomes "better at meeting what arrives" (16762); the facilitator "disappears" (16034). Any arc for either book that keeps a peak carries the condition this sets. The peak must be the reader recognizing the oscillation as theirs, not a height reached.

## V. How the books function

### What a book can and cannot do

The author fixed the limits early. "Nothing transforms because of the audience," and so "a core trust of the book is that the book can't verify that you've done the work" (266). The book "is meant to facilitate and not like change, fix or heal or like proclaim righteousness" (244). The model drew the consequence: "A book can't transform someone, but it can create the conditions where transformation becomes more likely" (271). The book is "a series of invitations" (281), "the score of a piece of music rather than the performance itself" (501).

### The mechanism: form directs attention, and meaning stays free

Four passages converge on one mechanism:
- The author described his own facilitation as "being present and welcoming what is and then also guiding, directing, moving... following that, tracking that, not getting attached to the impartiality, but also having a... certain agency or telos" (214).
- The model listed among the facilitator's principles "Attention can be guided without interpretation being dictated" (1484).
- Drawing on David Grove's Clean Language, the model stated that "the facilitator can constrain the form of inquiry while remaining radically unconstrained about its content" (1709).
- The author held that "constraints are... in design... exactly what gives fluency" (1074).

Together these answer the author's life-question. Enough form is form that steers the reader's attention with purpose and leaves to the reader what they find and what it means. The model's own answer to the same question says it briefly: "structure should preserve the possibility of surprise" (1195). The mechanism also restores the purposefulness the late architectures underweight. The books are not passive: they direct attention firmly, and they claim nothing about what the reader finds.

### The conditions for recognition, as requirements on the books

At 7:30 the model named five conditions under which recognition tends to occur (311). Each is a requirement on the books:

| Condition (311) | What the books need in order to meet it |
|---|---|
| "interruption of automaticity" | scenes, figures, and questions that break the reader's habitual frame |
| "safety with enough friction to notice what was hidden before" | a voice and a contract the reader can trust, with real resistance in them |
| "participatory practices rather than just ideas" | practices small enough to do while reading |
| "language that gives distinctions" | pairs, named precisely |
| "permission not to solve the experience" | unranked polarities, and contraction treated as information |

### Book I: how it works on a facilitator

The facilitators-first move chose the reader for whom the book's backstage is its content. At 7:53 the author said the book's own axioms "fall away from the book." In the same breath he named who still wants them: "the astute reader of the book," "The person who is facilitating, the facilitation of facilitation," someone "getting skilled" in a workshop, and the author himself, for his own orientation (576). At 9:57 he made that reader the first audience. So Book I is the book that can "show the score to the musicians" (1516). Its reader needs to see why each invitation was built as it was: "Why did you choose this invitation? What were you watching for? What could go wrong? What did you notice in yourself?" (1524–1527). Book I teaches by being an instance of what it teaches: "The book teaches the facilitator to facilitate in the same way that the book itself was created" (9163).

Its authority moves from content to process. "Become authoritative about the conditions under which authority itself can be relinquished" (1584) lets the book say "I have something substantial to teach you" and "What you discover here belongs to you" together (1590–1594). Its contract is "I will not give you a bag of techniques. I will invite you to examine what you think you are doing when you facilitate another human being" (17060), and its authority comes from "discernment and synthesis" (17038). Technique enters late, "Because technique without ontology becomes manipulation" (15577). The book's movement runs "Recognition → Relation → Participation → Stewardship of the field" (16970). Its ten steps run from "I want to help" to "I become a steward of another person's conditions for becoming" (16818–16854). It ends with the facilitator's withdrawal: "The facilitator succeeds by increasing the other's capacity to facilitate themselves" (16050).

### Book II: how it works on a practitioner

Book II is built as a recognition. Its prologue says "You are already participating" (16084), and its method is named only at its end: "Now the book finally names what the reader has been learning" (16658). The reader meets the name of something they have already been doing. That is the structure of the fable the author called "amazing," of the woman who meets the lover who has "always known her" (156). It is also the structure of "Recognition of what one already is" (2161), carried to the scale of a book's architecture. The fable's text is not in the transcript; if the books use it, it must be read in its source.

Its contract is "I will not tell you how to become a better version of yourself. I will invite you to develop a different relationship with the fact that you are already becoming" (17077). Its authority rests on "embodied realization": "I have actually lived the questions I'm inviting you to inhabit" (17048–17050). Its characteristic danger is becoming "another performance target" (14606). Its movement runs "Recognition → Relation → Participation → Stewardship of the self" (16974). Its ten steps run from "I want to improve myself" to "I become a steward of my own becoming" (16862–16898). It ends in return rather than arrival: "The practitioner does not finally arrive. They become better at meeting what arrives" (16760–16762).

### Order, mirror, and the shared capacity

The transcript gives two reasons for writing Book I first. The author's reason is that writing for facilitators removes "the duty to prove authority on the topics" (1358). The model's reason is an order of discovery. The first book establishes "I cannot make another person become what I want them to become," and only after that does the second become possible: "I cannot make myself become what I want myself to become through coercion either" (17096–17100). A first book for facilitators may also "assume sophistication" and "speak from practice rather than marketing" (17110–17124).

The two books mirror each other one concern at a time (13854–13871). Sovereignty, for example, is "Preserve another's" in Book I and "Cultivate one's own" in Book II, and authorship is "Don't author another" and "Author oneself." The model concludes that "Book II is not Book I turned inward. It is Book I's deepest ethical principle applied recursively" (16808). It names the capacity beneath both books "participatory stewardship": "the capacity to remain authorially present while participating in a reality one does not completely control" (16910–16926).

## VII. The system

### Principles

1. **The book-need rule.** Every type, field, and relation names the function of the books it serves (Part VI).
2. **Notation, not music.** "Treat the ontology as musical notation, not the music" (1315); "The explicit system should be porous enough for the implicit system to breathe" (10649).
3. **Life, then corpus, then book.** "The direction must remain: Life → Corpus → Book not: Corpus → Book → Life" (6692–6698), because the reverse "would mean living to populate the system" (6700).
4. **Retrieval may be delegated; authorship may not.** "Delegate retrieval without delegating authorship" (8912); "AI facilitates. Human authors" (12373).
5. **Structure serves encounter.** "Structure must serve encounter" (4605); "If maintaining the model becomes more interesting than participating in the ecology, the model has failed" (9447).
6. **The system models traces, not participation.** "Model the traces of participation, not participation itself" (12048).

### Eleven types of content

Each name below is a candidate, as every name in the vault is.

**Concern** is a live question the books serve, stated as a question, with its form in each book.
- It serves functions 2, 5, 7, and 9, and it organizes everything else.
- Its fields are the question; what is at stake (which absorbs the earlier "Value"); its Book I form, with another; its Book II form, with oneself; where it happens, by quadrant (I, We, It, Its); and its exit, meaning what treating it as a concern hides.
- It is articulated by Pairs, illuminated by Loci, crossed in Crossings, opened by Practices, met in Encounters, realized in Passages, and projected by each Book.
- Six projections propose it (5852; 8572; 11378; 13281; 17572; 19024). The mirror table supplies its two book forms (13854–13871).

**Pair** is the unit of the books' conceptual content: a distinction or a polarity (Part IV).
- It serves functions 2, 3, and 4.
- Its fields are its kind; the two terms; for a distinction, the confusion it corrects and where the difference shows up in experience; for a polarity, the collapse on each side, the movement between the poles, and the view that holds both; the way in, which is a Practice, Encounter, or Figure that lets the reader feel the pair before it is named (17203); its company, the Loci that drew it, quoted; and its forms in each book.
- It articulates a Concern, is opened by Practices, is carried by Figures and Craft moves, is drawn by Loci, and is lived in Encounters.
- No projection proposes it as an entity. It takes over most of what "Concept" held (5884; 8661; 17552), and its form is the author's "this and that" (156).

**Practice** is a small, repeatable way of participating that lets a Pair or Concern be tasted.
- It serves functions 2, 3, and 4, and Book II throughout.
- Its fields are the invitation, worded to direct attention and not to supply meaning; what it opens; its scale (alone, with another, in a group); its lineage, a Locus; its smallest living form ("small enough to remain alive," 16499); how it goes wrong (performance, coercion); and the author's encounters with it.
- It opens Pairs, comes from Loci, is lived in Encounters, and is offered in Passages.
- Five projections propose it (4660; 8572; 14656; 17513; 18489).

**Figure** is an image or story the books use to let the reader hold a Pair or Concern.
- It serves functions 2, 3, and 4.
- Its fields are the image; what it lets the reader hold; its origin (the author, a tradition, or the model); and what it over-determines, which is its risk, as with the connotations the author himself flags in "patriotism" (770).
- It carries Pairs and Concerns and comes from Encounters or Loci.
- No projection proposes it as an entity, though the transcript is full of figures (Part IX).

**Encounter** is a dated, lived event in which something happened, with the author's note on it.
- It serves functions 1, 2, 6, and 9. It supplies Book I's cases and grounds Book II's authority.
- Its fields are when, where, and who (personal material held with care); the conditions it happened in; the standpoint it was approached from, a Locus; what happened; the note, "This is what I currently notice about what happened" (8649), dated and provisional; what changed; what remains; and which Concerns and Pairs it touches.
- It meets Pairs, Practices, and Loci, grounds Durable perspectives, and is a source for Passages.
- It is the one entity every entity model proposes (4636; 8604; 11206; 13380; 17475; 19030). The author named it first as the entity: "it would just be encounter as the entity" (1260).

**Locus** is a cultivated way of seeing: a tradition, a thinker, or a practice lineage.
- It serves functions 5 and 7.
- Its fields are its own vocabulary, quoted in its own terms before any translation (9244); what it discloses; what it obscures; what it would decline in the books; the three questions the forebears answer asks of each (2024–2032) — "What did this person discover about the conditions under which recognition becomes possible?", "What did they mistake for the whole?", and "What does their work make available that my emerging ecology doesn't yet know how to make available?"; its sources, with page-level addresses; and whether it arrived at its finding independently of the others or descends from them.
- It illuminates Concerns, draws Pairs, supplies Practices, and enters Crossings.
- It absorbs Tradition, Person, Work, and Source (4640; 3210, "a cultivated locus of recognition").

**Crossing** is one Concern seen from two or more Loci, with what survives the difference recorded.
- It serves functions 5 and 6.
- Its fields are the Concern; the Loci; the correspondence, with both sides quoted; the difference; the remainder, meaning what resists synthesis; what survives; and the author's note.
- It brings Loci to a Concern and yields a Durable perspective or an entry in the Remainder.
- No projection proposes it as an entity. The method is stated three times (5255–5287; 9248–9270; 18670–18706), and a Crossing is the unit of the author's "explorable composite" (2975). The model's sentence for it is "you have to perform the crossing" (3144), and its question is "What survives the difference?" (3164).

**Durable perspective** is what the author has come to see that survived Crossings, stated in his own words.
- It serves function 6. It is the books' thesis-bearing content, offered as an exemplar.
- Its fields are the statement, in the author's words; the Crossings it survived; the Encounters it lives in; and what it still cannot see.
- It is yielded by Crossings, lived in Encounters, and realized in Passages.
- The cartography proposes it once as an entity (4668) before it is dropped; the phrase is the author's (2975).

**Craft move** is a device by which a page directs attention and leaves meaning to the reader.
- It serves functions 1, 3, 7, and 8. In Book I it is also content, because the facilitator reader learns from the book's own moves.
- Its fields are what it does to attention; what it leaves free; the facilitation move it scales from; where it is used; and how it goes wrong, which is when it manipulates.
- It carries Pairs, Practices, and Encounters onto the page and is used in Passages.
- No projection proposes it as an entity. Its rules are scattered through the chapter outlines and the content map (14342–14361; 15581–15621; 17193–17221). This is the largest gap the overlay reveals: every projection modeled the inquiry, and none modeled how a page works on a reader.

**Passage** is drafted language in the author's voice, the smallest unit where "the work becomes yours in language" (17678).
- It serves the manuscript.
- Its fields are its Book; a candidate position in that Book; what it realizes (a Concern, Pairs, a Practice, an Encounter, a Durable perspective); and the Craft moves it uses.
- The hierarchy and the content map propose it (17672; 19036).

**Book** is a projection of the shared material through one locus of practice.
- It serves function 8 and holds each book's function-level decisions.
- Its fields are its locus of practice (with another, or with oneself); its first reader; its contract; the basis of its authority; its movement and developmental steps; its opening move; its ending move; and how much of its own method it shows (Book I shows the score; Book II names its method last).
- Its instances are Book I and Book II. The third, civilizational book stays a horizon and is not built ("I would not write that book yet," 17152).
- Four projections propose it (11455; 13760; 17706; 18922).

### Fields every type shares

**Standing** records how far the author has taken a piece of content. The transcript supplies four levels (12415–12429) and implies two more:

| Standing | Meaning | Who may set it | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| surfaced | brought by research, by a conversation, or by an AI, and not yet met by the author | anyone | 11524–11534 ("AI identifies something worth investigating") |
| encountered | the author met it himself | the author | 12417 |
| inferred | the author drew it from what he met | the author | 12421 |
| believed | the author holds it | the author | 12425 |
| claimed | the author will stand behind it in public | the author | 12429; 17664 |
| written | it lives in a Passage | the author | 17672 |

The ladder makes visible the rule that "Nothing becomes part of the author's philosophy merely because the AI said it" (14414). It also reconciles the validity of the whole transcript with the author's authorship. Every sentence of the transcript is valid material at the level of surfaced or higher; the author's own turns already stand at encountered or believed; and only the author raises anything further. This document itself stands at surfaced.

**Scale** records the level of participation in question: the self ("How do I participate?"), relation ("How do we participate together?"), or the ecology ("What conditions make participation more or less possible?") (17814–17824).

**Lens** records where something happens, using Wilber's quadrants, which in the hierarchy "become a coordinate system, rather than an ontology" (17834). The four questions are "What does this feel like?", "What does this look like behaviorally?", "What shared meanings sustain it?", and "What structures produce it?" (18724–18738).

**Provenance** gives every quotation an address: a transcript line, a page, or a date.

### Relations, stated in words

The two-book handoff says the graph "should primarily express: what illuminates what" (14668–14670). The relations are these:

- A Pair articulates a Concern.
- A Locus illuminates a Concern, disclosing some of it and obscuring some of it.
- A Crossing brings two or more Loci to one Concern and yields a Durable perspective or an entry in the Remainder.
- A Practice opens a Pair.
- A Figure carries a Pair or a Concern.
- An Encounter meets a Pair, a Practice, or a Locus, and grounds a Durable perspective.
- A Craft move carries a Pair, a Practice, or an Encounter onto the page.
- A Passage realizes Concerns, Pairs, Practices, Encounters, and Durable perspectives, in one Book.
- A Book projects a Concern, either with another or with oneself.
- A Commitment protects a function of the books.

### One shape at four scales

The system's working rhythm has the same shape as three other sequences in the transcript: the books' mechanism on the page, the conversation's five navigating questions, and the model's reading of the Śaiva terms as a grammar. The model's disclaimer stands, and this is offered "Not as a claim that the Sanskrit concepts literally map one-to-one onto the whole model. Rather: as a resonant generative grammar" (7015–7019).

| The grammar (7013) | The five questions (7424–7442) | The system | The page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saṅkoca, a chosen contraction | "From where am I seeing?" | Locus | a Pair narrows attention to one difference |
| Encounter | "What is actually happening?" | Encounter | a scene, practice, or question the reader meets |
| Vimarśa, awareness recognizing itself | "What has become apparent?" | the note on an Encounter | a Craft move returns the reader's noticing to the reader |
| Recognition | "What remains when I move?" | Crossing, then Durable perspective | the author's exemplar, offered as his |
| Participation | "What becomes possible now?" | Practice; Passage | the invitation |
| Renewal | the first question again | the next Encounter | the ending that returns the standpoint to the reader: "Now notice what happens when you participate" (10271) |

The cartography already called the five questions "almost a complete research methodology in five questions" (7444). In this design they are also the way through the system, one type for each question.

### The rhythm of the work

The work moves as "a pulse, not a pipeline" (12103): Locus, Encounter, note, Crossing, Durable perspective, Practice or Passage, and then the next Encounter. Any step may be skipped, and an encounter that leads to nothing is "equally valid" (8834–8836). An AI's place in this rhythm is correlative. It may surface anything: candidate Pairs, Loci, and Crossings, research invitations (11524–11534), and whole architectures like the seven in this transcript. "The user decides what becomes real" (11558), and no AI raises standing.

### Four tests

1. **The book-need rule** (Part VI): each type, field, and relation names the function it serves.
2. **The evaluation function**: "If an architectural decision produces a more sophisticated database but makes the user less likely to sit down and encounter the material: reject it" (12313–12315).
3. **The recursive test**: "Does the thing doing the facilitating increase the other's capacity for participation, or does it gradually take participation away?" (14934). The system is asked this question like everything else.
4. **The five red-team questions** (14568–14618), especially "Have I encountered this, or have I merely recognized that it sounds true?" (14582).

### What the system leaves unmodeled

The reweave names two architectures and forbids letting the first swallow the second. The explicit architecture holds what can be named. The implicit one holds "resonance, recognition, embodiment, mystery, transformation, aliveness, intuition, significance, emergence" (10629–10641), and "The temptation will always be to make the explicit architecture large enough to contain the implicit one. Don't." (10643–10645). The system models traces. Recognition itself, the reader's and the author's, stays outside it.

## X. What this carries into the repository phase

The next phase reads the vault against this design. These are the questions to bring to it, in the order they matter to the books:

1. Which of the eleven types the vault already holds under other names, and where its existing forms (its claims, its thinker notes, its reading and encounter records, its topic maps, its drafts) already serve a function in Part V.
2. Whether the vault holds the two kinds of content the transcript leaves thinnest, Crossings in the author's own judgment and Exits, and where it holds them.
3. How the standing ladder meets the vault's existing ways of recording how developed a piece of content is and how firmly it is held.
4. Where the pairs in Part IX already live as claims, and which of them have no home.
5. Where the vault holds material that no function in Part V needs, which is where coalescing would begin.
6. Whether the per-concern profile can be generated from what the vault holds, so that it stays current without being maintained by hand.
