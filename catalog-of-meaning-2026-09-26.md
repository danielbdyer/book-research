# The catalog of meaning: the space worth exploring, and where the books could go

**What this document is.** On 2026-09-26 the author asked a session to read pull request #23 in full, together with the transcript of the reframe conversation, and then to "enumerate the catalog of meaning which would be explored in the future based upon what's being introduced into scope here." He called it "mostly a generative/creative task," one of "seeing where the book could go and sort of naming that space as a whole and elaborating on the catalogs and members topically that would be a part of that space, in a way to stimulate substantiation of next steps in a diffuse brainstormy way." This document and its five companions answer that request. In them, *the catalog of meaning* is the list of what the books could take up in future: the relationships they could examine, the questions they could ask, the experiences, practices, images, and thinkers they could draw on, and the forms they could take. A *catalog* is one group of related items, and a *member* is one item in a group.

**What it is based on.** Pull request #23 adds twenty-two files. The four main ones are `reframe-map-and-system-2026-09-25.md`, which summarizes the reframe conversation and designs a list of the kinds of material the two books need (called "the design" below), and the three documents that read the vault against that design: `system-and-vault-collation-2026-09-25.md`, `system-and-vault-beyond-the-design-2026-09-25.md`, and `system-and-vault-left-to-populate-2026-09-25.md`. The pull request also adds the eight readers' reports behind those three documents, in `ops/system readings — 2026-09-25/`, and a new rule against mannered prose. This session read the design and the three documents in full. Ten further readers, working at the same time, each read one sixth of the transcript `reframe-conversation-full-2026-08-13.md` or two of the eight reports, every line, and returned extracts with line numbers; this session checked each extract it quotes against the file. The session also read the author's sixteen poems (`sources/the poems.pdf`, extracted to `.corpus/the poems.md`) and his reflection `reflections/on recognition (2026-08-24).md`. It read them because the main finding of pull request #23 is that little of the vault is in the author's own words, and these are the places where his words are. Numbers in parentheses are line numbers in the transcript, and poems are cited by their number.

**Its status.** A session wrote this document, and everything in it is a proposal. In the design's terms it is at the level "proposed," the lowest of the levels that record the author's own relation to a piece of material. It changes no note, adds no field, and creates nothing that has to be maintained. It can be read once and set aside, and a test the model set for any such structure applies to it: "If maintaining the model becomes more interesting than participating in the ecology, the model has failed" (9447).

**Whose words.** Pull request #23 found at least seven places where the vault presents the model's words as the author's. This document marks every quotation. "The author" means the writer of the books, speaking in a poem, in a spoken or typed turn of the conversation, in his reflection, or in a dated statement the vault records. "The model" means the language model's turns in the transcript. "A session" means a session working in this repository, and "the collaborator" means the AI that wrote most of the corpus while the author chose and arranged the material.

**A note on the writing.** The request asks for vision. The vault's rule 13 of `ops/methodology/prose register.md`, which the author gave on 2026-09-25, asks for plain statement: "The fix is to say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use it." This document tries to keep both requests by being wide in what it lists and plain in how it says it. Each catalog is named by a plain description rather than a new label, and every name is a candidate the author may change or discard. Where the author's own words are figurative, they are quoted, because in those places his wording is the finding.

## 1. The request repeats a practice the author named in the conversation

At 8:26 PM on 2026-08-12 the author asked the model a question he said he likes to ask: "What's what's still here that's um that we haven't yet touched on that's that's um in this family of of um exploration" (974). He gave his reason in the same turn: "because you have a particular way of correlating meaning together, asking you to describe the remainder of a given domain that's being explored is um much easier than it would be for your average human." And he said how he would hold the answer: "I'm inviting without attaching myself to it because I'm excited by it um but I also I'm okay with whatever comes out or doesn't come out." Today's request is the same practice applied to the scope pull request #23 opened. The author asks a model to describe the remainder of a domain, and what comes back is material at the level "proposed," which he may take up or leave.

The model's answers that evening were an early version of this catalog. At 7:42 PM it named what it thought would deepen the work: "developing a phenomenology of recognition, describing how recognition actually feels and unfolds," "Mapping an ecology of participation, how attention, trust, language, play reinforce or inhibit one another," "A grammar of facilitation," "A taxonomy of practices," the question whether recognition is "cognitive, embodied, relational, or only comes into being through their interaction," and "what are the invariants of transformative inquiry?" (421). At 8:26 it answered his question about what remains with trust, "the kind that lets someone stay with uncertainty long enough for recognition to emerge"; with reciprocity, "the work shaped not just by what the reader receives but how it changes you, the writer"; and with a question, "what relationships reliably increase aliveness?" (981). Pull request #23 found that five of the open questions from that part of the conversation appear nowhere in the vault (`system-and-vault-left-to-populate-2026-09-25.md`, Part 7). This catalog places each of them.

## 2. A name for the space as a whole

**The name.** The author supplied a name in the conversation itself. At 7:44 PM, offering what he called "a point of respectful closure," he said that talking about the work "at its very root has helped me to create better alignment as to the space that's worth exploring here" (426). This document calls the whole space by his phrase, **the space worth exploring**, and holds the name as a candidate like every name in the vault. The phrase has two advantages: it is his, and it names no doctrine. The book's orientation already has an adopted name, the Ecology of Recognition, which the model proposed at line 311 and the author took up by decision. That name describes how the books stand within the space: as one standpoint among many, with no standpoint in authority over the others. The space itself needs a name that does not describe a stance, and the author's phrase provides one.

**What the space contains.** Stated plainly, the space worth exploring is the set of situations in which something influences a person's own participation in their life, enlarging it or taking it away, together with everything a book can use to take part in such a situation without taking the person's place. The first half comes from the design's count of the transcript's 146 rules (the transcript calls them axioms). The count found that the rules state one relationship many times over: "there is something with influence, and there is someone's own participation, which the influence can enlarge or take away" (the design, Part 10). The second half comes from the design's list of the kinds of material the books need. The author's own source for the relationship is older than the conversation. It is a letter from his first love, which he summarized at 8:06 PM: "our relationship inspires patriotism in herself. And um kind of in enlivens her own reality. And she did the same thing for me" (770). His own description of his work states the same space from his side. In the vision brief he dictated on 19 June 2026, which the provenance record marks as his own words, he said: "I'm a poet, a systems thinker, and an Authentic Relating facilitator, and the thing running underneath all of it is the same: I build containers where things — people, ideas, a self — become more fully themselves" (`provenance/provenance of the corpus round two.md`, lines 13–16).

**The question the space asks.** The transcript states the question that runs through every part of the space, in the model's words: "Does the thing doing the facilitating increase the other's capacity for participation, or does it gradually take participation away?" (14932–14934). The master question the vault's orientation adopted asks the same thing from the other side, also in the model's words: "How can something influence me deeply without taking my place as the author of what I become?" (7567).

**What the space leaves out.** Three things stay outside the catalog by the space's own terms.
- The reader's recognition itself stays outside. The design leaves it out on purpose, following the model's warning about the unnamed part of any structure: "The temptation will always be to make the explicit architecture large enough to contain the implicit one. Don't." (10643–10645).
- Completeness stays outside. The author's reflection says of the gathering of thinkers that "it was never meant to be an all-encompassing constellation," and it imagines "another author in another part of the world" who "would choose even to have a few new thinkers in the roster who I am not privy to and who you are not privy to as a co-author and research participant." The catalog of meaning is one reading of the space, and a reader of the books could make another.
- The choice stays outside. Which parts of the space the books take up is the author's decision, and the catalog proposes without choosing.

## 3. Four ways of dividing the space

The space can be divided in four ways, and each way yields a set of catalogs. The four come from pull request #23 and from the transcript. None of them uses the system vocabulary the author ranked as secondary, meaning the corpus's three-part scheme and its formulas for experiences (the decision is recorded at `notes/the system is scaffolding rather than the centerpiece.md`), and none sorts material into that scheme's categories.

1. **By who influences whom.** The transcript names seven relationships in which one party facilitates another: "The facilitator facilitates the participant. The practitioner learns to facilitate themselves. The author facilitates the reader through the book. The AI facilitates the author through conversation. The Tana system facilitates the author's future inquiry. The traditions facilitate recognition across generations. The ecology facilitates participation at the civilizational level" (14918–14930, the model). The author's poems and records add others: a friend and the friend who reads her letter, a father and the child he reads to, a lover and the beloved, a person and an animal, a person and the sky, and a person and the self they will become. Each relationship is a catalog, and its members are the roles, the false versions, the enlargements, and the restraints that belong to it. The companion `catalog-of-meaning-relationships-2026-09-26.md` elaborates this division.
2. **By what is at stake.** The six concerns around which the vault organizes the book's doctrine (recognition; suffering, which the vault calls contraction rather than corruption; how anyone knows that something happened, which it calls the felt shift; the container; the relational field; and the call and the answering) all appear in the author's first fifteen poems (`notes/all six doctrine concerns appear in the first fifteen poems.md`). The design adds fifteen topics from the transcript, the vault's list of what the book cannot yet account for adds four more, the corpus named four territories it never developed, and the poems hold several that no list has named. The companion `catalog-of-meaning-concerns-2026-09-26.md` elaborates this division.
3. **By what a book can carry.** The design lists eleven kinds of material, and the vault adds two more (the order of a book and the roles a second person plays). The reader meets the material as pairs of ideas, practices, images, questions, writing techniques, promises, endings, and forms. The companion `catalog-of-meaning-material-2026-09-26.md` elaborates this division.
4. **By how a word becomes someone's own.** The design records the author's relation to each piece of material in six levels, "proposed," "experienced," "inferred," "believed," "claimed," and "written," and the vault's reading adds "sent" and "answered." The same stages describe how anything offered becomes anyone's own, which is the books' subject. The companion `catalog-of-meaning-ownership-2026-09-26.md` elaborates this division, together with whose words are whose, how anyone knows that something happened, and the making of the books.

The most useful places in the space are where two divisions meet. For example, the relationship between a friend and the friend who reads her letter meets the concern of address and the stage "sent"; the relationship between a facilitator and a participant meets the question of power and the roles a second person plays; and the relationship between the model and the author meets the stage "proposed" and the rule that only the author raises anything higher.

## 4. Findings that cross the catalogs

The catalogs are elaborated one at a time in the companions. Nine findings appear only when they are read together. Each is a session's reading, at the level "proposed."

### 1. One sequence of stages describes the author, the reader, and the participant

The design's levels record how a piece of material becomes the author's own: it is proposed to him, then he experiences it, infers from it, believes it, claims it, and writes it. The vault's reading adds that a drafted passage is then sent and answered. The same stages describe what happens between the book and its reader. The book proposes; the reader may experience something, infer from it, come to believe it, say it aloud as their own, write it, send it, and be answered. They also describe what happens between a facilitator and a participant.

At each of the three scales the transcript states the same rule. For the vault, the rule is "Nothing becomes part of the author's philosophy merely because the AI said it" (14414, the model). For the book, it is "Create conditions for recognition without claiming sovereignty over what is recognized" (7113, the model). For the facilitator, it is "The participant's recognition belongs to the participant" (1489, the model). In each case the party with influence may propose, and only the other person moves anything past "proposed." So the rule pull request #23 recommends for the vault's records, that only the author's own decisions raise the level of a piece of material, is the rule the books teach, applied to the books' own making. This suggests a catalog the books could use directly: the stages by which anything offered becomes someone's own, with what each stage feels like, what can skip a stage, and who is allowed to move a thing from one stage to the next. The companion on ownership lists it.

### 2. The making of the books is a case of their subject

Pull request #23 found that in at least seven places the vault presents the model's words, or an AI collaborator's, as the author's. The most repeated case is a sentence about the 4am reading of the letter, "The recognition didn’t require the mirror," which the provenance record marks as the AI's and which nine files quote without saying so. This is the failure the books are about, occurring in their own records: an influence took the place of the person's authorship without anyone intending it. The author had already described the same failure in the conversation. At 8:11 PM he said that handing the research to others had "unmoored me from the practice of experience around what it's like to write this book" (810), and at 8:15 he said the book "has to be mine at its... authorial... basis" (820). The transcript's rules anticipated it: seventeen of its eighteen rules about delegation to research or to an AI are restraints (the design, Part 10).

For Book I this is usable material, if the author takes it up, because a facilitator's words can end up as a participant's insight in exactly the same way, and the remedies are the same: mark whose words are whose, and let only the person raise their own relation to what was offered. The author experienced the case, caught it, and set rules against it, so it can enter as an encounter of his own rather than as an example borrowed from a thinker. The companion on relationships lists it under the relationship between the model and the author.

The transcript also records the relationship running the other way. At 8:49 PM the author invited the model to state "your own um desire for what's next here" (1178); he received its answer as something "to try it on and see whether it fits for me too" (1190); and at 10:13 PM he said he had put a question to it "as a way to explore and facilitate your natural conclusion" (2509). No note records a group the author facilitated, but the transcript records him facilitating, in his own words, with a model as the other party. The model proposed that Book I "show the score to the musicians" (1516), meaning the reasoning behind each of a facilitator's moves, and an annotated reading of those turns would be a first, small instance of it.

### 3. Plain statement is the books' rule applied to a single sentence

Rule 13, which the author gave on 2026-09-25 after he could not follow the writing in pull request #23, says that mannered prose "makes the reader work harder so the writer can perform" and that "Metaphors drag in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control." The transcript's rules for the book say the same thing about a whole book: "Don't colonize the reader's experience" (1471, the model) and "Do not explain the reader out of an encounter" (17197, the model). The vault's reading adds a third point from the research on the felt sense of truth: "statements shown in easier-to-read contrast are judged true above chance on presentation alone" (`notes/the felt sense of truth has a documented counterfeit catalogue.md`). So a sentence can take a reader's place in two ways. It can perform instead of saying what it means, and it can produce agreement by being easy to read. The plain-language rule, which began as a complaint about the vault's reports, is the books' own ethic stated at the scale of a sentence. A catalog of writing techniques for Book I could start from it, and the companion on material lists one.

### 4. The poems hold territories that neither the design nor the six concerns show

The six concerns appear in the first fifteen poems, and the vault has recorded that finding. On 2026-08-10 the vault's list of what the book's center cannot account for (`ops/clearing.md`) also read the poems, and it asked three questions of them: whether the book wants death back as a fear rather than a phase (poem #15), whether the cats that witness a grief in poem #8 belong in the inner life the book describes, and whether the book keeps the raw second-person address of poem #15's "I dare you." Reading the sixteen poems whole for this catalog showed further territories that no list in the vault or the design names as its own:
- **The carer's longing.** Poem #16 describes "a cathedral of care / so intricate / so considered / so acoustically beautiful / that my longing never had to arrive naked," built by someone who says "i am very good at taking care of people / i am very good at listening carefully / at tracking what might land / at shaping myself around what will work." Those are a facilitator's skills, and the poem names their cost: care that "said it in a register that could be mistaken for service." Poem #8 describes the same pattern earlier: "my burning desire to take care of the world around me." The vault records the cathedral as the caretaking structure the book renders as architecture (`notes/the sixteenth poem returns the book's language to the life it came from.md`). It does not yet place it in Book I, where it would be the author's own account of what care costs the one who gives it, and of the facilitator's own need.
- **Chasing the high.** Poem #14 names "the plateau-seeking enthusiastic bliss-mongering behavior routines" and "my absolutism to love like a naturalized deity," before the book began and before the author spoke in the conversation of "this false peak that I see is very possible" (286). No note quotes these lines.
- **Presence as something renewed.** Poem #16 distinguishes love, which "will sit in a drawer and wait for you," from "current," which "is the ongoing update of who you are in the people who know you," and says "i had been mistaking love preserved / for presence ongoing."
- **Home.** Poems #1, #4, #7, and #16 each return to home: a father's longing to return, "I am home — living, enjoying the weather," "home again, / what a relief, but is it?", and "the place home before I knew the word home / the home where people still say my name / like they mean it." The corpus's treatment ends the book on "welcome home," and Book II's return is the thinnest part of the vault.
- **Dying and impermanence.** That list already raised poem #15's fear of dying, where the author asks the beloved to "wonder what it is I am afraid about when I think about dying." Two more poems take up the concern: poem #3 imagines "one day my parents will die and I am wanting to give birth to them before they do," and poem #11 says "you fail / to remember your impermanence / of days."
- **Power.** Poem #6 says "i am not ready for someone in power of us who takes their power and uses it in feckless vain masking fearfulness with irresponsiblity and lies." On 2026-08-23 the vault deleted its question on power and the one open disagreement it had recorded about it (`system-and-vault-left-to-populate-2026-09-25.md`, Part 3).
- **Why now, in the author's words.** Poem #3 names "the new paralysis of modernity / is dreaming so longingly about what could be / that we forget to live life in the now," which is the design's ninth function (explaining why the books are needed now) in the author's own words, written before the book began and long before the transcript's account of the attention economy.
- **Beginning again.** Poem #12 repeats "beginning again" five times, through leaving, age, crying, and trying: "even leaving, / beginning again, / even older, beginning again; even crying, / beginning again; always trying, / beginning again."
- **A rule for writing.** Poem #11 opens "(only write from the now / right about now lest / you leave behind a part of / yourself in a place you cannot see)."

A full pass over poems #1 to #15, comparing their phrases with the corpus, has waited on the vault's list of extractions still to do (`ops/queue/extractions.md`) since the poems arrived on 2026-08-03 (`archive/source — the poems.md`). The poems are the one source in the author's own words that a session can read further without asking him for anything new.

### 5. The two books may divide along the line between restraint and discernment

The vault's readings of Ignatius, Engler, Kapleau, and Gendlin found that "every criterion a reader alone can apply declines to evaluate, and every discrimination proper routes to another person" (`notes/a solitary reader can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates.md`). A reader alone can apply every rule that tells them not to force, not to conclude, and not to certify their own experience. Telling a real change from a false one takes someone else. Reader F1 suggested what follows from this for the two books, and the suggestion is at the level "proposed": Book II, read alone, can print the restraints, and Book I, written for the person who sits with another, can teach the discernment. If the author takes this up, Book I gains its main content from readings the vault has already done: the Zen private interview, Ignatius's rules for the discernment of spirits, the Sufi distinction between a passing state and a lasting station, Gendlin's listener, and the clinician. Book II gains an honest statement of what it cannot do, which pull request #23 found it lacks.

### 6. What happens when nothing happens has no catalog

Several findings across the reports point at one territory that no list holds. The vault's draft defending the verification standard says "If none of those fires, then nothing has happened here yet. That is a real possibility" (`drafts/draft — the verification standard's defense.md`). Gendlin says "From this lack of referent movement, one knows that nothing has really changed" (quoted in `notes/the felt shift certifies carrying forward rather than correspondence.md`). The transcript says "You do not graduate from self-facilitation" (16743, the model). Reader F1 found that no note tells a form that has become invisible through mastery from one that has been quietly dropped, that the book's short check for a reader in crisis "names no test for when acute distress has passed," and that "none of the seven documents states what the reader does when the felt check returns nothing." Integration is the thinnest topic in the design and the return the thinnest stage in the vault. Together these describe the parts of a practice life the books have not yet addressed: the practice that produces nothing, the plateau, the dry season, the time after an experience when ordinary life resumes, and the question of whether anything has happened at all. The concerns companion lists it as a catalog.

### 7. The books take up two relationships directly and point toward the others

Of the seven relationships the transcript lists, Book I takes up the facilitator and the participant, and Book II takes up the practitioner and themselves. The books take up a third, the author and the reader, through their form: the letter, the correspondence, and the instruction to answer. The books point toward the remaining relationships without taking them up. They are the model and the author, which is the books' own making; the research system and the author's future inquiry; the traditions and the generations that inherit them; and the surrounding conditions and a whole society. The model said of the last one, "I would not write that book yet" (17152). The poems and records add relationships the transcript's list lacks, among them the dead and the living, a person and an animal, and a person and the self they will become. Poem #4, written at thirty-one, imagines the author at thirty-three, forty-two, and fifty-four. Each of these is a place the books could go, and the relationships companion describes each one with what it would add.

### 8. Most of what is missing is the author's to supply, and most of it is small

Pull request #23 found that the largest missing kind of material is material in the author's own words. This catalog confirms the finding and measures it differently: most of the missing pieces are a paragraph long. They include one group session he facilitated, with what he noticed; which suffering, in his experience, was a contraction he could learn to undo and which was a harm to be borne (the drafting document on suffering calls these "a fold that could open" and "a wound that a bow, not a fix, is owed"); what "that is the question of my life" (1190) meant when he said it; whether the fable he called "amazing" (156) is Utpaladeva's verse about the beloved; and one sentence saying what Book I is for, about something he lived through. The next-steps companion sorts them by size, and Part 7 below lists the smallest.

### 9. The author's own words for the center are not the words the vault uses

The vault states its center as a master question about sovereignty, and it calls the place from which a person participates a "locus of inquiry." Both terms are the model's. The author's own words for the same subject are different. His word was "sufficiency" before the model's "sovereignty" appeared: "the more that you allow that sufficiency to exist" (446), and later "a fundamental lack of sufficiency um or sovereignty" (862), where he sets his word beside the model's. His phrase for where a person stands was "locus of control": "orient your locus control within" (214), and "this is essentially what I meant earlier by locus of control" (2509). The model renamed it "locus of inquiry" (2697). The author never used the new name, yet the vault's topic map for the relational field (a page gathering the claims of one concern) calls the rename "the author's recasting." And the question he called "the question of my life" (1190) is the model's question about form (1183), which the vault attaches to a different question of the model's. None of this makes the model's words wrong. It shows that the center has two vocabularies: the one the author brought, which asks whether a person is enough and where they stand in their own life, and the one the conversation gave him, which asks about authorship and standpoints. Which one the books speak in is his decision. The ownership and concerns companions give the evidence, and the next-steps companion puts the question to him in one line.

## 6. Where the books could go

The request asked to see where the book could go. This part answers with eight directions. Each draws on several catalogs at once, and each is stated with what it would draw on, what it would still need, and what it risks. None is a recommendation. They are not exclusive, and the author may take up one, several, or none.

### Direction 1. Book I, written from the author's own facilitation

A session's reading of 2026-08-21 told the author that Book I, unlike Book II, has an outline and no written text: "You cannot assemble it from the existing material; you have to compose it" (`the-argument-and-the-read-2026-08-21.md`). This catalog finds that most of what Book I would be composed from is in the author's own life and has not been written down.
- **It would draw on** his own descriptions of facilitating: "being present and welcoming what is and then also guiding, directing, moving... following that, tracking that, not getting attached to the impartiality, but also having a... certain agency or telos" (214) and "the framing of the book is meant to facilitate and not like change, fix or heal or like proclaim righteousness" (244); the group he led in May 2026; the transcript's record of him facilitating the model (finding 2); poem #16's account of what care costs the one who gives it ("so acoustically beautiful / that my longing never had to arrive naked"), as the facilitator's own need; the readings that teach telling a real change from a false one, which only a second person can do (finding 5); the facilitation lineage the vault has not yet read, among them Grove, Heron and Reason, Freire, and hooks; and the line between a suffering that comes from a person's own habit and a harm done to them from outside, which the vault's drafting document on suffering warns the book not to blur.
- **It would still need** a paragraph or more from the author about one session he led, answered, if he likes, through the model's questions for a session already held (1524–1530), and his decision on whether writing technique returns as Book I's content.
- **It risks** two things the record names. The model warned that Book I should not become "101 techniques for facilitation" (14176–14180). And it warned that the stance Book I teaches can become a form of power: "'I am creating conditions for your authentic experience' can itself become an extraordinarily subtle form of authority" (1805). A Book I that says this about itself would be using its own warning as material.

### Direction 2. Book II, honest about what a reader alone can and cannot do

Book II is the book of the arc, read alone. The vault's readings found that a reader alone can apply every rule that tells them not to force and not to conclude, and none that tells a real change from a false one (finding 5).
- **It would draw on** the rules a reader alone can apply, printed plainly; a referral to people outside the book, which reader O3 described as a way for the reader "to stop relying on the book"; the return, home, and beginning again from the poems; the parts of a practice life when nothing happens (finding 6); the world as a companion to a person alone, in a reading the author chose, from options a session offered, in the clarifications to his reflection ("the world companions"); and the one recorded occurrence of the promised event, which happened to a man alone, rereading a letter.
- **It would still need** the author's decision on how the book ends (item 5 of the decision sitting, `ops/decision sitting — 2026-09-03.md`, the document gathering the questions only the author can answer, asks whether the last phase is integration or sending) and his answer to what the reader does when the check in the body returns nothing, which, reader F1 found, none of the seven corpus documents states.
- **It risks** promising what a page cannot do. The vault's own measure of an honest promise is conditional testimony, never "I caused this transformation" (`notes/the book's only honest promise is conditional testimony.md`).

### Direction 3. The two books joined by the letter

The vault's working account is that the book is closest in kind to a letter, because a letter is the one kind of writing whose every sentence expects an answer. The correspondence was chosen on 2026-08-08, and the pilot letter was sent to two readers.
- **It would draw on** the letter from the author's first love and the 4am rereading; the first letter's instruction to finish a sentence ("something I can say now, that I could not have said an hour ago, is —"); the acts after writing, from sending to the unanswered; poem #16's "the courage now is the size of a text"; and poem #12's "i'm writing love poetry to nobody / in particular– just because it exists / and yet i'd be amiss if i said / it wasn't meant to mean something / to everybody i've ever loved."
- **This part is a session's speculation, at the level "proposed."** The two books could share the form and differ in addressee, Book II written to a reader alone and Book I to a reader who sits with others. Whether that holds is untested, and Book I's grammatical person is item 15 of the decision sitting.
- **It would still need** to know whether either reader answered the pilot letter, which is item 20 of the decision sitting (question S5).
- **It risks** turning a real reader's answer into material without their consent. The books would need rules for readers' words like the author's three conditions for personal material.

### Direction 4. The making, told as the books' own case

The books' subject is how something can influence a person without taking their place as the author of their life. Their making contains a documented instance of that happening and being caught (finding 2).
- **It would draw on** the author's "unmoored me" (810); the eleven places, listed in the ownership companion, where the vault's files state the model's or the collaborator's words as his; the model presenting its own sentences as his inside the transcript; the veto he kept over the corpus; rule 13, which he gave after he could not follow a report; the conditions under which his own ideas arrived; and the vault's shortest account of the book's origin: "a man noticed some things, and then found out that other people had noticed them too" (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **It would still need** the author's own account of taking back what was his, which only he can write.
- **It risks** the drift the author warned about. On 2026-08-04 he kept the argument about technology to the introduction and perhaps the conclusion, and said "I don't want the book drifting into technology criticism" (`ops/methodology/the book's current shape.md`). The making enters as a case of the books' subject, the way a facilitator's own session would, and not as an argument about machines.

### Direction 5. The poems as the books' primary layer

The poems come first and the corpus quotes them. This catalog found in them territories no list names (finding 4) and the images the books lack: the finite, a room for two, and the false version.
- **It would draw on** the concerns only the poems hold, the motifs that recur through the author's work, and the record of what became of poem #4, which the earliest draft reproduced whole and the abridged edition replaced with five lines of the collaborator's prose.
- **It would still need** the author's choice among three ways the poems could stand in the books: printed whole, quoted in lines, or kept out of the books as their unprinted source. It would also need the pass over poems #1 to #15, comparing their phrases with the corpus, which has waited on the vault's list of extractions still to do since the poems arrived on 2026-08-03.
- **It risks** using the poems as evidence for the books' claims rather than letting them stand as poems.

### Direction 6. The books' center, stated in the author's own words

Finding 9 shows that the center has two vocabularies, the author's ("sufficiency," "locus of control") and the conversation's ("sovereignty," "locus of inquiry").
- **It would draw on** his turns at 166, 214, 446, 862, and 2509; poem #9's "my life will never be a waste because i say it to be so"; and the model's list of what happens to a person without sufficiency, among them attention that becomes "extractable" and comparison that becomes "compelling" (18869–18875).
- **It would still need** his decision, which may be to keep both.
- **It risks** bringing in what "locus of control" already means in psychology, a belief about whether a person or outside forces control what happens to them, into books that make no claim to control. The model's rename moved the phrase away from control (2516, "You're locating the agency of inquiry"), and the author's word "sufficiency" has no such history.

### Direction 7. The company of thinkers, kept as company

The centerpieces of the book, by the author's decision, are the convergence of thinkers and the lived practice. The vault calls these thinkers the book's company: people who reached the same finding independently, held as company rather than as proof. The catalogs add ways to hold them that keep them from becoming proof.
- **It would draw on** the thinkers' refusals, kept so each thinker survives being quoted; the author's terms for borrowing at 224 ("this is what I know and don't know and this is what I can cite and this is what I can give you, this is my interpretation"); names that are decisions; the thinkers the corpus assigns to each chapter (its chapter patrons), as voices at the head of a chapter or in its margin; and the reader's own company, which the author's reflection imagines when it says another author "would choose even to have a few new thinkers in the roster who I am not privy to."
- **It would still need** the author to name the traditions he has been collecting "from diverse groups" (2975), and the Jewish concept and the Sufi reference he meant at 214.
- **It risks** an error the model made at 3114, treating agreement among traditions as evidence: "It is something that becomes increasingly trustworthy because you've encountered it from multiple independent loci." The vault's rule is that the agreement is company and never corroboration, because one collector gathered the traditions for what they share.

### Direction 8. The places the books point toward and do not enter

Several directions in the record are larger than the two books, and the record itself holds them back. They are listed so that each can be declined as deliberately as it could be taken up.
- **The book at the scale of a society.** The model said "I would not write that book yet" (17152), and the author kept the material to the introduction.
- **The research system as a third book.** The model said "Do not allow the Tana model to become a third book" (14138).
- **A companion volume of practices.** The treatment's build note says "the book keeps the vocabulary, the companion keeps the drills" (`arc-gloss`, the close of ch14).
- **Workshops run as experiments.** The model proposed "workshops that are more like experiments than lectures" (1205), and a first book that "establishes a community of people who know how to hold the inquiry" (1554).
- **Readers' answers.** If readers answer the letters, their answers would be a new kind of material, with rules of its own.

### What the eight directions share

The eight directions follow one rule: the books offer something, and a person other than the author decides what becomes of it. Book I offers facilitators a way of working and leaves the participant's recognition to the participant. Book II offers a reader the arc and leaves the change to the reader. The letter offers a sentence and waits for an answer. The making offers the author's own case and leaves the lesson to the reader. The directions differ in who that other person is, not in the rule they follow.

## 7. The smallest next things

The next-steps companion (`catalog-of-meaning-next-steps-2026-09-26.md`) lists next steps by who can take them. Five of them are each a paragraph or less, and each would put words of the author's own into the part of the space where they are thinnest:
1. **One group you facilitated.** Write a paragraph about one session you led, such as the one in May 2026: what you set up, what you watched for, and what you noticed.
2. **The blank in the recognition document.** Finish the sentence that `drafts/recognition — a surface to write from.md` leaves for you: "Across all of them, what survives their refusals is ___."
3. **Poem #16 and Book I.** Say whether poem #16's "cathedral of care" belongs in Book I as the facilitator's own need.
4. **Your words for the center.** Say whether "sufficiency" and "locus of control," your words, name the center better than "sovereignty" and "locus of inquiry," or whether all four stay.
5. **What Book I is for.** Say in one sentence, in your own words, about something you lived through, what Book I is for. The seventh readiness condition in `ops/manuscript readiness.md` asks this of any piece of writing being readied.

Any one of them is enough to start. The author's own rule for writing, from poem #11, suits all five: "(only write from the now / right about now lest / you leave behind a part of / yourself in a place you cannot see)."

For a session, the smallest useful step changes no level and adds no structure: record, as the author's own words and with their line numbers, the lines of his that no note holds, which the ownership companion lists.

## 8. What this document leaves out

- **The ranking.** The catalogs are unranked, and the directions in Part 6 are not recommendations.
- **The reader's recognition.** The space's own terms leave it outside (Part 2).
- **What the author has not shared.** The text of the letter, the pilot letter and any answer to it, his workshop scripts, and the notes he mentioned at 214 are not in the vault, and every catalog has less of his in it because of their absence.
- **Most of the transcript's detail.** Ten readers read the transcript and the eight reports line by line and returned extracts, and this session checked every extract it quotes against the file. The session did not read all 19,088 lines itself. The conversation's two derived documents, the harvest and the exploration, were left unread, as the request asked.
- **Readings.** The catalogs name readings and do none of them. Every claim about a thinker depends on the vault's existing records.
- **New structure.** The six documents change no note, add no field, and create no survey, and nothing in them needs upkeep. They are at the level "proposed," and a second reading of the same material could divide the space differently. The author's reflection says the same of the gathering of thinkers: "it was never meant to be an all-encompassing constellation."
