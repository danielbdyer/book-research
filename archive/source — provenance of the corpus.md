# source — provenance of the corpus

Full title: "Provenance of the Corpus — A reconstruction of how the seven documents came about, from the conversation record." Eleven pages, prepared 2026-08-02 by an agent with access to the author's prior conversation threads, answering a request written in the author's voice and recorded at `ops/queue/source conditions request.md`. It is organized as seven numbered answers matching that request's seven questions, followed by a flagged-overstatements section of seven items, a gaps ledger, and an appendix of the principal threads cited with their dates.

## Standing

This document has **provenance** standing under `ops/methodology/source standing.md`, a third standing added on its arrival. It is about neither the book nor the manuscript but about how the other seven documents came to exist, which makes it evidence about the corpus rather than about the book's subject.

Three properties govern how it is read, and the first two are its own statements about itself.

**Its coverage is bounded, and it says so first.** Only conversations outside Projects were searchable, so any work that lived inside a Project is invisible to it. Its timestamps attach to threads rather than to individual messages, so every date is a thread-level anchor — "in the thread stamped July 7," not a time of day. And the earliest artifact, the twenty-eight-chapter seed, "arrives in this record already written, on April 9, 2025, described in your own words as co-authored with an AI," with the composition itself off-record. It states the consequence plainly: "Every question about the figures' true origin bottoms out at that wall."

**It separates speakers, and no other source does.** It marks the author's turns `[D]` and the collaborator's `[C]`, and says the distinction "carries most of the weight." Where a retrieved passage was a model-written summary rather than transcript, it says so. This is the property that makes the document useful, because the other seven are undifferentiated: they present finished prose without indicating which hand wrote which claim.

**Its critical passages were commissioned.** The request it answers asked to be told where the author had overstated something and where a correspondence looked constructed rather than found. Its flagged-overstatements section is therefore requested work rather than an adversarial reading, and the vault records it on those terms.

## Citation

Cited as `provenance`. Its sections are numbered where they answer the request's questions and named where they do not: `provenance §1` through `provenance §7`, plus `provenance: method and coverage`, `provenance: flagged overstatements`, `provenance: gaps ledger`, and `provenance: appendix`. Quotations from the record it retrieves carry the speaker mark and the date the document gives them — `provenance §1, quoting [D] Jul 7, 2026`.

## A note on its contents

This document is more personal than the other seven. It quotes the author's own turns, references a personal experience brought into a conversation, and its appendix lists direct URLs to private conversation threads. The repository is private and the document is the author's own material, which is why it sits in `sources/` on the same footing as the rest; the author may prefer it held outside version control, and that is their call to make.
