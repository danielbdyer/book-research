# reading — deci koestner and ryan, meta-analysis of rewards and intrinsic motivation (1999)

**What was read.** The findings of Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999), "A Meta-Analytic Review of Experiments Examining the Effects of Extrinsic Rewards on Intrinsic Motivation," *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6), a meta-analysis of 128 experiments. The abstract and headline effect sizes were verified 2026-08-10 against web sources reporting them; the paper itself (hosted as a scanned PDF) and the authors' 2001 restatement (at selfdeterminationtheory.org) would not text-extract in-container. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 34.

**How it is cited.** `Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999`, and `Deci 1971` for the founding Soma-puzzle experiment, by author-year — the stable form for these papers. The effect sizes are quoted below as reported; the full paper was not read in-container, so the mechanism detail and the reward-type moderators should be confirmed against the paper before manuscript use.

**Why this reading.** [[deci and ryan measured that instrumentalizing an intrinsic activity corrupts it]] claimed the undermining effect is the strongest independent, empirical arrival at the book's anti-instrumentality clause. The failable question: does the meta-analysis establish that instrumentalizing an intrinsic activity — doing it for an external reward — measurably degrades the motivation?

## What the reading settled

1. **The undermining effect is confirmed, with numbers.** The meta-analysis found that tangible rewards made contingent on the activity significantly undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation: engagement-contingent d = −0.40, completion-contingent d = −0.36, performance-contingent d = −0.28, "as did all rewards, all tangible rewards, and all expected rewards." Doing an intrinsically motivated activity for an external reward measurably reduces the later free-choice pursuit of it — the empirical form of the book's clause that a practice done *for* a somewhere is corrupted as practice.

2. **The contrast sharpens it.** Positive feedback (verbal reward) enhanced free-choice behavior (d = 0.33) and self-reported interest (d = 0.31). So it is not reward as such but reward experienced as controlling — the instrumentalizing frame — that does the damage, which is the book's own distinction between a practice expressing realization and a practice aimed at a payoff.

**Verdict for the note.** The undermining effect is a genuine, measured, independent arrival at the anti-instrumentality clause, and it gives the clause the empirical arm the note claimed. With Dōgen (now read) and Aristotle (pending) it makes the clause a three-tradition convergence. The note moves from privated to full at abstract-and-effect-size depth. The refusal stands: this is a theory of motivation, not of realization, and it posits three needs as ends, so it is company for the practical half of the clause and is not agenda-less.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The full paper, in-container.** The 1999 paper and the 2001 restatement are scanned or binary PDFs that would not text-extract here; the mechanism (cognitive evaluation theory, the perceived locus of causality shifting from internal to external) and the reward-type moderators are held from the abstract and secondary summaries and should be confirmed against the paper before manuscript use.
- **Deci 1971 at its own text.** The founding Soma-puzzle experiment is held from general knowledge and the meta-analysis, not read here.
- **The metaphysical half.** The undermining effect reaches "instrumentalizing corrupts," not the book's stronger "the doing *is* the realization"; that half stays with Dōgen and Aristotle.
