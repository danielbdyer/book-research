# reading — the answerability triad (austin, bakhtin, levinas)

**What was read.** Three thinkers named-but-unseated in the call-and-the-answering concern, whose crossover notes had attributed their lines from memory (reading-queue cluster E, and the answerability sub-cluster of entry 94). Each was read at the primary text where a scanned translation could be reached, and at reputable scholarship block-quoting the primary with a page cite where the primary was lending-restricted; no quotation here rests on memory.

- **J. L. Austin, *How to Do Things with Words* (1962; 2nd ed. 1975).** Lectures I–IV read at the Internet Archive OCR full text (page markers preserved, matched to the standard pagination). Lecture IX (the "securing of uptake" passage) could not be reached in that scan — the fetch truncates the long file near p. 55 — so it was verified from Guy Longworth, "Illocution and Understanding" (WRAP/Warwick, 2019), which block-quotes Austin pp. 116–117 with his emphasis. **Jacques Derrida, "Signature Event Context" (1972)** read at both published translations in full: Alan Bass, *Margins of Philosophy* (Chicago, 1982), pp. 307–330, and Samuel Weber / Jeffrey Mehlman, *Limited Inc* (Northwestern, 1988), pp. 16–17.
- **Mikhail Bakhtin.** *Toward a Philosophy of the Act* (trans. Vadim Liapunov, Texas, 1993) and *Speech Genres and Other Late Essays* (trans. Vern McGee, Texas, 1986) read at full scanned translations, located to printed page. *Art and Answerability* (trans. Liapunov, Texas, 1990) and "Discourse in the Novel" in *The Dialogic Imagination* (trans. Caryl Emerson and Michael Holquist, Texas, 1981) are lending-restricted at the Archive, so their two lines were verified through reputable scholarship reproducing the verbatim wording with page cites (Petrilli and Ponzio in *Southern Semiotic Review*, 2015; a *Bakhtiniana*/SciELO article; literariness.org's "Key Theories of Mikhail Bakhtin," corroborated across sources).
- **Emmanuel Levinas.** *Otherwise than Being* and *Totality and Infinity* (both trans. Alphonso Lingis) read at full-text scans of the standard Lingis editions (Duquesne; Nijhoff 1979), confirmed against the printed running-head page numbers. The one line that turned out to belong to *Ethics and Infinity* (trans. Richard Cohen, Duquesne, 1985) was verified through several reputable secondary sources; that primary was not reached.

**How it is cited.** By work and its stable locator: Austin by lecture (`Austin, How to Do Things with Words, Lecture IX`, with the standard page where a block quote fixes it); Derrida `Derrida, "Signature Event Context"` (the operative sentence is word-for-word identical in the Bass and the Weber/Mehlman translations — cite whichever edition the manuscript uses); Bakhtin by work and page in the named translations (`Bakhtin, Toward a Philosophy of the Act, p. 40`); Levinas by work and page in the Lingis editions (`Levinas, Otherwise than Being, p. 114`). Where a translation term is load-bearing the term is given as the translator set it (Liapunov's "non-alibi in Being," "never-repeatable"; Lingis's "here I am / me voici"). The originals — Austin's English aside, Bakhtin's Russian and Levinas's French — were not read; a copyright edition is owed before any of these lines reaches the manuscript.

**Why this reading.** The call-and-the-answering surface (`drafts/the call and the answering — a surface to write from.md`) assembles the company for the reader's reply, and three of its poles — Bakhtin's leaning word, Austin's uptake, Levinas's summons — carried the mark `[from memory, grounding owed]`, so the both-halves rule (a correspondence quotes both sides or names the missing side with a page-level address, never from memory) was unmet for them. The failable question for each: does the named work carry the line the crossover note attributes to it, in the wording the note uses? The answer is yes for the substance in every case, and five candidate wordings and one attribution were wrong and are corrected below.

## What the reading settled

1. **Austin — the performative needs uptake, and brackets the literary case; the note goes full.** Austin coins the term — "I propose to call it a performative sentence or a performative utterance, or, for short, 'a performative'" (Lecture I, p. 6) — and locates a performative's success in the hearer: "the performance of an illocutionary act involves the securing of uptake" (his emphasis), and "I cannot be said to have warned an audience unless it hears what I say and takes what I say in a certain sense" (Lecture IX, pp. 116–117). The refusal is the load-bearing half: a performative is "in a peculiar way hollow or void if said by an actor on the stage, or if introduced in a poem, or spoken in soliloquy … ways which fall under the doctrine of the etiolations of language" (Lecture II, p. 22) — so by Austin's own boundary the book's performative is the excluded case, and licensing it takes Derrida against Austin: "a successful performative is necessarily an 'impure' performative" ("Signature Event Context"), because a general iterability, "without which there would not even be a 'successful' performative," is the condition of every performative rather than its corruption. This grounds both halves of `[[the book's law that it performs what it describes is a performative the reader's recognition completes]]` (Austin *and* Derrida now read), which moves from nascent to **full**, the Austin/Derrida tension kept rather than dissolved. Corrections carried into the note: "takes **what I say** in a certain sense" (not "takes it"); "the doctrine of the **etiolations** of language" (plural); "in a peculiar way" modifies "hollow or void," not "parasitic"; "securing of uptake" is italicized by Austin.

2. **Bakhtin — the word leans toward its answer, and the deed has no alibi.** In *Toward a Philosophy of the Act* the binding term is **"non-alibi in Being"** (Liapunov's hyphenated form; Russian *ne-alibi v bytii*): "That which can be done by me can never be done by anyone else," and "I occupy a place in once-occurrent Being that is unique and never-repeatable, a place that cannot be taken by anyone else and is impenetrable for anyone else" (p. 40). Its weight is obligation before comfort — the alibi is the evasion refused ("I can try to prove my alibi in Being … I can abdicate from my obligative (ought-to-be) uniqueness," p. 42), and the deed's "heaviness, compellentness" (p. 42) is the whole point. The 1919 essay seals the pairing: "Art and life are not one, but they must become united in myself—in the unity of my answerability" ("Art and Answerability," p. 2); *answerability* renders *otvetstvennost'*, whose root *otvet* is "answer," so to answer is at once to respond and to be responsible. The dialogic half: "The word in living conversation is directly, blatantly, oriented toward a future answer-word: it provokes an answer, anticipates it and structures itself in the answer's direction" ("Discourse in the Novel," p. 280); and "Any understanding is imbued with response and necessarily elicits it in one form or another: the listener becomes the speaker" ("The Problem of Speech Genres," p. 68). Corrections carried into the notes: **"non-alibi in Being"** (not the paraphrase "there is no alibi in being"); **"done** by me … **done** by anyone else" (not "accomplished"); **"once-occurrent … never-repeatable"** (not "unrepeatable"); "the **answer's** direction" (not "answerer's"). One usage caution the vault keeps: Brandist notes that "responsibility" is the more literal rendering and that "answerability"'s dialogic ring partly postdates 1919 — worth knowing wherever the book leans on the pun.

3. **Levinas — the summons singularizes the one who answers; the note goes full.** The face "resists possession, resists my powers" (*Totality and Infinity*, p. 197) and "opens the primordial discourse whose first word is obligation, which no 'interiority' permits avoiding" (p. 201); "The face speaks. The manifestation of the face is already discourse" (p. 66). The responsibility is asymmetrical and singularizing: "I am then called upon in my uniqueness as someone for whom no one else can substitute himself" (*Otherwise than Being*, p. 59), and "The non-interchangeable par excellence, the I, the unique one, substitutes itself for others" (p. 117); the self is "an identity by pure election," marked by "this impossibility of slipping away and being replaced … here I am" (p. 145), and "The word I means here I am, answering for everything and for everyone" (p. 114). The weight is kept: "the more just I am, the more guilty I am" (p. 112; also *Totality and Infinity*, p. 244). This grounds `[[being summoned by another's need makes you irreplaceable rather than dissolving you into them]]` — whose sole external witness is Levinas — which moves from nascent to **full**, and grounds the Levinas pole of `[[to be addressed as you is to be summoned into being as the one who can answer]]` (two of its four witnesses, Buber and now Levinas, read; Benveniste and Rosenzweig owed). One misattribution corrected: "I am responsible for the Other without waiting for [his] reciprocity, were I to die for it. Reciprocity is his affair" is from *Ethics and Infinity* (Cohen trans., p. 96), **not** *Totality and Infinity*. One paraphrase corrected: the circulating "no one can substitute himself for me who substitutes myself for all" fuses the two real lines at *Otherwise than Being* pp. 59 and 117, given above. One page corrected: the face "resists possession" is p. 197, not the widely copied 198.

4. **The concern's central pair now stands on read ground.** `[[no one can answer from the reader's place so the answer the book asks for is irreplaceably their own]]` — the via-positiva turn of "non-alibi in Being" — names Bakhtin and Levinas as its two witnesses, both now read, so it moves from nascent to **full**, the affirmative reading (irreplaceability as dignity) kept honest by the refusal both thinkers insist on (the non-alibi binds; the election is heavy). The book's own side of the correspondence was already its own; the thinkers' side is now quotable rather than remembered.

**What moved.** Three crossover notes to `full` (the performative-uptake note, the summons-singularizes note, the non-substitutable-answer note); five to `privated`, each with the triad pole read and a companion pole still owed (see below); and one already-privated note (`[[to be addressed as you is to be summoned into being as the one who can answer]]`) gains its Levinas pole, now resting on two grounded witnesses of four. The call-and-the-answering surface's Austin, Bakhtin, and Levinas poles change from `[from memory]` to read, with the liturgical-Amen pole marked mixed (its Bakhtin half read, its liturgy half owed). Grounding meter: 199 → 191.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The companion poles of the five privated notes.** Each is a convergence where only the triad pole is now read: **Iser** and **Gadamer** for `[[the book's meaning is completed in the reader's answer so the reply is the writing's final movement]]` and `[[to understand the book is already to have begun answering it]]`; **Gadamer** for `[[a sentence built to be answered hands the reader the next move where one built to be believed closes over them]]`; **Altman** (*Epistolarity*) and **Cortázar** (the *lector cómplice*) for `[[to be read as a correspondent rather than an audience gives the reader a share in the authorship of what the book becomes]]`; and the **liturgical Amen / Kaddish** (1 Cor 14:16; the *minyan*) for `[[the book leaves its last word structurally vacant so the reader's own amen can be the act that finishes it]]`. Wolfgang Iser is seated as a chair, but these notes cite him from memory; his own primary (*The Act of Reading*, 1978) is a separate reading. **Benveniste** (*Problems in General Linguistics*) and **Rosenzweig** (the *hineni*) remain owed on the address-summons note.
- **The Austin items resting on OCR and on secondary.** The performative, felicity, and etiolation passages (Lectures I–II) rest on the Internet Archive OCR — clean, and every page marker matched the standard citations, but a print or library scan of the 1975 2nd edition (pp. 6, 12, 22) would make the trace unimpeachable. The uptake passage (pp. 116–117) rests on Longworth's block quote, which is strong but is a secondary reproduction.
- **The two Bakhtin lines resting on reputable secondary.** "Art and life … in the unity of my answerability" (*Art and Answerability*, p. 2) and "oriented toward a future answer-word" (DI, p. 280) were confirmed through scholarship quoting the primary with page cites, the primaries being lending-restricted; the *Toward a Philosophy of the Act* and *Speech Genres* lines were read at the primary scans.
- **The reciprocity line's edition detail.** *Ethics and Infinity* (Cohen) prints the line at p. 96 in some printings and p. 98 in others, and some drop "his" before "reciprocity"; that primary was not reached, so the exact word and page stay between editions.
- **The originals, for manuscript quotation.** Bakhtin's Russian and Levinas's French were not read, and the English translations are copyrighted; a proper edition is owed before any of these lines is set in the book, as with the Buber precedent.
