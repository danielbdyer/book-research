# reading — gendlin, focusing and a process model (1978, 1997)

**What was read.** Four bodies of Gendlin's work, extending the earlier reading of his 1964 paper (`archive/reading — gendlin, a theory of personality change (1964).md`):

- ***Focusing*** (1978; rev. 1981 Bantam) — read at the complete text of the 2003 Rider/Random House "updated edition" (© 1978, 1981, 2003), whose core text (felt sense, the six movements, "what the body knows") is the same as the 1981 Bantam revised edition. Because the e-book pagination does not map to Bantam's, *Focusing* is cited by **chapter/section title**, which is stable across editions.
- ***A Process Model*** (1997; edited edition Northwestern UP, 2017/2018) — read at a full transcription of the primary text with chapter and section markers intact; the opening cross-checked against Northwestern UP's own description.
- ***Experiencing and the Creation of Meaning*** (1962) — the 1962 body pages were **not** reachable (archive.org holds it borrow-only; the Focusing Institute's online-library HTML is behind a bot challenge), so its claims are grounded through **Gendlin's own "Preface to the Paper Edition, 1997,"** bound into the Northwestern paper edition, supplemented by his summary of the book's chapters.
- **The 1968 outcome study** — Gendlin, Beebe, Cassens, Klein & Oberlander, "Focusing ability in psychotherapy, personality and creativity," in Shlien (ed.), *Research in Psychotherapy*, Vol. 3, pp. 217–241 (APA, 1968). The primary is bot-blocked; grounded through Rob Parker's Focusing-Institute chapter "Focusing Oriented Therapy: The message from research," which block-quotes it with page cites.

**How it is cited.** *Focusing* by chapter/section (`Gendlin, Focusing, ch. 3 "What the Body Knows"`); *A Process Model* by chapter (`A Process Model, ch. I`); *Experiencing and the Creation of Meaning* by the 1997 Preface page (`ECM, Preface to the Paper Edition, p. 2`) and by its structural apparatus — the seven "functional relationships" (ch. III) and ten "Characteristics" (ch. IV-B), **not** numbered "definitions," which belong to the 1964 paper; the 1968 study as `Gendlin et al. 1968` at *Research in Psychotherapy* Vol. 3, pp. 217–241.

**Why this reading.** The felt shift is the book's single test for whether anything is true, and that whole standard rested on **one name, Gendlin**, with only the 1964 paper read — the "one-name load" the felt-shift surface names as its sharpest limit. This reading discharges the load's primary-text obligation for the concern's implicit-knowing and felt-signature notes, whose Gendlin attributions had stood from memory.

## What the reading settled

1. **The implicit exceeds the explicit, and carrying-forward creates rather than retrieves.** In his 1997 Preface to *ECM*, Gendlin's own words: we think with "our more intricate experiencing" and the implicit "is more ..... than any formed form" (his ellipsis marks the ineffable "more"); and, decisively against retrieval, "The kind of transition I call 'direct reference' … lifts out (creates, finds, synthesizes, differentiates .....) a 'this,' which was not a this before. When we seem to find what 'was' there, we have actually moved further … No equation is possible between implicit and explicit," so "we commonly but wrongly say that they 'were' implicit" (Preface, pp. 2, 6, 7); "no concept, rule, or distinction ever equals experiencing, -- but may carry it forward." This grounds `[[what you already carry is a living implicit rather than a stored answer so knowing it stays your own act]]` and `[[reading is a carrying-forward that lets the reader's own implicit unfold into form]]` (and the "unthought known" note's Gendlin bridge).

2. **The felt sense and the "body shift" (Focusing).** "A felt sense is not a mental experience but a physical one. Physical" (ch. 3); it "encompasses everything you feel and know about the given subject at a given time—encompasses it and communicates it to you all at once rather than detail by detail" (ch. 3). The shift: "it always has that easing and sometimes very beautiful sensation of bodily release. It feels like exhaling after holding your breath … I call it the body shift" (ch. 3); "A felt shift feels like a release. That is how you recognize it" (ch. 5). The word that fits: "a word, phrase, or image—if it fits exactly—provides a 'handle' … the whole felt sense stirs just slightly and eases a little. This is a signal, as if it said: 'This is right'" (chs. 4–5). This grounds `[[aliveness has a specific felt signature the reader can learn to recognize and trust]]` and `[[when the true word arrives the body confirms it so the search for the exact word is navigation]]`.

3. **The wordless knowing is precise-to-itself, but not "exact."** *Focusing* renders the precision as the felt sense recognizing when a handle "fits … just right" and giving a shift only then, while insisting "the seeming fit will never be exact, for experience is richer than patterns" (Appendix A). This grounds the Gendlin pole of `[[your wordless knowing is precise to itself and recognizes its own right words by a felt fit you can trust]]`.

4. **The body's own direction (Focusing, ch. 3):** "Your body knows the direction of healing and life. If you take the time to listen to it through focusing, it will give you the steps in the right direction," and "Every bad feeling is potential energy toward a more right way of being if you give it space to move toward its rightness."

5. **The interaction-first ontology (A Process Model).** "Body and environment are one, but of course only in certain respects … The body is a non-representational concretion of (with) its environment" (ch. I); "'Their' interaffecting precedes their being many," so "they interaffect each other before they are a they." This grounds `[[your body already found its way through this terrain and reading is that bodily competence coming to speech]]` and `[[the reader carries an implicit sense of the whole and each part carries that whole forward]]`.

6. **The movement-not-correspondence certificate is strengthened, and re-specified.** *A Process Model*: "Carrying forward is an occurring that changes the implying in a way which it implied itself changed, so that it is no longer 'the same' implying" (ch. II), and "Carrying forward is always something that might not happen." *Focusing* says the same practically: "You cannot—and should not—trust any single set of words, any one feeling, any one body-message that comes. But you can definitely trust the whole series of steps by which your body moves to resolve and change a wrong state of being" (ch. 3). This confirms and systematizes the 1964 reading's finding — the shift certifies that the process **carried forward**, not the correspondence-truth of a fixed content. One refinement to carry: Gendlin does not abolish truth but re-specifies it as a non-representational "responsive order," so phrase the claim as **"not correspondence to a fixed content"** rather than "not truth" flatly.

7. **The 1968 outcome study.** Focusing ability (the Experiencing Scale, EXP) predicted who benefited in therapy, confirming Kirtner and Cartwright (1958); but therapy did **not** reliably raise EXP (successful clients gained ~0.6 on average, unsuccessful clients "hardly changed at all") — and it was precisely this stability finding that led Gendlin to develop an explicit method to *teach* focusing. This settles the outcome-research question in the reading queue's Gendlin-remainder entry.

## What the reading did not settle — and the one claim it falsified

- **It falsified `[[a practice round is finished when the body has refused at least one formulation]]`.** That note staked its validity on this exact reading ("whether the six movements mark the stopping point at the shift or at the rejection decides whether the completion signal can be stated this way at all"). The reading answers: the six movements — Clearing a space, Felt sense, Handle, Resonating, Asking, Receiving — terminate at the **shift ("Receiving")**, and Gendlin even relaxes that ("IF … YOU HAVE SPENT A LITTLE WHILE SENSING … AN UNCLEAR HOLISTIC BODY SENSE … THEN YOU HAVE FOCUSED. It doesn't matter whether the body-shift came or not"). The body's refusal of non-resonant handles ("If you get a quick answer without a shift … just let that kind of answer go by") is a **within-process correction**, not a completion criterion. So the note is **composted**: the mechanism it names is real, but "refusal ends the round" is not Gendlin's, and the honest completion signal is the shift.
- **"Felt sense" is 1978 vocabulary, not 1962.** *ECM* (1962) says "felt meaning" and "comprehension"; Gendlin himself glosses ECM's "comprehension" as "what is now called a 'felt sense.'" The *concept* of a felt datum of a whole is in ECM as "felt meaning," but the *phrase* "a felt sense of all that" is *Focusing*'s and is cited there.
- **"An exactness of its own" is not verbatim in *Focusing*.** Do not quote it. Use "fits … just right" and "the seeming fit will never be exact, for experience is richer than patterns."
- **"Before body and mind are split apart" is a gloss, not an APM quote.** APM's founding unity is body–**environment** ("Body and environment are one"; "interaffecting precedes their being many"); the mind/body split is what APM's concepts overcome, not where the felt sense originates.
- **Unreachable primaries, owed before manuscript quotation.** The 1962 *ECM* body pages (borrow-only) and the 1968 primary paper text (bot-blocked) were not reached; the ECM claims rest on Gendlin's own 1997 Preface and the 1968 findings on Parker's Focusing-Institute chapter (verified-via-reputable-secondary). A copyright edition of each is owed before a line reaches the book.
- **The felt-shift companion poles still owed** on the notes now moved to privated: Dzogchen (rigpa recognition vs. the concept), Bollas (the unthought known), the *spanda* / *kiñcit calana* gloss (Kṣemarāja), the later Heidegger's "the word lets the thing be present" and Abhinavagupta's *camatkāra*, and Bhartṛhari's *pratibhā*.
