# reading — the completion family (hudson's art of accomplishment, mclaren's language of emotions)

**What was read.** Two living practitioners whose chairs rested wholly on the corpus's account, read on 2026-09-02 as readings A4 and A6 of the practice-model program. **Joe Hudson**: the Art of Accomplishment's own published transcripts — "Introduction to VIEW" (podcast E01), "Emotional Inquiry," "Feel over Figure," "Stages of Emotional Development" — the Substack essay "How to actually feel your emotions," the About and Welcoming Fear pages, the Lenny's Newsletter interview, and the complete transcript of Modern Wisdom #813 (2,845 lines). **Karla McLaren**: her own site — the emotional-vocabulary page, "The second key to emotional genius," "The Gifts in Your Emotions," "Grief: The deep river of the soul," "Healing rituals for grief," "Welcoming the new Language of Emotions," "Why loneliness is not an emotion," the About page — plus her freely published Reading Guide, which quotes *The Language of Emotions* (2010) with page numbers, and her 2004 *Skeptical Inquirer* essay. The book itself was not read whole; the lending-restricted scan was deliberately not pulled. Grades: Hudson **A** throughout (his own transcripts); McLaren **A** for her site and for the Reading Guide's page-cited quotations of the 2010 text. One item stays at **C**: a search result asserting that she recommends Focusing-oriented therapy could not be located in her own words and is not asserted.

**How it is cited.** Hudson by episode or essay title — `Art of Accomplishment, "Feel over Figure"`; `Modern Wisdom #813`. McLaren by page where the Reading Guide supplies one — `McLaren, The Language of Emotions (2010), p. 33` — and otherwise by the page title at karlamclaren.com.

**Why this reading.** The survey of the vault's practice-models found that completion is three schematics rather than one, that Hudson and McLaren were held as "two accounts of one practice," and that Gendlin's dissent at the primary text was unarbitrable because one side was primary-read and the other corpus-relay (`ops/practice-model ecology.md`, section 7). Hudson carried `thesis-bearing` load on zero primary contact ("this chair has no second half at all"), and McLaren's grief finding was the corpus's strongest single case for the relational field. Four questions were set: whether "every feeling, fully welcomed, completes itself" is Hudson's own formulation or received from the affect-completion line through Gendlin; whether his completion has a witness in it; whether McLaren's messengers carry fixed content or content found in the working; and whether her grief practice requires a witness.

## What the reading settled — Hudson

1. **The completion clause is the corpus's, not his.** Across everything read, "complete" never appears as a predicate of a feeling. His words are *welcome*, *allow*, and *homeostasis*, and he defends the first against its nearest neighbour: "It was like the difference between I can accept my emotions and I can welcome my emotions. I can't wait to feel jealous again. I can't wait to feel angry again. it's that turn" ("Emotional Inquiry"); "And it's welcome. It's not accept" (Modern Wisdom #813). His engine is non-recreation rather than completion: "If there's an emotion that I wasn't allowed to feel, I recreate that feeling over and over again, until I fully allow that feeling and then I don't need to recreate it" ("Feel over Figure"); "our body has just found that homeostasis. It's like, okay, I'm back to balance." The theory of suffering the corpus gives him is his: "the emotion itself isn't what's painful, it's your resistance to it that makes it painful" (Substack); "When you're resisting sadness, it does last forever. It looks like depression."

2. **Not received: neighbour, not parent.** He credits the design of his questions to "the way that somatic experiencing and a lot of the occupational therapy stuff works" ("Emotional Inquiry") and never names Reich, Lowen, or Perls. Asked about Focusing by name, he placed the difference himself: "my experience in things like focusing, they all have great benefits. I'm not in any way ranking one above the other… In every place else it was an acceptance. It wasn't a welcoming. it wasn't a warm embrace." VIEW's provenance he gives as observed convergence: "I went around the world looking for people, found people, and realized what they all had in common." Adyashanti, Theravada Buddhism, and "Case," the consultant he set out to reverse-engineer, are the names he gives; Nonviolent Communication appears only as a negative example. One headless lineage stands: "the emotional repression is held in our muscles. When you hold an emotion back, whatever it is, you have to constrain a muscle" is Reichian in substance and unattributed anywhere.

3. **The witness the chair recorded is falsified, with one narrow exception.** The core tool is a solo guided meditation — "Do it. Experiment with it. Play with it"; "outside of the group and one-on-one work this is the place you want to focus is doing that two or three times a day"; "It doesn't take but five minutes and it's just moves" — and his written instructions address a lone reader. The exception belongs to the expression phase, not the welcome: "if you're expressing fear, really important to have that with loving attention to not do that alone" (Modern Wisdom #813). His most emphatic procedural rule cuts against a relational reading: "the last part is to just not do it at anybody. This is the most critical thing is, don't do it at people" ("Feel over Figure"). The failure condition set for the reading was "received and solitary"; it is solitary and not received, so the chair's load survives with one half corrected.

4. **VIEW verified, and its native domain is conversation.** "Vulnerability is to speak your truth even when it's scary"; "Impartiality is not trying to achieve an outcome for yourself or others"; "Empathy is to be with a person in their emotions"; "Wonder, it's a lot like curiosity except for you're not looking for the answer" ("Introduction to VIEW"). "Practically, it's a way to have conversations. It's a communication methodology," and "It is not the technique… if you see this and view this as a technique, it won't work." The counterfeit guard the vault should hold beside Gendlin's fn. 13: "as soon as you start doing it not as a form of welcoming, not as a form of invitation, but as a form of management, the tool just stops working."

## What the reading settled — McLaren

1. **The three questions need correcting on all three.** Her instrument is a gifts list plus a pair of *internal* questions — her word — and the pairs changed between editions. Anger: "What must be protected? What must be restored?" (2010) → "What do I value? and What must be protected and restored?" (post-2023). Fear: "What action should be taken?" → "What am I sensing? and What action should be taken?" Sadness has two questions in both editions — "What must be released? What must be rejuvenated?" — with the instruction "Remember to ask both questions; sadness is not just about loss," so the corpus keeps the half she warns against keeping. Grief is a distinct emotion, not a grade of sadness: "grief arises when you have no choice about letting go"; its questions are "What must be mourned? How do I honor what was lost?"

2. **Channeling verified at p. 33, and the two failure modes withdrawn since.** "…to direct or convey something along a chosen pathway in a conscientious manner. If we can learn to properly channel our emotions, we can begin to work with them in vibrant and ingenious ways. We can interpret the messages our emotions carry and make use of the instincts our emotions contain" (2010, p. 33). She claims the coinage: "I worked hard to create a 'middle path' option of channeling the emotion properly." The 2010 book holds both repression and expression fail — "neither approach works because neither one accepts emotions as important messengers" (p. 33); expression "tends to train our brains into expressing the emotion the next time… we lose the message in the tumult" — and her current position retracts the categorical claim: "Expression and repression aren't problems in and of themselves. They're fine in many instances; they're only problems when they're the only choices you have." "Venting" is not her word; hers is "expression," which she recommends by name ("When a snake crosses your path, express your panic and jump and yell a little!").

3. **The content is found; the questions are made instruments.** "I worked directly with them, as if they were personalities, and asked them what they were for… That's why I created a verbal question to ask when any emotion comes up"; "In this newly revised edition, I've updated most of the emotions and their questions"; "I don't have a simple, step-by-step process for dealing with the situation above, and I am really suspicious of people who do, because interaction is so incredibly situation-specific." The model is two-level: each emotion's *job* is stable; the *answer* is found in the working. The Barrett objection does not stand as a tension — she cites Barrett as a source ("In her books *How Emotions Are Made* and *7½ Lessons about the Brain*, researcher Lisa Feldman Barrett writes about how we co-regulate each other's emotions") and her first key is constructionist in spirit: "unvalence your emotions, because there is no such thing as positive or negative emotions." The space-around survey's "are the messengers found or made?" stages a fight she is not in.

4. **The grief-witness claim is falsified at her text; the ceremony is hers and the witness is the book's.** "Your grief process can be undertaken individually, or in a community or family group." The solitary route is sufficient and the body is its agent: "Your body is a brilliant mourner, and if you trust it, it will convey you into the river of tears and bring you back out safe again." What she requires is ritual — a container, not a person: "create a shrine for the dead (or the loss) so that you can create a container for your mourning"; "a secluded physical space where you can work through your grief in your own time"; and community is an explicit option inside the ritual: "Inviting community into your grief ritual… If you include others, let them place their own items in the grief shrine as well." Her other channeling practices are likewise solitary — Conscious Complaining is done "in safety and privacy" (p. 149). "Another nervous system present while the thing moves through" and grief that "does not complete in a sealed flask" are the book's own doctrine. Her genuine relational claim is about a grief-impaired culture: "When we see someone actively dropping into grief and mourning, we often turn away… And that's the real death – of compassion, of community, of feeling, and of understanding." And the communal ritual the corpus reports as her finding she credits by name: "With my husband Tino, I had the great good fortune to participate in a number of indigenous West African grief rituals with the late, great Sobonfu Somé, who was a ritual keeper from the ancient Dagara tribe" — a self-flagged debt in the vault's six-relation scheme, which the corpus generalizes away.

## What the reading settled about the family

- **Three schematics, and the fault line moves.** Hudson and McLaren are the pair that actually disagree, and they disagree about expression. His second phase is obligatory and muscular — "you can't unhold the emotion… until you've actually done some expression"; "The muscles aren't going to learn from just sitting in meditation position… There is a need for the emotion to actually move your body" — with a choreography per emotion; her 2010 account is that expression "trains our brains into expressing the emotion the next time" and costs the message. His constraint on expression is directional (never at a person); hers is informational. They share the negatives: both refuse repression, and both refuse watch-it-from-above meditation in nearly the same words (McLaren: "the focus of this meditative practice is to continually watch and calm down and repress your emotions").
- **Hudson is nearer Gendlin than the vault implied**, and said so in the one place it counts — acceptance versus welcoming, not a different object of attention. Both attend somatically and specifically, both refuse the explanatory move, both carry an anti-counterfeit clause; but his engine is homeostatic non-recreation, not carrying forward.
- **The Gendlin–McLaren disagreement narrows to direction.** Gendlin's fn. 13 objects that an emotion is "sheer" and rests a person short of "the complex ground of the emotion." McLaren does not claim the emotion carries pre-formed content; she claims it names the file and the content is found by working. What survives: for Gendlin the emotion is in the way of the complex ground and one goes past it; for McLaren the emotion is the correct address of the complex ground and one goes into it. Both hold the content is found rather than fixed.
- **None of the three uses "completes itself."** What all three share is narrower than completion: a feeling attended to in the body without being managed changes state, and all three refuse the observational stance that watches from above. The phrase belongs to the book.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The book at length.** *The Language of Emotions* was read at the Reading Guide's page-cited quotations and at her site, not whole; the 2023 revised edition was reached only through her own account of it. Hudson's paid course material was not heard; the Adyashanti debt is named by him and unlocated.
- **The Focusing-oriented-therapy attribution** (Grade C) stays open.
- **Whether the book's grief-witness doctrine has a witness of its own.** With McLaren corrected, the claim that grief structurally requires a second person rests on the first-telling's anthropological grounding and on the book's own chemistry, and its outside witness is now Somé's tradition at one remove. That is a reading question for the relational field rather than for either chair.

## Seatings

The seating of both chairs remains the author's. Hudson stays `coaches`, load `thesis-bearing` (the load survives its failure condition); McLaren stays `emotion-work`, load proposal `names-existing-practice`, with the clause corrected so that the practice she names is channeling and the witness clause is the book's. No new chair was created; Sobonfu Somé is flagged as a candidate for the author, since the corpus's communal-grief material descends from her tradition through McLaren.
