# reading — dogen, bendowa on shusho itto (c. 1231)

**What was read.** The Sōtō Zen Text Project's key-terms essay "Shusho Itto (Oneness and equality of practice and realization)" by Rev. Seijun Ishii of Komazawa University, translated by Rev. Issho Fujita, at sotozen.com, which quotes Dōgen's *Bendōwa* (c. 1231) directly — the eighth question-and-answer that defines *shushō-ittō*, together with the *Eihei Kōroku* dialogue and the *Zazenshin* tile-polishing story the essay adduces. Retrieved 2026-08-10. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 32. The depth is an authoritative traditional-scholarly source quoting *Bendōwa*, not the full *Bendōwa* read whole; a complete translation (Waddell and Abe, *The Heart of Dōgen's Shōbōgenzō*; or Tanahashi) would confirm the surrounding argument.

**How it is cited.** By Dōgen's stable work-and-section locator: `Dōgen, Bendōwa (eighth question)`. Quotations are from the Fujita translation via the Sōtō Zen Text Project and are marked below; the Sino-Japanese terms (*shushō-ittō* 修証一等, *shōjō no shu* practice-within-realization, *honshō myōshu* 本証妙修, *hongaku* 本覚) are given as the source gives them and should be confirmed against a printed critical edition before manuscript use.

**Why this reading.** [[dogen holds that the practice is the realization enacted rather than its means]] recorded that the book's anti-instrumentality clause rests on Dōgen alone, in his voice and unread — the situation the verification standard was in with Gendlin. The failable question: does Dōgen, at his own tradition's authoritative gloss, define practice as the enactment of realization rather than a means to it, and does his position genuinely conflict with the book's arc-toward-a-peak and its transformation promise?

## What the reading settled

1. **Practice and realization are one, and practice is not a means to a future realization — confirmed in the tradition's own words.** *Bendōwa*'s eighth answer: "To suppose that practice and realization are not one is a view of those outside the way; in Buddha Dharma they are one and the same. Because practice within realization occurs at the moment of practice, the practice of beginner's mind is itself the entire original realization" (Fujita trans.). The essay states the anti-instrumentality form precisely: Dōgen "inverts it to mean that it is necessary to have **a practice without anticipating realization**." This is the book's clause — the doing is not for a somewhere — in its source, and it grounds the clause the corpus had stated only in Dōgen's paraphrased voice.

2. **Practice is nonetheless necessary — the anti-quietism the book shares.** Dōgen guards the same flank the book does: he "was deeply concerned that the teaching of 'the self is originally buddha' might lead people to disregard practice," and held that "we practice precisely because we are already buddha." *Bendōwa*: "Although this inconceivable dharma is abundant in each person, it is not actualized without practice, and it is not attained without realization." Already-there-ness requiring practice rather than cancelling it is the book's own posture — reading is remembering, and still a practice — so the convergence is deeper than a borrowing.

3. **The refusal is refined, not confirmed as first stated.** The note's refusal read that Dōgen's *hongaku* (original enlightenment, nothing to attain) cuts against the book. The reading complicates this: Dōgen's relation to *hongaku* is contested inside his own tradition. Noriaki Hakamaya's critical-Buddhism reading holds that Dōgen "strongly criticized" original-enlightenment thought and that the "practice after perfection" nuance (*honshō myōshu*) is exactly what he rejected. So Dōgen is not a nothing-to-attain quietist. The genuine remaining tension is narrower and sharper: Dōgen's zazen is "practice without the goal of becoming a buddha," while the book promises transformation and stages an arc toward a peak — so the fork is goallessness against a promised destination, not original-enlightenment against effort.

**Verdict for the note.** Dōgen is a genuine and close source for the anti-instrumentality clause, closer than the note first claimed, because he shares the book's insistence that already-there-ness still requires practice. The clause is grounded in his tradition's authoritative gloss quoting *Bendōwa*. The refusal is bounded to the goallessness point rather than to a blanket original-enlightenment quietism, and the note is updated to say so. The seating of Dōgen remains the author's.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The full *Bendōwa*, and the primary edition.** This is a reading of an authoritative essay quoting *Bendōwa*, not the full text; the surrounding argument and the *shōjō no shu* (practice-within-realization) material should be confirmed in a complete translation before manuscript use, and the terms checked in the Sino-Japanese.
- **Genjōkōan.** The oneness of practice and verification in *Genjōkōan*, and the "to study the self is to forget the self" line the note had also named, were not read here.
- **Whether Dōgen would accept the book's transformation promise.** The goallessness tension is now precisely stated but not resolved; only a fuller reading of Dōgen on attainment, beside the makyō material the book already holds, would settle how hard the fork bites.
