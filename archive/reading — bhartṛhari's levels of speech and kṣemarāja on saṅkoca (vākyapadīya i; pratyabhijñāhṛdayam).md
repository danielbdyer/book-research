# reading — bhartṛhari's levels of speech and kṣemarāja on saṅkoca (vākyapadīya i; pratyabhijñāhṛdayam)

**What was read.** Two readings, on 2026-09-02, as readings A3 and A7 of the practice-model program. **The levels of speech:** *Vākyapadīya* I in the GRETIL Sanskrit (Ramseier/Grünendahl) at verses 1.157–1.173, including the eleven verses the file marks with an asterisk; K. A. Subramania Iyer's translation with the *Vṛtti* and Vṛṣabhadeva's glosses at I.1, I.5, I.14, I.131, I.132, I.142, I.143; K. Raghavan Pillai's translation (1971) at verse 143 and note 36; Ṛgveda I.164.45 in Aufrecht's edition; on the Trika side, Abhinavagupta's *Parātrīśikā-vivaraṇa* in Jaideva Singh's translation (*A Trident of Wisdom*) and André Padoux, *Vāc*, which quotes the KSTS Sanskrit of the PTV and of *Tantrāloka* III with Jayaratha in its footnotes; and, for the attribution history, Desnitskaya (2024, 2025). **Saṅkoca:** Kṣemarāja's *Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam* in the GRETIL Sanskrit, read continuously for sūtras 4–11, 13–17 with the vṛtti, matched line for line against Jaideva Singh's translation with his page numbers; *Spanda Kārikā* II.5 in Singh's edition and as Kṣemarāja quotes it at sūtra 16. Grades: **A** for Bhartṛhari (primary Sanskrit, two independent published translations) and for the *Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam*; **A−** for Abhinavagupta (Singh's English and Padoux's Sanskrit footnotes, not a continuous reading of the PTV Sanskrit); Somānanda's *Śivadṛṣṭi* II stayed at secondary (Padoux p. 191; Desnitskaya's English abstract; Nemec's dissertation title). This builds on the 2026-08-28 Śaiva record, which grounded the bondage reading at sūtra 9 and left the productive one untested.

**How it is cited.** A numbering warning first: the same verse is **I.142** in Iyer, **143** in Pillai (Rau's kārikā count), and **1.159** in the GRETIL e-text, which counts the *Vṛtti*-only verses in sequence; the vault cites Iyer's number and says so. `VP I.142 (Iyer)`; `VP 1.164* (GRETIL)` for the asterisked definition-verses; `PTV, Singh p. 8`; `TĀ III.236 (Padoux p. 170)`; `PH sūtra 4 vṛtti, Singh p. 57`.

**Why this reading.** The ladder of speech is the one structure in the graph with an explicit anti-degradation clause — "the descent is not degradation — vaikharī is parā at full extension" — and so the cheapest way to fill the holarchy family's empty cell from inside the book's own source tradition; it was carried at corpus depth, and the vault's note had softened the corpus's correct attribution into an error ([[a word at madhyama can be perfected forever a word at vaikhari can be answered]]). And the live tension on contraction carried the treatment "test the classical reading at the primary text," with the author's reflection newly supplying a first-person report of a clench "that didn't obscure the vision but just made it transfigured" ([[the productive reading of contraction is the book's revaluation rather than the tradition's doctrine]]). The questions that could fail: whether the descent in the *Vākyapadīya* is an unfolding or a fall; whether any classical Trika source reads *saṅkoca* as productive.

## What the reading settled — the levels of speech

1. **Bhartṛhari names three; *parā* is the Trika's addition, made against him.** The only verse in the root text that names the levels: "*vaikharyā madhyamāyāśca paśyantyāścaitadadbhutam | anekatīrthabhedāyāstrayyā vācaḥ paraṃ padam*" (VP I.142, Iyer) — "the supreme and wonderful source of the knowledge of the **threefold** word… of the Vaikharī, the Madhyamā and the Paśyantī" (Iyer); "threefold as Vaikhari, Madhyama and Pas'yanti" (Pillai 143). *Para* occurs here only as the adjective in *paraṃ padam*, "the supreme step," said of grammar. The kārikās never define the three; the definitions sit in verses GRETIL marks with an asterisk (1.165*, 1.166*, 1.167*), which are exactly the ones Iyer's *Vṛtti* introduces with "Again, it has been said—". Bhartṛhari's own "fourth" runs downward: the *Vṛtti* quotes Ṛgveda I.164.45, "Three of them are kept in a cave and do not move. Men speak the fourth part of the word," so his *turīya* is the spoken quarter, the lowest. The one honest nuance: Iyer's note on I.14 records "a stage beyond Paśyantī called *Parā Prakṛtiḥ*" in the *Vṛtti* — reached by ascent, and not a named fourth level of *vāc*. The Trika's own testimony is unambiguous — Singh: "The grammarians believe that there are only three stages of vāk… The Trika philosophers, on the other hand, maintain that there are four" (*A Trident of Wisdom*, p. 126, n. 13); Padoux: "adding to vaikharī, madhyamā, and paśyantī a fourth (or rather a first) level which is above them and contains them: parāvāc" (*Vāc*, p. 168); "the absence, in Bhartṛhari (and in the Vṛtti), of parāvāc" (p. 188); "in chapter 2 of the Śivadṛṣṭi Somānanda strongly refutes Bhartṛhari's view according to which paśyantī is the supreme plane of the Word" (p. 191). The corpus itself had this right — atlas §VI, "Bhartṛhari's three, crowned with a fourth by the Śaivas" — and the vault's note had softened it.

2. **At Bhartṛhari the descent is a fall; the question fails there.** The soundless is ranked above the sounded: "*tayor api ca ghoṣiṇyā nirghoṣaiva garīyasī*" — "To the two which have resonance, that which has none at all is superior" (VP 1.164*). "The supreme form of the Seeing One is devoid of all correct forms, it is not mixed up and it is beyond worldly usage" (*Vṛtti* on I.142). The three definitions add sequence and body on the way down (paśyantī "indivisible and entirely without sequence… the Inner Light," 1.167*; madhyamā "has sequence and has the mind alone as its substratum," 1.166*; vaikharī "assumes the form of phonemes when the air strikes against the places of articulation," 1.165*). The soteriology climbs: grammar is "the door to salvation, the remedy for all the impurities of speech" (I.14), and the practitioner "goes beyond sequence… After having reached the undifferentiated state of the word, he comes to the source of all differentiation: Intuition (*pratibhā*)" (I.14 *Vṛtti*), taking his stand "on the word which lies beyond the activity of breath" (I.131 *Vṛtti*). The manifestation is *vivarta*, "the assumption by the One… of the unreal forms of others. It is like the appearances in a dream" (I.1 *Vṛtti*), and Brahman "soiled by Nescience." The one mitigation, kept: the descent does not defile — vaikharī "coloured by adventitious impurities, is not really affected by them" (1.170*), paśyantī "like the last digit of the moon, is never overwhelmed" (1.168*). It still does not complete.

3. **At Abhinavagupta the clause is his, real, and ontological rather than valuational.** "The parāvāk which is non-dual i.e. identical with the (supreme) consciousness is present in all experients always in her integral nature… uniformly in all states i.e., even at the level of paśyantī, madhyamā and vaikharī" (PTV, Singh p. 8); "up to vaikharī in which manifestation of difference of all the existents has proceeded fully, this parāvāk full of the wondrous delight of her own self… continues pulsating" (p. 9); "The state of vaikharī in the parā (supreme) state is not impossible" (p. 155; Sanskrit "*na hi tatraiva vaikharyā asambhavaḥ*," KSTS p. 158 via Padoux n. 123); "All the three… exist simultaneously in parā" (p. 157); Padoux's summary, "Vaikharī is nothing but the completion of the process commencing with paśyantī… from parā and, metaphysically, without actually ever leaving her" (p. 219). But the Trika still ranks — vaikharī is "gross" at *Tantrāloka* III.236, "so as to show that the other two states are subtle and supreme" (Jayaratha, Padoux p. 170) — its practice reverses the process by *śodhana* up to the Absolute (Padoux p. 219), and Kṣemarāja describes the descent of *vāc* as concealment: parā "conceals her real form as parā and produces in the empirical subject ever-new *vikalpa*-activity" (PH sūtra 12 commentary, Singh pp. 79–80). So the defensible form of the clause is that nothing of parā is lost on the way down — a real holarchy, which the vault keeps — and not that the uttered word is the goal.

## What the reading settled — saṅkoca

1. **Kṣemarāja holds three things the tension note assumed he did not.** The contraction is assumed out of freedom: "*prakāśa eva yataḥ svātantryāt gṛhīta-prāṇādi-saṃkocaḥ*" — consciousness "through the sovereignty of its free will, assumes the limitation of prāṇa etc." (PH sūtra 7 vṛtti, Singh p. 64); the Lord "conceals by His free will, pervasion of non-duality" (sūtra 9, pp. 71–72); Citi "having concealed her own nature, takes on contraction" (sūtra 5, p. 59). The contraction is itself consciousness: "*saṃkoco 'pi vicāryamāṇaḥ cid-aikātmyena prathamānatvāt cinmaya eva*" — "Contraction also, on close consideration, consists of *cit* only… otherwise… it becomes mere nothing" (sūtra 4 vṛtti, Singh p. 57) — the strongest line the reading found, and stronger than anything the vault carried. And degrees of contraction generate the knowers and their worlds: "as the contraction (of consciousness) is gradually less, there are the stages of Īśa, Sadāśiva and Anāśrita-Śiva… Where, however, contraction… is predominant, there occurs the knowership of the Void etc." (sūtra 5, pp. 59–60); the Lord "carries out the five processes even in the condition of *saṃsāra*, by limiting His consciousness-power" (sūtra 10, p. 74).

2. **The three named modes are all present at primary.** *Krīḍā*: Spanda Kārikā II.5, quoted at PH sūtra 16 — he "views the entire world as the play… and is liberated while alive" (Singh, *Spanda-Kārikās* p. 119; PH p. 92). *Svātantrya*, with the limited standpoint as a freely taken role: "this Self's assumed roles accepted of his own accord like the roles accepted by an actor" (sūtra 8, p. 65); "the differences in the roles are due to the various gradations in which that absolute free will either chooses to reveal or conceal itself" (p. 68) — concealing and unveiling two settings of one freedom. *Vilaya*: "With reference to its appearance as different, it is His act of concealment (*vilayakāritā*)" (sūtra 10, p. 75); "He performs these five acts eternally even when he assumes the form of an empirical ego" (Singh, Introduction p. 23), with *anugraha* "the *raison d'être* of the first four *kṛtyas*" (pp. 119–120).

3. **What he withholds is the valuation.** The same sūtra 9 vṛtti ends: "this (ātman) is called *saṃsārin*…, poor in Śakti. With the (full) unfoldment of his śaktis, however, he is Śiva himself" (pp. 72–73); sūtra 12 names the contracted condition "being deluded by one's own powers" (p. 79); the liberated are those who know "the universe as an unfoldment (*vikāsa*) of the essential nature" (p. 75); and Abhinavagupta uses *saṅkoca* in the phonematic emanation for a diminishing (PTV, Singh pp. 162–163). So the tradition supplies the book's mechanism — contraction as freely assumed, as itself consciousness, and as the condition under which a knower and a world appear — and withholds the book's commendation of the bounded vantage. "A locus is productive precisely because it is not everything," as praise, is the book's own, and it is a narrower thing than the tension assumed.

4. **Against the author's phrase.** Only the Trika supplies both halves of "a fundamental clenching that didn't obscure the vision but just made it transfigured as not directly perceivable": the experient "appears as if contracted (*saṃkucita iva ābhāti*), because his real nature is not manifested," and "the contraction also… consists of *cit* only" — nothing masked by something else; the same light wearing a contracted shape. Bhartṛhari supplies the "not damaged" half and refuses the other: what covers, for him, is *āgantuka mala* and *avidyā*, a veil in the strict sense, the word the author refused. One honest limit: both texts describe the change as one of *manifestation* (*anabhivyakta-svarūpatva*), which is close to and not the same as a vision fully present and merely not directly perceived; a drafting session should say "manifestation" where the tradition says it.
