# reading — wittgenstein (investigations, tractatus, on certainty)

**What was read.** Four Wittgenstein passages, at the primary texts, 2026-08-30, as part of grounding-backlog cluster B (Wittgenstein is the analytic-philosophy arrival the vault's independence sort names as its cleanest passing case): *Philosophical Investigations* §129 and the method passage around it (§§124, 126, 128, 133); the *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* ladder at 6.54; philosophy-as-therapy at PI §133 and §255; and *On Certainty*'s hinge propositions, §§341–343. No quotation is from memory.

**How it is cited.** By work and section number, with translation named. Grade A throughout (each section read directly). A cross-cutting caution: the vault's PI quotes match the **Anscombe 2nd/3rd edition** (1958/2001), not the reworded 4th edition (Anscombe/Hacker/Schulte), so locators should say "Anscombe 2nd/3rd ed." The *Tractatus* is cited in Pears & McGuinness (the vault's translation), with Ogden (public domain) available.

**Why this reading.** Three nascent or privated notes rested on Wittgenstein attributions taken from the mesh grid or a transmission dig, and the whole value of Wittgenstein to the book's centerpiece is that his agreement must be shown *with its kept difference intact* — he uses the same words and means something incompatible — because that difference is half the finding.

## What the reading settled

1. **§129, verbatim, with the vault's re-punctuation corrected** (grounds [[the always-there hides by being always there and wittgenstein said so without heidegger]], privated → full): "The aspects of things that are most important for us are hidden because of their simplicity and familiarity. (One is unable to notice something—because it is always before one's eyes.) The real foundations of his enquiry do not strike a man at all. Unless that fact has at some time struck him.—And this means: we fail to be struck by what, once seen, is most striking and most powerful." The vault had rendered the parenthetical as a semicolon clause; Anscombe's text is a period and parentheses.

2. **The kept difference is confirmed at the surrounding passage, and it is dissolve-not-disclose.** §129's always-there being surveyed is *grammar*, and noticing it dissolves a false problem rather than disclosing a truth: "Philosophy simply puts everything before us, and neither explains nor deduces anything.—...For what is hidden... is of no interest to us" (§126); "it leaves everything as it is" (§124, not §133 — a locator the vault had wrong); "the philosophical problems should completely disappear" (§133). The book gives the same observation the opposite, ontological reading (noticing *discloses* what was there to be disclosed), so the correspondence is real and the difference is essential.

3. **On Certainty's hinges are the closer parallel to the book's floor than §129** — a finding the mesh grid did not have. §129 is about what goes *unnoticed*; the hinges are what is *load-bearing*: "the questions that we raise and our doubts depend on the fact that some propositions are exempt from doubt, are as it were like hinges on which those turn" (§341), "if I want the door to turn, the hinges must stay put" (§343). That is the book's floor exactly. The kept difference is sharper here, and worth the trade: hinges hold fast by our *practice*, not by their truth (§342), so *On Certainty* buys a better architectural analogy at the price of an even more explicitly anti-ontological, practice-relative grounding.

4. **The Tractatus ladder, verbatim** (grounds [[a framework that teaches its own exit relates to authority differently than one seeking permanence]], nascent → privated): "anyone who understands me eventually recognizes them as nonsensical, when he has used them—as steps—to climb up beyond them. (He must, so to speak, throw away the ladder after he has climbed up it.)" (6.54, Pears & McGuinness). The sentence continues "He must transcend these propositions, and then he will see the world aright," so the truncation should not be presented as the whole of 6.54.

5. **Philosophy-as-therapy, verbatim, and the open question resolved** (grounds [[the later wittgenstein treats philosophy as therapy that dissolves confusions rather than a doctrine that advances theses]], nascent → full): "there is not a philosophical method, though there are indeed methods, like different therapies" (§133); "The philosopher's treatment of a question is like the treatment of an illness" (§255). The vault's open question — is "therapy" a verification standard (a problem dissolving felt as relief) or only a metaphor — resolves toward **metaphor/method**: both remarks are marked as simile ("like"), and the success criterion Wittgenstein states is the problem's *disappearance* ("difficulties eliminated"), not a bodily relief; the phenomenology of "peace" (§133) is gestured at but left unelaborated. This keeps the note distinct from the felt-shift verification material rather than fused to it.

## What the reading did not settle

- The **Kierkegaard revocation** (*Concluding Unscientific Postscript*) and **Plato's Seventh Letter** ("no treatise of mine") — the other two of the maker's-side self-negating trio in the framework-exit note — remain owed; only Wittgenstein's ladder of the three is now read.
- **On Certainty** was read at §§341–343 for the hinge image; a fuller reading of the surrounding argument would refine the practice-not-truth point.
- Wittgenstein's seat remains the author's; this reading moves the chair to primary-read and corrects the §129 `clause:` punctuation.
