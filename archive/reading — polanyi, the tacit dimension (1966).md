# reading — polanyi, the tacit dimension (1966)

**What was read.** Michael Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension* (Doubleday, 1966; Chicago reissue), read via reputable sources quoting the primary — the opening thesis and the face-recognition example confirmed verbatim across multiple independent sources (the University of Chicago Press edition's quoted text, infed.org's Polanyi article citing p. 4, and the standard Polanyi's-paradox literature), and the from-to / indwelling structure (subsidiary and focal awareness) confirmed at concept level and named at its page-level address (the first chapter, "Tacit Knowing"). The full primary was not read whole in-container: the one downloadable scan (monoskop) carries no text layer, so a text-extractable copy is owed before any further passage is quoted. This is the same standing the vault's Buber-tree and Abram/Kimmerer readings carry — read via reputable sources, honest about what is verbatim — and it discharges cluster B's Polanyi slot (a participation-law primary text, "five of six unread").

**How it is cited.** `Polanyi, The Tacit Dimension` with the page where the source establishes it (`p. 4` for the opening); the from-to structure is cited to the first chapter by name rather than by a verbatim sentence, since only its concept — not a single quotable line — was retrievable in-container. Quotations below are Polanyi's own words as confirmed across sources and are marked; the indwelling structure is named, not quoted.

**Why this reading.** Three via-positiva harvest notes lean on Polanyi from memory — that the unspoken exceeds and grounds the spoken, that a wordless knowing recognizes its own fit without stating its rule, and that a book can be dwelt in until its words go transparent — and the book's boldest promise ("you already know everything in this book") reads as a claim about the architecture of knowing that Polanyi states directly. The failable question: does Polanyi's own thesis carry "the unspoken exceeds the spoken and grounds it" and "we know more than we can specify," or is the harvest importing a stronger or different claim?

## What the reading settled

1. **"We can know more than we can tell" — the thesis is Polanyi's own opening, verbatim.** He begins *The Tacit Dimension*: "I shall reconsider human knowledge by starting from the fact that we can know more than we can tell" (Polanyi, The Tacit Dimension, p. 4). The unspoken is not a deficiency awaiting completion but the ground the explicit stands on — his claim that all explicit knowing rests on an unspecifiable tacit substrate. This grounds the Polanyi pole of `[[you know more than you can tell and that surplus is a reservoir the book draws on rather than a lack it fills]]`.

2. **The face example — a knowing certain of its object yet unable to state its grounds — verbatim.** "We know a person's face, and can recognize it among a thousand, indeed among a million. Yet we usually cannot tell how we recognize a face we know" (The Tacit Dimension). This is exactly the note's claim that a wordless knowing is precise to itself while unable to specify its rule, and it grounds the Polanyi pole of `[[your wordless knowing is precise to itself and recognizes its own right words by a felt fit you can trust]]` — the recognition sure of its target without a stateable criterion.

3. **Indwelling and the from-to structure — grounded at its page-level address.** Polanyi's central mechanism is that we attend *from* the subsidiary particulars we have interiorized *to* the focal whole; once a tool or a term is indwelt, attention runs from it to the world rather than to it. The structure is named at the first chapter (not a single verbatim line was retrievable in-container), and it grounds the Polanyi pole of `[[there is a knowing-through as well as a knowing-about and the book asks to be dwelt in until its words go transparent]]` — the words going transparent when dwelt in.

4. **The refusal is kept, and it is Polanyi's own.** Polanyi is emphatically not anti-articulation: the tacit is the ground *for* speech, and explicit articulation "vastly extends" the powers of the mind. And the pianist who turns her focal attention onto her own fingers falters — attending *to* what should be dwelt *in* dissolves the performance — so "transparency" is the gift and over-analysis its shadow, kept strictly as shadow. The book's use of Polanyi is for the awakening-not-implanting architecture, not for a mysticism of the ineffable.

**Verdict.** The Polanyi pole of the three tacit-knowing notes is grounded — two passages verbatim, the third structure named at address — and each note moves nascent → privated, its companion pole owed (Gendlin's *A Process Model* and *Focusing* for two, William James's acquaintance/knowledge-about for the third). Polanyi is already seated (`[[michael polanyi]]`, the one clean outside witness to the participation law); this reading confirms the tacit-knowing attributions the harvest made and leaves the seating untouched.
