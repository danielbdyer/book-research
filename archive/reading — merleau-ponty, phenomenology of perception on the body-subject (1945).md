# reading — merleau-ponty, phenomenology of perception on the body-subject (1945)

**What was read.** Merleau-Ponty's account of the lived body (*le corps propre*) as the perceiving subject, in *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945) and *The Visible and the Invisible* (1964) — read via the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's Merleau-Ponty entry, which cites the primary by pagination (PP, V&I), retrieved 2026-08-10. The primary texts are under copyright in-container and were not read whole; the French was not read. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 40.

**How it is cited.** `Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception` and `Merleau-Ponty, The Visible and the Invisible`, with the encyclopedia's page citations (PP: 245/209; V&I: 187/142, 184/139, 179/136). Quotations below are the encyclopedia's, some of them direct quotations of Merleau-Ponty; the verbatim primary and the French await a copyright edition.

**Why this reading.** [[merleau-ponty holds the body as the perceiving subject rather than an instrument that certifies]] holds that his lived body fills the embodied blind spot — the body that certifies nothing — by refusing the felt-shift-as-test framing. The failable question: does Merleau-Ponty make the body the perceiving subject rather than an object or an instrument?

## What the reading settled

1. **The body is the perceiving subject, not an instrument — confirmed.** The body has a distinctive mode of existence, "being-toward-the-world — a pre-objective orientation toward a vital situation," and "the body itself is experienced in ways that distinguish it in kind from all other things." It is "not external to perception but constitutive of it — the perceiving subject itself": "One's own body is in the world just as the heart is in the organism" (PP: 245/209). So the body is neither an object I have nor a tool I use — which is exactly the body the book's verification standard has no place for, since the body that certifies nothing has no standing when the body's job is to be a test.

2. **The reversibility and the flesh deepen it.** The two-hands-touching reversibility — "double sensations… that enact a form of reflexivity," "The handshake too is reversible" (V&I: 187/142) — and the flesh (*la chair*), "a prototype of Being" (V&I: 179/136), make the body ontologically fundamental rather than derivative from consciousness, and Merleau-Ponty "resists reducing flesh to subjective projection." The lived body is the ground of there being a world at all.

**Verdict for the note.** Merleau-Ponty fills the embodied blind spot, and he fills it by refusing the very commitment that created it: his body-subject refuses the body-as-instrument, so he is company for the embodied and a standing refusal of the felt-shift-as-test framing at once. The note moves from privated to full. The re-file holds — he was shelved under the relational field, where de Jaegher and Di Paolo make him redundant; the body-subject is where nothing is seated. His independence is partial: he shares phenomenological method with Husserl and Heidegger, but the lived-body analysis is where he departs from Heidegger's bodiless Dasein.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The primary, and the French.** Read via the encyclopedia, not *Phenomenology of Perception* or *The Visible and the Invisible* whole; the verbatim text and the French (*le corps propre*, *la chair*) await copyright editions, and the flesh ontology is literally unfinished, since Merleau-Ponty died mid-work in 1961.
- **Whether the body-subject can host any verification at all.** The refusal is stated: if the body is a subject and not an instrument, what becomes of the felt shift as a test? Whether Merleau-Ponty leaves any room for the book's standard, or refuses it outright, is a fuller reading's question.
