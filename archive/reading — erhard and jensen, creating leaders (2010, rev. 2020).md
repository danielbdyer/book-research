# reading — erhard and jensen, creating leaders (2010, rev. 2020)

**What was read.** Werner Erhard and Michael C. Jensen, with Kari L. Granger, "Creating Leaders: An Ontological/Phenomenological Model," Harvard Business School NOM Unit Research Paper No. 11-037, Barbados Group Working Paper No. 10-10, Simon School Working Paper No. FR-10-30. Dated 22 August 2010, revised 24 January 2016, second revision 26 October 2020. An earlier version is chapter 16 of *The Handbook for Teaching Leadership* (Snook, Nohria and Khurana, eds.). Read complete — seventy-two pages, about 23,100 words — from the SSRN copy (abstract 1681682), retrieved 2026-08-07, extracted with pypdf.

**How it is cited.** By the paper's own lettered sections and page numbers, which are stable across the SSRN revisions: "Erhard and Jensen 2010/2020, §I." Quotations are verbatim; the extraction preserves the text cleanly and nothing was altered.

**Why this paper.** The author asked on 2026-08-07 to look into Landmark, est and Heidegger more deeply. The vault already holds this cluster as personal provenance rather than as outside research: the author did the Landmark Forum in 2017, encountered Heidegger and est through it, and carried the three as one unit for seven years before the scholarly genealogy was written ([[the central correspondence was lived nine years before it was assembled]]). The evaluation at [[werner erhard]] recorded his works as "the est training and the Landmark Forum curriculum (no primary text)."

## What the reading settled

**1. The record that he has no primary text is false, and the correction is large.** Erhard has co-authored a peer-circulated academic literature stating his model in his own words. This paper is one item; it names an eleven-year course development, cites its companion course materials as 795 downloadable pages on SSRN (abstract 1263835), and lists further papers by the same authors. A vault claim that the Landmark room has no readable statement of itself cannot stand.

**2. He cites Heidegger directly, by book and page, and says what he takes.** His footnote 4: "Ontology as we use the term is meant as it is explicated by Martin Heidegger (1927) in *Being and Time*, his groundbreaking book on ontology and its methodology of phenomenology," and he rules out the metaphysical senses — "not ontology in its medieval metaphysical sense… not as a synonym for 'noumenon'." His footnote 5 states the debt: "We are indebted to Heidegger, and to those other thinkers who drew on Heidegger's ideas in their own work in the field, for having developed ontology and phenomenology as rigorous disciplines that we were able to draw on." He quotes *Being and Time* at page 33: "Ontology is possible only as phenomenology."

**3. The thing he takes is the clearing, which is Heidegger's disclosive structure rather than a theory of speech.** "It is as though the context for leader and leadership that a person brings to a leadership situation creates a kind of 'clearing' in which that situation shows up for them, and that clearing shapes and colors the way in which that situation occurs for that person. Specifically, that clearing (context) determines the way in which the circumstances they are confronted with occur or show up for them, and what they can see of possible futures" (§I, "The Context Is Decisive").

**4. The operation he performs on the clearing is creation, which is where the corpus's fault line is correctly drawn.** The course gives students "an opportunity to examine and eliminate the grip of their everyday common-sense worldview and their existing frames of reference" and then "an opportunity to create for themselves a context for leader and leadership" (§I). The corpus's contrast between his speech-that-makes and Heidegger's speech-that-lets is therefore aimed at the right place, and it is aimed one level lower than the corpus states it: the two are not working on different objects but applying different verbs to the same one.

**5. His own formulation of mastery is middle-voiced, which is the resolution the corpus proposes for the fault line it draws.** "We call the kind of context that has the power to leave you with the being and actions of effective leadership as your natural self-expression 'a context that uses you'. When a context uses you, there is nothing to remember and no rules to apply." He contrasts it with knowing something: "When you have learned something, that is, when you have an epistemological grasp of it, appropriately you remember what you learned and apply it. However, there can be a point where what you have been trying to learn actually becomes a part of you… instead of you using what you have learned, it has become for you 'second nature', it so to speak uses you."

**6. His access distinction is the book's verification stance in other words.** He separates learning "as being and action are lived and experienced on the court" from learning them "as being and action are observed and commented on from the stands," and states that only the first gives "actionable access." The book's own rule is that nothing counts until the body countersigns it, which is the same refusal of third-person knowledge as sufficient.

**7. He names a lineage the vault does not hold.** The course's method of dislodging existing frames is credited to Jack Mezirow's transformational learning (Mezirow 2000), which appears nowhere in this vault.

## What the reading did not settle

- **Whether the est training and the Landmark Forum teach what this paper teaches.** This is a leadership course for university and executive students, developed from the same ontological approach and taught by the same author. It is not a transcript of the Forum, and no claim here about the Forum's content rests on it.
- **The scholarly treatment of the Erhard–Heidegger relation.** Bruce Hyde and Drew Kopp, *Speaking Being: Werner Erhard, Martin Heidegger, and a New Possibility of Being Human* (Wiley, 2019), is a full-length academic study that transcribes a Landmark Forum and reads it against Heidegger. It was verified as existing and was not read.
- **The safety record.** The est adverse-effects literature was verified at citation depth on 2026-08-07 and not read: Glass, Kirsch and Parris, "Psychiatric disturbances associated with Erhard Seminars Training: I. A report of cases," *American Journal of Psychiatry* 134(3), 1977, 245–247, reporting five cases of psychotic symptoms of whom one had a prior psychiatric history, with a second paper at 134(11), 1254; Higgitt and Murray, "A psychotic episode following Erhard Seminars Training," *Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica*, 1983; and the review by Finkelstein, Wenegrat and Yalom, "Large Group Awareness Training," *Annual Review of Psychology* 33, 1982, 515–539. That last is the closest thing the vault has found to an adverse-effects literature for a designed transformation container of the book's own shape.
