# reading — aristotle, metaphysics IX.6 on energeia and kinesis

**What was read.** Aristotle, *Metaphysics* Book IX (Theta), chapter 6 — the distinction between *energeia* (actuality, an activity complete at every moment) and *kinesis* (movement, incomplete until its end) — in the W. D. Ross public-domain translation at the Internet Classics Archive (classics.mit.edu), retrieved 2026-08-10. The *praxis*/*poiesis* distinction of *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.4–5 was named in the note but not separately fetched here. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 35.

**How it is cited.** `Aristotle, Metaphysics IX.6` (Bekker ≈ 1048b18–36), the stable locator that holds across editions. Quotations are Ross's and are marked below; read in translation, not the Greek, and *energeia*/*kinesis* should be checked against a scholarly edition before manuscript use — the same caution the vault's Aristotle-on-privation reading recorded.

**Why this reading.** [[aristotle distinguishes activity complete in itself from movement toward an outside end]] named Aristotle the canonical Western philosophical arrival at the book's practice-is-realization clause, standing privated on the loci. The failable question: does Θ.6 actually distinguish an activity complete in itself from a movement toward an outside end, in Aristotle's own words?

## What the reading settled

1. **The distinction is confirmed, verbatim.** Θ.6: "at the same time we are seeing and have seen, are understanding and have understood, are thinking and have thought (while it is not true that at the same time we are learning and have learnt, or are being cured and have been cured). At the same time we are living well and have lived well, and are happy and have been happy." The concluding line: "The latter sort of process, then, I call an actuality, and the former a movement." An activity whose end is present in it at every moment — seeing, thinking, living well — is an actuality (*energeia*); a process whose end lies outside and after it — learning, being cured, building — is a movement (*kinesis*). A practice of the first kind is not a means; it is already its own completion, which is the book's clause that a practice expresses realization rather than aiming at it.

**Verdict for the note.** Aristotle is a genuine independent arrival at the anti-instrumentality clause: Θ.6 gives it the canonical Western philosophical form. With Dōgen and Deci and Ryan (both read the same day) the clause is now a confirmed three-tradition convergence — Zen, experimental psychology, and Greek philosophy. The note moves from privated to full. The refusal holds and is the same shape the privation reading found: *energeia* is actualization under a teleological metaphysics, which sits closer to the book's genuine-novelty fork than to recognition-as-un-forgetting, so Aristotle warrants the structure of the clause and not the recognition frame around it.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The Greek, and NE VI.** Read in Ross's translation, not the Greek; the *praxis*/*poiesis* distinction of *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.4–5, and the "activity is its own end" of *NE* X on contemplation, were named but not separately read here, and would deepen the arrival.
- **Whether energeia's teleology can host recognition.** The refusal is stated, not resolved: whether the book's un-forgetting can be described in Aristotelian actuality-terms without importing his teleology is a comparison a fuller reading would test.
