# reading — heidegger, being and time II on being-toward-death (1927)

**What was read.** Heidegger's being-toward-death (*Sein-zum-Tode*) in *Being and Time* (1927), Division Two — read at the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's Heidegger entry, which characterizes and cites the primary by its German pagination (SZ), retrieved 2026-08-10. The primary text (§§46–53) is under copyright in-container and was not read whole; the German was not read. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 36.

**How it is cited.** `Heidegger, Being and Time §§46–53`, with the SZ page citations the encyclopedia gives (SZ 250, 263, 266, 267–8). Quotations below are the encyclopedia's renderings of Heidegger; the verbatim primary and the German terms (*eigenste*, *unbezüglich*, *unüberholbar*) should be confirmed against Macquarrie-Robinson or Stambaugh before manuscript use.

**Why this reading.** [[heidegger's being-toward-death is the finite the recognition doctrine leaves behind]] holds that being-toward-death is the unmined and already-refused half of the book's own foundation — the finite the recognition doctrine has no organ for. The failable question: is death characterized as the ownmost, non-relational, not-to-be-outstripped finitude that powers authentic disclosure, and does everydayness function as flight from it, the point the book refuses at "the tuesday is the temple"?

## What the reading settled

1. **Being-toward-death is confirmed as the finitude that individualizes and grounds authenticity.** Death is "the possibility of the absolute impossibility of Dasein" (SZ 250); "the anticipation of death… individualizes Dasein down to itself" (SZ 263); and "authentic Dasein experiences in its finitude a liberating chance to take ownership of itself. Rather than fleeing from death, authentic existence will anticipate or run forward toward death" (SZ 266). Finitude is not incidental — anticipating death (*Vorlaufen*) is what makes authentic, owned existence possible, and it individualizes Dasein: the non-relational point, that no one can take my dying from me. This is the finite as constitutive, which the recognition doctrine — nothing lost, only concealed — has no organ for.

2. **Everydayness as flight from death — confirmed, and it is the book's refusal.** "Inauthentic Dasein flees from this experience of nothingness, doubling down on its conformist adherence to everyday norms and identities" (SZ 267–8). Early Heidegger reads absorption in the ordinary as a falling-away from the confrontation with finitude. The book's axiom "the tuesday is the temple" affirms exactly the ordinary that early Heidegger reads as flight, which is why the vault's Heidegger note already records this as the book's second refusal, and why mining the finite here means facing the book's own foundation turned against it.

**Verdict for the note.** The being-toward-death structure and the everydayness-flight point are confirmed, at scholarly-secondary depth with primary page citations. Being-toward-death is genuinely the unmined and already-refused half of the book's foundation: the book kept Heidegger's disclosure and left his finitude, and positioned itself against it. The note moves from privated to full, with the caveat that the primary §§46–53 and the German remain for an author-supplied text. The refusal is double: Heidegger's death is non-relational, which contradicts "grief cannot complete alone," so the finite strains the relational at once.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The primary, §§46–53, and the German.** Read via the encyclopedia, not *Being and Time* itself; the verbatim text and the terms *eigenste*/*unbezüglich*/*unüberholbar* await a copyright text the container cannot reach, and this reading shares the gate with the queued aletheia-retraction reading.
- **Whether the book can hold a non-relational death beside a relational grief.** The double strain is named, not resolved; that is the author's, and it is what the clearing keeps open.
