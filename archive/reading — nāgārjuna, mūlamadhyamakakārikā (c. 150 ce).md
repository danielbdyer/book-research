# reading — nāgārjuna, mūlamadhyamakakārikā (c. 150 ce)

**What was read.** The **Mūlamadhyamakakārikā (MMK)**, Nāgārjuna's root verses, across the chapters the reading-queue entry 93 names — the emptiness-of-*svabhāva*, no-self, no-reification, two-truths, and nirvāṇa chapters — read at the primary Sanskrit and two reputable translations, and set against the Advaita side (already read) to test whether the disagreement is genuine. Sources: the Sanskrit at **GRETIL** (Bachman/Mahoney IAST, cross-checked against the corpusTEI recension) and **Siderits & Katsura, *Nāgārjuna's Middle Way*** (Wisdom, 2013, printing de Jong's critical edition and translating *from the Sanskrit*); **Garfield, *The Fundamental Wisdom of the Middle Way*** (Oxford, 1995, from the Tibetan); the comparative apparatus from **Jan Westerhoff** (the SEP "Nāgārjuna" entry is his) and **Richard King, *Early Advaita Vedānta and Buddhism*** (SUNY, 1995); the Advaita side at the **Chāndogya Upaniṣad** 6.8.7 and **Śaṅkara's Brahmasūtrabhāṣya** II.2.31–32 (Thibaut). No quotation is from memory.

**How it is cited.** By chapter.verse plus translator and edition — `Nāgārjuna, Mūlamadhyamakakārikā 13.8 (Siderits & Katsura)` — the canonical locator of record; the Sanskrit is given in IAST (GRETIL / de Jong). Prefer **Siderits & Katsura** for fidelity to the Sanskrit; **Garfield** translates from the Tibetan and diverges in sense at two load-bearing points noted below. No Nāgārjuna interlocutor chair exists in the vault; this reading, like the Plato reading, produces a record without seating a chair — and it makes Nāgārjuna a strong chair **candidate** for the author (load: adversary / independent-arrival, seat his to decide), given how load-bearing he already is in the corpus's own architecture.

**Why this reading.** `[[you are already free and the work removes a misunderstanding rather than adding an attainment]]` stakes the book's most important preserved disagreement on one seam: Advaita and Madhyamaka reach the same *move* (liberation is un-forgetting, nothing is attained, a mistake is dropped) by *opposite findings* — Advaita a permanent Self to recognize, Madhyamaka no self and emptiness of all inherent existence. If the Madhyamaka side reads "soft," the note collapses into "they all point to the same thing," which the stance forbids. The failable question: is Nāgārjuna's refusal to reify genuinely as hard as Advaita's affirmation? The answer is yes — decisively.

## What the reading settled

1. **The refusal is hard — Nāgārjuna refuses to reify a self, emptiness, or even "no-self."** No inherently-existent self: the self is neither the aggregates nor other than them, and "the self not existing, how will there be what belongs to a self?" (MMK 18.1–2). Emptiness is not itself a thing or a view: "Emptiness is taught by the conquerors as the expedient to get rid of all views. But those for whom emptiness is a view have been called **incurable**" (*asādhya*, MMK 13.8, Siderits & Katsura; Garfield's Tibetan-based "will accomplish nothing" is softer — his own note 76 grants that *asādhya* "can also be translated 'incurable'," so the harder rendering is faithful). And the sharpest edge: Nāgārjuna will not let even *no-self* harden into a positive doctrine — "the buddhas have taught that there is a self, and the doctrine of no-self, as well as the doctrine of neither self nor nonself" (MMK 18.6). Westerhoff states the upshot: Nāgārjuna "denies the existence of an ontological foundation," and emptiness "is not some kind of primordial reality *ante rem* but a corrective to a mistaken view of how the world exists" (SEP, "Nāgārjuna," §2). This grounds the "kept counter" pole of the core note at primary.

2. **The disagreement with Advaita is the two traditions' own, not the vault's imposition.** The axis is exact — Richard King: the Mahāyāna–Advaita debate "centered upon the applicability (or not) of the concept of a self (ātman) to ultimate reality," Advaita postulating "an Absolute (i.e. Brahman)" where Madhyamaka rejects absolutism ("no doctrinal formulation is acceptable in the ultimate sense … no statement (pratijñā) to put forward, or view to establish (dṛṣṭi)"). The traditions policed the boundary in both directions: Advaitins were charged as "crypto-Buddhists" (*pracchanna-bauddha*) by rival Vedāntins, Gauḍapāda specifically for deriving *ajātivāda* from Madhyamaka, while Bhāvaviveka charged the inverse. And Śaṅkara himself refutes the śūnyavāda in the Brahmasūtrabhāṣya (II.2.31, Thibaut): the doctrine "that everything is empty … is contradicted by all means of right knowledge," and he affirms against it "an absolutely unchangeable (Self) which cognises everything." So the Advaita affirmation of a plenary Self ("*tat tvam asi*," Chāndogya 6.8.7) and the Madhyamaka refusal of any positive ground are the traditions' own opposition — the seam is real.

3. **Madhyamaka reaches "nothing is attained / already free" — but by emptiness, not by a Self.** Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are not different — "there is no distinction whatsoever between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa … not even the finest gap" (MMK 25.19–20); the goal is "the pacification of all apprehending (*sarvopalambha*), the pacification of hypostatization (*prapañca*)," and "no dharma whatsoever was ever taught by the Buddha to anyone" (MMK 25.24, echoing the maṅgala's "*prapañcopaśamaṃ śivam*"); "fabrication ceases through emptiness" (MMK 18.5). Garfield's commentary: nirvāṇa "is not achieved," "does not amount to an entity … a way of engagement with nonentities by nonentities," "saṃsāra seen without reification." So Nāgārjuna arrives at the same *move* the book isolates — nothing added, already here — by the opposite *finding*. Both phrasings are load-bearing: Advaita's "one can only attain that which already is" because "Only Brahman is" (King), Madhyamaka's because there is no ground and nothing with *svabhāva* to gain.

4. **Emptiness is not nihilism — which bears on the separate "nihility" question.** Nāgārjuna equates emptiness with dependent origination and the middle way — "whatever is dependently co-arisen, that is emptiness; that, being a dependent designation, is itself the middle way" (MMK 24.18) — and makes emptiness the condition of everything's coherence: "all is possible when emptiness is possible; nothing is possible when emptiness is impossible" (MMK 24.14). Westerhoff: "the Mādhyamikas themselves are very clear that their position avoids both extreme views, the existence of *svabhāva* as well as its nihilistic opposite"; King: "Emptiness is not mere nothingness!", the two truths (MMK 24.8–10) circumventing nihilism. Nāgārjuna's refusal to reify emptiness (13.8) is therefore a guard against mistaking any reified state — a "false peak," a held recognition — for an inherently existing thing, which is why the corpus seats him as "the guard-rail against counterfeit *vikāsa*."

## What the reading did not settle

- **The Dzogchen pole (*ka dag*, primordial purity) on the core note is still owed** — cited from memory; the Advaita and Madhyamaka poles are now read, so the note's central disagreement is grounded on both sides, but the third convergent voice awaits its own reading.
- **The Nishitani "nihility" question is not settled by this reading, only sharpened.** What is now solid is that Madhyamaka's *śūnyatā* is explicitly not a nihilistic void (item 4). Reputable secondary sources indicate Nishitani himself treats "nihility" (relative nothingness) as a station passed *through* toward *śūnyatā* — his *Religion and Nothingness* Essay 3 is "Nihility and Śūnyatā," and (worth noting) he never cites Nāgārjuna directly there — so whether nihility is a distinct station the book's contraction scheme cannot represent still requires Nishitani's own text, which is unread. `[[nihility may be a station the contraction scheme cannot represent]]` stays an open question.
- **Grade and edition caveats.** The MMK Sanskrit is verified at GRETIL and de Jong (S&K); minor edition variants are flagged in the reading (18.2 *-nayoḥ*/*-yayoḥ*; 24.9 *gambhīraṃ*/*gambhīre*; 24.11 the retroflex *durdṛṣṭā*, a GRETIL digitization artifact). The Westerhoff and King statements are reputable-secondary (read verbatim at source); Śaṅkara and the Chāndogya are Thibaut/Lokeswarananda translations. Westerhoff's monograph *Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka* (2009) did not download in usable form, mitigated by his authorship of the SEP entry.
