# reading — laozi, daodejing (the wu-wei, ziran, and subtraction chapters)

**What was read.** The *Daodejing* (*Tao Te Ching*), chapters 2, 10, 17, 37, 40, 48, and 51 — the *wu-wei*, *ziran*, and subtraction chapters — read 2026-08-30, extending the vault's earlier reading of chapters 11 and 28 (`archive/reading — laozi, daodejing 11 and 28 (legge trans.).md`). Triangulated across four English translations read directly — James Legge (1891, Gutenberg #216), Arthur Waley (1934), D. C. Lau (1963), and Gia-Fu Feng & Jane English (1972), all at terebess.hu — against the classical Chinese of the Wang Bi recension (王弼本, zh.wikisource). This closes the earlier record's own open items (the *wu-wei* chapters, and a modern translation of *pu*).

**How it is cited.** By chapter number, which holds across editions, with the translator named because the renderings diverge in load-bearing ways: `Daodejing ch. 37 (Lau)`; the classical Chinese given where confirmed. Grade A throughout (each chapter read directly in at least two translations and checked against the Chinese), except Ellen Chen's ch. 40 rendering, which could not be opened directly (Grade C).

**Why this reading.** The vault cites Laozi across some two dozen notes — the facilitation moves (the helper who lets, withdraws, and is barely known), the subtraction path, the open center — mostly "from memory, unverified," and it often pairs him with Heidegger's *Gelassenheit* as a co-arrival at "letting." Two failable questions: do the chapters carry the *wu-wei*/*ziran* doctrine the notes attribute, and is the Heidegger–Laozi pairing a convergence of independent arrivals or a lineage?

## What the reading settled

1. **The *wu-wei* / wordless-teaching doctrine is exact (ch. 2, 37).** Ch. 2: the sage "keeps to the deed that consists in taking no action and practises the teaching that uses no words" (Lau; 是以聖人處無為之事，行不言之教). Ch. 37: "The way never acts, yet nothing is left undone" (Lau; 道常無為而無不為). Effortless non-forcing action that leaves nothing undone — grounds `[[the helper's restraint is the active ingredient rather than the absence of one and it is trained]]` and `[[the helper's core verb is to let, to let learn and let be and let grow]]` and `[[teaching without words keeps the astonishing thing a live mystery the person meets directly]]`.

2. **The *ziran* "we did it ourselves" chapter, with an ambiguity that matters (ch. 17).** The best ruler is a "shadowy presence" the people barely know (Lau), and "when his task is accomplished and his work done, the people all say, 'It happened to us naturally'" (Lau; 功成事遂，百姓皆謂：我自然). Two corrections: the vault's "we did it ourselves" matches no major scholarly translation verbatim (closest is Feng & English's "People say, 'We did it!'"); and 自然 (*ziran*) is genuinely ambiguous between spontaneity (Waley/Lau, "of its own accord" / "naturally") and self-credit (Feng & English, "We did it!") — the facilitation move rests on the agency sense, which the chapter supports but does not force. A textual variant: cite Waley or Lau, not Legge, for "the people barely know he exists" (Legge follows the 不知有之 variant). Grounds `[[the helper's standing back is what keeps the change the person's own]]` and `[[the surest sign a helper has done their work is that the person says we did it ourselves]]`.

3. **The subtraction path is exact (ch. 48, 28, 40).** Ch. 48: "In the pursuit of learning one knows more every day; in the pursuit of the way one does less every day. One does less and less until one does nothing at all" (Lau; 為學日益，為道日損). Ch. 28: the return to the uncarved block — Waley renders 樸 "the Uncarved Block," the modern term the earlier record wanted. Ch. 40: "Turning back is how the way moves" (Lau; 反者道之動); the transliterated "Returning (fan) is the movement (tung) of Tao" is Ellen Chen's, not Legge's. Grounds `[[there is a homeward path that goes by subtraction, letting go of what was added]]`.

4. **The wu-wei of enabling without owning (ch. 10, 51).** The same line closes both chapters: "Rear them, but do not lay claim to them. Control them, but never lean upon them; Be chief among them, but do not manage them" (Waley; 生而不有，為而不恃，長而不宰) — Waley tracks the Chinese more literally than Legge. The helper who enables without possessing.

5. **The genealogy: Laozi is a source, not a parallel.** The Daodejing carries a fully-formed, technically-vocabularied doctrine of effortless non-forcing action some twenty-four centuries before Heidegger, and the channel is historically real — Heidegger read Laozi, quoted him ("Tao... properly speaking means way"), and in 1946 attempted a translation of the Daodejing with Paul Shih-yi Hsiao. So where the book pairs "Heidegger's *Gelassenheit* and Laozi's *wu-wei*" as co-arrivals at "letting," the honest relation is a lineage (Laozi the ancestor), not an independent convergence; recorded at `[[heidegger's letting and way descend from daoism so pairing him with laozi is lineage not convergence]]`. Convergence language stays legitimate for traditions with no contact with Laozi (Pratyabhijñā, the practice lineages).

6. **The kept difference — the adversary edge.** The Daodejing dissolves the striving self and returns to the undifferentiated (復歸於嬰兒, return to infancy; 復歸於樸, to the uncarved block; 損之又損, subtraction upon subtraction) rather than restoring a sovereign first person — a genuine adversary to the book's restored "I." And *wu-wei* is the fruit of long cultivation, never laziness (Waley's tag on ch. 28: "the greatest carver does the least cutting").

## What the reading did not settle

- **The classical Chinese at depth.** The renderings were checked against the Wang Bi base text, but the reading is of the English translations; a Sinologist's edition (or the Mawangdui/Guodian recensions, which differ at ch. 37 and elsewhere) would refine any line taken to the manuscript.
- **Laozi has no interlocutor chair.** Despite being cited across some two dozen notes, Laozi is unseated. Following the Plato and Nāgārjuna precedent, this reading produces a record without creating a chair: **Laozi is a strong chair candidate the author may seat** (a source-tradition or reference-lineage seat; load likely independent-arrival for the traditions with no contact with him, and *precedent* / ancestor where the book's Heideggerian vocabulary descends from him). The seating is the author's.
