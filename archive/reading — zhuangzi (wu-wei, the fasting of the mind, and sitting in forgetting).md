# reading — zhuangzi (wu-wei, the fasting of the mind, and sitting in forgetting)

**What was read.** The *Zhuangzi* (*Chuang Tzu*): the Cook Ding passage (ch. 3, 養生主), the fasting of the mind (*xinzhai*, ch. 4, 人間世), the useless tree (ch. 1 and ch. 4), sitting in forgetting (*zuowang*, ch. 6, 大宗師), and the mind as mirror (ch. 7, 應帝王) — read 2026-08-30 in James Legge's (SBE 39/40) and Herbert Giles's public-domain translations, checked against the classical Chinese at ctext.org and zh.wikisource. Grade A throughout (each passage read directly in Legge and/or Giles with the Chinese confirmed).

**How it is cited.** By chapter, with translator named (Legge and Giles diverge): `Zhuangzi ch. 3 (Legge)`; classical Chinese given where confirmed.

**Why this reading.** The vault uses Zhuangzi for *wu-wei* as trained mastery and for forgetting-as-return, mostly unverified, and the Reinhard May reading had established a documented Heidegger→Zhuangzi contact channel that made one vault note influence-suspect. The failable questions: do the passages carry what the notes attribute, and what exactly did Heidegger have access to?

## What the reading settled

1. **Cook Ding grounds *wu-wei* as trained skill (ch. 3).** The cook cuts by "the natural lines," works "in a spirit-like manner, and do not look at it with my eyes" (臣以神遇，而不以目視), his knife "in use for nineteen years... as sharp as if it had newly come from the whetstone," and the mastery is explicitly developed — "When I first began... I saw nothing but the (entire) carcase. After three years I ceased to see it as a whole. Now..." — Lord Wen Hui learning from it "the nourishment of life" (得養生焉). This is the anchor that effortless action is the fruit of long cultivation, not a slackening; grounds `[[the helper's restraint is the active ingredient rather than the absence of one and it is trained]]`.

2. **The fasting of the mind — receptive emptying (ch. 4).** "Do not listen with your ears but with your mind; do not listen with your mind but with your qi... the spirit is free from all pre-occupation and so waits for things... such freedom is the fasting of the mind" (Legge; 無聽之以耳而聽之以心；無聽之以心而聽之以氣...唯道集虛。虛者，心齋也). The emptying that makes one receptive — the Daoist form of the prepared, receptive clearing. The third term is 氣 (*qi*): Legge renders it "the spirit," Giles "your soul"; Legge is the better public-domain anchor. New grounding, available to the receptive-clearing and facilitation notes.

3. **The use of the useless (ch. 1, ch. 4).** The great tree is spared the axe because its wood is good for nothing, and "All men know the advantage of being useful, but no one knows the advantage of being useless" (Legge, ch. 4 close; 無用之用, "the use of the useless"). The popular "usefulness of the useless" is a paraphrase; the verbatim is "the advantage of being useless."

4. **Sitting in forgetting confirmed (ch. 6).** "My connexion with the body and its parts is dissolved; my perceptive organs are discarded. Thus leaving my material form, and bidding farewell to my knowledge, I am become one with the Great Pervader. This I call sitting and forgetting all things" (Legge; 墮肢體，黜聰明，離形去知，同於大通，此謂坐忘). The vault's wording is confirmed as Legge, with one fidelity fix — SBE 39 reads "connexion," now corrected in `[[remembering and forgetting name one movement back to the ground you never left]]`. (大通: Legge "the Great Pervader"; standard scholarship "the Great Thoroughfare.")

5. **The mind as mirror (ch. 7).** "For the perfect man employs his mind as a mirror. It grasps nothing: it refuses nothing. It receives, but does not keep" (Giles; 至人之用心若鏡，不將不迎，應而不藏). This is Giles's phrasing, not Legge's (Legge's is substantially different) — the non-grasping receptive posture, available to the facilitation and clearing notes.

6. **The Heidegger contact channel, corrected.** Heidegger read Buber's 1910 *Reden und Gleichnisse des Tschuang-Tse* by 1930 — but that book is a *selection* (~54 parables plus Buber's afterword, made mainly from Giles's English), not "the whole Zhuangzi" as the vault's May record had said; corrected there, with the exact contents flagged for confirmation against Jonathan Herman's *I and Tao* (1996). The *zuowang* material (ch. 6) is very likely represented; the specific Cook Ding, fasting-of-mind, and mirror passages could not be confirmed as individually in Buber's selection from sources reachable here.

7. **The kept difference — the adversary edge.** Zhuangzi dissolves the striving self and the fixed standpoint (zuowang, "become one with the Great Pervader," 同於大通; the mirror that "responds but does not store," 應而不藏; the locus classicus being ch. 2, 齊物論, the equality of things) rather than restoring a sovereign first person — a genuine adversary to the book's restored "I."

## What the reading did not settle

- **The exact contents of Buber's 1910 selection** (whether Cook Ding, the fasting of the mind, and the mirror were individually available to Heidegger) — owed to Herman's *I and Tao* (1996).
- **A verbatim from ch. 2** (齊物論, the equality of things), the locus classicus for the dissolved standpoint — the theme is confirmed but no line was pulled.
- **Zhuangzi has no interlocutor chair.** Following the Plato and Nāgārjuna precedent, this reading produces a record without creating one: **Zhuangzi is a chair candidate the author may seat** (beside Laozi, a source-tradition or reference-lineage seat; load *independent-arrival* for the traditions he had no contact with, and *precedent* / ancestor where Heidegger's later work descends from the Zhuangzi he read). The seating is the author's.
