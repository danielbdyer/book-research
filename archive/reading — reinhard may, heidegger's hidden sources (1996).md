# reading — reinhard may, heidegger's hidden sources (1996)

**What was read.** Reinhard May, *Heidegger's Hidden Sources: East Asian Influences on His Work* (trans. Graham Parkes, Routledge 1996), and its supporting field, 2026-08-30, as grounding-backlog cluster B's both-ways-traffic test. May's book is copyright and could not be opened directly in-container; the case was triangulated across four sources — Graham Parkes's complementary essay "Rising Sun over Black Forest" (bound in the same volume, read in full from Parkes's own site), Stella Sandford's skeptical review (*Radical Philosophy* 120, 2003), Eric S. Nelson's scholarship (*Heidegger and Dao*, 2024), and the Kuki dialogue in Heidegger's *On the Way to Language* (1959). Two supporting primaries were verified alongside: *Being and Time* §44 on aletheia (via the *Minerva* 16 article quoting Macquarrie & Robinson), and the Zhuangzi *zuowang* passage (Legge, public domain) with Daodejing 40. No quotation is from memory.

**How it is cited.** May's documented claims are cited through Parkes with the essay page (Grade B, since a side-by-side German/Chinese quotation carrying May's own page could not be obtained); Zhuangzi and Daodejing at Grade A (public-domain translations); *Being and Time* §44 at Grade B (secondary quoting M&R verbatim with page).

**The stake.** The book's foundation is that Heidegger's *alḗtheia* (un-concealment / un-forgetting) and the Kashmir Śaiva Pratyabhijñā (re-cognition) reached "truth as un-forgetting" with no historical contact ([[aletheia and pratyabhijna converge on truth as un-forgetting]]). If Heidegger's un-forgetting carries a hidden East Asian debt, the founding convergence is demoted from independent arrival to influence.

## What the reading settled

1. **May documents a real Daoist and Zen debt — on adjacent themes, not on aletheia.** Parkes, reporting May: Heidegger's "formulations of his major thoughts on Being, Nothing, the clearing, and on the complex relations between language, Way, and Saying, were influenced by his readings of German translations of Daoist and Zen texts" ("Rising Sun over Black Forest," p. 79). The debt-bearing themes across three independent summaries (Parkes, Sandford, Nelson) are **Being, Nothing (wu), the clearing (Lichtung), the Way (Weg/Dao), Saying, and releasement (Gelassenheit/wuwei)** — and none of the three lists includes aletheia. The nearest adjacency is *Lichtung*, the site of unconcealment, but no source derives Heidegger's a-/lēthē etymology from a Daoist text.

2. **The contact channel is confirmed and dated.** "The first confirmed instance so far is Petzet's report of Heidegger's consulting the Buber edition of the Zhuangzi in 1930... the Buber edition was first published in 1910" (Parkes, p. 79, n. 2). So Heidegger demonstrably read Buber's 1910 *Reden und Gleichnisse des Tschuang-Tse* — the whole Zhuangzi, the *zuowang* chapter included. Parkes's calibrated verdict: "the conclusion is unavoidable that Heidegger was less than generous in acknowledging how much he learned from the East Asian tradition" (pp. 109–110) — under-acknowledgement of a real debt, not a proven line-by-line derivation. In the Kuki dialogue Heidegger concedes contact and dialogue, not borrowing ("we Europeans presumably inhabit a quite different house from the East Asians," WL 5).

3. **There is no Heidegger→Kashmir-Śaivism channel at all.** Zero occurrences of Kashmir, Śaiva, Pratyabhijñā, or Utpaladeva across Parkes, Sandford, Nelson, and a direct search. Pratyabhijñā is a Śaiva *rival* of the Buddhist emptiness May traces, not a form of it. **So the specific aletheia↔pratyabhijñā founding pairing survives May's thesis intact** — the two poles did not read each other, and Heidegger's documented East Asian reading runs to a different (Daoist/Buddhist) stream that does not touch the Śaiva pole or the aletheia term.

4. **What the book now owes, and it reinforces rather than threatens the honest frame.** On the responsible reading (Parkes's "unavoidable" conclusion), the book should own an adjacent Daoist debt on the Heidegger side of its general apparatus — which fits, rather than damages, the book's own origin story of noticing first and finding company after. This is folded into [[aletheia and pratyabhijna converge on truth as un-forgetting]] as the both-ways-traffic test passed.

5. **Two supporting-note consequences.**
   - *Being and Time* §44 grounds aletheia as un-*concealment* only ("Being-true means Being-uncovering," M&R p. 262; §7 reads it as "making manifest that which in some sense lies hidden"). The "un-*forgetting* via the river Lēthē" gloss is the later Heidegger's (the Parmenides lectures, GA 54), copyright-gated and unread; the vault's un-forgetting slogan owes that citation.
   - The note [[remembering and forgetting name one movement back to the ground you never left]] had paired Heidegger's un-forgetting with Zhuangzi's forgetting (*zuowang*) as a *no-contact* convergence. Because Heidegger read the Buber Zhuangzi, that pairing is now **influence-suspect**, not an independent arrival, and must be held as company-via-documented-contact. Its reserved *zuowang* quote was also a conflation of Graham and Watson; it is replaced with Legge's public-domain translation.

## What the reading did not settle

- **May's own pages.** The case is read at Grade B through Parkes and the secondary field; a library copy of May would let specific parallels be quoted with May's own page numbers.
- **The Parmenides lectures (GA 54)** — the primary home of Heidegger's Lēthē/un-forgetting reading — remain copyright-gated and unread; the un-forgetting gloss is owed there.
- **Sandford's exact page cites** (p. 42, p. 56) came via a page-summariser and are not independently confirmed; her critique is methodological (the "logic of comparison" is untheorized), and her theme list, too, omits aletheia and Kashmir Śaivism.
