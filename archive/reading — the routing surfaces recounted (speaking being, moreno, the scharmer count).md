# reading — the routing surfaces recounted (speaking being, moreno, the scharmer count)

**What was read.** Three items, on 2026-09-02, as readings A10, A11, and A12 of the practice-model program. **Hyde and Kopp**, *Speaking Being: Werner Erhard, Martin Heidegger, and a New Possibility of Being Human* (Wiley, 2019): Wiley's own thirty-page reading sample — the copyright page, the complete table of contents for all 550 pages, the complete Introduction (pp. 1–5), and the first sixteen pages of the December 1989 Forum transcript (pp. 7–16) with sidebars; pp. 17–532 remain unread — **Grade B**, cited by the book's printed pages. Because the sample does not reach the racket, the mechanism was read at two other primary sources that carry it: **Landmark Worldwide's own current syllabus** for the Forum (Day One, segments II–V; Day Three, segments II and IV), and **Charles Wayne Denison, "The Children of EST" (Ph.D. diss., University of Denver, 1994, UMI 9511962)**, an observational ethnography of multiple Forums, at its curriculum chapter. **J. L. Moreno**: *Who Shall Survive? A New Approach to the Problem of Human Interrelations*, first edition (Nervous and Mental Disease Monograph No. 58, 1934), complete OCR text, and *Group Psychotherapy: A Symposium* (Beacon House, 1945; = *Sociometry* VIII: 3–4), which he edited and in which his own essays appear — **Grade B** overall, since the 1953 third edition (the famous spontaneity definition) and *Psychodrama* Vol. I are lending-restricted and were quoted at second hand from two independent scholarly sources agreeing on wording and page. **The recount**: a function-count per name across atlas §IX in full, walked IX, arc-gloss ch. 7 and ch. 13, the first-telling, and the abridged draft, against the mesh grid's Tier 5 roster (its p. 8 pyramid plus the addendum's additions at p. 12), all searches word-bounded.

## What the reading settled — the Forum

1. ***Speaking Being* does not appear to treat the racket at all, and the reason is historical.** Sixty-odd sidebar topics across four days name Erhard's own distinctions (`Already Always Listening 75`, `Getting It and Losing It 44`, `Choice 217`, `The Three Levels of Truth 377`, `Primordial Metaphor: Clearing 397`, `Transformation as Technology 519`); *racket*, *winning formula*, and *empty and meaningless* appear nowhere in the contents. The Forum transcribed is Erhard's own, San Francisco, 27–30 December 1989; "in 1991 Landmark took over its ongoing development" (Hyde and Kopp 2019, p. 2 n. 1), and Rackets™ is a Landmark-era trademarked distinction.

2. **There is no dissolving mechanism and there is no declaration; the racket's operation is exposure of the payoff plus recognition of the pattern, resting on a prior separation of what happened from the story about it.** Landmark's syllabus, Day One §V: a racket is "an unproductive way of being or acting that includes a complaint that something shouldn't be the way it is… there is a certain payoff—some advantage or benefit we are receiving that reinforces the cycle of behavior… By recognizing this pattern, its costs, and how we have been keeping the pattern in place, we have the choice to interrupt the cycle." Its Day One order is decisive: Already Always Listening™ → The Hidden Power of Context → The Vicious Circle™ → Rackets™, and the Vicious Circle segment is the separating move — "it is a human tendency to collapse what happened with the story we tell about what happened… When we are able to separate what happened from our story or interpretation, we discover that much of what we considered already determined, given and fixed, may in fact not be that way." Denison recorded the leaders' own words: a racket is "'some persistent way of being, doing, or having, plus a complaint'… 'Your Racket runs your life so you don't have to'"; four costs (love, health-vitality, satisfaction-fulfillment, self-expression) and three observed payoffs — "They make me right… They help me dominate others… I use them to justify myself" — with the vault's fourth, winning/avoiding losing, attested only in course-note derivatives. And the goal, stated negatively: "The goal in The Forum is not to be cured of one's Racket, but to discover it and recognize when you are 'running your Racket'"; "In every observation of The Forum, the Forum leader said that there is technically no such thing as a Racket— 'It's not true. We made it up. It means nothing.'" Declaration belongs to Day Three ("Transformation, on the other hand, is an act of bringing forth or inventing"), and its object is a new possibility, not a racket. This is the failure condition the brief named: the est room's core diagnostic runs on seeing rather than making, which places it nearer the book's disclosure branch than the corpus's speech-acts placement allows.

3. **The vocabulary, located.** *Already always listening*: the syllabus's Day One §II and the transcript from the opening hour — "a lot of what is going on with you and me is something we already always are… I was already always pessimistic and then this thing came along" (p. 9); "Being human is an already always way of being that you walked into. It's like a rainstorm, and it's true you personally got wet" (p. 16). *The story versus what happened*: Landmark Day One §IV; Denison, "You made that up! It is not true! It's a story!" *Completion*: the authors' own definition, "Complete, for Erhard, does not mean simply finished, but rather whole, and lacking none of its essential parts" (p. 16, sidebar "Ontological Dialogue"), with his synonym "flatten." *Declaration*: confirmed and relocated to Day Three. *Occurring*: not found on the Forum side; it is Erhard's academic term, already held from the Harvard paper. *Being for*: not found. *Winning formula*: new to the vault — "a way of being you depend on for success / Compensates for 'there is something wrong here' / Is not satisfying," with its genesis "a moment in life when 'you couldn't be X, so you had to be Y'" (Denison).

4. **Hyde and Kopp read the Forum as disclosing, not creating, and state it as their thesis.** "The Forum conversation is ontological rhetoric: purposive speaking that communicates and makes present a context of meaning that, if left unexamined, shapes and limits our way of being. However, this context of meaning cannot be spoken directly. It becomes present by virtue of what is said" (pp. 3–4). "Our goal—Heidegger's, Erhard's, the authors'—is the unconcealment of a powerful aspect of human being" (p. 5); the Forum is "a carefully designed movement in language for the showing of being." On the historical relation: Erhard met Heidegger's work "During the transition to The Forum," through colleagues, and "saw that Heidegger's ideas and his own were communicating from the same unspoken realm… 'I learned from Heidegger,' said Erhard, 'nuances of what I had been saying'" (p. 3). This qualifies the vault's fault-line note, which rests on the leadership course's verb: the scholarly study of the Forum itself puts the Forum on the disclosive side. Their own limit is stated: "our assessment of Erhard's work is unequivocal… we have precluded a number of approaches that readers may wish to see addressed" (p. 2) — an advocacy document with an unusually good primary text inside it, and the transcript is edited (p. 4).

## What the reading settled — Moreno

1. **Spontaneity Training and the warming-up process are named methods in 1934, at the primary text.** Chapter XVII is titled "Spontaneity Training": "To accomplish this aim we have developed techniques for training spontaneity" (1934, pp. 321–22); "the problem of learning becomes not to induce and conserve habits but to train spontaneity, to train and develop man to the habit of spontaneity" (p. 324). The warming-up process is theorized under "Impromptu Function": "To act means to warm up to a state of feeling, to an 'Impromptu' state… Our experimental study of the warming-up process developing to an act led us to the observation of warming-up indicators. These are not mental signs only but physiological signs (altered breathing rate, gasping, crying, smiling, clenching the teeth, etc.)" (pp. 193–95). The famous definition — "Spontaneity operates in the present, now and here; it propels the individual towards an adequate response to a new situation or a new response to an old situation" — is the 1953 third edition's, absent from 1934, and is Grade B here. **Correction:** Moreno did not coin "spontaneity" or "warm up," which are ordinary English; what he coined and had in print by 1934 is the technical apparatus, and the priority over Spolin holds.

2. **The group as therapeutic agent, 1945, is the closest outside statement of the participation law from a named originator.** "Group psychotherapy is always 'group' therapy. It is the group itself that becomes the therapeutic agent as a result of the interaction between the individuals who form the group" (symposium, pp. 288–89); "The doctor and healer as the final source of mental therapeusis has fallen… one patient can treat the other. The role of the healer has changed from the owner and actor of therapy to its assigner and trustee" (pp. 316–17). His priority claim on the term "group psychotherapy" (1931–32) comes with his own hedge — "this writer at least has not been able to trace it, so far, in the literature prior to that date" (p. 502).

3. **Ancestor on the enactment, not on the facilitator.** The enactment is the facilitated room's shape: "whereas conduct in a life situation is irrevocable, here every phase of performance is open to correction" (1934, p. 325); "the individual craves to embody far more roles than those he is allowed to act out in life" (p. 326). But the director interpolates: "The participant actor (or auxiliary ego) is a powerful tool of the director in accelerating the warming up… provides the director with the opportunity to interpolate specific attitudes and behavior patterns" (symposium, pp. 458–59), and only sociodrama is exempted from "a mighty dictator-director." The book's held container withholds; on this axis Lewin's self-studying group and Johnstone's offer-grammar are nearer than Moreno is.

4. **Moreno would refuse the book by name, in his own glossary.** "Cultural Conserve. The technical conservation of cultural values, as the book or the film, which substitutes and preserves man's creative expressions" (1934, Glossary); "the illusion of the finished, perfected product whose assumed perfectibility was an excuse par excellence for forsaking its past" (pp. 363–64); and the case he chooses: "the extempore Dionysian plays of the old Greeks. It ended in the written drama; this was its natural fate… the creative process was conserved forever, and so then the unnecessary attempt at spontaneity could die out" (p. 338) — documented from inside his own room, where improvisers "ended in stereotypes" after a few months (p. 338). The sharpest refusal the vault has recovered for the book's form: a book *is the genre of the thing being refused*.

## What the recount settled

The count does not come back empty; it returns a third name and four more behind it, and the decisive function was one the Hudson-modelled count never looked for — chapter patronage. **Donella Meadows** holds two of the sixteen chapter-patron seats (the Molecules of Experience and the diagnostic chapter; arc-gloss pp. 9, 15; atlas §IX.9), is the only name below the front row with two; her credo is the Molecules chapter's "whole disposition" (walked IV, "we cannot control systems, but we can dance with them"); the executed draft uses her leverage ladder to legitimate the book's central method — "The molecular vocabulary, in this sense, is a paradigm intervention" (abridged pp. 19–20) — and to state its own architecture, "The architecture of this book is an architecture for dancing" (p. 46). **Priya Parker, Ursula Le Guin, and Brian Eno** each hold one medium alone in the five-name demonstration of the book's first axiom that the Triad is found rather than invented — "Alexander's rooms — literally; Gendlin's felt-sense sessions — procedurally; Parker's gatherings — socially; Le Guin's worlds — imaginally; Eno's generative systems — algorithmically" (atlas §II.2; walked II) — so the demonstration of the foundational axiom is three-fifths Tier 5. **Scharmer**, the queued name, is the weakest of the five and was already recounted by the grid itself: Tier 5 in the p. 8 pyramid, re-filed to Tier 4 in the p. 12 addendum ("Machinery-adjacent rather than directional; sits with the Loops"); he holds alone only "the open will ahead of the plan." Behind these, patron seats held alone by Kimmerer, brown, and Whyte; marginal single functions for Carse, Akomolafe, and Oliver (the book's last question is hers); Hendricks has zero occurrences anywhere in the corpus. None of the names above has a chair.
