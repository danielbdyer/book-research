# reading — abram and kimmerer on the more-than-human (1996 and 2013)

**What was read.** David Abram, *The Spell of the Sensuous: Perception and Language in a More-than-Human World* (1996), and Robin Wall Kimmerer, "Learning the Grammar of Animacy" (in *Braiding Sweetgrass*, 2013) — read via the authors' own statements and reliable sources quoting them (a full text of Abram is posted at Harvard and the Internet Archive; Kimmerer's essay is excerpted at Orion Magazine and Science Friday), retrieved 2026-08-10. The full books were not read whole in-container. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 42.

**How it is cited.** `Abram, The Spell of the Sensuous` and `Kimmerer, "Learning the Grammar of Animacy"`, by the stable titles. Quotations below are from the authors as reported by reliable sources; the full texts await a fuller reading before manuscript use.

**Why this reading.** [[abram's more-than-human makes the animate world a participant in the inner life]] holds that Abram's participatory perception makes the animate world a real participant in inner life — the independent arrival for the non-human blind spot, beside Buber's tree — and that the corpus already names Abram. The failable question: does the participatory-perception argument make the more-than-human world a participant, and does Kimmerer's grammar of animacy render the natural world as subjects rather than objects?

## What the reading settled

1. **Perception is participatory and reciprocal with a more-than-human world — confirmed.** Abram coined "the more-than-human world" for a nature "that thoroughly includes humankind… yet necessarily exceeds humankind," and his premise is "the fluid, participatory nature of perception, and the reciprocity between our senses and the sensuous earth," taking Merleau-Ponty's participatory perception as its starting point. His foundational line: "we are human only in contact, and conviviality, with what is not human." So inner life is co-constituted by an outer, living world that takes part in it — the non-human as participant, which the book's human-only field has no chair for.

2. **The grammar of animacy renders the world as subjects — confirmed.** Kimmerer: "'It-ing' turns gifts into natural resources. It gives us permission to see the land as an inanimate object. These beings are not 'it,' they are our relatives," and "In Potawatomi and most other indigenous languages, we use the same words to address the living world as we use for our family" — against the world as "a collection of objects." This is the independent company for Abram, an arrival from an indigenous language rather than from phenomenology.

**Verdict for the note.** Abram is a genuine independent arrival for the non-human blind spot, and the corpus already names him; Kimmerer is its independent company. With Buber's tree (the chain-link) the non-human now has both a chain-link and an arrival. The note moves from privated to full. The refusal holds: Abram's participation is sensory, pre-reflective, and outdoor — reached through the body's coupling with the land — and skeptical of the interiorizing move that lifts experience out of the sensuous world, which lands on a book of inner life read alone indoors.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The full books.** Read via the authors' statements and reliable sources, not *The Spell of the Sensuous* or *Braiding Sweetgrass* whole; a fuller reading would deepen the participatory-perception argument and Kimmerer's linguistics.
- **Whether "a communion of subjects" is compatible with the book's between.** Kimmerer (quoting Thomas Berry) holds "the universe… is a communion of subjects, not a collection of objects"; whether the book's human recognition-field can widen to that communion without losing its specificity is the author's question.
