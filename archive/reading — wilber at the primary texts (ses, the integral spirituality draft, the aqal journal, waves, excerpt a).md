# reading — wilber at the primary texts (ses, the integral spirituality draft, the aqal journal, waves, excerpt a)

**What was read.** Five Wilber texts, on 2026-09-02, as readings A1 and A9's Wilber half of the practice-model program: *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* (1995) complete in a digital reflow (closely at ch. 1, ch. 2 entire, ch. 3–4, ch. 6, ch. 7, ch. 9, the endnotes and bibliography); *What Is Integral Spirituality?*, Wilber's own June 2005 first draft of the 2006 book, 118 pp., complete; "Introduction to Integral Theory and Practice: IOS Basic and the AQAL Map," *AQAL: Journal of Integral Theory and Practice* 1(1), Spring 2006, pp. 1–38, complete, with journal pagination confirmed against the running heads; "Waves, Streams, States, and Self," *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 7 (2000), complete in a web reprint with its own pagination; and "Excerpt A: An Integral Age at the Leading Edge," 203 pp., at the passages on holons, prehension, perspectives, and deep structures. **Not reached:** *Eye to Eye* (1983/1996), *Integral Psychology* (2000), and the published *Integral Spirituality* (2006) — every host paywalled or login-gated; where those were needed, Wilber's own essay-length statements of the same doctrine stood in and the substitution is flagged.

**How it is cited.** SES by chapter and tenet number — `SES, Book One, ch. 2, tenet 5` — because the reflow carries no print pagination (Integral World gives the tenet sequence as pp. 35–78 of the 1995 Shambhala edition; a vault note citing a specific SES page still needs the print book). The draft as `Integral Spirituality draft (2005), p. 53`; the journal article as `AQAL Journal 1(1), p. 12` (real pagination); "Waves" by reprint page; Excerpt A by document page. Grades: **A** for all SES, draft, *AQAL Journal*, "Waves," and Excerpt A quotations; **B** for SES page-level locators, for the published *Integral Spirituality*'s wording and page, and for the *Eye to Eye* / *ReVision* pre/trans formulation; **A** for the negative finding on al-Qushayrī (exhaustive string search of five texts).

**Why this reading.** The author named Wilber's holarchy for the shape of his own recognition — "the both-and of sensing what was always there alongside the mystery (in a state of resolution opening up into another mystery)" — and the survey of the vault's practice-models found holarchy to be the one structural family the vault does not carry as a structure of experience (`ops/practice-model ecology.md`, section 6). Four separate jobs rested on him with no primary text read: the lattice ([[an enormous experience is interpreted by the structure it did not change]], privated on exactly this), the title's premise ([[the title's idiom names the integration of the three loops rather than parallel lines of development]]), the pre/trans distinction the book "has no substitute for," and the transcend-and-include verb. The chair's own advice was to read him in four passes rather than seat him once ([[ken wilber]]).

## Pass 1 — the holarchy: PARTIAL, and the partition is sharp

- **The tenets are systems-ontology, and Wilber says so.** Tenet 1: "Reality as a whole is not composed of things or processes, but of holons. Composed, that is, of wholes that are simultaneously parts of other wholes, with no upward or downward limit." Tenet 5, verbatim: "Each emergent holon transcends but includes its predecessor(s)… it preserves the previous holons themselves but negates their separateness or isolatedness or aloneness. It preserves their being but negates their partiality or exclusiveness. 'To supersede,' said Hegel, 'is at once to preserve and to negate'" (SES, Book One, ch. 2). His own gloss: "all the basic structures and functions are preserved and taken up in a larger identity, but all the exclusivity structures and functions that existed because of isolation, set-apartness, partialness, exclusiveness, separative agency — these are simply dropped and replaced with a deeper agency that reaches a wider communion." Tenet 8: "Each successive level of evolution produces GREATER depth and LESS span." The consciousness claim is not one of the twenty tenets but "Addition 1: The greater the depth of a holon, the greater its degree of consciousness," introduced as a tenet that "simply cannot be established in the it-language of instrumental and objectifying naturalism." And at the end of ch. 3: "They are all, all of them, how the universe looks from the outside… not one of them represents how evolution looks from the inside."
- **The interiority added in ch. 4 is deduced from outside, and Wilber holds it constitutively unavailable to first-person inspection.** "Notice that neither states of consciousness nor structures of consciousness are directly experienced by individuals… Structures of consciousness, on the other hand, are deduced from watching the behavior of numerous subjects… This is why structures of consciousness are almost never spotted by phenomenology" ("Waves," p. 7). "The individual subject never prehends its own deep structure, nor its own stages of development… 'transcend and include' refers primarily to tetra-inclusion (deep structures, for example, are inherited or included, but not in conscious awareness.)" (Excerpt A, p. 172). "I have studiously avoided taking a 1st-person stance within any of the 8 fundamental perspectives involved" (draft, p. 101).
- **A felt transcend-and-include exists in Wilber, outside the tenets.** The Whiteheadian moment-holarchy: "each moment transcends and includes its predecessors, inheriting a history of feelings (or objects that were once subjects) but also adding a creative novelty found nowhere in the past" (Excerpt A, pp. 32–35). And SES ch. 9, endnote 386, the nearest thing in Wilber to the author's description: "one has the overwhelming apprehension that one is recognizing something once known but long forgotten, that one is being ushered into a worldspace that in some extremely fundamental sense existed prior to its recognition: it was there all along, just not seen"; in the main text, "Enlightenment or awakening (bodhi, moksha) is not a bringing into being of that which was not, but a realizing of that which always already is."
- **"Each resolution opening into another mystery" is the author's, not Wilber's.** Wilber's version is the dialectic of progress: each level "solves or 'defuses' some of the central problems and limitations of the previous stage… but it also introduces its own new problems" (ch. 12), "new fears, new anxieties, new needs… and the ever-present possibility of new and higher pathologies" (ch. 6) — resolution opening into further problems, not further mystery.
- **Koestler provenance, with one correction.** "Arthur Koestler coined the term holon" (SES ch. 1; endnote 19, "Koestler, *Ghost in the machine*"; endnote 71 gives p. 50 for the depth/span definition). SES's bibliography cites the **1976 Random House** reprint, so its page references are keyed to that printing rather than the 1967 original. And "transcends but includes" is Wilber's own phrase, glossed on Hegel — Koestler coined the noun, not the verb.

## Pass 2 — the lattice: the sentence HOLDS, the vault's use FAILS as stated

- **The sentence and its locator.** "any person at any stage can have a peak experience of a gross, subtle, causal, or nondual state. But a person will interpret that state according to the stage they are at… That bold sentence was for us early researchers the breakthrough and real turning point" (draft, p. 53); "they will interpret that experience with the only equipment they have, namely, the tools of the stage of development they are at" (pp. 53–54). Priority is Wilber's; "my friend Allan Combs, working independently, hit upon an essentially similar idea, and so, in a painfully transparent bid for history, we named this the 'Wilber-Combs Lattice'" (p. 51). The states are the natural states, available at every stage "because even the lowest stages wake, dream, and sleep" (p. 52).
- **Wilber holds both that a single state changes nothing structural and that repeated practice does.** "With repeated practice of contacting higher states, your own stages of development will tend to unfold in a much faster and easier way… It is as if higher-states training acts as a lubricant on the spiral of development… until you can stably remain at higher levels of awareness on an ongoing basis, whereupon a passing state has become a permanent trait" (*AQAL Journal* 1(1), pp. 12–13); "you cannot skip actual stages, but you can accelerate your growth through them" (p. 13); "with practice, you will convert those states into stages, or permanent traits" (p. 10); "it is when discrete states are converted into enduring traits (structures) that development occurs" (SES ch. 9, endnote 383). The asymmetry that makes the lattice work: "You can have a peak experience of higher states, because many of them are ever-present. But you cannot have a peak experience of a higher stage, because stages unfold sequentially" (p. 12). So the two axes are distinct and non-equatable and interact asymmetrically; "independent axes" was the grid's phrase, not his.
- **No Sufi credit, and none owed.** *Qushayri*, *ahwal*, *maqamat*, *maqam*: zero occurrences across the five texts. "Sufi" once in SES, in a passing list. Wilber's "station" (draft, p. 97, "every stage is also a station in life") means stage in the ordinary sense and is not a translation of *maqām*.

## Pass 3 — the pre/trans fallacy: no first-person criterion, as positive doctrine

- **The fallacy in his words** (SES ch. 6, "The Pre/Trans Fallacy"): "since both prerational states and transrational states are, in their own ways, nonrational, they appear similar or even identical to the untutored eye… Freud was a reductionist, Jung an elevationist — the two sides of the pre/trans fallacy"; and the principle, "Spirit is indeed nonrational; but it is trans, not pre. It transcends but includes reason; it does not regress and exclude it." The canonical "pre-X to X to trans-X" formulation is Grade B, located at *ReVision* 3(2), 1980, and *Eye to Eye* (1996 ed., pp. 198–243), neither read.
- **The discrimination is constitutively third-person.** His method is subtraction by a researcher — "we will know, if nothing else than by a process of subtraction, what the transpersonal states are like"; "a simple study of Piaget is the quickest cure for elevationism" — and the rules are "invisible to introspection, invisible to meditation, and invisible to phenomenology in general… you can sit on your meditation mat for decades and never see anything resembling the stages" (draft, p. 21); "if you can't see the zone, you can't see the disease… the practitioners of contemplation today turn out inadvertently to be some of its most effective saboteurs" (p. 23). Since pre and trans are structure determinations and structures are unavailable to first-person inspection, no first-person criterion is possible on his terms. What he offers instead is the third strand: "you check your results, your data, with others who have completed the first two strands… In this community of peers, you compare and confirm — or reject — your original data" (SES ch. 7, "Validity Claims of Mysticism"). One handhold, marked as derived: "It transcends but includes reason; it does not regress and exclude it" is in principle self-checkable — are the capacities one had still intact? — but Wilber never presents it as a self-test and could not consistently do so.

## Pass 4 — lines of development: the title's premise is not his, and he denies it

- **The doctrine.** "the numerous developmental lines are to some degree independent modules, and these modules can and do develop in relatively independent ways (but not totally independently)" ("Waves," p. 4); the psychograph, "somebody who excels in cognitive development and is good at interpersonal development, but does poorly in moral and really poorly in emotional intelligence" (*AQAL Journal*, pp. 10–11).
- **The denial.** "To be 'integrally developed' does not mean that you have to excel in all the known intelligences, or that all of your lines have to be at level 3" (*AQAL Journal*, p. 11); "to be 'integrally informed' does not mean you have to master all lines of development, just be aware of them" (p. 12). The book's title, defined at abridged ch7 as "the felt condition in which all three are functioning and integrated," differs on three counts: cardinality and kind (a dozen-plus intelligences against exactly three functional loops), what varies (developmental altitude against present-tense firing or misfire), and the ideal (all three firing against a denial that all lines at the top is the goal).
- **What he does supply.** The psychograph as a diagnostic precedent — "More than one leader, spiritual teacher, or politician has spectacularly crashed through lack of an understanding of these simple realities" (p. 11) — and the "stone Buddha": "a person can stay in extraordinary states of formless absorption for extended periods — and yet be poorly developed, or even pathologically developed, in other lines and realms" ("Waves," p. 10); "this fractured development is taken as the paradigm of natural and normal spiritual development, and then students and teachers alike are asked to repeat the fracture as evidence of their spiritual progress" (p. 11). A third account of why the big experience did not hold ([[fractured development is a third account of why the big experience did not hold]]).

## His refusals, kept in his words

"The Integral map is just a map. It is not the territory… but neither do we want to be working with an inaccurate or faulty map" (*AQAL Journal*, p. 2). "if you can't see the zone, you can't see the disease" (draft, p. 23). On the book's central move: "you can have a realization of nondual ever-present awareness that is a pure UNITY experience right now, but that experience leaves out a great deal of the universe. Thus satori can actually be unity with a fragmented world. Generally speaking, this is not good" (draft, p. 57). On solitary verification, the three strands, with communal confirmation constitutive. On the totalizing charge: "AQAL is not the only way to do so, and it might not be the best way, it is simply the only way that I know of at this time… It is the result of a practice of inclusion, not a theory about inclusion" (draft, p. 104). Where he is not an adversary: "this map is just a third-person map, so don't forget the other important realities" (*AQAL Journal*, p. 36).
