# source — the poems

Sixteen poems, numbered and untitled, written across the author's life and supplied to the vault on 2026-08-03 with the framing: "the source material I've been writing all my life. Many (not all — number 16 as the divergent poem) of these predate the book, but certainly any phrases you see like 'the youngness is just beginning' are actually quotes from my poems and not the other way around."

The collection runs from a first poem about the author's father and the concept of home ("danny, this is your daddy speaking") through love poems, grief poems, and poems of self-instruction, to the sixteenth — the one poem that does not predate the book, written from inside its era, about the move across the country and the sentence that finally leaves the architecture.

## Standing

This document has **primary** standing under `ops/methodology/source standing.md`, a third standing created for it: the author's own prior work, upstream of the book, which the corpus quotes and descends from. A primary source asserts nothing about the book; the datum is the book's use of it. The corpus itself names this collection's existence — the ripeness rule's own parenthetical referent is "the author's own archive of poems and drafts" (atlas §V.3) — so the archive the house rule was written about is now in hand.

The direction of quotation is the standing's whole point, and the author states it: for poems 1–15, a phrase shared between poem and corpus originates in the poem. The sixteenth is exempted by the author's own flag and reads as the reverse flow — the book's language returning to the life.

## Citation

Cited as `poems`, by number: `poems #5`, `poems #12`, `poems #16`. The poems are untitled, so the number is the only anchor; quotation plus number locates any line.

## What the collection carries before the corpus

The concerns the book systematizes are in these poems in the author's unassisted voice, years earlier. A partial concordance, recorded here so the reduce pass has its trailheads: the father and home, with welcome as the father's first act (#1); shared perception as the ground of knowing ("thank goodness we share that perception," #6); surrender and effort held in one breath ("complete surrender / and trying one's very hardest," #7); endings that are not endings, "composure in the face of lack of closure" (#7); letting go without forgetting, and the caretaking bind ("we first must learn how to treat the otherness within ourselves right," #8); recognition and clearing in one couplet ("a crescendo clearing the mist / a recognition therewith," #10); writing from the now, and flowers from manure (#11); the return as practice, in a litany — "even leaving, beginning again; even older, beginning again; even crying, beginning again" (#12); shared seeing as completion — "we saw it because you saw it and you shared it to me" — with the kettle in the same poem (#12); grief, forgiveness of self, and the looking-glass (#13, #14); and already-there — "the inside is not required to be entered from the outside since I am already there" — with "Please try to see yourself forever as a source of light in this world" (#15). The figures of the book — the tuning fork, the orchard, the gold thread, the house with no ceiling — appear nowhere in the collection: the figures are seed-era, and what the poems hold is the layer beneath the figures.
