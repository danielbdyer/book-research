## Verified against the householder pricing ([[the householder road is priced long by every witness who grants it]])

- **Miller and C'de Baca, *Quantum Change: When Epiphanies and Sudden Insights Transform Ordinary Lives* (Guilford, 2001)** — the program documents sudden, vivid, permanent transformations in ordinary lives, and the ten-year follow-up (C'de Baca and Wilbourne, "Quantum change: Ten years later," *Journal of Clinical Psychology* 60(5), 2004) reports the changes sustained. The subtitle grants the householder setting and puts the change at minutes rather than years.
- **Tang and DeRubeis, "Sudden gains and critical sessions in cognitive-behavioral therapy for depression," *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology* 67(6):894–904, 1999** — the study finds large between-session improvements that carry about half of gainers' total change and still hold at 18 months. The meta-analysis (Aderka et al., *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology* 80(1):93–101, 2012) finds that sudden gains predict better follow-up outcomes, g = 0.62.
- One methodological caution is recorded beside both citations, because it limits what they can prove. Miller and C'de Baca recruited their subjects through a newspaper advertisement asking readers who had experienced sudden transformation to come forward, and interviewed them about events years past — so the sample selected itself for dramatic stories, and the data are memories rather than observations. The reading-queue entry proposed from this finding therefore asks a question that can fail: does quantum-change durability survive its method, or do the self-selection and the retrospective reporting account for the sustained-change finding? A finding of the second kind would restore the force of the claim that the householder road takes years. The entry is in `ops/reading queue.md` under the 2026-08-06 proposed additions.
