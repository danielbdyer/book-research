# reading — al-junayd and al-qushayri on fana and baqa

**What was read.** The Sufi doctrine of *fanā* (annihilation) and *baqā* (subsistence) in al-Junayd of Baghdad (d. 910), founder of the "School of Sobriety" (*sahw*), and in al-Qushayrī's *Risāla* — read via scholarly secondary sources, including Maryam Musharraf's "Sobriety in a Drunken Cosmos: The Paradox of Junayd of Baghdad" and academic treatments of *fanā*/*baqā* in early Sufism, retrieved 2026-08-10. The primary texts (al-Junayd's *Rasāʾil*; al-Qushayrī's *Risāla* in Knysh's translation) were not read whole in-container. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 41.

**How it is cited.** `al-Junayd (in al-Qushayrī's Risāla)` and `al-Qushayrī, Risāla (the fanāʾ/baqāʾ material)`, by the stable references. The al-Junayd definition below is quoted from al-Qushayrī via a scholarly source; the primary Arabic and the full *Risāla* await a fuller reading before manuscript use.

**Why this reading.** [[the sufi arc completes annihilation by return rather than by staying dissolved]] holds that al-Junayd's sobriety doctrine makes the return the completion of the annihilation — an independent arrival at the book's descent-and-integration and its householder axiom. The failable question: does the Sufi arc complete *fanā* by *baqā* — a return to responsible life — rather than by remaining dissolved?

## What the reading settled

1. **Annihilation is completed by subsistence and return — confirmed.** al-Junayd is "the founder of the main current of Sufism known as the 'School of Sobriety' (*sahw*)," and the doctrine is "the interdependence of *fanā*' and *baqā*'." al-Qushayrī quotes al-Junayd: "Sufism means that God causes you to die for yourself, while endowing you with a life in Him" — *fanā* as "oblivion from creation and self, followed by *baqā* as subsistence in God's attributes without separation." And the "second sobriety": the mystic returns to consciousness and distinction (*farq*) after annihilation (*jam'*, gathering), the return being the completion rather than a lesser state. Annihilation-and-return is one structure — an independent arrival at the book's claim that the peak is finished by the return.

**Verdict for the note.** The Sufi arc is a genuine independent arrival at the book's descent-and-integration and householder axiom, confirmed at scholarly depth with an al-Qushayrī quote of al-Junayd. The note moves from privated to full. The refusal holds and is confirmed: annihilation is *in God* and subsistence is *by God's attributes* — a theological destination the book's psychological register cannot assume — and al-Qushayrī frames *fanā* as the top of a long ascent through repentance, renunciation, patience and the rest, a communal and legally-grounded ("Junayd-style," Ash'arite-compliant) path, so the self-authorizing solo version is exactly what the tradition guards against.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The primary, and the Arabic.** Read via scholarly secondary sources, not al-Junayd's *Rasāʾil* or al-Qushayrī's *Risāla* whole; the primary Arabic and Knysh's full translation await a fuller reading.
- **Whether baqā's "return" is the book's ordinary Tuesday.** al-Junayd's return is to sobriety and to God's service; whether that is the same householder return the book means, or a more ascetic one, is a comparison a fuller reading would test, and it connects to the already-queued Hasidic *avodah be-gashmiyut* arrival.
