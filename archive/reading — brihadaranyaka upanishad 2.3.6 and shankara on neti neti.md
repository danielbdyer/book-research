# reading — brihadaranyaka upanishad 2.3.6 and shankara on neti neti

**What was read.** The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.3.6 (the *neti neti* passage) with Śaṅkara's commentary (*bhāṣya*) on it, in Swami Madhavananda's public-domain translation (*The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad with the commentary of Śaṅkarācārya*), verified 2026-08-10 against web sources reporting it, the full text being available at archive.org. Śaṅkara's *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* on liberation-as-ever-established was named in the note but not separately read here. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 37.

**How it is cited.** `Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.3.6` (the *neti neti* locus, also at 3.9.26) and `Śaṅkara, bhāṣya on BU 2.3.6`, by the stable text locators. Quotations are Madhavananda's and are marked below; read in translation, not the Sanskrit, and *neti neti* and *nirguṇa* should be confirmed against a scholarly edition before manuscript use.

**Why this reading.** [[advaita reaches the uncovering move through neti neti and an ever-attained freedom]] claimed Advaita states the book's uncovering move at its most absolute — liberation uncovered, not produced — while negating the first person the book restores. The failable question: does *neti neti* reach the real by stripping away (the privative), and does Śaṅkara treat knowledge as the removal of ignorance rather than the production of liberation, while negating the personal, qualified self?

## What the reading settled

1. **The negation is confirmed as the method — *neti neti*.** BU 2.3.6: "Now therefore the description (of Brahman): 'Not this, not this.' Because there is no other and more appropriate description than this 'Not this'" (Madhavananda). The real is reached by stripping away every specification — the privative grammar at its most absolute, an uncovering by subtraction, from a tradition with no contact with either founding pole.

2. **Liberation is uncovered, not produced — confirmed at the *bhāṣya*.** Śaṅkara's commentary holds that *neti neti*'s function "is to remove the obstructions produced by ignorance," and that Brahman "has no distinguishing mark such as name, or form, or action, or heterogeneity, or species, or qualities," the two negative particles used "so as to eliminate every possible specification." Knowledge removes the veil of ignorance rather than producing the real; liberation is a seeing, not an acquisition — a stronger statement of "nothing is added, only uncovered" than the Platonic recollection the vault already logs.

3. **The refusal is confirmed, and it is the sharp one.** The self Advaita uncovers is attributeless Brahman — no name, form, action, or qualities — so *neti neti* negates exactly the personal, qualified first person the book restores on its last page, and the world of specifications the book sanctifies as the ordinary Tuesday. Advaita is company for the uncovering grammar and an adversary to the book's destination.

**Verdict for the note.** Advaita is a genuine, near-family independent arrival at the book's uncovering move, confirmed at BU 2.3.6 and Śaṅkara's *bhāṣya*. It states "uncovered, not produced" at its most absolute and negates the restored first person, which is its value as company that argues back. The note moves from privated to full. The refusal stands.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The Sanskrit, and the Brahmasūtrabhāṣya.** Read in Madhavananda's translation, not the Sanskrit; the fuller doctrine that *mokṣa* is ever-established (*nitya-siddha*) and that knowledge alone, not action, liberates — from Śaṅkara's *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* — is held from general knowledge and the *bhāṣya*'s removal-of-ignorance clause, not separately read.
- **Whether the pedagogical reading softens the refusal.** Some scholars read *neti neti* as pedagogy rather than world-denial; the refusal above rests on the standard Śaṅkaran attributeless-Brahman reading, the one that bears on the book, and a fuller reading would weigh the alternative.
