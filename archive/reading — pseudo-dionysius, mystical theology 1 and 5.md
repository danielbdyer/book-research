# reading — pseudo-dionysius, mystical theology 1 and 5

**What was read.** Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, *The Mystical Theology*, chapters 1 and 5, in the public-domain Shrine of Wisdom translation (1923) at esotericarchives.com, retrieved 2026-08-10. Eckhart's treatise *On Detachment* (*Von Abgeschiedenheit*), named in the note as the seated Eckhart's own via negativa, was not separately read here. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 43.

**How it is cited.** `Pseudo-Dionysius, Mystical Theology 1` and `Mystical Theology 5`, by chapter. Quotations are the Shrine of Wisdom translation's and are marked below; read in translation, not the Greek.

**Why this reading.** [[pseudo-dionysius reaches the open center by stripping predicates away]] holds that Christian apophatic theology reaches the book's open center and its privative grammar by subtraction, from an unrelated Neoplatonic lineage. The failable question: does the *Mystical Theology* ascend to union by stripping away all things, and does chapter 5 carry the cascade of pure negations?

## What the reading settled

1. **The ascent by stripping away — confirmed.** MT ch. 1: "leave behind the senses and the operations of the intellect, and all things sensible and intellectual… that thou mayest arise by unknowing towards the union… into the superessential Radiance of the Divine Darkness." The transcendent is reached not by adding predicates but by leaving all things behind — a knowing by unknowing, the open center entered by negation, from a Neoplatonic lineage with no contact with either founding pole.

2. **The cascade of negations — confirmed.** MT ch. 5: "He is neither soul nor intellect… nor number nor order; nor greatness nor smallness… neither is He darkness nor light… nor can any affirmation or negation be applied to Him, inasmuch as the all-perfect and unique Cause of all things transcends all affirmation." The pure via negativa: every specification stripped, and even the negations finally surpassed.

**Verdict for the note.** Pseudo-Dionysius is a genuine independent arrival for the open center and the privative grammar, confirmed at MT ch. 1 and ch. 5 in a public-domain translation. The note moves from privated to full. The refusal holds: the negations terminate in a God beyond being — "the all-perfect and unique Cause of all things" — and the stripping is meant to leave the self behind in union with what is above being, not to send the first person home to a house with no ceiling. This reopens the 2026-08-07 semi-shelving of the apophatic lineage on the narrower open-center-and-privative-grammar terms.

## What the reading did not settle

- **Eckhart, and the Greek.** Read in translation, not the Greek; Eckhart's *On Detachment*, named as the seated Eckhart's own via negativa, was not separately read here — the vault holds his *Gelassenheit* via Caputo and his householder move via the Tuesday note, and the detachment treatise would deepen the arrival.
- **Whether "the Divine Darkness" is the book's open center or a different void.** Dionysius's darkness is the super-essential God; whether the book's open center as a site of ordinary disclosure is the same place, or only structurally similar, is the comparison a fuller reading would test — the same question the Daodejing reading left open.
