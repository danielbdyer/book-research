# reading — the kashmir śaiva loci (spanda, śiva sūtra, pratyabhijñāhṛdaya, ipk)

**What was read.** The primary loci of the tradition the corpus places beneath the book rather than in its company — read across five texts, each at the primary Sanskrit where reachable and at a reputable published translation:

- **Spanda Kārikā** (Vasugupta/Kallaṭa) with **Kṣemarāja's *Spandanirṇaya*** — the Sanskrit at GRETIL (M. K. Śāstrī / KSTS ed., 1925), the translation Jaideva Singh, *Spanda-Kārikās: The Divine Creative Pulsation* (Motilal Banarsidass), with Dyczkowski's *The Doctrine of Vibration*.
- **Śiva Sūtra** (Vasugupta) with **Kṣemarāja's *Śivasūtravimarśinī*** — the root text at sanskritdocuments.org (Kak) and GRETIL, the commentary read directly from Jaideva Singh's printed Devanagari of the *Vimarśinī* (*Śiva Sūtras: The Yoga of Supreme Identity*, Motilal); Wallis (hareesh.org) as a third witness.
- **Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam** (Kṣemarāja) — the Sanskrit at GRETIL (CC-licensed) and Pradīpaka's Trika edition (sanskrit-trikashaivism.com), the translation Jaideva Singh, *Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam: The Secret of Self-Recognition* (Motilal, full text on archive.org/IGNCA). **This text was gated on two prior attempts (2026-08-09, 2026-08-24); this pass reached it three independent ways, all agreeing character-for-character.**
- **Utpaladeva's Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā (IPK)** with the author's Vṛtti — the Sanskrit at GRETIL (Kaul Shastri 1921 / Torella 1994), the translation Raffaele Torella, *The Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā of Utpaladeva with the Author's Vṛtti* (2002), quoted with pages through Torella's own essays.
- **Abhinavagupta on *camatkāra*** — via Raniero Gnoli's *The Aesthetic Experience According to Abhinavagupta* (quoted verbatim through reputable sources), Torella's "Camatkāra" (*JIS* 34, 2022), and the *Sāhityadarpaṇa* III.2–3 (primary Sanskrit) for the *brahmāsvāda* comparison.

This is the first continuous primary reading of the tradition in the vault; the two source-tradition chairs (`kshemaraja`, `utpaladeva`) previously recorded "no continuous reading exists in this vault."

**How it is cited.** By text and verse/sūtra in a named edition: `Spanda Kārikā I.1`; `Kṣemarāja, Spandanirṇaya on I.1`; `Śiva Sūtra I.12 (Kṣemarāja's Vimarśinī)`; `Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam sūtra 9 (Singh p. 72)`; `Utpaladeva, Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā I.5.13 (Torella 2002, p. 120)`. The Sanskrit is given in IAST (the scholarly "as printed" form for GRETIL and Torella); Devanagari where an edition prints it. A copyright edition is owed before any line is set in the manuscript.

## What the reading settled

1. **The pulse (spanda) — *unmeṣa/nimeṣa* at Spanda Kārikā I.1, and *kiñcit calana* in Kṣemarāja.** The maṅgala verse is exact: "yasyonmeṣanimeṣābhyāṃ jagataḥ pralayodayau | taṃ śakticakravibhavaprabhavaṃ śaṃkaraṃ stumaḥ" (I.1) — Singh: "We laud that Śaṅkara by whose mere opening and shutting of the eye-lids there is the appearance and dissolution of the world." *Unmeṣa* and *nimeṣa* are the opening and closing (of Śiva's eyes / of awareness) by which the world arises and dissolves. Kṣemarāja's *Spandanirṇaya* on I.1 defines spanda by the verbal root: it is *kiṃcit calat*, "moving a little," so the Lord's being is "sadā-spanda … na tv aspandaḥ" (ever-vibrating, never motionless) — Singh: "known as spanda in accordance with the root-meaning … slight movement (kiñcit calatta)," "a throb … which excludes all succession." The qualifier *kiṃcit* ("a little") is the whole point: movement-in-the-changeless, not physical motion. Grounds `[[the felt catch is a doorway that gives passage rather than a peak to be stood upon]]` (unmeṣa/nimeṣa) and the spanda pole of `[[aliveness has a specific felt signature the reader can learn to recognize and trust]]` (kiñcit calana).

2. **The malas — the twice-gated reading, now at primary.** Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam sūtra 9: "cidvat tacchaktisaṅkocān malāvṛtaḥ saṃsārī" — Singh: "the individual (saṃsārī), though in essence the same as cit, is covered by mala through the contraction (*saṅkoca*) of its powers (*śakti*)." Kṣemarāja's vṛtti defines the three, each a contracted śakti: **āṇavamala** = *apūrṇammanyatā* ("considering itself imperfect," from contracted will); **māyīyamala** = *bhinnavedyaprathā* ("the apprehension of all objects as different," from contracted knowledge); **kārmamala** = *śubhāśubhānuṣṭhānamaya* ("doing good or evil," from contracted action). This grounds `[[the triads privations map the three malas corner for corner]]` and confirms the 2026-08-09 secondary check at the primary Sanskrit — with the third-corner precision the check suspected: **the corpus's "corner for corner" holds at the malas' own definitions for two corners** (āṇava↔the Wall's deficiency; māyīya↔the Door's separation), and the third is loose in a now-nameable way — **kārmamala's own core is contracted *agency* (*kiñcitkartṛtva*), not residue**; the "sediment/residue" the book maps to the Floor is Singh's explanatory *vāsanā*-gloss and, structurally, the fourth of the five acts, *bīja-avasthāpana* ("settling of the seed," sūtra 11), where a withdrawn experience "acquires the state of saṃsāra in germ." Sūtra 5 is a second explicit contraction-locus: "citir eva cetanapadād avarūḍhā cetyasaṅkocinī cittam" (Singh p. 59) — universal Citi *contracts* (*saṅkocinī*) into the individual citta. All three malas are contractions (*saṅkoca*) of consciousness's powers, "uncontracted powers appearing contracted," not added defects — which grounds `[[suffering is contraction never corruption]]` in the tradition's own words.

3. **Wonder (vismaya) — Śiva Sūtra I.12, glossed with camatkāra.** "vismayo yogabhūmikāḥ" (I.12 in Kṣemarāja's recension) — Singh: "The stations and stages of yoga constitute a fascinating wonder"; Wallis: "The stages of yoga are wondrous." Wonder *is* the yogic stations, not a novice's state. Kṣemarāja's *Vimarśinī* welds it to camatkāra: the great yogin, "nityaṃ" (constantly), by penetration into his own consciousness-mass, is full of "niḥsāmānya-atiśaya-nava-nava-camatkāra" ("extraordinary, pre-eminent, ever-new" wonder), a "muhur muhur āścaryāyamāṇatā" (astonishment again and again); and the *Śrīkulayukti* verse he quotes — "when the Self is known by the Self … the Self beholds wonder in the Self alone" — is a near-exact statement of awareness savouring itself. Grounds the Śiva Sūtra pole of `[[wonder is not the novice's state to be outgrown but the native air of the awake]]`.

4. **Reflexivity (prakāśa/vimarśa) — the crystal that cannot say "I."** Utpaladeva, IPK I.5.11: "svabhāvam avabhāsasya vimarśaṃ vidur anyathā | prakāśo 'rthoparakto 'pi sphaṭikādijaḍopamaḥ" — Torella (p. 118): "The essential nature of light is reflective awareness; otherwise light, though 'coloured' by objects, would be similar to an insentient reality, such as the crystal and so on." And IPK I.5.13: "citiḥ pratyavamarśātmā …" — Torella (p. 120): "Consciousness has as its essential nature reflective awareness … the sovereignty of the supreme Self." (**Locator correction:** the *pratyavamarśa* verse is I.5.13, the crystal-contrast I.5.11.) Kṣemarāja states it in his own register (*Parāpraveśikā*, via Singh): "vimarśa is akṛtrimam aham iti visphuraṇam" (the spontaneous flashing-forth of the uncontrived 'I'), "that aspect of prakāśa by which it knows itself." Grounds the tradition's side of `[[awareness has a reflexive dimension distinct from its illuminating one]]`.

5. **Recognition (pratyabhijñā) — the beloved standing unrecognized.** Utpaladeva, IPK IV.1.17, the verse that names the school — Torella (p. 218): "Just like the beloved, who after much insistence finally stands in the presence of the maid in love, though he is there before her he does not give her any pleasure until she recognizes who he is … so for mankind the self, who is yet the Lord of the world, cannot manifest his own glory until his qualities have been brought to light. For this reason the doctrine of the recognition of the Lord has here been expounded." The move adds nothing: recognition unites the qualities already known with the person already present; IV.1.16 vṛtti, "pratyabhijñā-mātrāt" — Śiva-nature attained "through mere recognition," in ordinary activity "just as it stands" (*yathāvasthita eva*); IV.1.18, the doctrine expounded "for people's effortless attainment" (*ayatna-siddhi*). Grounds the pratyabhijñā pole of `[[another person can only sound the note already strung in you so influence awakens rather than implants]]` (the beloved adds nothing; the note was already strung) and the Śaiva reach of `[[the self you are seeking has been nearer than the seeking itself]]` (*svātmāpi viśveśvaraḥ* — the sought Lord *is* one's own self, "there before her" the whole time). **Attribution guard:** the popular "a friend says *this is that very man*" is **not** in Utpaladeva's verse — the maiden simply "does not recognize" (*aparijñāta*) until recognition dawns; the "this is that man" (*so'yaṃ Devadattaḥ*) uniting-formula and the speaking friend are Abhinavagupta's commentarial elaboration and the stock recognition-structure, so the verse is Utpaladeva's and the "this is he" is Abhinavagupta's — kept distinct.

6. **Camatkāra — awareness savouring itself.** The recognition school's own equation, primary: Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam sūtra 11 vṛtti, "camatkārāpara-paryāya-vimarśana" — "vimarśana, whose other name is camatkāra"; sūtra 20 vṛtti, the perfect I-consciousness is "the uncontrived camatkāra of one's own Self" (*akṛtrimaḥ svātmacamatkāraḥ*); Singh's note 98, "camatkāra … the experience of 'Ah! How wonderful!' … an intuitive flash." Abhinavagupta unfolds the etymology (via Gnoli): camatkāra is "*camataḥ karaṇam*," the act of a tasting subject "immersed in the vibration (spanda) of a marvellous enjoyment," "an enjoyment which can never satiate." Torella: camatkāra, "inner deep savouring, marked by a sense of bliss," originates with Utpaladeva and is developed by Abhinavagupta. Confirms `[[the recognition school installs wonder at the definition of consciousness]]` at primary where it had rested on secondary scholarship.

7. **The Kṣemarāja chair's own planted formula, confirmed at its sūtra.** The book's planted center-formula, "*madhya-vikāsāt* — from the unfolding of the center comes the bliss of consciousness," is Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam **sūtra 17**: "madhyavikāsāc cidānandalābhaḥ" — Singh (p. 91): "By the development of the *madhya* (centre) there is acquisition of the bliss of *cit* (*cidānanda*)." Sūtra 16 gives the state it belongs to: *jīvanmukti*, "liberation even while alive," at the attainment of *cidānanda*.

8. **Two further loci grounded in passing.** The five cosmic acts (*pañcakṛtya*) — sūtra 10, "tathāpi tadvat pañcakṛtyāni karoti" ("he does the five acts like Śiva"): *sṛṣṭi, sthiti, saṃhāra, vilaya/tirodhāna, anugraha*; and the esoteric perception-circuit of sūtra 11 (*ābhāsana, rakti, vimarśana, bīja-avasthāpana, vilāpana*). And Śiva Sūtra I.1, "caitanyam ātmā" (Singh: "Awareness which has absolute freedom of all knowledge and activity is the Self"), and I.5, "udyamo bhairavaḥ" (*udyama* = *unmajjana*, the sudden emergence of consciousness, not effort).

## What the reading did not settle — and the corrections it forced

- **Five vault attributions corrected.** (i) *vikāsa/saṅkoca* is **not** in Spanda Kārikā I.1 — the root verse has only *unmeṣa/nimeṣa*; the expansion/contraction pair is in Kṣemarāja's *Spandanirṇaya* commentary on I.1 and in Dyczkowski's doctrine, so the citation is re-pointed there. (ii) The *pratyavamarśa* verse is IPK **I.5.13**, not I.5.11. (iii) The "friend says *this is he*" is Abhinavagupta's, not Utpaladeva's verse. (iv) *kārmamala* is contracted *agency*, not residue — the residue motif is *bīja-avasthāpana*. (v) "*brahmāsvāda-sahodara*" ("sibling of the tasting of brahman") is **Viśvanātha's** *Sāhityadarpaṇa* III.2–3 (14th c.), not Abhinavagupta's; the akin-but-distinct *doctrine* (aesthetic tasting resembles yet is not liberation) is Abhinavagupta's own (Locana, Abhinavabhāratī).
- **A scholarly crux to leave open.** At Spanda Kārikā I.1, which eye-movement maps to which cosmic event — *unmeṣa*↔arising or *unmeṣa*↔dissolution — is genuinely contested (Singh and Kṣemarāja read *unmeṣa*↔*udaya*; Lakshmanjoo reads the reverse). The vault's phrasing does not commit, and should stay uncommitted.
- **A translation nuance to keep.** Torella (2022) cautions that *camatkāra* in the Pratyabhijñā register is not reducible to *vismaya* as mere surprise — it is the reflexive *savouring* (vimarśa), not object-directed startle. The tradition draws the camatkāra–vismaya link firmly (aesthetically in Viśvanātha, yogically at Śiva Sūtra I.12), and the nuance is kept beside it.
- **What stayed at secondary grade.** Abhinavagupta's *Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī* gloss of *vimarśa* as *camatkāra* on I.5.11/I.5.13 was reached only through reputable scholarship (the Sanskrit ĪPV e-texts were bot-gated); Gnoli's camatkāra passage was confirmed through sources quoting him verbatim, the archive scan's OCR being unusable. The camatkāra equation is itself primary-grounded in the Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam (item 6), so the concern's grounding does not depend on the gated ĪPV; but a copyright edition of Gnoli and of the ĪPV is owed before their exact wording is set in the manuscript.
- **Reachability, for the record.** The gate that blocked the Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam twice was a URL-discovery problem plus a user-agent bot-filter (sanskrit-trikashaivism.com returns 403 to the proxy's UA, 200 to a browser UA), not a policy wall; GRETIL's CC-licensed Sanskrit and Singh's archive.org text both served cleanly. academia.edu, researchgate, scribd, and zenodo returned origin-side 403s throughout and were reached only through peer-reviewed articles quoting them.
