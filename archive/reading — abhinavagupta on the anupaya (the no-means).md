# reading — abhinavagupta on the anupaya (the no-means)

**What was read.** Abhinavagupta's *anupāya* (the "no-means," the fourth and highest of the four *upāyas*), from the *Tantrāloka* (āhnika 2) and *Tantrasāra* (ch. 2), read via the Lakshmanjoo Academy's authoritative teaching on the *upāyas* (Swami Lakshmanjoo, in the Kashmir Śaiva lineage), retrieved 2026-08-10. The primary Sanskrit and a full scholarly translation (Gnoli's *Tantrasāra*; Lakshmanjoo's *Light on Tantra*) were not read whole in-container. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 44.

**How it is cited.** `Abhinavagupta, Tantrāloka, āhnika 2 (anupāya)`, by the stable locator. Quotations below are the Lakshmanjoo Academy's renderings; the Sanskrit and a scholarly critical translation await a fuller reading before manuscript use.

**Why this reading.** [[the kashmir shaiva anupaya turns the negative way on method itself]] holds that the book's own Indic tradition, at its apex, states that there is no means — the negative way turned on method itself — which states the engineered-epiphany refusal from inside and deepens reading-is-remembering. The failable question: does *anupāya* hold that there is no method because one is already That, and is it reserved for the most graced?

## What the reading settled

1. **The no-means — confirmed.** *Anupāya* is "the means where there is nothing to be done" for attaining oneness with Universal Being; entry occurs "at once, without doing anything, without adopting means or anything." At the highest reach there is no technique at all, because you are already That, so any method would posit a gap that is not there. This states the book's front-matter doctrine — you already know, reading is remembering — from inside the book's own tradition, and it states the engineered-epiphany refusal at its sharpest: no apparatus can cause recognition, because there is nothing to be done.

2. **Reserved for the most graced — the refusal confirmed.** *Anupāya* sits atop the four *upāyas*, graded by the intensity of grace (*śaktipāta*): "If you have not the capacity [for *anupāya*], then you have to move to *śāmbhavopāya*. Then… to *śāktopāya*, and then to *āṇavopāya*." So it is an endpoint for the most graced, not a democratic starting posture — which is why the tradition would decline the book's offer of the no-means to every reader as a doorway.

**Verdict for the note.** *Anupāya* is confirmed as the source tradition's own apophatic apex, an internal deepening of the book's move rather than outside company. The note moves from privated to full. The refusal extends the tradition's already-recorded second decline (grace independent of effort): the no-means is for the most graced, not for everyone, so the book's offer of it to every reader is exactly what the tradition guards against.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The Sanskrit, and the full Tantrāloka.** Read via the Lakshmanjoo Academy's teaching, not the *Tantrāloka* āhnika 2 or *Tantrasāra* ch. 2 in a scholarly translation; the primary and Gnoli's edition await a fuller reading, and this connects to the already-queued extraction of the four *upāyas* in `ops/queue/extractions.md`.
- **Whether the book's "reading is remembering" is anupāya or a lower upāya.** The book offers its no-means to every reader; whether that maps to *anupāya* (for the most graced) or to a graded lower means is a distinction a fuller reading would draw.
