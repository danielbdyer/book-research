# reading — luria (vital), etz chaim gate 1 on tzimtzum

**What was read.** The tzimtzum passage of the *Etz Chaim* (Ḥayyim Vital's redaction of Isaac Luria's teaching), Gate 1, chapter 2, in the English and Hebrew at Sefaria (*Sefer Etz Chaim* 1:2), retrieved 2026-08-10, cross-read against scholarly treatments of the Cordovero–Luria difference (Scholem and his critics). The full *Etz Chaim* and the surrounding Lurianic myth were not read whole; the one gate that carries the contraction was read at the text. This addresses reading-queue tier 1b, entry 47 — the reading marked "read last, with the flag."

**How it is cited.** `Vital, Etz Chaim, Gate 1, ch. 2 (Sefer Etz Chaim 1:2)`, by gate and chapter. The English quotation below is Sefaria's translation and is marked; the Hebrew phrase is given from the same source.

**Why this reading.** [[tzimtzum is a withdrawal that makes room and is company for the container law only]] holds that Lurianic tzimtzum is a genuine structural cousin of the book's container-that-withdraws law, company for that law only, under two standing cautions: the valence is inverted (in tzimtzum the contraction is the generative gift, in the book contraction is the problem), and the corpus's only tzimtzum is a provenance-flagged machine pairing. The failable questions: does the *Etz Chaim* make tzimtzum a withdrawal that leaves an empty space for creation, is the Lurianic sense the withdrawal one rather than Cordovero's, and does anything survive the flag?

## What the reading settled

1. **The withdrawal that makes room — confirmed at the text.** *Etz Chaim* 1:2: "And then He contracted Himself, may He be blessed, in the middle point… And that light was then contracted and withdrew to the sides surrounding the central point, and then a void, air, and empty space were left, from a central point" — the Hebrew leaving מקום פנוי (*makom panui*, a vacated place) and חלל רקני (*chalal reikani*, an empty void). The first creative act is a withdrawal, not an outpouring: the Infinite contracts to open a space where a world that is not simply God can be. This is the same shape as the book's container, which makes an open center, and as the peak the performing law honors by withdrawing ([[the peak is the one unit the performing law can honor only by withdrawing]]).

2. **The Lurianic sense is the withdrawal sense — the Cordovero caution settled.** The note flagged that Moshe Cordovero's earlier tzimtzum differs and that which is meant must be verified. It is settled: Cordovero's tzimtzum is concentration and pedagogical concealment — the divine light condensing so as to be revealed and withstood, "a teacher withholding full knowledge" — while Luria's, as Scholem reads it and as *Etz Chaim* 1:2 states it, is withdrawal to leave an empty space. The book's cousin is specifically the Lurianic withdrawal, not the Cordoveran concentration; the note's reading of the term is the correct one.

3. **The valence is inverted — the first caution confirmed.** In the *Etz Chaim* the contraction is the generative divine gift: withdrawal is what makes creation possible, the necessary first grace. In the book, contraction is the problem — fullness folded into felt lack — so tzimtzum is company for the container-that-withdraws law and is **not** an arrival at [[suffering is contraction never corruption]]. The reading confirms the inversion rather than dissolving it: same structural move, opposite valence on what contraction is for.

**Verdict for the note.** Tzimtzum is confirmed as a genuine structural cousin of the container-that-withdraws law, at the primary text, company for that law only. The note moves from privated to full. Two things do not change. The refusal holds — the register is cosmic and theological where the book is psychological, and the *shevirat ha-kelim*, the shattering of the vessels that cannot hold the light, is corruption-shaped, which the book's "nothing is broken" denies. And the provenance flag stays: the reading gives the *note* a real primary-text half, but the corpus's own tzimtzum entry remains the machine-generated tzimtzum ≈ Winnicott pairing, which fails the book's did-the-author-notice-it-first test and enters as a candidate to work, never as an arrival.

## What the reading did not settle

- **The Hebrew beyond the one passage, and the whole myth.** Read Gate 1 ch. 2 at the text with the surrounding *Etz Chaim* and the *kav*, the vessels, and the *shevirah* taken from scholarly summary, not read whole; a fuller reading would test whether the shattering is as flatly corruption-shaped as the note's refusal states, or whether the *tikkun* that mends it rhymes with the book's householder repair.
- **Whether the literal-or-figurative tzimtzum debate bears on the book.** Later kabbalah split over whether the withdrawal is literal (a real absence of God) or figurative (a concealment only); the book's container is a held frame rather than a metaphysical claim, so the debate may not touch it — but which side the analogy leans on is a distinction a fuller reading would draw before the pairing is used in manuscript.
