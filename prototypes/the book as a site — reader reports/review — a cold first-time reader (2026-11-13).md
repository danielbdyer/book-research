# Cold reader report: "The Ecology of Recognition" prototype site

I read only the site file. I opened every view at 1280×900 and 390×844 and tried every interactive piece. Screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-book-research/1d8b05a5-c418-5484-84b3-868833dec948/scratchpad/review/cold/`.

## 1. After two minutes on the home page

It looks like a contemplative self-help book by someone well read in philosophy and therapy. Its argument is that you aren't damaged but "folded," and that getting better means remembering rather than learning. It borrows the shape of a psychedelic trip, without drugs, as its structure. It seems written for spiritually curious adults who know some therapy language. It wants me to "Step in" to an interactive house, read the opening, and send a line to a friend. I could not tell who wrote it, whether the book exists, or how I'd get it. The banner "A PROTOTYPE · PRIVATE TO THE AUTHOR" made me wonder whether I was meant to be there.

## 2. After reading everything

It is an unpublished book in draft by an unnamed poet and facilitator. It argues that suffering is a tightening rather than damage, that change is recognition, and that nobody changes as a spectator. It walks the reader through a house of rooms paced like a trip. A large, unusually honest body of reading sits under it. What it wants from me: small practices today, another person brought in, a sentence kept, a sign-up for "letters."

**What changed:** the site turned out to be two things stuck together. One is a reader experience. The other is a research dossier written *to the author*, and that dossier leaks into the reader pages. My trust in the research went up. My sense that there is a finished book went down. I learned the origin (a 4am letter, the author's poems) only from fragments. I learned there may be a second "facilitator book" only on the author-only page.

## 4. Where I got lost, bored, or confused, and where I'd have left

- **The voice switches to the author.**
  - On *The sources*: "When do you stop polishing and send? You settled this one by sending the first letter."
  - On *You're not alone*, I typed my own sentence and picked "I'm doing this alone." The site answered: "You haven't answered this one yet… does reading your friend's letter at 4am count as having another person there?" I have no such letter.
  - Also: "Name Laozi as the source. How to do that is yours to decide."
  - It felt like reading someone's private notes.
- **The silent room** says "FINISH IT YOURSELF." I didn't know what to do. "About this room" then gives the answer, which undoes the exercise.
- **"Say what you've noticed"** ignores what I typed and shows a canned card repeated lower on the page. **"Where do you stand?"** replies "Nothing was counted or sent," so why ask?
- **"Ask the sources"** says "Ask anything," but there is no input box.
- **"Write back"** has no recipient. It's a journal prompt.
- **The numbers conflict.** One page says 47 thinkers were read in their own words. Another says 64 in the research and 24 read in their own words.
- **Boredom** set in at "Did they really get there on their own?" and "The strongest objections," which read like a defense brief. **I'd have left there.**
- **On a phone**, the timeline stops around 1500 CE. Heidegger and every modern thinker sit off-screen right, with no hint that you can swipe.

## 5. Preachy, vague, hedgy, repetitive, or self-important

- **Preachy:** "This book is not a book. It is an offering." "Some sections will confuse you. Good. Stay with the confusion. It is working." The second excuses unclear writing in advance. "If you finish quickly, you have not finished." "The reader who finishes is not the reader who began."
- **Praising its own honesty:** "That's how you know no one is selling you a summit." "A book that is sure of itself can name what it hasn't settled." "A book worth reading can face its best critics." "Don't take our word for it." Show the honesty instead of announcing it.
- **Vague:** "Every moment of genuine presence is psychedelic in this sense."
- **Unparseable:** the author's quote "a fundamental clenching that didn't obscure the vision but just made it transfigured as not directly perceivable."
- **Hedgy:** "Still open" appears three times. "Whether that rules out a book is still being checked." On Heidegger, whose word is the book's center: "NOT YET, ONLY SECONDHAND."
- **Repetitive:**
  - "You already know everything in this book": home, house door, every footer.
  - "Nothing transforms from the audience": about five times.
  - "Notice whether you came to this with hunger…": three times.
  - "Being seen is a different event from being explained": three times.
  - The tuning-fork quote: twice.
  - "Step in": everywhere.

## 6. What moved me

- "Nothing is ruined. Something is folded, and folds can open."
- "You thank the guard, and the gate, astonishingly often, opens."
- "the long ordinary patience of a Tuesday afternoon in autumn."
- **The best line on the site:** "You will close the book and walk into your kitchen. The kitchen will be a kitchen."
- The grief room's note: "I don't need you to fix it. Could I tell you about it? I'd like you to be the one who hears it." I could actually send that.
- **The "I feel empty, and I can't tell if it's insight or something wrong" answer**, which points away from the book to a person. Nothing raised my trust more.
- "Did a sentence here feel true just because it was beautiful?" This is the most honest question on the site.
- "Something I can say now, that I couldn't have said an hour ago, is —"
- The poem lines: "it was nice to be vulnerable / enough to recognize the answer / I had always had—". I wanted more of the author's own voice.
- **The warmest hook on the site, buried mid-page:** "A man noticed some things. Then he found out other people had noticed them too."

## 9. Five changes

1. **Name the author and say what the book is, above the fold:** its status, and how to get it or follow it.
2. **Strip every author-addressed line from the reader pages.** That includes "You settled this one…", "your friend's letter at 4am", "yours to decide", "the drafts", and "Still open." The author-only page claims "Everything that was hedging… lives here," but it doesn't yet.
3. **Introduce each person in half a line the first time:** who, when, what field. Define or cut the capitalized coinages: the Stance, the Psychedelic Moment, the book's one breath, the second chair, and Triad/Primitives/Kata.
4. **Make the actions honest.** Rename "Write back" to "Keep a sentence," or give it a real recipient. Make "Ask the sources" and "Say what you've noticed" work, or remove them. Link the source texts. Reconcile 47, 24, and 64.
5. **Cut the repetition and self-praise by half.** Lead with "A man noticed some things…" and the poems. Push the dossier (the independence table, the objections, the corrections) one click deeper, behind one plain sentence: "We checked each thinker's own words; here's where they disagree with us."

## 10. Did I trust it?

**Partly.**

**Credible:**
- Every thinker has a "where they part ways" line.
- It admits McLaren never said what the drafts claimed.
- It admits Heidegger was read only secondhand.
- It makes no promises about outcomes.
- The "I feel empty" answer sends me to a person.
- "Nothing you type here is saved or sent."

**Not credible:**
- No named author.
- "We checked," with no "we."
- Conflicting counts.
- Source citations that are internal codes and file paths.
- Tony Robbins and Werner Erhard beside Buber and Plato with no framing.
- The last page says an AI made the site.

**Author's claim versus others' words:** quotation marks clearly separate quotes from paraphrase. I usually could *not* tell whose quote it was: the finished book, "the drafts," the author's poems, a third-person design note about "the reader," or a thinker. The home page's seven truths carry unattributed pull-quotes, and "Show sources" swaps those for codes like "walked p. 4."

**Did "Behind the site" help?** Yes, a lot. It explained the half-erased word, why the research pages say "you" to the author, that the site copies another site's structure, that it's AI-made and private, and that the house is one possible shape. It also confirms that the reader pages still carry material meant for the author.
