# Semantic legibility audit of the prototype site

**What was read.** I read the visible text of every reader page, with "Show sources" off as a reader sees it, and then #map as the author's notes. Quotations are copied verbatim; the text of each page is saved in `legibility/text/`, next to this report. How a reader would take a line is my own judgment; no readers were tested.

## The diagnosis

1. **The more certain a page sounds, the less support it shows.** The front pages state the ideas most absolutely. The honest material sits "under the house" and sometimes contradicts them.
2. **"You" means two people.** Some reader pages still address the author.
3. **The book's content is listed seven ways, with no key between the lists.**

## 1. What kind of statement is it?

**1.1 The book's opening lines have no speaker.** The home page's main heading is "You already know everything in this book. You've just forgotten. Reading is remembering." Without a label, a reader takes it as a factual claim about them, or as a sales hook. On #house the label "OVER THE DOOR" makes the same lines read correctly, as an inscription. *Fix:* label the home heading "How the book begins", and stop repeating the lines on three pages and the footer.

**1.2 Instructions are listed as truths.** "Seven things this book holds to be true" includes an instruction ("Don't storm your own gates. Thank the guard.") and an adopted word history ("Psychedelic means soul-manifesting."). *Fix:* move thesis 6 into the house as a practice. Rewrite thesis 5 as: "The book uses 'psychedelic' in its first sense, 'soul-manifesting' (Humphry Osmond's 1956 coinage). No substance is involved."

**1.3 The book's own words look like outside support.** Six of the seven home theses rest on a quotation from the book's own drafts, with no credit; only thesis 3 names its speaker (Gendlin). A reader sees quotation marks and assumes someone else agrees; the house quotations do the same. *Fix:* name every speaker ("— the book", "— the author, in a poem", "— Eugene Gendlin, 1978"). Where an outside thinker reached the same point, quote them instead. For thesis 4, quote Maturana and Varela: "a source of perturbations and not of instructions". The reverse also happens: claim 7 on #came-from ends with "The youngness is just beginning." with no quotation marks, though it is the last line of poem #5. Credit it: "as the author wrote in a poem years before the book".

**1.4 A proposed answer is shown as a settled one.** #noticed says: "The answer, from the author's own reflection: recognition is already shared, so a reader is never fully alone." According to #map, these answers "are proposals in your research, not decisions you've made." *Fix:* head the section "How the book might answer" and mark each answer "Still open".

**1.5 The site describes a feature that does not exist.** #readings says: "Ask the sources. Ask anything the thinkers wrote about." There is no box to ask in, and #map says this version leaves out "an assistant that answers anything". *Fix:* cut the section.

## 2. What supports it?

**2.1 The central agreement between two traditions is stated most strongly where it is least supported.** The home page says: "A German philosopher in 1927 and a school of Kashmir nearly a thousand years earlier never met. Both found the same thing: the deepest truth isn't learned. It's remembered." "Never met" is obvious from the dates; the real evidence (a scholar found no route from Heidegger to the Kashmir texts) appears only on #came-from. Meanwhile #company marks Heidegger as read "NOT YET, ONLY SECONDHAND", and #noticed reports: "In 1964 Heidegger wrote that his key word, aletheia, 'may not be equated with truth.'" *Fix:* "A German philosopher in 1927 and a Kashmir school nine centuries earlier, with no route between them that scholars have found, both described truth as something uncovered rather than learned. They disagree about much else; this site shows where."

**2.2 The house teaches the body's response as proof, and the sources page denies it.** The "Find the word" room on #house says: "When it fits, something lets go. That's how you'll know." Thesis 3 says: "When the right word comes, the body says so." #readings says Gendlin "used it more narrowly: it shows that your inner experience has moved", and it cites Nisbett and Wilson's subjects, who "changed their attitudes in the absence of any subjective experience of change". Nothing reconciles these. *Fix for the house:* "When it fits, something often eases. That tells you something moved, not that the word is true." *Fix for thesis 3:* "When a word fits, the body often eases first."

**2.3 The counts contradict each other.** #readings says "47 thinkers read in their own words". #company says "64 thinkers in the research" and "24 read in their own words". The chart shows 28. On a site that asks for trust because it counts carefully, nothing does more damage. *Fix:* use one definition everywhere, for example "24 of 64 thinkers read in their own words; 47 readings in all."

**2.4 The reader is never told what "the drafts" or "the research" are.** Phrases such as "The drafts credit her with it", "the record says yes" and "21 times the original corrected the draft" stand in for evidence the reader cannot identify. *Fix:* open #readings with "So far the book exists as several drafts and a set of research notes. Wherever the drafts described a thinker, we went back to the thinker's own text."

**2.5 The author's own noticing, which the site says came first, is buried.** The home page says: "A man noticed some things. Then he found out other people had noticed them too." The dates that show this order sit in a bare table at the bottom of #came-from. This is the site's one real underclaim. *Fix:* add under the home sentence: "He was saying these things in poems for years before he read the Kashmir texts in March 2026." In the same way, the word "who?" on #house shows its grounding line ("a question that requires a witness even to be asked properly") only with sources on; print that line under it.

## 3. How firmly is it held?

**3.1 The site drops the book's own qualifier.** The draft text on #opening says: "We are not, mostly, broken." Home thesis 1 says: "You are not broken. … Nothing is ruined." Yet #company makes room for a grieving reader who does not "want to be told the loss isn't real." *Fix:* "Most of what feels like damage is a part of you that tightened, often to protect you."

**3.2 "It tells you what is already true"** (home; #came-from claim 5). This turns "holds to be true" into a guarantee. *Fix:* "It tells you what it holds to be true, and asks you to come and see for yourself."

**3.3 An open question is closed in the house.** #house says: "Grief finishes in company." and "Someone belongs in the other chair. You already know who." #readings calls the grief claim "the book's own" and lists "Can this work be done alone, with just a book? … Still open." *Fix:* "The book holds that grief finishes in company; McLaren, whom it drew on, thinks grief can also be done alone. If someone comes to mind, they belong in the other chair."

**3.4 What the book is designed to do is stated as a promised result.** #arc says: "You'll think you're reading a book of ideas. The pacing walks you through the arc." #map says the site stops short of "promising outcomes". *Fix:* "The book is paced to follow that arc." Likewise, beside the countdown on #house: "You can step into the first one for ten seconds and remember everything you had forgotten about being human." ("The first one" refers to nothing on the page.) *Fix:* cut it.

**3.5 Some lines treat agreement as proof.** #noticed says: "so that when this book says two people found the same thing independently, you can trust it". #came-from says: "That's how you know no one is selling you a summit." The author's stance is that agreement is company, not proof. *Fix:* "…that statement is accurate. It is company, not proof."

## 4. Do the claims add up to one argument?

**4.1 The site lists the book's content seven ways, with no key between them:** seven theses (home), eight claims (#came-from), six "ideas the book is built on" (#readings), four picker ideas (#noticed), seven situations (#company), six arc phases, and eight rooms. The central thesis has five names: "folded" (home), "obstructed" (#came-from claim 3), "blocked" (#opening), "tightened" or "clenching", and "contraction". A reader cannot tell whether these are one claim or several. *Fix:* state the argument once on the home page, open every page with one line saying which part it serves, and use "folded" outside quotations. Suggested wording:

> "The book makes one claim: what you are looking for was never lost, only hidden. How it gets hidden: a part of you tightened. How it comes back: you recognize it, in the body, by taking part rather than watching. Who else has seen this: people across centuries who got there separately. The house lets you try it; the thinkers show who got there too; the reply is your part."

**4.2 Two pages of claims compete, and the thinkers are kept apart from both.** The seven home theses and the eight claims on #came-from overlap on about four items, worded differently. The agreement among thinkers, one of the book's two centerpieces, gets one band on the home page. *Fix:* make #came-from the long form of the same seven claims. Under each one, add a line from #company: "Also seen by … · where they part: …", the way #arc links each phase to its room.

## 5. Does each word make sense the first time it appears?

- **"the clearing"** is used as a heading on #came-from but defined only in the #opening draft. *Fix:* gloss it at first use: "the open space where things can show themselves".
- **"recognition"**, the main word of the title, is defined only on #readings. *Fix:* add to thesis 2: "The book's word for this is recognition."
- **"the Stance"** (#house label) is a capitalized name that #opening calls scaffolding. *Fix:* rename it "Coming back".
- **"the gap"** (#arc and the house's breath tracker) is never defined. *Fix:* "the pause at the top of the breath, the silent room".
- **"madhya –"** (Sanskrit for "center") is never translated, and it is hidden on screens narrower than 1100px, so phone readers never meet the planted word the silent room finishes. *Fix:* show "from the unfolding of the center —" at every width.
- **"Why now: because you are living in what could be."** (#came-from claim 7) reads as praise of possibility, but it means the opposite. *Fix:* "because so much of life is spent longing for what could be instead of living what is."
- **"You haven't fallen asleep. You've forgotten."** (#came-from claim 1) answers another site's slogan ("Wake up"), which the reader never saw. *Fix:* "You haven't lost anything. You've forgotten where it is."
- **Other phrases a reader cannot place:**
  - "The archive preserves; the orchard ripens." (the author's maxim about sending his work; cut it)
  - "Book I, the facilitator's book" (readers do not know there are two books; cut it)
  - "codex" (say "book")
  - "summit", which means the silent room on #house but "all traditions agree" in claim 4 (keep it for the room)
  - chart notes such as "nothing descends into §129" (rewrite plainly; Heidegger's dense entry loads first)

**The two readers called "you".** These lines speak to the author:
- "One question for you: does reading your friend's letter at 4am count as having another person there?" (#noticed)
- "You haven't answered this one yet." (#noticed, #company)
- "Is reading 'contraction' as something useful the tradition's idea, or yours?" (#readings)
- "When do you stop polishing and send? You settled this one by sending the first letter." (#readings)
- "How to do that is yours to decide.", under the column heading "What the book owes" (#noticed)

*Fix:* move them to #map; on reader pages say "The author has not settled this."

## Ten highest-leverage changes, ranked

1. Move every line that addresses the author to #map.
2. Make the counts agree (2.3).
3. State one argument on the home page and use one name for the folding. Make #came-from carry the same seven claims (4.1, 4.2).
4. Fix the felt-shift contradiction (2.2).
5. Give the evidence and the disagreement for the two traditions, and say "company, not proof" (2.1, 3.5).
6. Name the speaker of every quotation (1.3).
7. Restore the qualifiers (3.1–3.4).
8. Gloss the five key words at first use, and show the planted word at every width (section 5).
9. Move the dated origin to the home page (2.5).
10. Cut or relabel what is not real or not meant for readers (1.4, 1.5, and the phrases in section 5).

## What already works and should be kept

- The heading "Seven things this book holds to be true", which credits the claims to the book. Extend it.
- "WHERE HE PARTS WAYS" under each thinker, and the "read in their own words / through a scholar / not yet" markers.
- On #noticed, the independence table's "NOT EVIDENCE: They were chosen because they share this idea."
- The corrections on #readings (McLaren, Gendlin, Rogers), the site's strongest trust-builders.
- On #reply, "Did a sentence here feel true just because it was beautiful?" Move it near the top of #readings.
- Words explained on contact: "contraction" in thesis 1, "composting" by its heading, and the opening lines framed by "OVER THE DOOR".

## Appendix: contrast and structure (partial)

The focus changed before the rendered-page sweep, so axe-core, tap-target and reading-ease checks were not run. These ratios are calculated from the stylesheet's color values; the structure notes come from the markup.

- **Light-theme failures** (normal text needs 4.5:1):
  - `--ink-3` on paper: 4.17 (`.anchor`, `.label`, `.chip.gradeb`, chart ticks)
  - `--ink-3` on paper-2: 3.83 (`dt`, `th`)
  - `--thread` on paper: 4.17 (`.tool .label`, `.layer .when`)
  - `--thread` on `--thread-soft`: 3.57 (`.chip.turned`)
  - paper text on `--thread`: 4.17 (`.btn2.gold`, `.bigbtn:hover`)

  Fix: `--ink-3: #5A6476` gives 5.13 on paper and 4.71 on paper-2; `--thread: #7A570D` gives 5.65, and 4.72 on its soft background.
- **Failures in both themes:**
  - `--night-faint` on night (summit and band labels, "Finish it yourself", the summit's details) is 2.55 in light and 2.18 in dark. An alpha of .62 gives 4.94.
  - The summit heading at opacity .55 is 4.11 at 17.6px. Opacity .7 gives 6.02.
- **Text size:** uppercase monospace text runs below 12px: `.label` 11.2px, nav links 11.5px, `.chip` 9.9px.
- **Structure:**
  - #opening has no h1.
  - `.arcbox` and `#chart` are marked `role="img"` but contain links and buttons, which hides those controls from screen readers.
