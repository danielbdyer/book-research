# Corrigibility audit: can this site, and the book it presents, be shown wrong?

*Scope: the reader-facing views of `site.html`, read against the vault for whether a reader's life, another person, time, the thinkers, or a critic could correct each claim, and whether the site shows it can be wrong.*

## The finding in brief

The site already holds most of what it needs: the thinkers' corrections of the drafts, a question testing the body's "yes" against beauty, a question testing thesis 2, and a "Not quite" button. All sit on the last pages, away from the claims they test. The home page and the house state the theses with no edge. Several lines also turn any outcome into confirmation. The fix is mostly moving and pairing material that already exists.

## 1. Lines that protect a claim from being disproved

1. **The felt "yes" taken as proof.** The Find-the-word room says: "When it fits, something lets go. *That's how you'll know.*" Its last step announces "That release is the felt shift." The vault holds the opposite. Gendlin's 1964 paper defines correctness as "the experienced effect which certain symbols produce", so the shift certifies that something moved, not that the word is true (`notes/the felt shift certifies carrying forward rather than correspondence.md`). The counterfeit catalogue records a bodily yes that the sentence itself produces: rhymed sayings are judged more accurate than the same idea unrhymed, and 37% of wrong solutions drew aha ratings above the average for correct ones (`notes/the felt sense of truth has a documented counterfeit catalogue.md`). The Sources page says so too; the room where the reader actually practises says the reverse.
2. **The reader's "no" recast as a guard to thank.** Thesis 6 says: "The part of you that says no has been protecting you… the gate, astonishingly often, opens." Every refusal, including a refusal of the book, becomes something to work past. The vault treats refusal as its best signal: a felt sense "precise enough to refuse a wrong word" is what separates it from fluency (`notes/the felt sense rejects formulations…`). Surrender against will is also a disagreement the research keeps open (status live), and thesis 6 settles one side of it.
3. **The participation law used as an escape hatch.** "Whatever changes in you, you did it." Read backwards, it says that if nothing changed, you stayed in the audience. The site never blocks that reading.
4. **Confusion and speed counted as success.** "Some sections will confuse you. Good… It is working." "If you finish quickly, you have not finished." They are the draft's own words and fit Read the opening, but the Claim page promotes the second to a thesis.
5. **The result presupposed.** The ten-second timer ends on "Remember?", next to a promise to "remember everything you had forgotten about being human."
6. **Injury reread as protection.** "What feels like damage is a part of you that tightened, often to protect you. Nothing is ruined." The vault's discrimination finding is that a reader in regression who holds the book's vocabulary "has been handed a more fluent way to misdescribe their condition," and no first-person guard catches that case (`notes/a solitary reader can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates.md`).
7. **An objection answered by redefinition.** To "This work can't be done alone," the site replies "recognition is already shared, so a reader is never fully alone." The vault records this as "a candidate drawn from the author's reflection, not his decision."

**Keep these:** the "Not quite" button; "It doesn't matter whether the body-shift came or not"; and, on Write back, "Did a sentence here feel true just because it was beautiful?" and "Did something new arrive, rather than something remembered?" Each is a working falsifier placed far from the claim it tests.

## 2. Calibration

**Stated more firmly than the vault holds:**

- **"It tells you what is already true"** (home). "Seven things this book holds to be true" is good scoping, and this line undoes it. The author's stance is "there seems to be something there" (`ops/methodology/authorial stance.md`).
- **The arc as an accomplished effect.** The site says "The pacing walks you through the arc," "The prose thins and the margins fall silent," and "The reader who finishes is not the reader who began." The draft's summit chapter is its second-longest, at 2,172 words (`ops/chapter concordance.md`). The treatment's storyboard says the peak's "Sentence rhythm elongates, repeats, spirals" (arc-gloss p. 13), which contradicts the "thins and breaks" the site quotes. The drafts disagree about the peak, and the draft does neither yet.
- **The founding convergence:** "Both found the same thing: the deepest truth isn't learned. It's remembered." The site reads Heidegger through a secondary source only ("not yet, only secondhand"). The "remembering" gloss rests on later lectures the vault has not read. In 1964 Heidegger wrote that aletheia "may not be equated with truth", and the site quotes that elsewhere. The two traditions also differ on how the seeing was lost: Heidegger says concealment, the Kashmir school says contraction, and the author's own reflection sides with contraction.
- **"Every tradition here says the same"** and **"Every tradition says to bring this to someone."** The vault's finding covers four traditions plus Wilber. Unanimity is the exact framing the vault corrected.
- **The objections' answers** are presented as the book's; the author-only page calls them "proposals… not decisions."
- **The Claim page, item 4:** "That's how you know no one is selling you a summit" offers disclosure as proof of honesty. It promises to say "exactly where they part" and does not.
- **The alone question** (Company page). Gendlin is listed among those who "say you need another person," but he says solitary work is "often possible, though always unsteady and difficult." Winnicott is listed as showing it "can work alone," but his capacity to be alone is "being alone while someone else is present."

**Hedged more than needed:**

- **"Can this be done alone?"** appears twice as "Still open." The vault holds a crisp position: a reader alone can run every restraint (keep going, don't act on it yet, check it over time) but none of the judging. Judging which thing is happening goes to a person who has been there. That position is stronger, and easier to refuse, than "Still open."
- **Author-directed lines in reader views:** "You haven't answered this one yet," "You settled this one by sending the first letter," and the 4am-letter question. Readers take them as addressed to them.

## 3. What would change the book's mind

| Thesis | You could find it false for you if… | The book would revise it if… |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Folded, not broken | you loosen what tightened and find an injury or absence, not a fold | a state that is not contraction proves real (Nishitani's nihility, held open in the vault), or loss stays loss (the Five Remembrances the site already quotes) |
| 2. Nothing missing | something arrived that you never had (Write back already asks this) | readers often answer "new," or the tradition's objection holds: it recognizes "the Lord," and the book has not said *what* is recognized |
| 3. Your body knows | a word felt right and later proved wrong, or you changed and felt nothing (Nisbett and Wilson 1977, p. 236) | the release follows the prose rather than the matter and does not change what you can say next, which is the vault's own test |
| 4. No one can do it for you | something changed you that you did not do: a person, grace, time | Buber holds, and the site quotes him: real meeting "can never take place through my agency, nor… without me" |
| 5. No substance needed | you walk the book and nothing like an arc happens (the etymology is checkable: Osmond, 1956) | readers report the arc as a concept they read about rather than something they went through |
| 6. Thank the guard | you thank it, the no stays, and the no is right | the will side the book keeps open (Robbins) proves right for some readers |
| 7. You stay the author | you catch the book telling you what your experience meant | that happens; "That release is the felt shift" is one current instance |
| The arc | your experience ran as returning, not as a climb and a descent | the author's own record, which "runs on returning more than on stages" (`ops/arc ecology.md`), wins |

## 4. Where this book changed its mind (drafts for readers)

1. **The body's yes.** "We used to say the release you feel when a word fits tells you something is true. Gendlin, who named it, claimed less: it tells you something moved. A well-made sentence can produce the same release. Check it again in a few days, and with someone who knows you."
2. **The agreement we built in.** "Early drafts said many traditions agree, with no dissent. They had been gathered on one day for exactly the thing they were then said to share. Their agreement is company, not proof."
3. **Grief and the witness.** "The drafts credited Karla McLaren with the idea that grief needs a witness. Her book says grief 'can be undertaken individually, or in a community.' The witness is this book's own idea."
4. **Contraction.** "We said the Kashmir tradition sees our tightening as useful. It calls it bondage. Reading it as a vantage worth having is this book's move, not theirs."
5. **Two arrivals that were one.** "We counted Heidegger and Laozi as separate discoveries of 'letting be'; Heidegger took 'the Way' from Laozi and said so. Lewin and Erhard turned out to be one line of teaching, not two."
6. **Noticing your own change.** "We assumed you would notice if you changed. A 1977 study found people whose opinions moved a long way and who reported no change. So your 'nothing happened', and your 'everything changed', are not the last word."
7. **Where the noticing started.** "The pairing of Heidegger with the Kashmir school was shown to the author in July 2026. His own noticing came earlier and in plainer words, in a poem about 'the answer I had always had' and in a room in 2017." (This entry is the author's to place. The dating is in `notes/aletheia and pratyabhijna converge on truth as un-forgetting.md`.)

Entries 1, 2 and 6 would do the most for trust: they correct the book's central instruments, not a detail about one thinker.

## 5. Rootedness

What each thesis rests on, as the site shows it, against the strongest root the vault holds:

- **Thesis 1.** The site shows a line from a description of the book. The vault holds the author's own "a fundamental clenching that didn't obscure the vision," and Kṣemarāja on the experient who comes to "appear as if contracted," with the tradition's refusal.
- **Thesis 2.** The site shows an unattributed summary from the drafts. The vault holds poem #4, written before the book ("the answer / I had always had"), Utpaladeva, Plato, and Wittgenstein (an independent arrival, read in his own words).
- **Thesis 3.** The site shows Gendlin's popular line. The vault holds his 1964 limit on it, and the reader's own check, the "Not quite" button.
- **Thesis 4.** The site shows a draft line. The vault holds the author's 2017 room (now only in the timeline), and Maturana and Varela and Johnstone, read in their own words.
- **Thesis 6.** The site shows a draft line with no thinker and no date. No tradition read stands behind it; say so.
- **Thesis 7.** The site shows a draft and a vault file path. The vault holds Rogers, Ignatius (whose companion is forbidden to persuade), and the old letter's "my kickstand, my jumper cable… my blank page."

To make the kind of root legible without footnotes, give each thesis one plain sentence whose opening words name the kind: "I noticed this first…" (dated); "Others noticed it without me…" (with where they part); "You can check this…" (the body or your life); "This one is the book's own; no tradition we read holds it"; "This one we are less sure of."

## 6. The trust posture

The confident voice earns trust when it states the book's position and shows its edge: "holds to be true," "doesn't promise what will happen to you," and the "We checked" section. It spends trust when it predicts what will happen inside the reader ("astonishingly often, opens," "That's how you'll know," "The reader who finishes is not the reader who began"), when it universalizes ("every tradition"), and when it answers objections by redefining their terms. The vault's own rule fits: provisionality "attaches to the claim's scope — this year, in my experience — and never dilutes the claim's assertion" (`notes/a claim stated crisply enough to be refused…`). Keep the theses unhedged, with no "perhaps," and pair each with one line of scope or one way to be wrong. Leave the practices' imperatives and the refusal-intact quotations as they are.

## The ten highest-leverage changes, ranked

1. **Give every thesis three lines: the claim, where it comes from, and how it could be wrong for you.** Use section 5's openings and section 3's table, and move "Did something new arrive…?" onto thesis 2.
2. **Find-the-word room:** replace "That's how you'll know" with "When it fits, something often lets go. That tells you something moved, not yet that the word is true. Check it again tomorrow, and with someone who knows you." Bring the "felt true just because it was beautiful?" question into the room.
3. **Add "Where this book changed its mind"** near the top of The claim (section 4), and link each thesis to its correction.
4. **Thesis 6:** "Don't storm your own gates. Thank the guard, and listen to it. Sometimes the gate opens. Sometimes the no is right, and that is worth knowing too."
5. **Thesis 4's close:** "Nothing changes in you without you. Not everything that changes in you is your doing. And if nothing changed, that is not proof you did it wrong."
6. **The arc as design, not effect:** "The book is written to carry you through that curve; whether it does is yours to find out." Attribute "The reader who finishes…" as the draft's hope, and stop describing a quiet summit the draft does not have.
7. **The home convergence band:** "A German philosopher in 1927 and a school in Kashmir a thousand years earlier never met, and both describe the deepest truth as uncovered rather than learned. They disagree about how it got covered, and Heidegger later took back the word 'truth.'"
8. **Replace "It tells you what is already true"** with "It tells you what one person noticed, who noticed it before him, and where they disagree. Then it asks you to come and see."
9. **Replace "Still open" on working alone** with the held position: "Partly. Alone, you can keep going, hold off acting, and check it over time. Telling which thing is happening takes someone who has been there." Label the objections' answers "what the book is trying."
10. **Clean up overreach and leakage.** Remove "every tradition." Regroup Gendlin and Winnicott. Move author-directed lines to Behind the site. Scope thesis 1: "This is not a claim about illness or injury; if something in you feels damaged, bring it to someone who can tell the difference."

## Appendix: spot-check errors (real errors only)

About sixty items were checked. All the draft quotations, and nearly all of the thinkers' quotations, match the repository exactly. The errors:

- **"47 thinkers read in their own words"** (Sources; repeated on Behind the site) counts reading *records*. One record covers six thinkers, and several were read through a scholar (the chart itself marks Heidegger, Abhinavagupta, Dōgen, Merleau-Ponty, al-Junayd, and Polanyi this way). The Company page says 24 of 64, which contradicts it. "21 times the original corrected the draft" has no stated definition and cannot be reproduced.
- **"157 of 502 notes"** is now 157 of 516; the count is hardcoded and will keep going stale.
- **al-Qushayrī's "The tree that has grown on its own without a gardener…"** is a saying of his teacher al-Daqqāq, quoted in the Risāla (Knysh p. 406).
- **"This book is a mirror, not a mandate"** is on mythic-os p. 1, not p. 2.
- **The home page's "Tuesday is the temple"** cites first-telling with no page. It is p. 12.
- **The home hero reads "You've just forgotten";** the source (abridged p. 1) and the house both read "You have just forgotten."
- **Poem #12** reads "beginning again;", not "beginning again."
- **The chart's "where they part" text** has a source in its data (`parts_src`) that the chart never displays.
