# The facilitation ecology — six surrounding territories

## 1. What was asked, and the answer in brief

**The author asked for surrounding territories at different experiential levels, named three, and asked for three more.** His words were: "Let's explore a few more surrounding concerns at different experiential levels. What about biological systems, or ecological systems? What remains true at invariant scale, and what is being facilitated? What of physiological approaches like Jonny Miller's nervous system mastery? What about exploring the specific moves each of the coaches make in more catalogued or granular detail? You can see how I'm offering related territory - please select and explore another three yourself to explore alongside mine." His three territories are living systems, meaning chemistry, cells, organisms, and ecosystems; the body and the nervous system, centered on Jonny Miller's course; and the coaches the corpus gathers, catalogued move by move. The three chosen for this round each follow one defining feature of the book. The book's vehicle is the arc of a psychedelic experience walked with no substance, so the first is what psychedelic guides do before, during, and after a session. The book's form is a text read alone, so the second is how a text or a work of art facilitates with no one present. The book's foundation is recognition, so the third is the pointing-out traditions, the teaching practices of the traditions that name recognition as their goal.

**Seven researchers gathered 287 entries, and every one of the 1,645 quotations in them was found by script in the raw text of the address it gives.** Living systems has 42 entries, eight of which are regularities the researcher proposes hold across scales; the body and the nervous system has 34; psychedelic guides has 34; texts and art has 33; the pointing-out traditions has 33; and the coaches have 111, between nine and eleven for each of the eleven coaches or groups. The entries for the five territories other than the coaches are in `ops/facilitation ecology — six surrounding territories, the entries.md`. The coaches' moves are catalogued separately in `ops/facilitation ecology — the coaches' moves.md`, one section per coach, each opening with an index of that coach's moves. Section 12 states how the work was checked and where it is thin.

**What is facilitated, in every territory, is a process that belongs to the one being helped.** A catalyst speeds a reaction toward the end point its own energies favor. A chaperone protects a protein while the protein's own sequence decides its shape. A nurse plant shelters a seedling that does its own growing. A steadied nervous system settles by itself. A reader makes their own sense of a page. A psychedelic guide supports what the treatment manual of the Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies (MAPS) calls the participant's "inner healing intelligence." A coach's question is answered by the client. And in the pointing-out traditions the teacher points while the student does the looking. The facilitator supplies conditions: a lower barrier, a route, shelter, a changed shared surrounding, a pace, a hand, a question, a gesture, and in some traditions a verdict. Every territory also records measured cases in which the facilitator supplies more than conditions, and section 2 sets them beside the rule.

**Five regularities are proposed as holding at every scale the territories cover, and two more at the scale of persons and groups; all seven are proposals with counter-cases, not laws.**

1. A facilitator changes how easily a change happens more often than which change it is.
2. The same help helps or harms depending on the state of the one who receives it.
3. Facilitation works only within bounds of threshold, dose, and timing.
4. Much facilitation works by changing a shared surrounding rather than by acting on the one helped.
5. What lasts depends on the surroundings the person returns to.
6. At the scale of persons and groups, protective responses that have outlasted their cause ease under signals of safety more reliably than under force.
7. At the same scale, telling a real change from its counterfeit is done by someone other than the one who changed, wherever it is done at all.

One property that the corpus's own image asserts holds only at the smallest scale. A catalyst is never consumed, but living facilitators are changed, outgrown, or displaced by what they helped.

**Seven findings bear most directly on the book.**

- The first-telling's image of a witness as "a catalyst that's never consumed, but without which nothing proceeds at all" claims more than the chemistry does, because without its catalyst a reaction proceeds more slowly rather than not at all (section 3). Read literally, the image supports grief completing more slowly alone, which is a weaker claim than the vault's note that grief cannot complete alone.
- The same sentence of reassurance helps some readers and harms others, and no text can tell them apart. The Tibetan traditions state this as a principle, calling the most direct method "powerful medicine" for the prepared and "deadly poison" for others, and they assign the telling-apart to a teacher (sections 7 and 8).
- The school that named recognition wrote its treatises to lead readers to it and addressed them to readers on whom grace had already descended. This is the closest precedent the survey found for the book's front matter, "Reading is remembering," and it carries a condition the book does not state (section 8).
- In a published coaching session Joe Hudson told a client, "You know this place, man. This is not your first time here," and his co-host reports "tears of recognition." It is a documented instance of the book's promised event set off by a second person (section 5).
- The corpus's sort of the coaches into five theories of where suffering lives does not survive the coaches' own words for four of the eleven, and one vault note says the Hendricks never appear in the corpus when the arc-and-gloss draft names them on its first page (section 5).
- Several of the corpus's phrases and figures now have sources. "State before story" appears in Jonny Miller's writing, and Deb Dana writes "Story follows state"; the pairing of set and setting predates Timothy Leary; and the percentages that the vault's note on resistance cites from an unread synthesis are confirmed at the primary studies (sections 4 and 6).
- The author's directive on the book's center names "the polyvagal work" as knowledge established outside the project, and in 2026 Paul Grossman and 38 co-authors concluded "that the PVT is untenable"; the narrower findings about the body that do hold up are listed in section 4, and the choice is set out in section 10.

**One question is for the author alone.** A coaching school named Aletheia, founded by Steve March, is taught inside Jonny Miller's course, and its central question is "What if nothing is missing? What if you're already a whole human being?" Whether the author has met March's work is something only he can say (section 4). This round changes no note in `notes/`; the corrections it found are listed in section 9 for the pipeline, the fixed sequence of steps through which notes are changed.

## 2. What is facilitated, and what holds at every scale

**This section answers the author's two cross-cutting questions, and everything in it beyond the quoted findings is a proposal.** The living-systems researcher proposed eight regularities from verified entries in that territory. This synthesis tested each against the other five territories and added two that appear only when the territories are read together. None of them is a finding that any single source states. Each is given with the entries that support it, the entries that break it, and the scales over which it was checked.

### What is facilitated, territory by territory

| Territory | What is facilitated | What the facilitator supplies | Where the facilitator supplies more than conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living systems | a reaction's approach to the end point its energies favor; a protein's folding; a seedling's growth; a colony's building | a lower energy barrier, a route across a membrane, protection during folding, shade and soil, a trace or a chemical signal | the Spemann–Mangold organizer changes what neighboring cells become; steric chaperones supply structure and prions impose a misfolded shape; goslings attach to the first moving object they meet |
| The body and the nervous system | the nervous system's own change of state: settling, the completion of a protective response that was cut short, the release of held tension | safety, a slow pace, a trusted hand, a feedback display, a question, a breathing pattern | pain reprocessing therapy's authoritative reassurance that the pain is not damage; instructions to reinterpret arousal as excitement |
| Psychedelic guides | the participant's own process, which the MAPS manual calls their "inner healing intelligence" | screening, preparation, a room, music, eyeshades, company, and interventions timed late | ceremonial leaders who supply the reading of the experience; a retreat center that taught visitors to read their visions in its own cosmology; the 1964 manual that told readers what each phase meant |
| Texts and art | the reader's or writer's own sense-making | a prompt, a time limit, a pronoun, a withheld application, a poem matched to a mood, the safety of knowing it is art | no text discriminates among its readers, so the documented harms are the same text reaching the wrong reader |
| The coaches | the client's own noticing of a belief, a resisted feeling, a state, or their own part in a pattern | fixed questions, distinctions, bodily instructions, presence, and in groups a witnessing crowd | Peter Crone's diagnosis of which belief is operating; Tony Robbins's produced high state and his confrontation; David Deida's circle, which assigns an act and a consequence for backing off |
| The pointing-out traditions | the student's own noticing of an awareness the tradition holds was already there | an occasion: a question, a gesture, a shout, a silence, an argument, an experiment | the teacher's verdict on whether recognition happened, which is also where the documented harm concentrated |

**Across the six territories, what is facilitated is a process the one being helped already carries, and what varies is how much the helper adds.** In chemistry the addition to the end point is exactly nothing: "the total rate (catalyzed plus noncatalyzed) can only increase in the presence of the catalyst and never decrease." The MAPS manual compares healing to a wound, where "the doctor does not direct or cause the healing that ensues." Byron Katie's booklet says, "No one can give you freedom but you." Ramana Maharshi wrote that God and the guru "will only show the way to release." The right-hand column of the table holds the counter-cases, and they occur at every scale from molecules to institutions.

### Candidate regularities the territories share

**1. A facilitator changes how easily a change happens more often than which change it is.** The support is exact in chemistry, where a catalyst cannot shift an equilibrium, and strong for small proteins, where "the chaperones do not appear to affect the final state of the protein." It includes Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela's claim that an interaction can trigger a change in a living system but cannot specify it, which the vault holds at the primary text, and the statements of the MAPS manual, Katie, and Ramana above. It breaks in the organizer, in steric chaperones and prions, and in imprinting; in nurse plants, which let seedlings live where they could not live alone; in Igor Branchi's account, in which a person's context can steer the outcome; and in every tradition in which a teacher supplies the meaning. The researchers checked it from molecules to institutions, and it stands as a tendency with measured exceptions at every scale. The book already takes the side of the tendency (`notes/the book can only be auxiliary to a sovereign reader.md`), and the exceptions show that the book's refusal to supply meaning is a choice, since supplying meaning is available and works in its own way.

**2. The same help helps or harms depending on the state of the one who receives it.** At the synapse, connections that start with a low chance of releasing transmitter facilitate under repeated firing and those that start high weaken. An audience helps well-learned responses and hinders new ones, in Robert Zajonc's account. Raised plasticity "is not inherently beneficial by definition" (Branchi). Repeating "I'm a lovable person" made people with low self-esteem feel worse and those with high self-esteem feel slightly better (Wood and colleagues, 2009), and expressive writing left recently separated adults who were searching for meaning worse off than neutral writing did (Sbarra and colleagues, 2013). A Kagyu text calls the most direct method "powerful medicine" for some and "deadly poison" for others. Hudson adapts his anger practice to the person, asking someone who tends to lose control to become "an actor playing the role of you getting angry." The sources read record no counter-case, only settings where the state of the one receiving help was not measured, over scales from the synapse to a population's response to news reports of suicide. It is the best-supported of the cross-territory proposals. Its consequence is that in every territory the facilitator's decisive act is reading the state of the one being helped, which is the one act a text read alone cannot perform (`notes/a solitary reader can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates.md`).

**3. Facilitation works only within bounds of threshold, dose, and timing.** The living-systems entries measure thresholds, saturation, and critical periods from molecules to organisms, and Paul Sabatier's principle holds that a catalyst must bind what it acts on strongly enough to act and weakly enough to let it go. In the body the bounds are small doses, alternation between difficulty and ease, and the brief loss of consciousness that rapid breathing with breath holds can cause. In psychedelic work they are screening and dose; in texts, expressive writing that raises distress at first; in the pointing-out traditions, the calm that the Mahāmudrā lookings presuppose and the "medicine" that is "poison" to the unprepared. Among the coaches, Michael Singer tells people to practice on "the low-hanging fruit" before any crisis, and Thomas Hübl writes that collective healing "cannot happen for large groups and countries while violent conflict is actively unfolding." The regularity breaks where one of its supports is contested, as hormesis is, and where a critical period is weak enough that learning continues after it. It is well supported from molecules to societies, as measured quantities in chemistry and biology and as practitioners' judgments everywhere else. The book has no measure of dose for its reader, which is part of what the pacing of its arc tries to supply.

**4. Much facilitation works by changing a shared surrounding rather than by acting on the one helped.** In ecology the change is shade, soil, habitat, or a chemical signal. In stigmergy, termites and ants respond to traces that earlier work left, which produces structures "without need for any planning, control, or even direct communication between the agents." In the psychedelic setting, the music predicted later reductions in depression and the drug's intensity did not. Miller's third group of regulation methods changes one's surroundings or company; Hübl builds a group's "coherence" before any trauma surfaces; and a participant in Jerry Colonna's Reboot bootcamps wrote that "the magic comes not from any particular exercises or tools, it comes from the people involved." It breaks in direct partnerships, in enzymes that bind their substrate, in surroundings that one species shapes to exclude others, and in the unsupported claim that help passes between trees through fungal networks. It is well supported from bacteria to crowds. A text is a changed surrounding of this kind, and stigmergy is the living case closest to one.

**5. What lasts depends on the surroundings the person returns to.** Branchi's account holds that context and appraisal steer the direction of a change that biology only permits. Gregory Walton and David Yeager, reviewing trials of brief written interventions, concluded that if the reader's context does not afford the new way of thinking, "treatment effects are unlikely to persist." Psychedelic integration, and the apprenticeship model that tests a revelation over time, make the same point for a session. Crone divides his work into awareness and then "practice"; the vault's reading of Robbins records his concession that a changed state does not last unless something underneath changes; a Conscious Leadership Group coach reports a client whose change came together with medical leave, quitting her job, and a new living situation; and Hübl's sequence puts ending violence, aid, and reparation before group work. A 2026 critique of the regulation trend states that "no amount of individual regulation will undo systemic harm," and Patrul Rinpoche's instruction is that "until stability is attained" the practitioner must keep meditating. The one apparent counter-case, a written intervention whose effect on grades lasted three years without the students' awareness, is a case of persistence where the surroundings allowed it. The regularity is well supported from cells to societies where it was measured, and it is the most direct link between these territories and the organizational supplement's finding about structures.

**6. At the scale of persons and groups, protective responses that have outlasted their cause ease under signals of safety more reliably than under force.** A trusted partner's hand lowers the brain's response to threat and a stranger's hand does not (Coan and colleagues). In difficult psychedelic sessions, letting go was the response most often rated helpful (84.5 percent), and the Zendo Project's rule is "Through, Not Down." Among the coaches, Hübl says of a participant's distance, "The distancing is part of your intelligence, it's not something that we want to get rid of"; Colonna reads his inner critic as protective: "The crow's mission is to preserve your ability to be loved, to feel safe, and that you belong"; Crone begins every session with acceptance of the feeling; Hudson says, "if you can't love the thing, love the resistance to the thing"; and a Conscious Leadership Group coach turned the group's model in 2025 toward acknowledging a defended state before asking anyone to leave it. In the pointing-out traditions Loch Kelly asks a distressed part of a person, "Is this part aware of me?" The regularity breaks where moves built on force have adherents: Robbins's "Let me show you where the God damn weeds are," Deida's consequence for freezing, the Zen shout, and the firewalk. The measured evidence against force is narrow, since venting anger increased aggression in one experiment and a 2024 meta-analysis found activities that raise arousal "ineffective overall" at reducing anger, and no study compares the two families of moves directly. The regularity holds from one nervous system to a room of a thousand and is not tested below the person. It is the vault's own doctrine that suffering is almost always a protective contraction (`notes/suffering is contraction never corruption.md`), reached independently in the coaches' own words.

**7. At the same scale, telling a real change from its counterfeit is done by someone other than the one who changed, wherever it is done at all.** Every tradition that checks recognition does it in person, including a Tibetan procedure built to tell a real experience from one "mostly mixing it up with what you've read or heard." Psychedelic apprenticeship tests a revelation with an experienced guide over time, and research on psychedelic insight warns that an insight that feels true "can be difficult to revise." The same text helps and harms different readers without knowing which. The regularity breaks in a minority position that is itself a tradition: a strand of Zen, Douglas Harding, Katie, and Hübl in some moves hand the verdict to the person ("You know your process," Hübl told a participant), and Shin'ichi Hisamatsu taught that one confirms one's own awakening, though the mainstream calls the risk of self-delusion "high." It holds for persons and groups, and below the person checking has no meaning. It extends the vault's finding that a reader alone can run the guards that do not evaluate and none of those that discriminate (`notes/a solitary reader can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates.md`) from the readings the vault already held to all six territories.

### What does not hold across scales

- **"Never consumed" holds only for molecules.** A catalyst leaves a reaction unchanged, but nurse trees die sooner for the cactus they sheltered, pioneer plants are replaced by what they made possible, and Hübl says a facilitator must do their own integration work "for the rest of my life" because the work acts on the one who does it. The first-telling's image carries the molecular property into a human relation, where the cases gathered here show the opposite.
- **Help passing between trees through fungal networks** is not supported by published evidence (Karst and colleagues, 2023), although the image spread widely.
- **Social facilitation in cockroaches**, one of the field's founding results, did not replicate in 2020, while social inhibition did.
- **Facilitation mattering more as conditions grow harsher** is contested even in plants, where it was proposed. The other territories offer parallels that were not measured: difficult psychedelic sessions were riskier with an "absence of physical comfort and social support," and Hübl says a group lets up material "way too threatening for an individual to allow."
- **Slower recovery before a tipping point** is measured in ecosystems and only weakly in individual mood, and a study of adolescents did not find the signal a year before depression increased.

## 3. Living systems: chemistry, cells, organisms, and ecosystems

**"Facilitation" is a technical term in several of these fields, and in each it names something that makes a process easier without making the process.** A catalyst lowers the energy barrier a chemical reaction must cross and leaves the reaction's end point unchanged: "Catalysts are not consumed by the reaction and remain unchanged after the reaction," and "the total rate (catalyzed plus noncatalyzed) can only increase in the presence of the catalyst and never decrease." In facilitated diffusion, channel and carrier proteins let molecules cross a cell membrane they could not cross alone, always down their own concentration gradient: "Being passive, facilitated transport does not directly require chemical energy." In synaptic facilitation, a nerve impulse that closely follows another releases more transmitter because of calcium the first one left behind, and synapses that start with a low chance of release tend to facilitate while those that start high tend to weaken. In community ecology, Mark Bertness and Ragan Callaway argued in 1994 that "positive interactions - facilitations" between species had become important enough to warrant "formal inclusion into community ecology theory." And in social psychology, Floyd Allport named social facilitation in 1924 as an "increase in response merely from the sight or sound of others making the same movement."

**In every case the researcher read, what is facilitated is the system's own process, and the facilitator supplies conditions rather than the result.** The reaction settles where its own energies already favor; a protein reaches the shape its own sequence specifies; a seedling survives where it could not survive alone; a person performs a response they have already learned. What the facilitator supplies is a lower barrier, a route, a site, protection, or changed surroundings such as shade, habitat, a trace, or a signal, and not the energy, the direction, or the form of the result. The exceptions are measured and are recorded in section 2.

**Four findings bear directly on the book.**

- **The corpus's catalyst image claims more than the chemistry does, and the chemistry sits closer to the book's solitary form than the image does.** The first-telling says that grief carries "a witness inside the equation — the way some reactions require a catalyst that's never consumed, but without which nothing proceeds at all" (`first-telling` p. 9, as quoted in `ops/second person ecology.md`, the vault's survey of the jobs a second person does for someone who is changing). In chemistry the uncatalyzed pathway stays open, so a reaction proceeds without its catalyst, only more slowly. Read literally, the image would say that grief completes more slowly without a witness, which is a weaker claim than the vault's note that grief cannot complete alone (`notes/grief cannot complete alone.md`). The phrase "never consumed" also holds only for molecules: nurse trees, pioneer plants, and human helpers are changed or displaced by what they help.
- **Most molecular chaperones protect a folding protein without supplying its shape.** For small proteins the folded shape follows from the sequence of amino acids, and "the chaperones do not appear to affect the final state of the protein." This is the clearest molecular case for Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela's claim, which the vault holds at the primary text, that an interaction can trigger a change in a living system but cannot specify it (`notes/an interaction can trigger a change in a living system but cannot specify it.md`). The same sources record the exceptions at the same scale: a few "steric chaperones" do supply structure, and prions impose their own misfolded shape on normal proteins and cause disease.
- **In ecology the facilitator is often displaced by what it helped.** Young saguaro cacti establish under paloverde trees, and Joseph McAuliffe showed in 1984 that the saguaro then hastens the tree's death: "This interaction appears to accelerate the local loss of individual nurse trees, resulting in a predictable pattern of species replacement." In Joseph Connell and Ralph Slatyer's facilitation model of succession (1977), the first plants on bare ground change it so that it becomes "less hospitable for the original species' own ecological demands" and are replaced. The vault holds the facilitator's disappearance as a facilitator's aim (`notes/the facilitator disappears, success is increasing the other's capacity to facilitate themselves.md`); ecology shows it as a consequence rather than a choice, and facilitation cascades, in which each helped species helps the next and the helpers persist, are the counter-case.
- **The paper behind the vault's plasticity note has now been read, and it supports less than the note claims.** Romain Nardou, Gül Dölen, and colleagues (*Nature*, 2023) showed in adult male mice that several psychedelics reopen a period in which social contact can be learned as rewarding, and that "the time course of critical period reopening is proportional to the duration of acute subjective effects reported in humans." The paper cites other studies for the claim that what gets reorganized depends on context and does not test it, and the sentence the vault's note quotes on that point (`notes/the plasticity window reorganizes only what attention is on.md`) does not appear in the paper; the synthesis the note draws on attributes it to "the researcher" without saying where it was said. Igor Branchi (*Psychological Medicine*, 2026) supplies a published theory for the note's claim: biological changes such as raised plasticity act "through permissive causality, shaping a space of possibilities rather than instructing a specific outcome," the person's context and appraisal steer the direction, and "plasticity is not inherently beneficial by definition," so an open period spent in harmful conditions can steer change toward harm.

**Five further entries are worth the book's attention.**

- **The presence of others strengthens what is practiced and hinders what is new.** Robert Zajonc's theory holds that an audience raises arousal, which helps well-learned responses and impairs new ones, and he advised "that the student study alone ... and arrange to write examinations surrounded by many other students." His famous cockroach result did not survive a preregistered replication in 2020: "Although we replicated the social-inhibition effect, there was no evidence for a social-facilitation effect." The finding bears on when a witness helps: after a response is learned rather than while it is being learned.
- **A system nearing a tipping point recovers more slowly from small disturbances.** Marten Scheffer and colleagues proposed "generic early-warning signals" for "a wide class of systems," and Ingrid van de Leemput and colleagues found such signals in people's mood ratings before shifts into and out of depression, concluding that "mood may have alternative stable states separated by tipping points." A reply called the idea one that "still needs empirical proof," and a later study of adolescents did not find the signal a year ahead.
- **In panarchy, renewal after collapse draws on memory kept at slower levels.** C. S. Holling's adaptive cycle moves through growth, conservation, collapse, and reorganization, and panarchy, the nesting of such cycles, names a link called "remember," in which a level renewing itself draws on potential stored in a larger, slower level. This is a parallel to the book's claim that change recovers what was already there, held as a parallel and not as evidence; the cycle's proponents call it "a tool for thought."
- **Stigmergy is coordination through traces.** Termites and ants coordinate by responding to marks that earlier work left in the environment, and the result is "complex, seemingly intelligent structures, without need for any planning, control, or even direct communication between the agents." It is the living case closest to a text: a trace one agent leaves prompts another agent's action with no one present.
- **The best-known natural image of trees helping their kin is not supported.** Justine Karst and colleagues reviewed the evidence on forest fungal networks in 2023 and found that "the claim that mature trees preferentially send resources and defence signals to offspring through CMNs has no peer-reviewed, published evidence." The case shows a cross-scale image of care spreading before its own field supported it, which is the risk the vault's rule of company rather than proof guards against.

## 4. The body and the nervous system

**Jonny Miller's Nervous System Mastery teaches a person to notice their own state early and shift it by choice.** The five-week online cohort moves through nervous-system basics, interoception (sensing the inside of one's body), ways of shifting state, emotions, and the redesign of one's surroundings and support. Its opening framework is R.I.S.E.: "Rise stands for reactivity, I is interoception, S is self-regulation and E is emotional fluidity." Its three states of the nervous system are, in Miller's words, "drawing from Polyvagal theory," and it sorts ways of shifting state into three groups: "top down" (changing the story), "bottom up" (changing the body's input, such as the breath), and "outside in" (changing the surroundings or the company). The entries record each named protocol with its steps: a five-minute check of awareness, posture, and emotion; two breathing recipes, "espresso breath" to raise arousal and "breath of calm" to lower it; a humming breath; a ten-second three-word state check before a meeting; an "unclenching loop" ("notice a clench, try a move ..., track what changes, then keep what worked and drop what didn't"); co-regulation exercises and a weekly small-group practice called the Relational Dojo; and a practice for feeling anger. The course's own outcome figures (91 percent completion, 87 percent reporting lower stress within four weeks) come from its own surveys without a comparison group.

**Miller's own theory is close to the book's account of suffering, and it is not independent of Joe Hudson.** In his 2026 essay "How to Unclench" he writes: "Feeling a feeling isn't what makes an experience unbearable. Our suffering comes from clenching against what we feel." He holds that unclenching "is like falling asleep—you cannot force yourself to do it, but you can create the conditions for it to arise." The book's doctrine, in the corpus's words, is "Nothing in you is broken. Something in you is folded" (`notes/suffering is contraction never corruption.md`). The difference is kept: Miller treats clenching mainly as the cause of suffering and aims at its release, while the vault also holds contraction as a productive, bounded point of view (`notes/contraction is a productive locus and not only a privation.md`). Miller names Wilhelm Reich and Alexander Lowen among his sources, the body-psychotherapy lineage that the vault found the abridged edition cites only through its descendants. Hudson teaches a masterclass in Miller's course and interviewed him, so their agreement counts once.

**Two findings touch the corpus's own wording.**

- **"State before story" appears word for word in Miller's writing.** The corpus credits the phrase to Tony Robbins (`walked` IX; the atlas), and the vault's reading of Robbins found it is not his wording (`notes/the transformation field divides into five theories of where suffering lives.md`). Miller writes, in an essay his site dates 7 August 2024: "Working with clients, I will often talk about 'state before story'—the idea that the stories we tell ourselves and the perspectives we inhabit are a function of our nervous system state." Deb Dana, who applies polyvagal theory, writes "The mind narrates what the nervous system knows. Story follows state." Neither finding shows where the corpus took the phrase from.
- **A coaching school named Aletheia is taught inside Miller's course.** Steve March, "the founder of Aletheia and originator of Aletheia Coaching," gives a masterclass in the course's library (dated 29 October 2024), and his method replaces the question of self-improvement with another: "What if nothing is missing? What if you're already a whole human being?" The name is the word in the book's title, and the question is close to the book's front matter. The researcher did not find how March's school chose the name. Whether the author has met March's work is a question only the author can answer.

**The science beneath these practices is uneven, and the theory most of them use, polyvagal theory, has been rejected by specialists.** Paul Grossman judged each of the theory's basic premises "either untenable or highly implausible" in a 2023 review; Stephen Porges replied in 2025 that the critics attack claims the theory never made; and in 2026 Grossman and 38 co-authors wrote that "major tenets of the PVT are not supported by past or current knowledge" and concluded "that the PVT is untenable." This touches the vault directly. The author's directive on the book's center names "the polyvagal work," beside McLaren's emotion research and Gendlin's felt shift, as knowledge established outside the project on which the book's system vocabulary may rest (`ops/methodology/center of gravity.md`), and the abridged edition's research summaries describe the polyvagal states (`ops/queue/declines.md`, which records those summaries and declines to restate them as claims). Section 10 sets out the choice this leaves. The better-supported findings are narrower. Interoceptive accuracy, a person's sense of their own accuracy, and their attention to the body are "distinct and dissociable," which the vault already holds (`notes/attending to the body is not the same as reading it accurately.md`); Miller recommends a questionnaire of body attention as "an objective benchmark," though it measures attention rather than accuracy. Slow breathing reliably raises heart-rate variability, while its effects on mental health are smaller and rest on trials mostly at moderate risk of bias. A month of daily cyclic sighing lifted mood more than mindfulness meditation in one small exploratory trial. Somatic Experiencing, Peter Levine's method, rests on one randomized trial against a waiting list; Alexander technique lessons reduced back-pain disability in a trial of 579 people; the Feldenkrais method has "no conclusive evidence for any medical benefits"; and tension and trauma releasing exercises rest on pilot studies. In pain reprocessing therapy, a clinician tells the patient that the pain is not a sign of damage, and two thirds of those treated in one trial became pain-free or nearly so, against a fifth given a placebo.

**The hand-holding studies measure what the book's solitary form lacks.** James Coan and colleagues found in 2006 that married women facing the threat of an electric shock showed less threat-related brain activity while holding their husband's hand, and less still the better their marriage. A 2017 study of 110 people found the same for a familiar partner and none for a stranger: "we did not observe any regulatory effects of handholding by strangers." Coan's social baseline theory reads this as the brain expecting company, so that being alone is the costly condition. The effect depends on the relationship rather than on a body being present, which bears on the vault's live disagreement over whether the inner work needs a second person.

**The territory records harms and a critique the book should keep.** Miller's "espresso breath" pairs rapid breathing with breath holds, which can cause a brief loss of consciousness, and the Wikipedia article on Wim Hof reports drownings among people practicing similar breathing in water; the essay does not mention the risk. In a 2002 experiment, angered people who hit a punching bag while thinking of the person who had angered them became angrier, and "Doing nothing at all was more effective than venting anger"; Miller's anger practice differs in aim but has not been tested. And two 2026 critiques of the regulation trend argue that "no amount of individual regulation will undo systemic harm" and that "Nervous system care is not a substitute for justice—it's a support for those fighting for it," which is the same warning the organizational supplement found in Deming and Maslach, and the vault's own rule that a difficulty is attributed to conditions and never to the reader (`notes/pathology belongs to conditions and capacity to persons.md`).

**What is facilitated here is the person's nervous system changing state, and several sources say it cannot be forced.** The change is settling, the completion of a protective response that was cut short, or the release of held tension. The helper, partner, device, or text supplies conditions: safety, a slow pace (small doses, and movement back and forth between difficulty and ease), a trusted hand, a feedback display, or a question. The exceptions supply meaning with authority, as in pain reprocessing therapy and in instructions to reinterpret arousal as excitement.

**Where the coaches differ most is who supplies the content and how much force the move uses.** Katie and Singer supply a fixed procedure and no answers. Hudson and Hübl supply questions and their own presence, and Hübl adds a trained team and a crowd. The Hendricks and Colonna offer readings tentatively and wait for the person to confirm or reject them. Crone and Robbins supply a diagnosis and a confrontation, and Robbins's events produce a high state on purpose: his method is to "entertain" first, then educate, then have people, "While you're in this state," do "things that you won't forget." Deida's circle assigns an act with a consequence. In the survey's five positions on who decides the content (`ops/facilitation ecology.md`, section 6), the coaches' moves occupy all five, from the person deciding everything, as in Katie's four questions, to no live facilitator at all, as in Singer's practices done alone, with Crone's and Robbins's moves at the position where the facilitator directs or interprets with authority.

**One question recurs from a person to a couple to a team, and it stops holding where someone else's power keeps the pattern going.** The question is what keeping a pattern going gives the one who keeps it, in the four forms listed above. Dethmer answers the objection that some people lack the power to change what keeps them stuck by saying that radical responsibility "has nothing to do with roles and decision rights," and he writes that at a further level "You can take as much responsibility as you want for" war and starvation. That is where the claim overreaches. Colonna's split between complicit and responsible sits closer to a vault note, still nascent, meaning stated but not yet substantiated by a reading, that places authorship between "everything happens to me" and "everything is my responsibility" (`notes/sovereignty occupies the middle between everything happens to me and everything is my responsibility.md`), and his 2023 book extends the question to "systems of oppression." Katie's turnaround and Robbins's confrontation of abuse victims are the documented cases in which the question, asked of a person harmed by someone else, turns into blame.

**Several coaches' later work moves toward positions the book already holds, which weakens the corpus's charge against the field.** The walked document charges all five theories with a "radical interiority of cause" (walked IX). Singer tells people who meet a public atrocity to ask "Now, what can I do to help?" and to work to "pass gun laws or do whatever it is that needs to be done." Hübl's December 2025 article orders the work so that ending violence, aid, and reparation come before group healing. Colonna's *Reunion* asks, "How have I been complicit in maintaining systems of oppression that I say I don't want?" A 2025 Conscious Leadership Group practice begins by acknowledging a defended state rather than asking the person to leave it. And Deida now describes his edge practice as a stage that can end in "zero." The charge still fits the procedures of Katie, Crone, and Hudson, which give no account of how a group changes.

**Two moves bear directly on the book's live disagreement over whether its inner work needs a second person.** Hudson's "You know this place" is a documented instance of recognition set off by a second person, and Hudson also teaches a welcome practiced alone, so he stands on both sides of the disagreement recorded at `notes/the solitary form stands against seven witnesses who hold a second person constitutive.md`. Hübl, whom that note counts unread among the seven, says in his own words that a connected group lets up content "way too threatening for an individual to allow," and also that "there's nowhere written in the universe that we have to do it alone"; on the researcher's reading, his requirement of a group is a matter of degree rather than a rule. His mechanism, in his own words, is "a nonmoving, frozen part of life," which is a presence held in place; the vault's formulation that "what was never metabolized lives on as absence" fits the symptoms he names ("I feel numb, I feel dissociated") better than his account of their cause (`notes/what was never metabolized lives on as absence and hubl reached it through inherited trauma.md`).

**The documented criticism the vault's Robbins note said was owed is now recorded, with his firm's denials.** The note named "the documented criticisms of his own practice" as open work (`notes/tony robbins.md`). BuzzFeed News reported in 2019, from leaked recordings and interviews, that Robbins told abuse victims at his events that they were playing the victim, and it quoted experts on domestic violence who called berating traumatized women "a dangerous strategy"; it reported cold rooms and days that ran "from early in the morning to well past midnight, with few breaks for food and water," with internal emails about "sleep deprivation and dehydration." His firm denied that his comments harmed anyone, and one woman he had confronted told BuzzFeed the exchange helped her leave her husband. The catalog records the same kind of material for every coach where the researchers found it, and records where they did not search.

## 6. What psychedelic guides do before, during, and after a session

**The guide's work is arranged around the same phases as the book's arc, and each entry records the phases it serves.** The book walks its reader through preparation, onset, deepening, peak, descent, and integration with no substance, so the people who hold that arc with a substance are its nearest practical neighbors. Their practice rests on set and setting, the principle that an experience depends on the mindset a person brings and on their surroundings. The terms were used "at least as early as 1958 by Ludwig von Bertalanffy and popularized by Timothy Leary in 1961" (the Wikipedia article), and Leary wrote that "The nature of the experience depends almost entirely on set and setting." The vault's note calls the pair "Leary's coinage" (`notes/set and setting.md`), which the dating corrects.

**Preparation builds trust, screens out people at risk, and gives the person ways to steady themselves.** The Johns Hopkins safety guidelines (Johnson, Richards, and Griffiths, 2008) name "overwhelming distress during drug action" as the most likely risk and list the safeguards: excluding people with a personal or family history of psychosis, "establishing trust and rapport between session monitors and volunteer before the session, careful volunteer preparation, a safe physical session environment and interpersonal support from at least two study monitors." In an Imperial College trial, the patients' own rating of their bond with the therapists predicted rapport before the session, "greater emotional-breakthrough and mystical-type experience" during it, and lower depression afterward. Oregon, which licenses psilocybin services for personal development, requires a preparation session between 24 hours and 90 days beforehand and gives the client the right "to approve and meet any facilitator" first.

**During the session, the guide's rule is to support the person's own process and to time any intervention late.** Participants lie down with eyeshades and headphones and are told to direct attention inward and to "trust, let go, be open." The MAPS treatment manual for MDMA-assisted therapy calls the participant's capacity to heal their "inner healing intelligence" and compares it to a wound, where "the doctor does not direct or cause the healing that ensues"; it asks therapists to "help the participant avoid the misconception that the therapists are the source, rather than the facilitators, of her/his therapeutic experience." It defines its method by timing: "The essence of what we mean by 'non-directive' rests in the timing of interventions." Therapists first encourage a stuck participant to feel the stuck feeling fully, note avoidance and wait, phrase suggestions as invitations, and prefer the word "breathing" to the command "breathe." In one of the manual's sample exchanges, a therapist reassures a participant who fears the unknown that "nothing is going to come up that is not already there." The Zendo Project, which supports people in difficult states at festivals, teaches four principles, among them "Sitting, Not Guiding" and "Through, Not Down," and Oregon makes a nondirective approach a legal duty of licensed facilitators.

**When a session turns difficult, letting go helped most, and the absence of comfort and company raised the risk.** In a survey of 1,993 people about their most difficult psilocybin experience, taken without guides, 39 percent rated it among the five most challenging experiences of their lives and 11 percent put themselves or others at risk of harm; the risk rose with "absence of physical comfort and social support," and 84 percent said they had benefited. In a survey of 529 people, the responses most often rated helpful were "I tried to let go, accept or surrender to the experience" (84.5 percent) and "I tried to observe my mind and not fight it" (77.3 percent). These studies are the primary sources for figures the vault's note on resistance cites from an unread synthesis (`notes/suffering in an intense state is the intensity multiplied by the resistance.md`), and the figures are confirmed. Music matters as much as the drug's strength in one small study: "the nature of the music experience was significantly predictive of reductions in depression 1 week after psilocybin, whereas general drug intensity was not."

**Integration is the person's own work of making sense of what happened, and two lines of research warn against trusting its certainty.** Geoff Bathje and colleagues define integration as a process in which a person "revisits and actively engages in making sense of, working through, translating, and processing the content of their psychedelic experience," and they note that "treating integration as a separate phase of psychedelic experiences probably imposes Western dualistic thinking." Hayley McGovern and colleagues (2024) propose that psychedelics increase "the quantity and subjective intensity of insights and thence beliefs, including false ones," and that an insight that feels true "can be difficult to revise since it is not clear what information could contradict it." Christopher Timmermann, Rosalind Watts, and David Dupuis (2022) answer with what they call psychedelic apprenticeship: a guide holds a revelation with "gentle agnostic respect," neither confirming nor dismissing it, and the revelation is tested over time with an experienced guide. From psychedelic therapy they reach the conclusion the vault had already stated in its own words, that "the body reports and the relation verifies" (`notes/the solitary felt shift rests on one name while the paired felt shift has convergent company.md`).

**Ceremonial traditions differ from the clinics on the question of who decides the content.** In the Native American Church's night-long peyote meeting, a Roadman leads a fixed order while singing passes around the circle: "The singer sings four songs, concludes his set, and passes the staff, gourd, and drum to the next relative to sing." In the Mazatec velada, the sabia chants and speaks through the night and supplies the reading of the illness. At one neo-shamanic ayahuasca center in Peru, facilitators and fellow participants taught visitors to read their visions within the center's cosmology, which Dupuis called a socialization of hallucinations. And the psychedelic field has its own precedent for a text that accompanies a person through phases: Leary, Metzner, and Alpert's manual of 1964, adapted from the Tibetan Book of the Dead, "gives specific instructions on how one should regard them and act during each of these different phases," with passages written to be read aloud during sessions. The vault's inquiry into the arc records Ignatius's *Spiritual Exercises* as the only design precedent the corpus names for the arc's shape and records no psychedelic one (`ops/the arc as kindness.md`); this one exists, and it tells the person what their experience means, which the book declines to do.

**The field's record of harm shows where the safeguards failed.** In a MAPS trial in Canada, the two co-therapists were a married couple, and one of them began a sexual relationship with the participant after active treatment while the other knew and did not report it. MAPS wrote in 2019 that its protections, including two providers at every visit, video, monitoring, and supervision, were not sufficient: "none of these measures were sufficient." In 2024 an FDA advisory panel voted against MDMA-assisted therapy, citing among other concerns that participants could tell whether they had received the drug, and three papers from MAPS trials were retracted. A review of psychedelic trials argues that "treatment effect sizes in psychedelic RCTs are likely over-estimated due to de-blinding of participants and high levels of response expectancy," which is the vault's own admission about expectancy applied to the field's evidence (`notes/response expectancy is a two-sided coin, the mechanism of the manufactured artifact and of genuine change alike.md`).

**What is facilitated is the person's own process, and the guide supplies the room, the company, and the timing.** The clinical and harm-reduction traditions withhold interpretation on purpose; the ceremonial traditions interpret with authority; and the measured evidence says that the person's own acceptance and their relationship with the guide predict how a session goes, while the certainty a session produces does not prove itself. The book keeps the phases and loses the guide: it cannot close its reader's eyes, time an invitation to what it sees, hold a hand, or test a revelation with the reader afterward. The corpus names that loss itself: "the codex is a one-chair technology" (`walked` p. 25, as `ops/the arc as kindness.md` quotes it).

## 7. How a text or a work of art facilitates with no one present

**A text with no one present can do measurable good, small on average, and the research shows what it cannot do alone.** In James Pennebaker's expressive-writing paradigm, a written instruction asks a person to write for fifteen minutes or more on three or four days about their deepest thoughts and feelings about an upsetting experience. A meta-analysis of 146 randomized studies found "a positive and significant average r-effect size of .075," and writing raised distress immediately afterward, which "was unrelated to health outcomes." Writers who benefited used more words of insight and causal thinking as the days went on, which Pennebaker and a colleague read as a sign that "the formation of a narrative is critical." Treatment for depression delivered entirely by book or program, with no contact with a therapist, has a small effect in adults, which depends on how much of the material people complete.

**The same text helps some readers and harms others, and it cannot tell them apart.** Repeating the self-statement "I'm a lovable person" made people with low self-esteem feel worse, while people with high self-esteem felt slightly better (Wood, Perunovic, and Lee, 2009). Recently separated adults who were already searching for meaning did worse after expressive writing than after neutral writing (Sbarra and colleagues, 2013). Students who ruminated had more depressive symptoms after a cognitive self-help workbook (Haeffel, 2010), and students told to journal about their emotions alone, without their thoughts, reported more illness symptoms (Ullrich and Lutgendorf, 2002). At the scale of a population, reports of suicide can make vulnerable readers "feel they have permission to do it," while reports of people who found other ways through a crisis go with lower suicide rates. These are measured cases of the limit the vault records, that a reader alone "can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates" (`notes/a solitary reader can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates.md`).

**The field of bibliotherapy names recognition as the first step of its method.** Arleen Hynes and Mary Hynes-Berry's four-step process (1986) begins with "Recognition – the reader is caught by something in the reading," and continues with examination, juxtaposition, and self-application, the last three carried by a facilitator's questions. They call the literature the catalyst, neutral until it makes an impression on the reader, which is company for the book's image of itself as a tuning fork silent until struck (`notes/the book is a tuning fork and the reader is the striking surface.md`). The difference is kept: their recognition is being caught by a line, and the book's is coming to know again what one already is.

**The research on letters bears on the book's chosen genre.** Letter writers "underestimated how positive recipients would feel" and overestimated how awkward recipients would feel (Kumar and Epley, 2018), and in Martin Seligman and colleagues' comparison of exercises, writing and delivering a letter of thanks produced the largest short-term gain. Narrative therapy's letters, which a therapist writes to a client after a session, return the client's own words and close with questions "to be considered at the beginning of the next session." Both bear on the vault's claim that the book's native genre is a friend's letter read alone (`notes/the book's native genre is the letter from a friend read alone.md`), with a difference the second makes plain: a therapeutic letter comes out of a conversation, and the book's letters go to readers the writer has not heard.

**Being addressed as "you" places a reader inside an event, and being absorbed lowers the reader's guard.** Readers took the actor's point of view "only when directly addressed as the subject of a sentence" (Brunyé and colleagues, 2009), while fiction's "you" "is not a direct address to any given reader even if it purports to be." Readers absorbed in a story "found fewer false notes in a story than less-transported readers" (Green and Brock, 2000), which bears on the vault's concern that a fluent book induces a trance whose assent it cannot tell from recognition (`notes/the book is one of the automatisms it must interrupt.md`). Being moved is felt as strongly for fiction as for one's own life and comes with "very low ratings for causation of the event by oneself and for having the power to change its outcome" (Menninghaus and colleagues, 2015), which gives measured features to the vault's tension that the book's beauty invites appreciation instead of practice (`notes/beauty recruits the wrong posture.md`). And listeners' chills to recited poems "tend to cluster (a) towards the end of a poem, (b) towards the end of a stanza, and (c) towards the end of single lines," which bears on the book's design of a last line left open for the reader's answer.

**Some texts withhold their answer, and some methods match the reader's state before moving it.** C. H. Dodd defined the parable as a story that leaves "the mind in sufficient doubt about its precise application to tease it into active thought," and Umberto Eco argued that literary texts are "fields of meaning, rather than strings of meaning"; the vault already holds Kierkegaard's withheld result and Wolfgang Iser's textual gaps. Poetry therapy and music therapy share the iso principle: a therapist first offers a poem or a piece of music that matches the person's present mood and then moves gradually toward another. A text fixed in advance cannot identify the reader's mood, and the trial evidence for the principle is mixed.

**Whether a text's effect lasts depends on the life the reader returns to.** A brief written intervention that framed adversity at college as common and passing raised Black students' grades over three years, and "Senior-year surveys indicated no awareness among participants of the intervention's impact" (Walton and Cohen, 2011). Walton later concluded with David Yeager (2020) that such effects last only where the reader's surroundings support the new view: "Does the context in which the intervention is delivered afford the way of thinking offered by the intervention? If not, treatment effects are unlikely to persist." That is the text's version of Branchi's point in section 3 and of the organizational supplement's warning about structures.

**What is facilitated is the reader's or writer's own process, and what no text can do is discriminate.** The text supplies structure: a prompt's wording, a time limit, a pronoun, a withheld application, a poem matched to a mood, and the safety of knowing that it is art, which the Distancing-Embracing model describes as "personal safety and control over continuing or discontinuing exposure." It never supplies the reader's conclusion. What the sources show no text doing alone is telling which reader it is harming, choosing a different poem for a reader in a different state, or acting at the critical moment, which the art therapist Edith Kramer called the "third hand."

## 8. The pointing-out traditions: how a teacher occasions recognition, and who confirms it

**Every tradition surveyed separates the occasion of recognition from the thing recognized, and none claims to supply the thing.** The pointing-out traditions are the teaching practices of the traditions that name recognition as their goal: Tibetan Dzogchen and Mahāmudrā, Zen, Advaita Vedānta as Ramana Maharshi taught it, the "direct path" that runs from Atmananda Krishna Menon to present-day teachers, Douglas Harding's experiments, and Kashmir Śaivism, the school on which the book's foundation rests. The first of the three statements attributed to Garab Dorje is "Introducing directly the face of rigpa itself," rigpa being the Dzogchen word for the awareness to be recognized (Adam Pearcey's translation), and the tradition holds that the awareness was present before anyone introduced it. Ramana Maharshi wrote that "God and the Guru will only show the way to release; they will not by themselves take the soul to the state of release," and asked, "Does he who is Rama require the help of a mirror to know that he is Rama?" (*Who Am I?*, in T. M. P. Mahadevan's translation). Tulku Urgyen Rinpoche, as Wikipedia quotes him, said that after the instruction "there is the chance of either recognizing it or not." And the Kashmir Śaiva account of grace, in the words of Wikipedia's article on śaktipāta, is that "It cannot be imposed by force, nor can a receiver make it happen."

**The teachers' moves are few, and the entries record each with its steps.** A teacher turns attention back on the one asking: Wikipedia summarizes that in his dialogues "Ramana emphasised the importance of exploring the nature of the questioner, and not the question," and Joseph Campbell recalled that Atmananda "gave me a little meditation: 'Where are you between two thoughts?'" In the Ninth Karmapa's Mahāmudrā series, the meditator looks at each of five aspects of the mind and "repeatedly asks oneself 'What is it?'" (Wikipedia's summary of Reginald Ray). A teacher interrupts thought, as in Patrul Rinpoche's instruction to rest the mind and then "Suddenly let out a mind-shattering 'phaṭ!'" and as in the shout of the Linji school of Zen. A teacher gestures without words, as the Buddha holds up a flower in the Flower Sermon, a legend whose "earliest known version of the tale appeared in 1036," or as Juzhi answered every question by raising one finger. A teacher keeps silent, as Ramana did for most of his teaching life. A teacher says a turning word at the moment someone asks for it, as Baizhang answered "Don't ignore cause and effect." A written experiment has the reader turn a pointing finger around: "Now point where others see your face" (Harding's pointing experiment at headless.org). And an author sets out an argument for another person to follow, as Utpaladeva's treatise does, below.

**A pointer directs attention and cannot do the looking, and a copy of a pointer conveys nothing.** In the koan of Juzhi's finger, an attendant copied the raised finger with no understanding behind it, and the commentary concludes, "Enlightenment, which Gutei and the boy attained, has nothing to do with a finger" (Nyogen Senzaki and Paul Reps's translation, as Wikipedia quotes it); the story also has the master cut off the boy's finger, which is legend and is recorded as legend. The Rinzai "checking questions," twenty to a hundred follow-up questions that test a student's first insight, became "a standardised set of questions and answers," and the secret answer books were published in a critique in 1916 (Wikipedia's article on the koan). Zoran Josipovic, writing on research into nondual awareness in 2025, warns that "Mistaking such pointers for nondual awareness is akin to mistaking the proverbial finger pointing at the moon for the moon itself." The vault's list of measured ways a felt sense of truth can be produced without the truth (`notes/the felt sense of truth has a documented counterfeit catalogue.md`) now has the traditions' own emblem of the counterfeit beside it.

**Most of these traditions answer the book's central question, whether a text alone can occasion recognition, with no, and each gives a reason.** Atmananda Krishna Menon held that "A book, as such, is dead and inert, and cannot answer a new question arising from the one dealt with in the book," and "Therefore nobody can get at the essence of Truth through books or through language alone" (his talks as recorded in *Notes on Spiritual Discourses of Shri Atmananda*); asked whether a sage who has died can become a new seeker's guru, he answered, "No. Never." His is the most exact objection to the book in the survey, and the book's letter form, written so it can be answered, meets it only in part, because a letter cannot hear the reply. Zen defines itself as "A special transmission outside the scriptures Not founded upon words and letters," a stanza attributed to Bodhidharma that "in fact, is not Bodhidharma's, but, rather, dates to the year 1108" (Wikipedia's article on Bodhidharma, following Heinrich Dumoulin), and Albert Welter argues that the scenes of private transmission from mind to mind in the Chan records are "no more than an artifice to bolster Chan pretensions." Patrul Rinpoche says his instruction "is entrusted to my heart disciples, sealed to be secret." A Kagyu text that the Dalai Lama cites calls the most direct method "powerful medicine" for people ready for it and "deadly poison for those who progress through graded stages" (Wikipedia's article on the pointing-out instruction). That principle states from inside the Tibetan traditions the divergence the vault already records from the Kashmir Śaiva side: the book offers every reader the method with no means that the tradition reserves for the most graced (`notes/the kashmir shaiva anupaya turns the negative way on method itself.md`, and disagreement 8 in `ops/second person ecology.md`).

**A minority in each tradition answers yes, with conditions attached.** Zen keeps a record of awakening without a teacher: Huineng awakened on hearing the Diamond Sūtra chanted, and Peter Gregory writes of Zongmi's first awakening that "it came about as a result of reading several lines of scripture" (as Wikipedia quotes him). The same article reports the mainstream judgment, that "The idea of wisdom without a teacher is often considered suspect among various Zen schools" because "the risk of self-delusion is high." Dahui Zonggao taught lay officials to hold a single word or phrase from an old case, such as the "no" of the koan of Zhaozhou's dog, until their doubt broke, in a practice that "does not use regular interviews and question and answer sessions between student and teacher," and "He often gave instructions through letters to his pupils" (Wikipedia's article on the huatou). That is the nearest precedent in the survey for the book's letter form, with the difference that Dahui wrote to students he knew and assigned their phrases himself. Douglas Harding designed his experiments to be done from a page with no teacher and insisted that "it's imperative to actually DO the experiment." Greg Goode's written experiments (2012) and Loch Kelly's short guided "glimpses," taught through books, audio, and an app, are present-day methods in the same position, and Rupert Spira states the position outright: "The direct path requires no affiliation to any particular teacher or tradition." Goode and Spira thereby depart from Atmananda, the founder of their own lineage. Chögyal Namkhai Norbu gave transmission by live webcast three times a year, and his community's rule was that "It is necessary to receive the explanation prior to viewing the webcast," so the distance was crossed by a living teacher at a set hour and not by a text.

**The school that named recognition wrote its treatises to lead readers to it, and it addressed them to readers already touched by grace.** Utpaladeva's treatise on recognition "presents itself as a rational inquiry relying only on the two means of knowledge accepted by all Indian philosophers: direct experience and inference," and "Abhinavagupta reads the treatise as a single 'inference for others'," an argument set out for another person to follow (Wikipedia's article on Utpaladeva, drawing on Isabelle Ratié). David Peter Lawrence writes that the two thinkers conceived the system as both philosophical argument and "an internalized form of tantric ritual that leads students directly to identification with Shiva" (the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy). Kṣemarāja, however, opens his short summary of the doctrine by addressing "some devoted people, who are undeveloped in reflection" whose immersion in the Lord "blossoms forth with the descent of Sakti," and Jaideva Singh's introduction describes the intended readers as "inclined as a result of Divine grace" (Singh's translation, 1963). The treatise is therefore the closest precedent the survey found for the book's front matter, "Reading is remembering," and it carries a condition the front matter does not state: the text serves a reader in whom grace has already acted. The vault's note on Ratié records that her piece on the treatise as itself a means was never located (`notes/isabelle ratie.md`), and this round did not locate it either.

**Two of the second person survey's reading questions now have partial answers, both from secondary sources.** The survey asked whether Śiva Sūtra II.6, "the guru is the means," makes the teacher one means among the four or the means as such (`ops/second person ecology.md`, question 3 of its reading list). In P. T. Shrinivas Iyengar's 1912 translation of Kṣemarāja's commentary, the sūtra stands in the section that "deals with the Shaktopaya," one of the graded means, and the commentary gives two readings: the guru "is the means, because he shows how they act," or "the Guru is the Shakti of Paramēshvara, the root of grace." The first reading places the teacher inside the graded scheme, and the second makes the word "guru" name grace itself, which the survey calls the giver that is no one; neither reading supplies the counter-case the question was looking for. The survey asked for Singh's translation, and a modern edition should be read before any line is quoted in the book. The survey also asked whether any of the nine intensities of grace in Abhinavagupta's *Tantrāloka* requires a guru (question 4). In Swami Lakshman Joo's presentation, as Wikipedia reports it, a person at the most intense grades is liberated "on his own," a person at the next grade "does not need instruction, but a simple touch, a look or simply being in the presence of his master is enough," and the middle grades need a guru's initiation and instruction; John Dupuche reports that Abhinavagupta sends those "whose intuition is hesitant" to a guru, to "study the scriptures or practice yoga." So some grades do need a teacher, and in these presentations the teacher serves a grace that has already descended rather than giving it. The *Tantrāloka* itself was not read, so the question stays open at the level of the page. The same sources raise a disagreement the vault should keep: the vault records grace as independent of effort and merit (`notes/both poles of the convergence refuse the engineered epiphany.md`), while Singh's introduction, in the researcher's paraphrase, says that grace is not caprice and has to be earned by moral and spiritual discipline.

**Every tradition that checks whether recognition happened does the checking in person.** In the Tibetan procedure the student is told, "If you had some experience, report back for confirmation. Don't assume you've understood," and the teacher "might ask you several questions, often to discern whether you're talking about a real experience or mostly mixing it up with what you've read or heard" (Bruce Newman, a student of the lineage, as Wikipedia quotes him). The check is built to rule out the kind of knowing a reader gets from a book. The same article quotes Orgyen Tobgyal Rinpoche on Tulku Urgyen Rinpoche giving the instruction to more than a thousand people in Taiwan: "Yet I later met only a few there who truly recognized their own nature." A qualitative study from the Varieties of Contemplative Experience project (Lindahl and colleagues, 2020) found Buddhist teachers deciding whether a student's distress was progress or a sign that help was needed, as when a practitioner asked whether a painful change in her sense of self was enlightenment and was told, "No, it wasn't enlightenment because you were suffering too much." These are further cases of the vault's finding that a reader alone "can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates" (`notes/a solitary reader can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates.md`).

**Where the power to confirm became a credential, the documented harm concentrated, and the vault held no record of it before this round.** An investigation that Rigpa commissioned, Rigpa being the organization Sogyal Rinpoche named after the Dzogchen word for awareness, found in 2018 that "some students of Sogyal Lakar (who were part of the 'inner circle') have been subjected to serious physical, sexual and emotional abuse by him," and that "there were senior individuals within Rigpa who were aware of at least some of these issues and failed to address them" (Wikipedia's summary of the law firm's report; the report itself would not load). A student of the Rinzai teacher Joshu Sasaki reported, "Joshu Roshi told me I would never get enlightened if I thought about these things." Sarah Caldwell concluded that Muktananda, who offered śaktipāta initiation to newcomers at large events, "engaged in actions that were not ethical, legal or liberatory with many disciples." Stuart Lachs argues that Zen's lineage stories give teachers a standing close to the divine, and the Zen teacher Bodhin Kjolhede recalls a teacher at one American center telling his disciples, "Just get Dharma transmission; once you get that, no one will be able to touch you." The book claims no power to confirm anyone's recognition, which removes the relationship in which these harms occurred and also removes the confirmation. Both consequences stay in view.

**What is facilitated is the person's own noticing of what the tradition holds was already there.** The teacher supplies an occasion, whether a question, a gesture, a shout, a silence, an argument, or an experiment, and in most of these traditions a verdict on whether the recognition happened. A book can print the occasions, and several teachers have printed them on purpose. What no text in the survey supplies is the verdict, and the traditions that keep the verdict in a teacher's hands are also where the survey found the harm.

## 10. Disagreements and questions the territories bring into view

**These are proposals for the author, each stated with both sides; resolving any of them is his act.**

1. **The catalyst image against the measured chemistry.** The first-telling: a witness is present "the way some reactions require a catalyst that's never consumed, but without which nothing proceeds at all" (p. 9). Chemistry: the uncatalyzed pathway stays open, and "the total rate (catalyzed plus noncatalyzed) can only increase in the presence of the catalyst." The image makes two claims, that the witness is never used up and that nothing happens without it, and the territories contradict both for living helpers. The book could keep the image as a figure, revise it, or let the doctrine that grief needs a witness rest on other ground.
2. **The front matter's address to every reader against the finding that the same reassurance helps some readers and harms others.** The front matter: "You already know everything in this book. You've just forgotten. Reading is remembering." Against it, repeating a positive self-statement hurt people with low self-esteem (Wood and colleagues 2009); a Kagyu text calls the direct method "deadly poison" for the unprepared; and Kṣemarāja addressed his summary of recognition to readers in whom grace had already acted. This extends the organizational supplement's sixth disagreement, which set the same sentence against the research on raised expectations (`ops/facilitation ecology — management and organizational psychology.md`, section 6). The question is whether the book states a condition, as Kṣemarāja did, or leaves the sentence unconditioned and accepts that it will reach some readers as a claim they cannot meet.
3. **The letter read alone against the witnesses, with new evidence on both sides.** Against the solitary form: Atmananda's "A book, as such, is dead and inert, and cannot answer a new question"; a partner's hand that lowers the brain's threat response where a stranger's does not; Hübl's group; Hudson's "You know this place"; and in-person checking in every tradition that checks. For it: Harding's experiments, Goode's and Kelly's written methods, Spira's "no affiliation," Zen's awakenings "as a result of reading several lines of scripture," Hudson's solitary welcome, and the small but real effects of expressive writing and self-help books. The new detail is that where a tradition holding a teacher necessary taught at a distance, in Dahui's letters and Namkhai Norbu's webcast, the teacher knew the student or taught live, while the methods written for strangers, Harding's, Goode's, and Kelly's, come from teachers who hold that a text can occasion the first seeing. This sharpens the question the scaffold records for the author, where the scaffold is the book's structure as read out of the graph (`ops/scaffold.md`, its seventh joint, one of the places where two structures meet and the author has to decide between them), and it adds no new position.
4. **Force against safety.** The book's doctrine sides with the coaches who include what resists (section 2, regularity 6). The corpus's own room also holds Robbins's confrontation and Deida's consequence for freezing, and the vault keeps the choice between surrender and will open, to be decided from the body (`notes/surrender and will are adjudicated from the body rather than from doctrine.md`). The territories add that two holders of the will side have moved in their own later words, Deida to "no resistance" and Singer to a surrender that includes discipline and action, and that the measured evidence against force is narrow.
5. **The polyvagal work as established knowledge, against the specialists' statement.** The author's directive on the book's center names "the polyvagal work," beside McLaren's emotion research and Gendlin's felt shift, as knowledge established outside the project on which the book's system vocabulary may rest (`ops/methodology/center of gravity.md`), and the abridged edition's research summaries describe the polyvagal states. In 2026 Paul Grossman and 38 co-authors concluded "that the PVT is untenable," after a 2023 review and Stephen Porges's 2025 reply that the critics attack claims the theory never made. Jonny Miller's three states of the nervous system are, in his words, "drawing from Polyvagal theory," and Hübl cites Porges's theory in his account of collective trauma. The narrower findings the territories confirm can carry most of what the drafts use it for: attention to the body and accuracy about it are "distinct and dissociable," slow breathing raises heart-rate variability, and a trusted partner's hand lowers the brain's response to threat where a stranger's does not. Whether the directive keeps the polyvagal work as its example, and whether the draft's summaries stay, are the author's decisions.
6. **Whether grace is earned.** The vault records, from secondary sources, grace that is independent of effort and merit; Jaideva Singh's introduction, in the researcher's paraphrase, says grace is not caprice and must be earned by discipline. The book's offer to every reader leans on the first reading. A reading can settle what Singh meant (section 11, reading 3).
7. **Whether a text can hold the posture of "gentle agnostic respect."** Christopher Timmermann and colleagues ask guides to hold a revelation with "gentle agnostic respect," neither confirming nor dismissing it, and to let it be tested between people over time. Of the jobs a second person does, it is the one that verifies without deciding. The question is whether the book can take that posture toward what its reader finds, in its own sentences, and where the testing over time would then happen.
8. **The Aletheia coincidence.** A coaching school named Aletheia, founded by Steve March, is taught in Jonny Miller's course; its question, "What if nothing is missing? What if you're already a whole human being?", is close to the book's front matter, and the researcher did not find how the school chose its name. Whether the author has met March's work, and whether it matters to him, only he can say.

## 12. How this was made, and its limits

**Seven researchers worked from one shared brief.** Each researcher took one territory, except that two divided the eleven coaches, and each used at most twelve web searches. The brief asked for every entry to state what is facilitated and how it holds across scales, and to place it by the survey's coordinates, with three bands added for molecules and cells, a single organism, and an ecosystem. Each researcher fetched the raw text of every page quoted, through the encyclopedia's raw source, the scholarly indexes' full-text services, or the page itself, because a page-fetching tool that summarizes can reword a quotation without saying so (`ops/observations/a page-fetching tool that summarizes can reword a quotation without saying so.md`).

**Every quotation, number, and vault path was checked by script, and the claims about the vault were checked by hand.** All 1,645 quotations were found in the raw text of the address given. The numbers in the entries' prose were checked against their sources, and the one not found by script, a trial's sample of 90, was confirmed in the abstract, which spells it "Ninety." Every vault path exists. Reading the researchers' statements about the vault and the corpus against the files found errors that were corrected in the entries before they were rendered, among them a line the corpus holds attributed to the vault, a corpus phrase paraphrased inexactly, and a statement attributed to an interview that no source supplies. The same reading of this document's drafts corrected an overstated account of the vault's arc inquiry and a conclusion attributed to the wrong authors, and every quotation in this document was checked against the raw text.

**The sources are uneven across the territories.** Of the 42 living-systems entries, 41 cite an encyclopedia article and 21 cite nothing else, and 21 cite a scholarly index or journal. Of the 33 pointing-out entries, 27 cite an encyclopedia and 17 cite nothing else, so many of the traditions' words come through Wikipedia's quotations of books that were not opened, among them Tulku Urgyen Rinpoche, the Dalai Lama, Matthew Kapstein, Stuart Lachs, and Robert Sharf. The body, psychedelic, and texts territories cite a scholarly index or journal in 18 of 34, 23 of 34, and 27 of 33 entries respectively. The coaches rest mostly on the coaches' own published texts and transcripts, and 18 of their 111 entries cite an encyclopedia at all.

**Specific sources are thin in named ways.** The Rigpa findings come from Wikipedia's summary because the report would not load. The grades of śaktipāta come through Lakshman Joo and Dupuche, and Kṣemarāja's commentary through a translation of 1912 read from a scanned text. Crone's words come from two interviews published by their hosts, and the sentence the corpus quotes from him was not found in either. Singer's own site returned a verification page, so his words come from Sounds True's transcripts. Wineland's entries rest on one interview. Colonna's *Reunion* was read only through its publisher's page. The BuzzFeed reporting on Robbins is reported and denied. Twenty-four entries are marked partly verified, for reasons each entry states: a source page with no date, a finding read only at second hand, a source that supports only part of the entry, or, for the eight regularities proposed in the living-systems part, the researcher's own synthesis.

**Several judgments are the researchers' and this document's, and they are marked as such.** The coordinates of each entry (who decides the content, scale, structure, and mental model) are the researchers' judgments. The rows headed "How it bears on the book" and "Where it differs from the book" are the researchers' readings of the vault. The seven regularities in section 2 are proposals. The territories were chosen by the author and by this session, and each is a best-of sample rather than a census: the living-systems researcher did not survey immunology, for example, and the pointing-out survey did not include Sufi or Christian contemplative teaching.
