# the map sections gather by arrival batch rather than by concern

CLAUDE.md's map law expects a map that outgrows roughly twenty-five entries to split within its section structure first, and it expects each map to group its claims by theme rather than in one flat list. Measured on 2026-08-15 (the census is in the 2026-08-15 entry of `ops/completed.md`), the largest maps' newest sections are named by which expedition delivered the material rather than by what the material asserts. `notes/relational-field.md` gathers 149 entries, of which 53 sit under "The reframe conversation: sovereignty, facilitation, and participation" and 41 under "What the transmission digs added"; `notes/book-structure.md` gathers 148, with 29 and 44 under the same two headings; `notes/recognition.md` gathers 99, with 36 under its reframe section; and `notes/interlocutors.md` holds 143 entries in its single "Notes in this map" section. Six maps carry a digs section and five carry a reframe section.

A provenance-named section answers where the material came from, which `archive/` and `ops/completed.md` already record, and defers the question the map tier exists to answer — what concern gathers these claims. The concern-level structure the two intakes actually carry is visible one tier down: `scripts/queries/clusters.sh` separates the sovereignty-and-facilitation mass from the second-chair-and-letter mass, and `notes/relational-field.md`'s members split 59 and 65 across those two regions, a division no heading in the map states. The adjacent prior is `ops/observations/every maintenance condition counts unfinished work and none counts unsynthesized work.md`, which named the absence of any synthesis-debt condition; this observation names the specific form the debt has now taken inside the maps. Input to /rethink: whether /reflect owes the two intakes a concern-sorting pass through the oversized maps, and whether the split rule should name this failure mode — a section named by an arrival event is a holding pen rather than a split.
