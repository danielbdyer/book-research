# the vault inverted composting when it borrowed the five ripening states

The vault's `state:` enum takes its five names from the metabolic arc at atlas §V.3 and abridged ch10 — nascent, privated, full, flourishing, composting — and the arc survey (`ops/arc ecology.md`, tension 8) found that the borrowing changed the terms in two ways nothing records. The corpus's composting is reanimatable: "Composting is not death. It is return to potential… Under the right conditions, it can be reanimated" (abridged ch10). `CLAUDE.md` glosses the vault's composting as "superseded but drawn on," and the one composted note, [[a practice round is finished when the body has refused at least one formulation]], was retired because a reading "failed the note's own stated check" — falsification, not ripening. And the corpus's ordering does not survive its own uses: abridged ch6's Stagnation formula gives a three-term lifecycle, "Nascent → Full → Composting," while `templates/claim-note.md` defines privated as "a complete claim incomplete in a specific named way," which on the vault's own definition is more developed than nascent and is not a rung at all.

The friction is that the vault's grounding gauge counts nascent notes as the number to drive down and treats the five names as a sequence, while its own template and its one worked composting say the names are conditions. What would close it: a decisions row stating what the vault means by each state and whether composting is terminal here, and a one-line correction to `CLAUDE.md`'s gloss, or a note that the vault's composting is deliberately not the corpus's.
