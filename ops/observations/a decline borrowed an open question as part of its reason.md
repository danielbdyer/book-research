# a decline borrowed an open question as part of its reason

On 2026-08-04 a session read the author's statement that the book is "likely epistemically based in a first-person context" as a decision about grammatical person. It then declined the seventeen trial chapters partly on that reading — the drafts are in immersive second person, therefore they could not be revised toward the settled form — and wrote the inference into `ops/queue/declines.md`, `ops/goals.md`, and `ops/methodology/the book's current shape.md`. The author corrected it the same day: whether the book says *I* or *you* is undecided, and he asked that neither a refusal nor a decision be allowed to collapse a dichotomy that has no strong consensus.

The mechanism worth noticing is not the misreading itself but what the vault did with it. A decline is a durable record by design — `ops/queue/declines.md` exists so that later passes do not re-litigate settled material — so a reason written into it is read by every later session as established fact. When that reason leans on an open question, the decline silently promotes the question to closed, and the promotion is hard to see because it lives inside a file whose whole purpose is to be trusted rather than re-examined. The same hazard applies to any durable surface that records a rationale: the decisions register, the completed log, and the orientation.

Two features of the vault made the collapse easy rather than unlikely. The author's hedges are load-bearing and the vault's prose register rewards firm declarative sentences, so "likely epistemically first-person" compresses naturally into "the form is first-person" — the register's own habit, in the same way the colon habit produced the frontmatter defect recorded in this folder. And the vault has a tier document precisely so that unsettled things stay unsettled, which means a wrong tier assignment is more damaging here than a wrong sentence elsewhere: tier one is read as buildable.

The fix applied was local. The inference was removed from the decline, the observation about the drafts was kept without it, the tier document now separates epistemic grounding from grammatical person and lists the person question in tier three, and the rule was recorded as a standing directive with the author's words. What is left for /rethink is whether the vault needs a general guard — a habit of marking, at the moment a rationale is written, which parts are load-bearing decisions and which are assumptions a later pass may reopen.
