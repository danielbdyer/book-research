# coverage of the sources is not coverage of the writing's needs

The first reach-list dry run (`ops/reach-list — the instrument.md`) simulated a drafting session for the felt-shift concern and assembled everything the vault could hand it. The assembly worked — statement, origin, limits, and practice material were all rich — and the exercise still found a shelf that was empty: the standard's *defense*, the exact thing the author had named as his main reading job, had no material at all until the same day's research round filled it. No existing check could have caught this. The maintenance conditions count structural defects; /verify checks schema, links, and descriptions; the coverage script measures the corpus's sections against the graph. All of them measure how well the vault covers *what the sources say*. None of them asks what a drafting session will *need*, and the two diverge exactly where the corpus itself is thin — which, by the center-of-gravity decision's own logic, is where the book's real work is, since volume in the corpus is not centrality in the book.

The dry run cost one session-hour and caught the largest gap in the vault's most important concern. What is left for /rethink: whether the reach-list becomes a recurring instrument — one dry run per major concern, refreshed when the concern's material changes — and whether the writing test's phrasing should be extended from "would a drafting session reach for this?" (an admission filter) to also ask "what would a drafting session reach for and not find?" (a coverage probe). The two questions are the same test pointed in opposite directions, and the vault has only ever run the first.
