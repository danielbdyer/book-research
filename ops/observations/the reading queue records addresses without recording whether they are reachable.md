# the reading queue records addresses without recording whether they are reachable

**What happened.** The session was handed four design-gating readings in a stated order and completed two of them. Winnicott's object-usage paper was found in full, free, at a university library page, and Vygotsky's chapter was found in full in a scanned Harvard edition. Sierra's *Depersonalization* and Storr's *Solitude* were not obtainable at all: both sit behind publishers' paywalls, the copies in the Internet Archive's lending collection open only to a borrower with a login, and this session's network policy refuses the archived-page host that would otherwise have served a snapshot. The Katz reading before them had a third outcome, which is the one worth naming: the essay was readable through a scan published on a document-hosting site, but only in the pages that site exposes as text, so the essay's epistemological argument was read at its own pages and its comparative body was sampled rather than read.

**The friction.** Every entry in `ops/reading queue.md` carries a page-level address, and the sharp-questions standard requires one. No entry carries anything about whether the text at that address can actually be obtained. The address answers *where to read* and says nothing about *whether reading is possible*, and those turn out to be different facts with different lifetimes: an address is stable, and reachability changes when a link rots, a paywall moves, or a session's network policy differs. The cost is paid twice. A session spends real effort discovering an access failure that a previous session may already have discovered, and — the worse half — a session that finds a partial text has to decide alone how much partial reading is enough, with no recorded convention.

**Why it is worth recording rather than fixing on the spot.** The obvious repair is a fifth part on every entry, next to Q, Stake, Address and Standing, saying what has been tried and what it returned. That is a change to a standing instrument's shape and to the sharp-questions directive that defines it, which is a /rethink decision rather than a session's. Two smaller things were done instead, and both are inside a session's remit: the two blocked entries now say in their own text that access was attempted on 2026-08-08 and what it returned, and each completed reading record states exactly which pages were read at their own text and which were not.

**What would make the case.** If a later session attempts one of these two again and rediscovers the same block, the repair has two instances and is worth proposing. If the next access attempt succeeds — a library copy, a different network — the observation shows only that reachability is per-session rather than per-entry, which is a weaker finding and would argue for recording the attempt rather than the verdict.
