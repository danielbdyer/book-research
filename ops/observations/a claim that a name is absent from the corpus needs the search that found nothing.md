# a claim that a name is absent from the corpus needs the search that found nothing

On 2026-09-26 a researcher cataloguing the coaches' moves for `ops/facilitation ecology — six surrounding territories.md` reported that `notes/the routing surfaces carry four more names alone than the sort filed as company.md`, and the reading record behind it (`archive/reading — the routing surfaces recounted (speaking being, moreno, the scharmer count).md`), say "Hendricks has zero occurrences anywhere in the corpus." A word-bounded search of the extracted corpus finds one: the arc-and-gloss draft's roster of the coaches, on its first page, reads "Crone, Hudson, Robbins, Katie, Singer, Hendricks, the CLG trio, Colonna, Wineland, Deida, Hübl."

## What it cost

The error is small in itself, but it stood for three weeks inside a note whose subject is which names the vault's sorting missed, and it shaped how the Hendricks were counted there. Neither the note nor the record says what search produced the zero, so the session could confirm that the claim was wrong and could not tell why: a search of the wrong folder, a search before the corpus was extracted, and a pattern that did not match are all possible.

## What it implies

A claim that something is absent from the corpus or the vault is a search result, and it can be checked only if it records the search: the pattern, the folder, and the date. The vault's rule to use word boundaries (`CLAUDE.md`, Search) prevents one kind of false match; this is the opposite fault, a false absence. No rule needs to change now. The correction to the note itself is listed for the pipeline in section 9 of the supplement.
