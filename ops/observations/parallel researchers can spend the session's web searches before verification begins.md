# parallel researchers can spend the session's web searches before verification begins

On 2026-09-26 a session surveyed the field of facilitative techniques at the author's request (`ops/facilitation ecology.md`). Seven researchers worked in parallel, and six of them gathered from the web: five by scale and one for the moves, stances, and mental models that cross every scale. The session's web searches are a single allowance shared by every researcher it starts, and the six used it up near the end of the gathering. The last checks, which were meant to confirm entries the researchers had marked as resting on general knowledge, had to rely on pages already fetched, and fetching a known address still worked where searching did not.

## What it cost

Four of the 299 techniques remain unverified, among them John Heron's six categories of intervention, which the survey would otherwise have used as a published precedent for its coordinate of who decides; three of the 37 stances are unverified; and one fact the session wanted for its own section on method, whether Ken Wilber's first book sorted the therapies by level, could not be confirmed and became a reading question instead. The survey states each of these in its section 15 and marks every unverified entry where it is used.

## What it implies

A survey that fans out to the web should reserve part of the allowance for verification before the gathering starts, for example by giving each researcher a stated number of searches and holding the rest back for the checks that follow. A second, smaller point from the same round: a technique counted as met by the vault included ones the vault names only as unread entries in its reading queue, so a survey that reports what the vault has met should say what it counts as meeting, as this one does in its section 9. Neither point needs a change to the vault's rules; both are for the next session that runs a survey of this size.
