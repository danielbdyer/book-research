# the register tripwire blocks well-formed sentences it cannot parse

The register rewrite of 2026-08-06 was blocked three times by `.claude/hooks/pre-write-register.sh`, and all three catches were false. The blocked sentences were "The convergence paragraph cannot be written at the vault's own standard," "The round's kept paragraphs landed at four notes," and "This round wrote a kept paragraph into four notes." Each has a named subject and a finite verb, and each was blocked by rule R2 in `scripts/queries/register-tripwires.py`, the rule that fires when a capitalized noun phrase is followed by a past participle from a fixed list.

The mechanism is visible in the script. R2 clears a line only when one of the words before the participle appears in a hand-written set called `FINITE`, which holds about sixty verbs. That set contains "can" and not "cannot," and it contains "is" and not "be," so "cannot be written" reads to the detector as a headless participle. It also contains no entry for "wrote," so any sentence of the form "X wrote a kept paragraph" trips on the adjective "kept," which is in the participle list because the vault uses the phrase "kept paragraph" as a term of art. A word list that must enumerate English finite verbs will keep failing this way, and the phrases the vault uses most — kept paragraph, recorded decision, stated position — are the ones most likely to sit in the participle list and be misread.

The script's own docstring anticipates this and says what to do: "if a catch is wrong, restructure anyway until the subject and verb are explicit — a shape this detector cannot parse will also slow a tired reader." That instruction is defensible and it was followed all three times, and it also has a cost worth recording. Each rewrite took a sentence that read well and replaced it with one chosen to satisfy a regular expression, which is a second author in the room with a vote and no stake in the meaning. The third rewrite went through two rounds before landing, and the sentence that finally passed is no clearer than the one that was blocked first.

A second session on 2026-08-07 raised the count. Writing up the Nisbett and Wilson reading tripped the same rule three more times, on "The vault's held position came from the blind repeat pass," "The three quoted passages are at pages 255, 231 and 233," and "The two quoted passages are on page 254" — and the Schwitzgebel write-up tripped it three times more, so nine false catches in total, all of them the same shape: a short noun phrase followed by "read," "held," or "quoted" used adjectivally, with the finite verb sitting after the participle rather than before it. That shape is common in a reading record, because a reading record's whole subject is passages that were read and quoted. The same session also produced two true catches, one fragment on a topic map and one bold headline standing where a sentence belonged, so the rule keeps earning its place while its precision on this one shape is poor.

Later the same day the count rose again, and the new catches point at a second gap in the same word list. Repairing the topic maps after the candidate list was cut tripped the rule twice on one paragraph, first on "The space-around survey proposed five additions to this map" and then on "The author ordered the candidate shelf pruned on 2026-08-07." Both are complete sentences whose finite verb is the very word the detector read as a participle, because "proposed" and "ordered" are past-tense forms that the participle list contains and the `FINITE` set omits. A third instance already sits unfixed in `ops/orientation.md` at line 9, where "The author stated what the research is about" trips on "stated" for the same reason. The running total is therefore twelve false catches against four true ones, and every one of the twelve is a regular English past tense standing where the detector expects a participle.

The observation is not that the gate should be removed. It caught two genuine fragments in `ops/reach-list — the address.md` on the same run, both of them real, and both had survived the branch until then. The observation is that a gate which blocks a write is a different instrument from one that reports, and that the false-positive rate measured so far — twelve false catches against four true ones — is the number a future /rethink should weigh when deciding whether the block belongs at the write itself or at the commit. The alternative already exists in the same repository: `.claude/hooks/post-bash-register.sh` runs the identical detector and reports rather than blocking.

The register pass of 2026-08-08 met the same shape twice more, in the diff-scan mode rather than at a blocked write. "A held container is not a metaphor" and "The written half above is a session's obligation" both fired R2, because "held" and "written" sit in the participle list and no finite verb precedes them inside the opening noun phrase. Both sentences were well formed, both were restructured per the script's instruction, and the running total is now fourteen false catches against four true ones. The same pass also took one false block at the write gate, on a sentence opening "Ten proposed arrivals" — "proposed" again — which was restructured to lead with a finite verb.

One narrower fix is available and does not touch the design. `FINITE` could be replaced by a check for any word ending in a finite inflection, or the participle list could drop the words the vault uses adjectivally in its own terms of art, beginning with "kept." Either change would remove nine of the twelve false catches recorded here and none of the true ones; the three newest, which turn on the past tenses "proposed," "ordered" and "stated," need those forms added to `FINITE` as well, or the inflection check that would make the hand-written list unnecessary.
