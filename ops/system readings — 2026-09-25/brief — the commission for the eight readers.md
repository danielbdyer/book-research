# Instructions for the eight readers (2026-09-25)

*About this version.* A session wrote these instructions on 2026-09-25 and gave them to the eight readers. On the same day, after the author said he could not follow much of the study's writing, a session rewrote them in plain language. The instructions are the same. The rewrite uses the plain names the design now uses, and it names each part of the design document by its current title instead of by the line numbers the readers were given, because the design has since been rewritten. The readers received the earlier wording, which is in the repository's history at commit `418f352`. The rewritten reports follow the plain names below, and they restate their section 0 in the third person, as what the reader read.

You are one of eight readers, four running on one model family and four on another, each reading one region of a research repository against a design. Your report will be merged with the other seven into three documents for the author. Read all of these instructions before you start.

## 1. What the repository is

`/home/user/book-research` is the research vault for a book by one author (he/him), who is a poet and a facilitator. The book is now two books, which differ in where the practice happens. **Book I** is for facilitators (it is about facilitating another person's participation) and is written first. **Book II** is for a person practicing alone (it is about facilitating one's own participation).

The vault stores the books' material as claims, one proposition to a markdown file in `notes/`, with the file name stating the claim. The claims are gathered on topic maps, pages that each gather the claims of one concern. Around them are the operational files (`ops/`); the records of readings of thinkers' own texts (`archive/`); reconstructions of how the corpus was made (`provenance/`); drafts (`drafts/`); the author's own reflections (`reflections/`); and the family files at the repository root (`the-*.md`), each of which reads the whole collection of notes by one organizing idea (the vault calls them lens families). `CLAUDE.md`, at the root, states the vault's standing rules; read it first, in full. Among other things, it says:

- By the author's decision, the book's two central parts are **the convergence of thinkers** (thinkers who reached the same finding independently, quoted with their disagreements with the book kept intact) and **the practice the reader actually does**, checked in the body. The system of terms the corpus builds (the Generative Triad, the twelve primitives, the molecule formulas, and the three loops) is of secondary importance, not the book's center.
- **The primary material takes precedence over the corpus.** A thinker's own work outranks the corpus's account of that thinker, and the author's poems come before the seven documents of the corpus, which quote them.
- **The stance** is that a convergence is company, not proof: the book cites thinkers who reached the same finding as others who noticed the same thing, not as evidence that it is true. The vault does not claim that any of this works.
- **The prose rules** require plain research diction: every claim names its source; figures of speech appear only inside quotation marks; and the vault's own coined words are explained before they are used.

## 2. The design you are reading against

On 2026-09-25 a session summarized the author's long conversation with a language model (the reframe conversation, whose transcript is `reframe-conversation-full-2026-08-13.md`) and designed, from that transcript alone, a list of the kinds of material the two books need. The design is in `reframe-map-and-system-2026-09-25.md`. It was written without reading the vault. Your task is to read the vault against it.

Before you read your region, read these parts of the design document in full:
- **Part 5, "How the books are meant to work":** the nine functions both books share, and what Book I and Book II each add.
- **Part 6, "What material each function needs."**
- **Part 7, "The design":** the six principles; the **eleven kinds of material** (topic, pair, practice, image, encounter, tradition or thinker, comparison, the author's view, writing technique, drafted passage, and book), each with its fields and connections; the four things recorded for every kind of material (the author's level, scale, quadrant, and source); the connections between kinds, stated in words; the design's **two lists** (the commitments, which are the nine rules the books must keep, each with a question to ask of a draft; and the open questions, which are the unresolved questions and choices); how the work proceeds; the four tests; the **topic table**, with one row for each topic and the columns Question, Book I, Book II, Pair, Practice, Encounter, Company, Comparison, Author's view, and What it hides; and what the design leaves out on purpose.
- **Part 10, "The 146 rules stated in the transcript,"** especially its section "How the rules fit the design," which shows that each group of rules is the condition on one connection.

Read the rest as you need it: Part 1 (the conversation); Part 2 (the seven outlines the model wrote, and where they move away from what the author said); Part 3 (the center, described as roles); Part 4 (the founding insight: the two poles, contraction and expansion, neither less than the other, and the pair as the form the books' ideas take); Part 8 (where the earlier lists and the open choices fit); Part 9 (a first filling of the design from the transcript alone: fifteen topics, about seventy pairs, practices, images, writing techniques, twenty-one traditions and thinkers, the author's statements, and open questions); and Part 11 (the questions for this study).

Two points about the design matter for judging how well the vault fits it:
- **The rule that every part must serve the books.** Every kind of material, field, and connection must name the function of the books it serves. Material that serves no function of the books is not a failure of the vault; it may belong to the vault's own tools and records rather than to the books' material. Say so plainly when that is your finding.
- **The author's level.** Material is *proposed* (brought in by research, by a conversation, or by an AI), then *experienced*, *inferred*, *believed*, *claimed*, or *written*, and only the author raises anything above "proposed."

## 3. The three questions

The author asked for exactly this (his words): "(1) the collation of existing material at a high level inside this system (2) where the existing material goes beyond or outside of the system, and how the system expands to receive it (3) where in the system is left to populate the existing material does not have any details or information."

For your region:

1. **Where the material fits.** Place the region's material in the design at a high level: by groups and kinds of material, with counts and representative examples, not note by note. For each of the eleven kinds of material, the four things recorded for every kind, the two lists, and the connections, say what in your region already serves it, where it is (paths), how much of it there is, and how well it fits: already present; fits once translated, with the translation stated; or partial. Where the vault already has its own structure that does the same work as a part of the design (for example, a family file that already reads the notes by concern, or a frontmatter field that already records how developed a note is), name the correspondence exactly, and say what each side has that the other lacks. Fill in the topic table for every topic your region concerns, with the evidence for each entry.
2. **Material the design cannot record.** Find the material that goes beyond the design or falls outside it. For each group of such material, give what it is (paths, counts, and one short quotation); why the design cannot record it (which kind, field, or connection fails); which function of the books it serves, by the rule that every part must serve the books; and **how the design would be extended to record it**: a new kind of material, a new field, a wider definition, a new connection, a new list, or a decision that it belongs outside the design for the books, in the vault's own tools and records. Show one real example from the vault written in the extended form. Also record **corrections**: places where the vault shows that the design is wrong, not only incomplete.
3. **What the vault does not yet have.** Find the places the design defines that your region leaves empty or thin: kinds of material with no examples, fields that no material records, connections that nothing records, entries of the topic table left blank, and functions of the books with nothing to draw on. For each, give what exists (nothing, a little, or material in a form a drafting session could not use); how the absence was checked (what you searched for and where, including the search terms); and what would supply it and who can: only the author; a primary reading; a drafting session; an extraction from the corpus in `sources/`; or the transcript.

## 4. Rules

- **Read only.** Do not edit, create, move, or delete anything inside `/home/user/book-research`. Do not run git commands that change anything. Do not run `scripts/bootstrap.sh`. You may run read-only commands (`rg`, `grep`, `find`, `wc`, `sed -n`, and `head`) and the read-only query scripts in `scripts/queries/` that print to standard output. Write only your report file, at the path your assignment gives, in the scratchpad.
- **Two shared aids** are in the scratchpad next to these instructions: `notes-index.md` (one line per note, with its title, its maps, its frontmatter fields, the kinds of its sources, its size, and its description) and `inventory.md` (every file outside `notes/`, with its size and its own description). Use them to work at a high level and to find what to open. Open the files themselves for anything you say about their content.
- **Accuracy.** Copy every quotation from the file, with its path. Every count must come from a command you ran or a list you actually made; say which. Separate what you found from what you infer.
- **Read closely rather than widely.** Read the maps and family files in your region in full. Read in full the notes the books depend on most (the ones the maps and family files cite most), and enough of the rest, through the index and targeted reading, to describe each kind. Say what you read in full and what you sampled.
- **Plain language.** Your report is working material for the merged documents, but write it plainly: complete sentences, named subjects, and, when you use one of the vault's coined words, a clause saying what it refers to.
- **Leave the author's decisions to the author.** Placing material in chapters, resolving tensions, giving thinkers a role in the book, and raising the author's level are the author's acts. Report what exists and propose structure; do not decide doctrine.

## 5. Report format

Write your report as markdown at your assigned path, with exactly these sections:

```
# Region report: <your region's name> (<your label, for example O1>)

## 0. What I read
Paths, and how fully (in full / first N lines / through the index only). The commands you used for counts.

## 1. Where the region's material fits in the design
### 1a. Kind by kind
A table: Kind of material | What in this region serves it | Where, with counts | Fit (already present / fits once translated: state the translation / partial) | Notes
One row for each of the eleven kinds, including those your region does not serve (write "nothing in this region").
### 1b. The four things recorded for every kind of material, and the design's two lists
The author's level, scale, quadrant, source, the commitments, and the open questions: what the region already records that corresponds, and how exactly.
### 1c. Connections
Which of the design's connections the region already records, and in what form (links with a sentence stating the relation, map sections, frontmatter fields, or the text of notes).
### 1d. Structures the vault already has that serve the design's purposes
Each correspondence, with what each side has that the other lacks.
### 1e. The topic table
For each topic your region concerns: the ten entries (Question, Book I, Book II, Pair, Practice, Encounter, Company, Comparison, Author's view, and What it hides), each marked present, thin, or absent, with the evidence.

## 2. Material the design cannot record
One subsection for each group of material: what it is; why the design cannot record it; the function of the books it serves (or none, and where it belongs instead); how the design would be extended; and one real example written in the extended form.
### Corrections to the design

## 3. What the vault does not yet have
One subsection for each empty or thin place: what exists; how the absence was checked (the searches run); and what would supply it and who can.

## 4. The three findings that matter most
One paragraph each, with evidence.
```

Aim for a thorough report, of roughly 5,000 to 9,000 words. Tables are welcome where they make a comparison clear. When you finish, reply with a summary of no more than 300 words that names the three findings that matter most and gives the path of your report.
