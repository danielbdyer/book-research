# reading a source on its own terms

This is an author correction, issued 2026-08-03, global and standing. It was issued when the vault opened its reduce pass over the poems by tracing which corpus phrases the poems predate, and identifying the poem the drafts place at the opening unit's heart as the fourth of the collection. The author's sentence:

> The poem to draft identity is not as required as you think. Read them as separate corpuses.

## The rule

A source is read first as the body of work it is: what it is about, what it develops, what it arrives at, what shape it has from end to end. Only after that is it compared with anything else the vault holds. The comparison pass is legitimate and is not being banned — it is being demoted, and it may not run first, because running it first determines what the source is allowed to be about.

For the poems specifically: the collection is a body of work of its own, written across a life, with its own subject and its own arc. It is not a concordance to the manuscript.

## What went wrong, stated plainly so it is recognizable next time

The vault had a matching apparatus ready — a source registry, a direction-of-quotation rule, an extraction queue keyed to the corpus's regions, and an archive note whose concordance section already listed the poems as "trailheads," each one a poem phrase paired with the book doctrine it anticipates. Working that apparatus produced findings quickly and they were true findings. Every one of them was of the form *this thing in the book is older than the book*. None of them was of the form *this is what the poems are*.

The result is a body of work converted into evidence about a manuscript. That is the same regression the center-of-gravity directive names — organizing new material by a taxonomy the project already has — moved from the system vocabulary to the corpus at large, and it is harder to see here because the tracing work is genuinely interesting and the dates genuinely line up.

## The general form

Three things make a source liable to this:

1. **The vault has a slot the material fits.** The fit is what makes it feel like progress; the fit is also what makes the material stop being itself.
2. **The comparison yields facts and the reading yields judgments.** Facts arrive faster and look more like output, so the pass that produces them wins on effort even when it loses on value.
3. **A source that arrived to answer a question gets read only for the answer.** The poems arrived alongside a statement about direction of quotation, and the vault read them for direction of quotation.

## What this asks of a reduce pass

Open by stating what the source is and what it is about, in the vault's own sentences, before any anchor is matched. Where the source has a shape across its whole length — an arc, a change of register, a recurring address — that shape is a finding and usually the largest one available. Correspondences with the corpus are recorded where they are load-bearing, in the substantiation layer or in the archive note, and they do not become the claims unless the correspondence is itself the proposition.

## What this does not license

Reading a source loosely, or writing about it impressionistically. Stating what a body of work is about is a claim like any other and carries the same burden: quoted, anchored, compressed, and parseable with no prior context. The prose register is unchanged. What changes is which question is asked first.
