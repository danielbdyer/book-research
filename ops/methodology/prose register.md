# prose register

This is an author directive, issued 2026-08-01 during the initial derivation, global and standing. The model is the author's THE_VOICE document (an operator-facing register spec from an unrelated project), adapted from an instrument's terminal surfaces to a research vault. The governing principle carries over unchanged: **evidential literalism** — every sentence asserts a proposition grounded in its evidence, about a concretely named subject, with no rhetorical figure standing in for that precision.

## Rules for all vault prose (descriptions, note bodies, link glosses, topic maps, CLAUDE.md, manual, ops documents)

1. **Complete sentences wherever a statement is made.** No telegraphic fragments, no "X = Y" constructions on a statement line, no headless descriptions. A description is a proposition with a subject.
2. **Statement on top, substantiation beneath.** A note leads with the claim expounded in plain sentences, parseable with no prior context. Direct quotes, section locations, Sanskrit terminology, and the atlas's notation live beneath the statement as recorded evidence.
3. **Notation is recorded vocabulary, not assertion.** The atlas's glyphs (◉ well, ⚡ tension, ≈ isomorph, ⊖ privation, ⟿ feeds-into) may tag a relation that the surrounding sentence has already stated in words. A glyph never carries the assertion alone.
4. **The corpus is quoted, never impersonated.** The book's aphorisms and formulations appear in quotation marks with a source anchor. The vault's own sentences around a quote state what the quoted material claims and where it stands in the argument. This includes the book's antithetical formulations ("contraction, never corruption"), which are its content; the vault's own prose does not use antithesis to simulate rigor.
5. **Exact referents.** Chapter numbers, part numbers, section symbols, and document names appear wherever a location is meant. "The atlas records at §IV" rather than "the chemistry section says."
6. **Evidence carried in the sentence.** Claims about the corpus state their basis: which documents carry the claim, where, and whether the sources agree. A claim appearing in all four source documents states that fact; a claim resting on one document names the document.
7. **Stative reporting; imperative direction.** Vault state is reported as state. Next moves are bare imperatives naming their object. Notes do not address a reader.
8. **One register at every scale.** A three-claim topic map and a three-hundred-claim topic map read in the same voice; only the depth grows.
9. **End on the move.** An operational surface (topic map, queue, goals) closes by naming what remains or what the next pass should do. Nothing terminates at "done."
10. **System references are self-explanatory.** A sentence that refers to the vault's own machinery names the file and states what the reference means in terms of work done or not done. "The evaluation is queued" fails this rule; "X has not been researched for this project; it is on the pending-evaluation list in ops/queue/candidates.md" passes it. A sentence must carry its meaning to a reader who has never seen the system's internals.
11. **Readable before dense — the rule has two poles, and both are the author's.** Issued mid-session on 2026-08-03, twice. First, against ornament: "I entreat you to author plainly and reverently or do not write much at all"; "It is arcane and ornate and communicates in a way that is opaque." The fault named there is gymnastics — inverted constructions, stacked appositions, the corpus's figures carrying a title or a statement line, sentences that must be decoded rather than followed. Second, when the session over-corrected into clipped fragments, against density: "short and declarative is not the only way to write… complex sentences are fine, but you must see that I cannot parse the gymnastics quite as well as you can. I worry other agents will continue this concise praxis of explication and get saturated in their own density too." The working rule both corrections point at: write so the reader never has to stop and unpack. A sentence may be long and subordinate or short and simple, so long as it unfolds in the order a reader takes it in — one idea handed to the next, dense noun phrases opened into ordinary words, the corpus quoted beneath the statement rather than doing the stating. Compression is not a virtue in itself; a note earns nothing by being shorter than its own clarity, and a page of staccato is as saturated as a page of filigree. The test: a tired friend could follow it on first reading. When in doubt, explain a little more, in ordinary words.

    **Extended 2026-08-05, after two rounds of this correction had themselves been delivered in twisty prose. The author's words: "I need you to be ultra precise and unambiguous but in a way that does not limit the quality whatsoever."** The fault that survives between the two poles above is the figure: a sentence built as a figure of speech rather than as a statement. Its forms, taken from the session's own replies: the mirrored pair, which makes the reader hold one sentence and map a second onto it ("Indirection is what prose looks like when… Explication is what it looks like when…"); the metaphor that carries the claim, which makes the reader translate before receiving anything ("both pans of the counterweight are in the rule"); the ending compressed to be quotable rather than clear ("not zero, not a paragraph"); and self-narration, prose announcing its own moves before making them ("the concession first, with its evidence"). No one of these is vague alone. Each adds a decoding step, and enough of them make a message twisty although every sentence parses. The working rule: state the point in the sentence's own subject and verb, near the front; one hard idea per sentence; a concrete noun wherever one exists ("you cannot click a link in chat" rather than "a spoken reference cannot be opened"); figures only inside quotation marks, where the corpus's phrasing is itself the finding. This costs no quality, and the author's demand has two sides on purpose. In this vault quality is exactness: the right fact, complete, in an order a reader can follow. The figures were decoration on that, not part of it.

12. **Meaning is explicated where it is made; a reference is a receipt, never the content.** Issued 2026-08-05, in the author's words, twice in one exchange: "I feel the indirection being displayed in your conversation and your writing right now speaks in references that I cannot quite understand. It is this *also* that is a tic that we must correct." And, asked to confirm the target: "What I mean by indirection is the same arcane, opaque, or otherwise ambiguous approach to creating meaning — not fully explicated." The fault is meaning made by gesture rather than by statement, and it has several faces that are one thing: a sentence whose content lives in another file ("the second instance of the defect the load axis was created for" hands the reader three handles and no claim); a link gloss that classifies its target instead of stating it ("the finding this belongs to, as its case of mis-addressed credit" — a category where a claim should be); a compressed handle doing the work of an explanation; an allusion standing where an assertion belongs. Pages written this way look rigorous because they are dense with anchors, and that is the counterfeit the vault already has on record for the body at [[felt shift versus felt confirmation]]: the form of evidence standing in for its content. This rule is rule 10 widened. Rule 10 required a reference to the vault's *machinery* to carry its own meaning; the same standard now governs a reference to the vault's *content* — a note, a finding, a directive, a decision — and every other compression that asks the reader to already know. The working rule: **make the claim in the sentence, then attach the reference.** At least one clause of what the referenced thing actually says travels with every pointer to it, and a link gloss states the target's content in words, never only its kind. The test: strike the wiki links and file paths from the paragraph and read what remains. If it still asserts what it asserted, the paragraph was saying; if it collapses into relations among unnamed things, it was pointing. And the counterweight is weighted from both sides, because this register's corrections have over-corrected once before (rule 11 records the swing from ornate to clipped): the opposite fault is recapitulation — restating a whole argument at every mention until nothing is compressed and every note bloats. One clause of content per reference is the balance point. Not zero, not a paragraph.

    **Amended the same hour, on the author's third correction: "I do not think you can find this tic by a simple regex. It's more of a stance in authorship that needs to be maintained going forward."** He is right, and the session that received the correction had already proved it: the day's first repair replaced the classification phrase "the pattern this belongs to" by mechanical substitution, and what came out was "the finding this belongs to, as its case of X" — the same gesture in a new string. A pattern-check finds yesterday's phrasings; the stance generates tomorrow's. So the tests above are diagnostics for catching the tic after the fact, in review, and they are not the rule. The rule is the posture the sentences come from: **each sentence is written facing the reader, from the claim outward — not facing the vault, from the accumulated context inward.** Indirection is what prose looks like when the writer's attention is on the web of what is already written. Explication is what it looks like when the attention is on the thing being said and the person receiving it. That posture cannot be automated, cannot be delegated to a query, and is not installed once; it is held sentence by sentence, and this file's job is to restate it where every session forms its voice.

## Relationship to the vocabulary policy

The vocabulary policy (see `vocabulary policy.md`) governs *names*: the reference surface is denotative; connotation lives in connected notes. This policy governs *sentences*: the prose around those names is expository and evidence-grounded. Together they define the vault's voice: plain names, complete sentences, cited poetry.

## The register countermeasure

Installed 2026-08-05 at the author's direction, after five corrections in one day each held for one reply and not the next, and hardened the same day by a critique round he directed. His instruction, quoted: the countermeasure must be stamped "holistically and non-ambiguously, to a fault of overdoing it — and, in a way that does not reduce quality." The deliberate redundancy below is that instruction followed. The same seven lines are stamped at every surface a session loads: this file, `CLAUDE.md`, `ops/orientation.md`, `ops/methodology/reporting to the author.md`, `ops/derivation-manifest.md` — which the sixteen pipeline skills read at invocation, and whose personality block now carries the countermeasure in its values — and the session-start hook, which prints them at the top of every session. A per-turn hook, `.claude/hooks/register-reminder.sh`, prints the compact form before every reply is composed, which puts the countermeasure at zero distance from the moment every recorded failure happened and re-arms it after context compaction, which displaced the session-start copy at least once on the day of installation. Two write-time gates, installed after the seventh correction's specimen was written into a file mid-turn where every reply-side guard was blind, arm the countermeasure at the file layer: `.claude/hooks/pre-write-register.sh` blocks a Write or Edit into the prose folders when the proposed content trips one of the three shapes past violations actually took — a bare participle opening a line with no subject, a noun phrase yoked to a participle with no finite verb between them, a bold label ending in a colon where a sentence should stand — and `.claude/hooks/post-bash-register.sh` runs the same detector, `scripts/queries/register-tripwires.py`, over the added lines of uncommitted prose after every shell command, because batch edits made through the shell never pass the file tools. The gates catch shapes, not the stance; a block always instructs recomposition, and line 6's reread remains the substance of the guarantee. `scripts/queries/countermeasure.sh` verifies ten signature phrases from this block at every document surface, plus the hook wiring itself, at every session start and every turn — a label check proved insufficient within a day, when one copy drifted to "a subject and a verb" where the line below says "a named subject and a finite verb." What a script can verify is the installation and its delivery; the stance itself is applied sentence by sentence, and the author has ruled that no regex finds it. This is the canonical copy. If any surface disagrees with this one, this one wins.

**The seven lines follow, and none is optional.** (Line 5 was added 2026-08-06 by the author's sixth correction; line 7, the file-scope rewrite of line 6, and the list-site extension of line 1 followed the same day from his seventh, after a flagged sentence in an archive record exposed that every enforcement point aimed at replies while the failure happened in a file. Each amendment moved every stamped copy and the script's signature list in one commit.)

1. Every statement is a complete sentence with a named subject and a finite verb. No fragment stands as a sentence, and the last sentence of each paragraph is checked first, because the record shows fragments concentrate at the closes — and in list-shaped prose the bullet's opening words before a colon are checked at the same priority, because there a headline substitutes for a claim.
2. A vault-internal term — chair, seat, load, privated, concordance row, the sort, the torch, and every other pipeline coinage — is unfolded in the sentence where it appears, or replaced with plain words. In replies to the author this is absolute: he authored the book the terms describe, not the terms.
3. A reference is a receipt, never the content. Every link or file path travels with one clause stating what it points to, and a paragraph must still assert everything it asserted when its links are struck out.
4. A figure of speech appears only inside quotation marks, where the quoted phrasing is itself the finding. Mirrored pairs, load-bearing metaphors, quotable endings, and self-narration are statements withheld, not style.
5. An abstract noun that stands for something specific is expanded where it stands: the sentence names the thing, not its category. Consequence-sentences are checked first, because the record shows abstraction concentrates exactly where a finding's meaning is stated — the founding specimen chained "the wound… the standard… the doctrine… the treatment… a lineage… a mechanism" where it should have named the solitary reading, the felt shift, the relational claim, the second chair, the study-partner tradition, and the listener who produces the movement.
6. Every reply to the author is reread as him before it is sent, in full, applying lines 1 through 5 — fragments checked at the paragraph closes, abstraction checked at the consequence-sentences. And every prose file the session writes — a claim note, an archive record, an ops surface, a queue entry — is reread the same way before it is saved, as the reader it will actually meet: the author, or a later session holding none of this session's context. There is no internal register; a trace written for the record is owed the same sentences as a reply, because the author and the next agent build on the record, not on the reply.
7. A subagent's report is raw material, never copy. Its findings are recomposed under these lines before they are filed — a finding filed in the agent's telegraphic register is the filing session's violation, not the agent's — and every research prompt a session sends states the register's demands, so what returns arrives already carrying its referents.

The test for line 2 is checkable in place: if a word is neither the book's own vocabulary nor plain English, it is the pipeline's, and it gets unfolded. He authored the book; every word the vault invented about the vault is ours to translate.

**The quality clause binds at equal force.** None of this shortens or flattens anything. A sentence may be long and subordinate when it unfolds in the order a reader takes it in. Richness is the right fact, complete, with its reason attached. The ban is on compression that withholds and gesture that defers, never on complexity that delivers. The five-word fragment that occasioned rule 7 — "Recorded on the chair, not resolved." — lost its reason; the full sentence that replaced it carried more. The affirmative test remains rule 11's: a tired friend follows it on first reading. When these lines bind, quality rises, because everything the reader was owed is on the page.

**How this block is amended.** A change here updates every stamped copy and the signature list in `scripts/queries/countermeasure.sh` in the same commit. Because the script verifies content signatures rather than the label, a partial amendment fails loudly at the next session start and keeps failing until every copy has moved — which converts the largest maintenance risk, silent divergence of the copies, into an error that cannot be missed.

## The repair pass

The rules above are held while composing, and the vault also needs a way to repair prose that was composed without them. That is `/recompose` (`.claude/skills/recompose/SKILL.md`), written by hand on 2026-08-06 after the author read a whole branch and could not follow it. It rewrites finished prose to this directive without changing what the prose claims, and its governing constraint is that separation: the register changes, and the claims, the evidence, the categories, the topic maps, the statuses and the wiki links do not. It works seven faults — the fragment, the pipeline coinage, the reference that points instead of saying, the figure of speech carrying a claim, the abstract noun left unexpanded, the opaque filename, and the citation with no address — and it records the content problems it finds in `ops/goals.md` rather than fixing them, because resolving is the author's act. Run it when he flags a file, when a research round has just filed a batch of notes, or before a branch merges. Do not run it as routine maintenance, because rewriting a sentence that was already clear costs something and gains nothing.

**When the author flags a sentence**, the session that receives the flag records it in `ops/register violations.md` in the same turn — the sentence quoted, the date, and which of the seven lines it broke. The log exists because the countermeasure's one measured pattern, that fragments concentrate at paragraph closes, was found only by counting past violations, and the next correction will need an address the same way rule 7 had one.
