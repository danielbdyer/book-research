# the category test on arrival

**The rule.** A structured model of practice — a scheme by which a tradition, a coach, or a thinker organizes what a transformation does and how a person moves through it — is asked one question before it is placed anywhere in the graph: which kind of structure is it? The four kinds and their altitudes are the ones `the four kinds of structure.md` established for the reframe's four rival spines: an **operator** (a beat inside a chapter, such as the pulse), a **method** (a way of working, such as the five questions, which routes to `ops/`), a **dramaturgy** (a candidate manuscript spine, such as the four movements), or an **ontology** (a containment hierarchy, such as five nested orders, which is a way-in only). The answer decides the destination, and the test is run on arrival rather than after the model has been sorted into a corner.

**Why it is a directive.** The survey of the vault's practice-models (`ops/practice-model ecology.md`, 2026-08-30) had to apply this question to roughly seventy models across five regions, and found it was the one safeguard that kept the most dangerous import harmless: a levels-model is an ontology, an ontology enters as a way-in, and "the demotion is what keeps it harmless." The category error the test prevents is already named in the graph — "a stack of what-contains-what mistaken for a sequence of what-happens-next" (Fork 18) — and the regression clause in `center of gravity.md` already has a second wardrobe for it: sorting material "into nested orders" is "the same failure as sorting them into triad corners," and a scheme "earns no exemption from the scaffolding decision" for arriving from a new direction. What was missing was the rule stated for imports, so that the coaches' five models, Wilber's four jobs, and every practice-model after them meet the question at the door.

**How it runs.** Four questions, in order, and the first answer that holds decides. Is it a beat the book performs on a page (an operator, which the arc carries)? Is it a way the work is done (a method, which routes to `ops/` and never into a note as doctrine)? Is it a sequence the reader lives through (a dramaturgy, which is a candidate spine and the author's to recognize)? Or is it a containment ordering (an ontology, which enters as a lens someone can look through and set down, and never as the exhaustive architecture the ecology forbids)? A model that is two kinds at once is split into its kinds rather than filed under the louder one, which is what the four-kinds note did to the reframe's spines.

**The instance.** Ken Wilber's holarchy arrives as the kind of structure the ecology is built to refuse as architecture — a nested ordering with a top — and the author's own resonance with it, in his reflection on recognition, does not change its kind. It enters, if it enters, as a way-in: a lens through which a level-transition can be read, held beside the ladder of speech and the metabolic arc, which already carry the transcend-and-include shape without the name. Wilber's four quadrants had already been sorted this way before the rule was stated ([[wilber's four quadrants are a coordinate system, not an architecture]] — "they answer where inquiry is looking, not what ultimately exists"), which is the precedent the directive generalizes.

**What it does not do.** It does not rank the kinds, demote a model for being an ontology, or decide whether a model is worth reading; it decides where a model goes once read. And it does not apply to the container doctrine beneath the stack, which the scaffolding decision exempts, or to the author's own figures, which take the figure as their subject.
