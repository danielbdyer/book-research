# the primary material

This is an author directive, issued 2026-08-05, global and standing. His words:

> "I really want to be clear that this research is and has always been about the primary material, and not the sources I've provided which indicate an imperfectly potentiated form of the essences I really care about."

The occasion was a session's report on the triad and the molecules, which he had asked to be examined for whether they hold merit outside the conversations that produced them. The statement is larger than that occasion. It says what the research is *about*, and it says the seven corpus documents are not it.

## What it changes

The vault already held this shape in one place. The poems carry the standing `primary` because the corpus quotes them and not the other way around, and the rule that follows — for material predating the book, a shared phrase originates in the poem — exists to keep the direction of flow straight (`source standing.md`, standing 3). This directive generalizes that direction. The corpus is downstream. It is a set of renderings, made in one compiled month, of things that existed before it and exist outside it, and where a rendering and its subject disagree the subject is not corrected by the rendering.

So the corpus's standing as *evidence* is unchanged and its standing as *authority over what the book is about* is removed. A corpus passage still establishes that the book has said a thing, in a particular way, at a stage; it does not establish that the thing is what the book is about, and it never did — the atlas's page-count already had a guard for exactly this reason (`center of gravity.md`, "volume in the corpus is not centrality in the book"). This directive extends that guard from weighting to subject matter.

## What the primary material is

**This list is the vault's reading of his statement, not his enumeration, and it is offered for his correction.** What he named directly was the contrast — primary material, and not the sources he provided. The five below are what the record supports as standing upstream of the corpus:

1. **The poems.** Sixteen, a lifetime's, already standing `primary` in `sources/`, with the sixteenth flagged by him as the exception whose flow runs the other way.
2. **The lived events.** The participation law lived in a room in 2017; the facilitation craft and the rooms it was practiced in; the 4am reading of a decade-old letter; the retreat of March 2026. These are held under `personal provenance.md` and its three conditions.
3. **The seed's figures.** The house with no ceiling, the tuning fork, the gold thread, the fourth wall — composed off the record, present fully formed in April 2025, and quoted by everything after them.
4. **His own statements, in his own words.** The tiered account of where the book stands (`the book's current shape.md`), the decisions register, and every statement dated to him in the notes.
5. **The thinkers' own works, read directly.** This is the entry a session is most likely to miss. The corpus's characterization of Gendlin is corpus; *Focusing* is primary. A chair written from a consulted work records what the vault expects a thinker to hold, which is not the same as having read them hold it — the condition already stated at `ops/manuscript readiness.md`, condition 2.

Entry 5 makes the reading queue this directive's first action rather than a separate task queued behind it. Reading the primary works of the thinkers the book leans on *is* the move from the corpus's account of the essences to the essences.

## Rules

1. **Where the corpus and the primary material conflict, the primary material governs, and the conflict is recorded rather than silently resolved.** A concordance row or a tension note, by the standing test in `CLAUDE.md`.

2. **"Imperfectly potentiated" positions the corpus; it does not weaken any particular passage.** The phrase is his and it is about the whole relation, not a licence to discount a quotation because it appears in a draft. A corpus passage is read exactly as `source standing.md` says to read it. What changes is that no amount of corpus material settles what the research is for.

3. **A session states which side of the line its evidence comes from.** The vault's citation practice already does most of this — `poems #5` and `abridged ch6` are visibly different kinds of anchor — but where a claim's whole weight rests on the corpus's account of something that exists outside the corpus, the substantiation says so.

## The risk this creates, and the guard

The corpus is still the only written statement of most of the book, and it is what a drafting session actually has to hand. A directive that positions it as imperfect can be misread as a reason to stop extracting from it, which would empty the pipeline in service of a principle the author did not state. He said what the research is about. He did not say the corpus is not worth reading — he said it renders something else.

The guard is the writing test, unchanged (`writing test.md`): material earns its place by supplying something writing can use. The corpus supplies a great deal of that and continues to. This directive changes which of two things a session reaches for when they disagree, and it changes what a session treats as the ceiling of the research rather than its floor.
