# craft evidence

This is an author directive, issued 2026-08-01 when the craft category was added, global and standing. The occasion was that three manuscript drafts entered `sources/` and the vault had no way to hold what they uniquely supply. Six of the seven source documents can only describe the book. The three drafts are the only documents in which the book meets a reader, and the only evidence that a move survives execution comes from them.

## What the category is for

A craft note records one mechanism by which the executed prose produces an effect, stated so that a future chapter can be built against it. The test is transferability: a craft note earns its place when a writer could apply what it records to a passage that does not yet exist.

## The governing constraint

Directive 4 of the derivation (`ops/derivation.md`) requires the vault to report the corpus's structure without relaying its self-evaluations. That directive is what makes this category workable rather than dangerous, and it produces one rule that governs every craft note.

**Record the mechanism, never the verdict.** The drafts and the treatment praise their own moves at length. "The most-underlined page in the book" (arc-gloss: ch6), "already written and perfect" (arc-gloss: ch11), and "un-improvable" (arc-gloss: ch6) are the manuscript grading itself, and they are worth nothing as evidence. Underneath several of them there is a mechanism that can be stated independently and checked against the text: that every molecule formula containing a minus sign yields an instruction while every formula without one yields only a description is a property of the prose, verifiable by reading the catalogue, and it holds whether or not any page was underlined. The first is excluded; the second is the note.

A practical consequence: a craft note that cannot state how its claim would be checked is not yet a craft note. Extent, position, person, sentence length, the presence or absence of a device, and the genre of an instruction are all countable. Force, beauty, and depth are not. A turn of phrase is itself harvestable when the phrasing is the mechanism: the sentence quoted and anchored, with the move it performs named, is a checkable record. What the rule excludes is the verdict on the phrase, never the phrase.

## Authorship conditions

The Arc and the Gloss treatment carries a second kind of material under the headings "undercurrent" and "the meta": standing observations about the author's relation to the manuscript, several of them marked as addressed to the author rather than to a reader. These are evidence about the conditions under which the book gets written, and the vault holds them because they bear on which chapter can be finished next.

They carry their own handling rule, which follows from the same directive. **An authorship condition is quoted as the author's own recorded self-assessment and is never restated as the vault's judgment of the author.** The vault may report that the treatment records a condition, quote it, and note where the executed draft corroborates or contradicts it. The vault does not assess the author, infer motive, or advise. Where a recorded condition can be checked against the drafts — a chapter the treatment calls unfinished can be measured — the check is reported as a measurement and nothing more.

## Relationship to the other policies

The prose register (`prose register.md`) governs how a craft note is written: complete sentences, statement above substantiation, quotes marked and anchored. Source standing (`source standing.md`) governs what a craft note may rest on: craft evidence comes from draft sources by definition, since only a draft executes, and a draft's argument for its own merits is never the evidence. The admission standard in `notes/methods.md` applies without relaxation.
