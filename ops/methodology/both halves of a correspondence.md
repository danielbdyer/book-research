# both halves of a correspondence

This is a methodology correction, issued 2026-08-05 after the author asked whether the recent notes were as precise as the register asks. They were not, and the defect was not the one either of us expected. The prose tics were real and countable. Beneath them sat something that changes what the notes are worth.

## The measurement

Thirteen notes written between 2026-08-05 morning and evening each assert a correspondence: a construct the book states operationally already exists, under its own name, in a named literature. Marcia's foreclosure, Minuchin's enmeshment, Reich's character armor, Rogers's conditions, Maslow's plateau, Leary and Zinberg's set and setting, Lewin's action research, Erikson's moratorium, Dewey and Kolb's cycle, Aristotle's *steresis*, Wierzbicka's primes, Burke's screens, Whitehead's occasions.

Every quotation in all thirteen is from the corpus. Not one of them quotes the thinker.

The Reich note quotes the outward survey on hysteresis and the abridged edition on the Gatekeeper, and gives Reich's construct in the vault's own words. The Maslow note quotes `abridged ch12` and `walked IX`, and paraphrases the plateau correction that is the whole reason he is there. The Marcia note quotes `abridged ch6` twice, and states Marcia's four identity statuses without a word of his.

## Why it matters more than the tics

A correspondence claim says two texts say the same thing. Showing one of them asks the reader to take the match on trust, and the reader who most needs to check it is the author, who is deciding whether to cite somebody. The register already forbids this and the rule was applied in only one direction: rule 4 requires the corpus to be quoted and never impersonated, and rules 5 and 6 require exact referents and evidence carried in the sentence. Applied to an isomorph, those rules govern **both** halves. A paraphrase of the outside half is the same failure as a paraphrase of the corpus half, and it is harder to notice because the note looks well-evidenced — it is full of anchored quotations, all of them from one side.

The fluency risk the vault already records is the mechanism: a well-formed note reads as true because it is well-formed. A one-sided correspondence note is the most fluent possible form of an unchecked claim.

## The rule

**A note asserting that the book's construct is somebody else's shows both halves, or names the half it cannot show and says why.**

Three ways to satisfy it, in order of preference:

1. **Quote both.** The corpus with its anchor, the thinker with work and location. This is available whenever the primary work has been read.
2. **Quote one and address the other.** Where the primary work has not been read, do not paraphrase into a resemblance. Give the claim a location a reader can go to — work, year, and the part of it where the construct is defined — and state the construct in the vault's own plain words rather than in a wording that might be mistaken for the thinker's.
3. **Say the correspondence is unchecked.** One sentence in the note, not buried in a closing paragraph about what the reading must settle: which half is quoted, which half is not, and that the match is therefore proposed rather than established.

**Never quote from memory to close the gap.** A fabricated or half-remembered quotation attributed to a real thinker is worse than any paraphrase, and this vault has no way to verify one. Where a formulation is famous enough to be tempting, that is exactly the case where the temptation should be refused.

## What this does not require

It does not require reading the primary work before writing the note. A chair written from secondary knowledge is legitimate and the vault has forty of them; what is not legitimate is a chair that reads as though the reading has been done. The difference is one visible sentence.

It also does not apply to a claim about the corpus alone, or to a chair that records what a thinker holds without asserting that the book holds it too. The rule attaches to the correspondence, not to the thinker.

## The check

None exists yet, and the naive form would fire on every interlocutor note. What is available now is a reading discipline rather than a query: when a note's finding is *these two say the same thing*, count the quotations by side before the note is committed. The measurement above took one script and found thirteen; the same script would have found them before they were written.
