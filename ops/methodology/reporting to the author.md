# reporting to the author

This is an author directive, issued 2026-08-05. His instruction: "Keep ensuring you give full context as you write as we've talked about in our research repository guidance… and now ensure you extend that to me too a little more."

## The asymmetry it corrects

`ops/methodology/prose register.md` governs what a session writes into the vault, and it is demanding: complete sentences with named subjects, the statement leading and the substantiation beneath it, exact referents, no shorthand carrying an assertion alone, and rule 11 — a tired friend should be able to follow the writing on first reading.

Every one of those rules was written for a reader who arrives with no context, because that is what a future session is. None of them was applied to what a session says to the author, on the tacit assumption that he carries the context already.

He does, for the book. He does not, for the vault. **The vault's working vocabulary is the pipeline's invention, not his** — concordance row, tension note, chair, load, hole, seat, the sort, the grid, `[e]`, treatment under trial, the crossing. He authored the book those terms describe; he did not author the terms. A session that uses them as handles in conversation is doing to him precisely what the register forbids doing to a future session: using a word as a referent before the referent has been given.

The failure that produced this directive was exact. A /rethink proposal was reported as "Draw the concordance-row versus tension-note line: a difference between two states of one element is a row; a disagreement that would change what the book is earns a note." That sentence states a rule and nothing else. It does not say what either destination is, what each one buys, what goes wrong when the sorting is left to judgment, or what would change if the rule were adopted. It was legible to the session that wrote it and to nobody else, and the author said so.

## The rule

What a session writes to the author is held to the same register as what it writes into the vault, with one addition that follows from the asymmetry above.

1. **A term is given its referent before it is used as a handle.** The first appearance of any vault-internal term in conversation says what it names. This is the register's exact-referents rule applied to a reader who has the book in his head and not the apparatus.
2. **A finding is reported with what produced it.** Where it came from, what was actually measured or read, and what was inferred rather than found. The evidential rule does not stop at the repository boundary.
3. **A proposal states the problem before the answer.** What is currently going wrong or unhandled; one real instance, quoted or pointed at, of each option under consideration; what changes if it is adopted and what continues if it is not. A proposal reported as its conclusion alone asks him to approve a sentence rather than a decision.
4. **The strength of the evidence is stated with the recommendation.** Two independent events and one observation with a single data point are different situations, and a report that ranks them together has hidden the thing he most needs in order to decide.
5. **Compression stays required.** This is not a licence to lengthen. The register's fourth rule holds — the sources are highly styled and their style is contagious — and a long report is easier to write than a clear one. What is being asked for is context, not volume.
6. **In conversation, rule 12 of the register binds hardest.** Added 2026-08-05, from the author's correction of the same day: indirection — meaning made by gesture, reference, or compression rather than fully explicated — is a tic of the same standing as the ones the register already names. A note's wiki links at least resolve; a chat reply's references cannot be opened at all, so a sentence spoken to him must stand entirely on what it says. Every pointer in conversation carries the content it points at, in a clause, or it does not appear.
7. **A reply is reread as its reader before it is sent, and the reread starts at the paragraph closes.** Added 2026-08-05, after a reply reporting the first primary reading ended a paragraph with "Recorded on the chair, not resolved." Five words, three faults at once: no subject and no verb; a vault-internal term used as a handle; and an ending compressed to sound finished rather than to say something. The author's instruction was that this register never appear in conversation again, and that the fix cost no quality — and the two demands turn out to be one, because the fragment was not compressing its content, it was withholding it. The full sentence that should have been written — "I recorded the disagreement in Gendlin's evaluation note and left it open, because seating decisions are the author's and only one side has been read" — is longer by a few words and richer by the reason. The record shows where the faults concentrate: at the last sentence of a paragraph, where a summary cadence reaches for a clipped close, and in replies written directly after hours inside the vault's files, which carry the vault's vocabulary outward unless it is translated at the boundary. So the practice has a named moment and a named order: before sending, reread the reply as him; check the last sentence of every paragraph first; confirm every statement has a subject and a verb; unfold or drop every vault-internal word. This rule exists because four prior corrections described the fault and did not name when to catch it.

## What this does not change

The author's own instructions about brevity, and his two corrections of 2026-08-03 — first against ornate prose, then against the over-correction into clipped density — govern here as they govern in the vault. Rule 11 is the test in both places: a tired friend could follow it on first reading. This directive says that he is the tired friend as often as a future session is, and that the vault had only ever been writing for the other one.
