# source standing

This is an author directive, issued 2026-08-01 on the admission of three manuscript drafts, global and standing. The occasion was the arrival of documents that differ in kind from the four the vault was built on. The four founding sources — the atlas, the walked document, the first telling, and the space-around survey — describe a book. The three added on 2026-08-01 are the book, in three states. A rule that treats both kinds alike would let the drafts' own advocacy for their ideas enter the vault as evidence that those ideas are right.

## The three standings

1. **Descriptive.** A descriptive source states what the book holds, how it is built, and where its strains are. It speaks about the manuscript from outside it. The four founding documents have this standing. A descriptive source is evidence about the project's current position.

2. **Draft.** A draft source is a state of the manuscript itself — prose written to be read by a reader of the finished book, or a treatment written to plan that prose. The three documents added 2026-08-01 have this standing. A draft is evidence that the book *has said* a thing, in a particular way, at a particular stage. It is not evidence that the thing is right, and its own arguments for its merits carry no weight the same arguments would not carry from any other book.

3. **Primary.** Added 2026-08-03 with the author's poems. A primary source is the author's own prior work, upstream of the book, which the corpus quotes and descends from. It asserts nothing about the book; the datum is the book's use of it, and the direction of quotation is the standing's whole point — for material predating the book, a shared phrase originates in the primary source. The author's flag governs exceptions (the sixteenth poem is book-era, and its flow runs the other way).

   **Amended 2026-08-08, in the author's words, holding the middle between two poles.** A session had read "primary" as licensing doctrinal inference — sweeping the poems for theses, counterweights, and a second verification standard — and the author backed that use out the same day: "I don't feel the poems should be used as a primary source here." His refinement, issued when the back-out risked reading as demotion: "I don't want to overcorrect on this. What I do align with is that the poems are important to the book. And, they are not in the same class of contribution as the research itself. They are a part of the idiosyncrasies of the book for me, as a part of my offering, for now." The standing therefore has three faces, and a session holds all three at once. The poems keep their provenance face: the direction-of-quotation rule above is untouched, and a shared phrase still originates in the poem. The poems gain a named offering face: they are part of what the author gives the reader — the book's idiosyncratic, personal layer — and work that treats them as unimportant misreads them. And the poems do not have an evidence face: they do not ground doctrinal inferences, counterweights to the corpus's claims, or verification standards the way the research — readings of thinkers' own works, and extraction from the corpus — does. The closing "for now" is his and is kept: the standing is held loosely, and revisiting it is his act.

   **Thematic correspondence is inside the faces, by the author's same-day instruction.** He asked for the withdrawn poem material to return in fit form: "I think there can be some ways in which the themes of the poems do fit with the themes of the claims, so I'd like to bring that back too." Recording that a theme the claims already state is present in the poems — as [[all six doctrine concerns appear in the first fifteen poems]] does for the six doctrine concerns — is provenance-and-offering work: it says what the poems hold and where a drafting session can reach for the personal layer beside a claim. What stays out is narrower and stays out cleanly: a thesis the claims do not state entering the graph on the poems' authority.

The standing is recorded in each archive note and named in the source registry in `notes/methods.md`. Where a claim's evidence is drawn from a draft, the substantiation layer says so.

**Amended 2026-08-05.** The author stated that the research "is and has always been about the primary material, and not the sources I've provided which indicate an imperfectly potentiated form of the essences I really care about." The three standings above are unchanged as rules for reading evidence. What the amendment adds is a relation above them: the corpus is downstream of what the research is about, so the direction-of-flow rule that standing 3 states for the poems holds generally, and no quantity of corpus material settles what the book is centrally concerned with. The directive is `ops/methodology/the primary material.md`; its most operational consequence is that a thinker's own work is primary and the corpus's account of that thinker is not.

A third kind of document was considered for a standing of its own on 2026-08-02 and routed elsewhere instead: a reconstruction of how the corpus was made, commissioned by the author from the conversation record. It is testimony about the corpus rather than a document of it, and the vault already had a category that fits — a work consulted, quoted inside the notes that use it, with no archive note and no entry in `sources/`. The rule that decided it: a document needing a new standing before it can be admitted usually belongs somewhere the vault already has. By author decision of 2026-08-03 the four reconstruction documents are physically held in `provenance/` — persisted, not promoted; they remain consulted works, and their personal material is governed by `ops/methodology/personal provenance.md`.

## Rules

1. **A draft is quoted for what it says, not credited for what it argues.** The drafts argue at length for the framework they present, in a register built to persuade a reader. A claim may record that a draft makes an argument, and where; the claim does not inherit the argument's force. This is the standing extension of the prose register's fourth rule (`prose register.md`): the corpus is quoted, never impersonated.

2. **Agreement across documents counts only when the documents are independent.** The atlas and the walked document both name "the Arc & Gloss (2nd ed.)" among the threads they were compiled from (atlas colophon; walked colophon). Where those three agree, one document is being read three times. The vault does not describe such agreement as convergence, corroboration, or independent arrival. The claim recording the compilation relation is [[the atlas was compiled from the arc and gloss treatment]].

3. **Where drafts disagree, the disagreement is the finding.** The three drafts differ on the triad's corner names, on the grammatical person the book speaks in, on the scope and sequence of the chapters, and on how much of the chapter-one poem is printed. Each divergence is recorded as evidence about an unsettled question, and none is silently resolved by preferring the latest, the longest, or the best-written state. The author settles them, in writing; the vault holds them open.

4. **Recency is not authority, and neither is completeness.** The abridged edition is the most finished object in `sources/`, and one of its central terms was subsequently vetoed by the author (see [[the prose drafts use the vetoed triad names]]). The mythic operating system edition is the least finished and carries a chapter the author names a high-water mark of voice. A draft's stage tells the vault where the document sits in the sequence, not how much of it survives.

## Relationship to the admission standard

The admission standard in `notes/methods.md` is unchanged and applies to draft material without relaxation: a passage becomes a claim only if it would actually change how the project thinks. The drafts are long and their prose is designed to carry a reader forward, which makes the fluency risk named at [[felt shift versus felt confirmation]] sharper here than in any of the founding documents. The test to apply to a draft passage is whether it states something the vault did not already hold, not whether reading it was persuasive.
