# reference by name

This is an author directive, issued 2026-08-01 after the transfer-surface pass, global and standing. The occasion was that the vault had begun referring to the book's material by chapter number — "chapter 6 supplies the formula," a concordance whose rows ran one through fifteen — and the author identified the bias that produces: a numbered reference asserts that a settled chapter progression exists. It does not. The multi-step arc of the psychedelic experience is what the author holds; the chapter sequence that arc has been fitted into is not yet settled, and the sources disagree about it. The mythic operating system edition's contents propose an ordering unlike the one the fifteen-chapter arc uses, and the vault must be able to hold both without confusion about what is being discussed.

## The rule

**The referent is the named unit.** A named unit is one of the book's main topic-ideas considered as a piece of the manuscript, referred to by its name: the House with No Ceiling, the Molecules of Experience, the Third Entity, the Stance, the Kata, the Psychedelic Moment. The name is stable across every table of contents the corpus contains. The number is not — it changes with the scheme, and in one scheme the unit is a page rather than a chapter at all.

Three consequences follow.

1. **Statements name the unit.** Vault prose says "the molecules material states the formula," not "chapter 6 states the formula." Where a unit's name is genuinely unknown or the source discusses a position rather than a topic, the sentence says so rather than reaching for a number.
2. **Numbers are citation locators and appear only in source anchors.** `abridged ch6` remains the correct anchor, because it tells a reader where to look in a specific document. The anchor is a coordinate in one file; it is not the name of the idea. A sentence that carries a proposition names the unit, and the anchor beside it supplies the location.
3. **Note titles never lead with a number or a count** where a name would serve. A count that distinguishes two documents from each other is a document descriptor and is permitted in prose; a count standing in for the content of a unit is not.

## What this does not forbid

Ordering claims remain fully available and are among the most useful things the vault holds. The arc's shape — what precedes what, where the peak falls, what the descent does — is the author's live commitment, and a claim about sequence is a claim about named units in an order. "The ripening material precedes the peak in the fifteen-chapter arc and is absent from the earlier contents" is a sequence claim stated correctly. "Chapter 10 precedes chapter 11" is the same claim stated in a way that will be wrong the moment the scheme moves.

Nor does it forbid reporting a source's own numbering. Where a document numbers its chapters, the vault may record that it does; the record is a fact about the document.

## Application

Applies forward, and was applied retroactively on 2026-08-01 to every note produced by the draft harvest and to `ops/chapter concordance.md`, which was re-keyed so that the named unit is the row and each document's placement is a column. The concordance's purpose depends on this: it exists so that the corpus's several tables of contents can be commingled at will, which is possible only when the rows are named rather than numbered.

The prose register (`prose register.md`) already requires exact referents, and this directive specifies which referent is exact for this material. The vocabulary policy (`vocabulary policy.md`) requires the reference surface to be denotative, and a unit's name denotes its concern where a number denotes only a position in one arrangement.
