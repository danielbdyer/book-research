# writing test

This is an author directive, issued 2026-08-02, global and standing. The author's sentence, which this directive operationalizes: pay attention to that which will actually satisfy future writing, and not which will supply our attention with more work to do.

## The test

At every admission decision, ask: **would a drafting session reach for this?** A note earns its place by supplying something writing can use — a claim to state, a figure to quote, a phrase whose move is named, a mechanism to build against, a fact that decides a manuscript question. The prior standard, "would it change how the project thinks," remains the floor; this is the bar above it.

Three sorting consequences:

1. **Material that only describes the vault's own state is operational, not a claim.** It routes to `ops/` — observations, the queues, the concordance, the inventory — and does not enter `notes/`.
2. **Analysis of an absence is worth one seed, not an essay.** Where the corpus names a territory and supplies nothing, the record is the proposition, the strongest fact, and the nearest held material. The work of filling the gap belongs to the queues.
3. **A finding's payload leads.** The quotable equation, the measured number, the working phrase sits at the top of the note; provenance caveats and standing policies are cited, not re-argued per note. The policies are global so that the notes do not have to carry them.

## The failure this guards

The derivation's highest-rated risk is vault work displacing manuscript work. This directive names the mechanism by which that happens invisibly: each meta-artifact is individually justified, and the sum is an archive that describes itself. The test catches it at the unit of one note, where the sum is made.
