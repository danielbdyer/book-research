# naming and epistemic humility

This is an author directive, issued 2026-08-02, global and standing. It extends `vocabulary policy.md` from the reference surface to prose, and it adds an epistemic clause the earlier directive does not carry.

## The decision

The author's sentences, which this directive operationalizes:

> The Guild, the Doors, the Stance, the Gatekeeper, and other such symbolic phrases are entirely unlikely to be epistemically necessary to persist as an ongoing referent. It would be better to be concrete about it.

> Nothing, and I say *nothing* about the book is inherently decided upon, even to its foundations — so we need to treat everything in equal measure as its own foray into the world, both with reverence and epistemic humility, letting the thinkers guide the way instead of our own constellation names being the subject material of interest.

> It's perfectly fine to catalog and observe, but let's not get over attached to the base point of view being the extension point — it is one level beneath that, in truth.

Three rules follow.

**1. The concern leads; the corpus's name is attributed.** A figure or coinage from the corpus is a label the project put on a concern, not the concern. Vault prose states what is being talked about in plain terms and, where the crosswalk earns its place, records the corpus's name once with its source: "the Kashmir Śaiva tradition, which the corpus calls the valley (atlas §IX.6)." The name never carries the sentence alone. A note whose title needs a corpus coinage to be readable is a note that has not yet identified its own subject.

**2. A name is a candidate until the author settles it, and the author has settled none.** Where a genuinely new concern has no historical prior and a noun is the honest way to hold it, naming it is right — and it is recorded as a candidate name, held lightly, with the concern stated independently of the name so that a later renaming costs nothing. This is the reference directive's reasoning applied one layer down: numbered reference was banned because it asserts a settled chapter progression that does not exist, and confident naming asserts a settled vocabulary on the same terms. The book's foundations are not exempt.

**3. The extension point is one level beneath the naming layer.** New material attaches to findings and to the people who reached them, not to the constellation the project has drawn over them. When a thinker's contribution can be stated either as what they found or as which of the corpus's rooms they sit in, the finding is the vault's version and the room is a cataloguing convenience. Cataloguing and observing the naming layer is legitimate work; treating it as the surface new work grows from is the error this rule names.

## Three exemptions, and why each one holds

1. **Quotations.** The corpus is quoted verbatim and anchored. A quotation carrying "the Guild's one law" keeps its words; the vault's own sentence around it does not adopt them.
2. **Source anchors.** `arc-gloss: the five rooms` and `atlas §IX.6` are coordinates in a document, exactly as `abridged ch6` is. They locate evidence and are never renamed, on the same reasoning that keeps chapter numbers alive inside anchors while banning them from prose (`reference by name.md`).
3. **Image-category claims and `notes/figure glossary.md`.** These exist precisely to carry the figures; there the figure is the subject and the note is the crosswalk. An image claim titled with a figure is the directive working, not failing.

## What this does not license

It does not license paraphrasing the corpus into blandness, dropping a figure the book earns, or deciding on the author's behalf that a name is wrong. The figures stay in the graph with their sources. What changes is which layer the vault's own prose speaks from, and how firmly any name is held.

## The failure this guards

A vocabulary that is only ever used becomes a vocabulary that is never examined. Once the vault's prose refers to a concern exclusively by the project's own coinage, the coinage stops being a proposal about the world and becomes the furniture the research is arranged on — at which point the question of whether it is the right name can no longer be asked from inside. The guard is the same one the center-of-gravity directive applies to the system vocabulary, moved from the system to the whole naming layer, and applied without the exemption that directive granted to the book's foundations.
