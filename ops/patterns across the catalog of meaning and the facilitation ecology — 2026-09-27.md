# Patterns across four reports of 26 September 2026

This page is a reading of the catalog of meaning, the survey of facilitation techniques, and the survey's two supplements. It was written on 27 September 2026 and rewritten on 10 October 2026 under the author's rules for plain writing.

## Summary {#summary}

**This reading finds ten patterns that recur across the four reports, and it makes three proposals for the book; each proposal is the author's to accept or reject.** The next section names the reports and defines the terms this page uses.

The ten patterns are listed here and described in Parts One to Three:

1. In every field the reports surveyed, a helper mostly supplies conditions for a change, and the person or system being helped makes the change itself.
2. The fields mostly agree on that point, and their main disagreement is about who interprets a change and decides what comes next.
3. A protective response that has outlasted its cause decreases more reliably when safety is signaled than when force is used.
4. Helping changes, exhausts, or replaces the helper in every living case gathered; only a chemical catalyst is left unchanged.
5. The same help benefits one person and harms another, depending on each person's state, so a helper has to judge that state, and a text cannot judge the state of the person reading it.
6. Documented harm concentrates where a helper has the authority to confirm whether a person's change is real.
7. Much of what looks like a person's own difficulty is produced by the structure around them, and whether a change lasts depends largely on the person's surroundings.
8. The work that determines whether a change happens and lasts is done before and after the moment of change, and the vault (this research repository) has the fewest notes on the time after.
9. In the corpus (the seven documents that describe or draft the book), in the vault, and in the research tools, quotations and attributions had changed from their sources, and comparing them with the original text corrected them.
10. Many apparent agreements among teachers come from one shared teacher or lineage, and the independent agreements are between fields that could not have influenced each other.

The three proposals are described in Part Four:

1. Book II, the planned book for a reader working alone, could direct its reader to people the reader already knows. In the evidence gathered, an existing relationship reduced the response to threat and the presence of a stranger did not, and the abridged edition, the latest draft of the book, already gives one instruction of this kind: "Think of someone who truly sees you" (p. 7). The evidence is two laboratory studies, one event in the author's life, and his own practice as a facilitator. The research on whether remembering a person has the same effect has not been read.
2. The author's word "sufficiency" means that a person is already enough, and "sovereignty," a word introduced by the language model in the reframe conversation, means a person's authority over what their experience means and over what they become. Sufficiency may name what the fields agree on (pattern 1), and sovereignty may name what they disagree on (pattern 2). This is an interpretation of the reports, not a finding in them.
3. The book's arc is the six phases it takes its reader through: preparation, onset, deepening, peak, descent, and integration. The return is the last part of the arc, in which the reader goes back to ordinary life. The evidence indicates that the lasting part of a change happens in the return (patterns 7 and 8), the vault has the fewest notes on it, and the author's poems contain material for it.

The author's next steps are all optional:

1. Decide whether "sufficiency" and "locus of control" (your words) or "sovereignty" and "locus of inquiry" (the language model's words) name the book's central subject better, or whether all four stay (the catalog's fourth step; proposal 2 adds one consideration to it).
2. Write a paragraph about one group you facilitated: what you set up, what you watched for, and what you noticed (the catalog's first step; patterns 4 and 5 concern this work).
3. Decide whether poem #16's "cathedral of care" belongs in Book I, the planned book for people who facilitate others (the catalog's third step; pattern 4 concerns it).
4. Write a paragraph from your own life about returning to ordinary life after something changed (new in this reading; proposal 3 concerns it).

The catalog's other two steps are unchanged: finishing the sentence that the drafting document on recognition (`drafts/recognition — a surface to write from.md`) leaves blank, and stating in one sentence what Book I is for. Three smaller decisions are also the author's: whether to keep, revise, or remove the sentence in *The First Telling*, a description of the book written as letters to a friend, that calls the witness a catalyst (pattern 4); whether he has met Steve March's coaching school named Aletheia; and whether his directive on the book's central subject (`ops/methodology/center of gravity.md`) keeps "the polyvagal work" as its example of established outside knowledge, now that 39 specialists have called the theory untenable (`ops/facilitation ecology — six surrounding territories.md`, section 10). The catalog paired its steps with the author's rule for writing in poem #11: "(only write from the now / right about now lest / you leave behind a part of / yourself in a place you cannot see)."

## Part One. What the fields agree on {#part1}

### 1. Helpers mostly supply conditions for a change {#p1}

**In every field the reports surveyed, a helper mostly supplies conditions for a change, and the person or system being helped makes the change itself.**

#### Evidence

- The territories supplement found the pattern in fields that define facilitation in their own terms, so in those fields it is not only a result of how the material was selected. In chemistry a catalyst cannot shift a reaction's equilibrium, and it can only speed the reaction: "the total rate (catalyzed plus noncatalyzed) can only increase in the presence of the catalyst and never decrease." For small proteins, "the chaperones do not appear to affect the final state of the protein." A nurse plant shelters a seedling, and the seedling does its own growing. The treatment manual of the Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies (MAPS) compares healing to a wound, where "the doctor does not direct or cause the healing that ensues." In her booklet, the coach Byron Katie writes, "No one can give you freedom but you," and the Indian teacher Ramana Maharshi wrote that God and the guru "will only show the way to release."
- The survey sorted techniques by their structure, and supplying conditions was among the most common structures both outside the vault (46 techniques) and inside it (56 items). The most common arrangement in the whole field is the one in which the facilitator shapes the process and the people supply the content, which describes 114 of the 299 outside techniques.
- The management supplement found that J. Richard Hackman's research places a team's effectiveness mainly in how the team is designed and supported, with coaching a smaller factor.
- The catalog found the same rule stated three times in the transcript, each time in the model's words. For the vault: "Nothing becomes part of the author's philosophy merely because the AI said it" (line 14414). For the book: "Create conditions for recognition without claiming sovereignty over what is recognized" (line 7113). For a facilitator: "The participant's recognition belongs to the participant" (line 1489). The catalog's eight directions for the books "follow one rule: the books offer something, and a person other than the author decides what becomes of it."
- The vault before 26 September states the pattern as the book's orientation: facilitation "stands two steps back from the outcome, tending the conditions for the reader's own facilitation rather than producing a transformation" (`ops/orientation.md`). The author said the same of himself in June 2026, in the vision brief he dictated for another project: "I build containers where things — people, ideas, a self — become more fully themselves" (`provenance/provenance of the corpus round two.md`, lines 15–16).

#### Exceptions

Every field surveyed contains cases in which the helper supplies more than conditions, so the pattern is a strong tendency and not a rule. The Spemann–Mangold organizer in an embryo changes what neighboring cells become, prions impose a misfolded shape on normal proteins, and goslings attach to the first moving object they meet. Ceremonial leaders in the psychedelic traditions supply the interpretation of the experience. In pain reprocessing therapy the clinicians tell the patient, with authority, that the pain is not a sign of injury. Among the coaches, Peter Crone and Tony Robbins supply a diagnosis and a confrontation. In management, several accounts of how a change becomes a person's own are ones in which "a superior grants the freedom and can withdraw it" (the management supplement, section 3.6).

#### Implication for the book

The book already follows the tendency: a vault note states that the book "can only be auxiliary to a sovereign reader" (`notes/the book can only be auxiliary to a sovereign reader.md`). The exceptions show that supplying meaning also works, in its own way and at its own cost, so the book's decision not to supply meaning is a choice among methods that work. The book could present it as a choice and not as the only possible method.

### 2. The fields disagree mainly about who interprets a change {#p2}

**Fields that differ on nearly everything else agree that the change is the person's own, and their main disagreement is about who decides what the change means and what comes next.**

#### Evidence

- The survey sorted every technique by who decides the content of the work, and it found the field spread across five positions, which the table below defines and counts.
- The management supplement found the same question published in 1958, when Robert Tannenbaum and Warren Schmidt arranged a manager's options along a line from the manager deciding alone to the group deciding within limits the manager sets.
- The territories supplement found the coaches at all five positions, from Katie's four questions, where the person decides everything, to Michael Singer's practices done alone, with Crone's and Robbins's moves at the position where the facilitator directs with authority. Psychedelic clinics withhold interpretation on purpose, and ceremonial traditions interpret with authority. The pointing-out traditions agree that no teacher supplies the awareness a student recognizes, and they disagree about whether a teacher confirms it.
- The survey compared the two forms the author is considering, a book that sets the order in which the reader meets things and a collection of practices the reader can use in any order, and found that "the two forms differ in who decides the sequence, and they need not differ in who decides the meaning" (section 11).

#### The positions of the book and of the author

The survey placed both on this scale. All 13 of the book's practice forms are at position E, with no facilitator present. Of the 25 practices in the author's own record, 15 are at position B, the most common position in the field (the survey, section 9).

| Position | Who decides the content | Outside techniques (299) | The author's practices (25) | The book's practice forms (13) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | The person decides all of it. | 22 | 5 | 0 |
| B | The facilitator shapes the process, and the people supply the content. | 114 | 15 | 0 |
| C | The facilitator offers content as an option. | 62 | 0 | 0 |
| D | The facilitator directs or interprets with authority. | 30 | 0 | 0 |
| E | No facilitator is present; a structure, a text, or a custom does the work. | 71 | 5 | 13 |

The counts are from the survey, section 6.

#### Exceptions

Some methods also disagree about what is facilitated. Robbins's events produce a heightened state on purpose, and David Deida's weekly circle of men friends assigns each man an act and a consequence. The management supplement records a failure particular to position B: consultation that is "power sharing on a pseudo-level," and "a request for views that, when disregarded, leaves people worse off than not asking" (section 6, disagreement 4).

#### Implication for the book

For the book, the main question this pattern raises is who interprets the reader's experience. A book can set the order of its chapters and practices, as a guided path does, and still leave the interpretation to the reader.

### 3. Signals of safety reduce protective responses more reliably than force {#p3}

**In persons and groups, a protective response that has outlasted its cause decreases more reliably when safety is signaled than when force is used.**

#### Evidence

- The territories supplement found it in laboratory studies, in psychedelic care, and in coaching. A husband's hand reduced married women's brain response to the threat of a shock, and in a later study of 110 people, "we did not observe any regulatory effects of handholding by strangers." In a survey of 529 people about difficult psychedelic sessions, the response rated most helpful was "I tried to let go, accept or surrender to the experience" (84.5 percent), and the rule of the Zendo Project, which trains volunteers to support people having difficult psychedelic experiences at festivals, is "Through, Not Down." Among the coaches, Thomas Hübl said of a participant's distance, "The distancing is part of your intelligence, it's not something that we want to get rid of"; Jerry Colonna says of his inner critic, "The crow's mission is to preserve your ability to be loved, to feel safe, and that you belong"; and Joe Hudson says, "if you can't love the thing, love the resistance to the thing."
- The management supplement found that the research on psychological safety, William Kahn's conditions for engagement, and Edgar Schein's account of the two fears in learning "each describe, in measured or itemized terms, the conditions under which people will say what is true in front of others" (section 1).
- The survey found moves at every scale that leave a person's protection in place, among them "asking permission before offering, by which a helper leaves the choice of hearing advice with the person" (section 7).
- The vault before 26 September records the pattern as doctrine: suffering is a contraction, almost always protective, and never a corruption of the person (`notes/suffering is contraction never corruption.md`).
- The abridged edition already uses this approach: "Think of someone who truly sees you." It continues, "The breath drops. Some grip you did not know you were holding releases. You become more yourself, not less, in the presence of being seen" (p. 7).

#### Exceptions

Methods that use force have adherents among the coaches the corpus cites: Robbins's "Let me show you where the God damn weeds are," Deida's agreed consequence for freezing, the Zen master's shout, and the firewalk. The measured evidence against force is narrow, and no study compares the two kinds of method directly. The management supplement also records that safety does not decide the direction of a change. Schein's term for the stage in which a person becomes ready to change is unfreezing, and he wrote that "unfreezing creates motivation to learn, but does not necessarily control or predict the direction of learning" (quoted in section 6).

#### Implication for the book

The book's account of suffering as a protective contraction agrees with this evidence. A book cannot signal safety by being present in person, and proposal 1 in Part Four describes what it can do instead.

### 4. Helping changes or costs the helper {#p4}

**A chemical catalyst is the only facilitator in the reports that is left unchanged by what it facilitates; in every living case gathered, the helper is changed, exhausted, or replaced as a result of helping.**

#### Evidence

- The territories supplement found that the phrase "never consumed" applies only to molecules. A catalyst is not used up by a reaction, but nurse trees die sooner because of the cactus they sheltered, and pioneer plants are replaced by the plants whose growth they made possible. Hübl says that a facilitator must keep doing their own integration work for life: "I need my personal integration, otherwise I cannot discern my own trauma and the collective trauma."
- The management supplement found burnout measured as "a prolonged response to chronic emotional and interpersonal stressors on the job," and Arlie Hochschild's research on emotional labor found that displaying a feeling a job requires is "more harmful to employee health" than coming to feel it (section 3.11).
- The catalog found this cost named in poem #16, one of the subjects in the poems that no list in the vault names (finding 4). The poem describes "a cathedral of care" built by someone who says "i am very good at taking care of people," and it states the cost: "and the cathedral is real / the cathedral has saved me more than once / and also — i am tired of paying its full invoice."
- The vault before 26 September describes burnout as "duty with the holding place removed" (`notes/burnout is obligation minus sanctuary.md`).

#### Exceptions

The survey has little on this pattern. None of its seven ways of sorting techniques records what a technique costs the helper, so the gap may come from how the survey was designed and not from the field.

#### Implication for the book

*The First Telling* describes the witness as "a catalyst that's never consumed, but without which nothing proceeds at all" (p. 9). That sentence applies a property of molecules to a relationship between people, and every living case gathered shows the opposite. Book I, written for people who help others, is the place to describe what helping costs the helper, and poem #16 already describes it in the author's words.

## Part Two. What a book read alone cannot do {#part2}

### 5. A helper has to judge each person's state, and a text cannot {#p5}

**The same help benefits one person and harms another, depending on the state each person brings, so the act that most affects the outcome is judging that state, and a text cannot judge the state of the person reading it.**

#### Evidence

- The territories supplement found it at every scale studied, from synapses to readers. Repeating "I'm a lovable person" made people with low self-esteem feel worse and people with high self-esteem slightly better. Expressive writing left recently separated adults who were searching for meaning worse off than neutral writing did. A text of the Kagyu school of Tibetan Buddhism calls the most direct method "powerful medicine" for people ready for it and "deadly poison for those who progress through graded stages," and a student of the Tibetan lineage writes that a teacher questions a student's report "to discern whether you're talking about a real experience or mostly mixing it up with what you've read or heard."
- The management supplement found that "more than one third of the feedback interventions in one large review lowered performance" (section 1), and it collected tests that distinguish real dissent from assigned dissent and felt certainty from accurate judgment.
- The survey stated what a facilitator at position B does that the book's forms at position E cannot: "What a facilitator at B does that a text cannot is to see the person now and adjust" (section 13). One of the moves it lists is tracking, in which "the facilitator continuously watches the person's present experience as it shows in posture, breath, voice, gesture, and small changes of expression" (section 7).
- The catalog stated the consequence for the two books: "Book II, read alone, can print the restraints, and Book I, written for the person who sits with another, can teach the discernment" (finding 5).
- The vault before 26 September had already found that "The discrimination is second-person without exception" (`ops/second person ecology.md`, section 4), meaning that telling a real change from a false one is always done by someone other than the person who changed.
- Poem #16 describes the skill in the author's own words: "i am very good at taking care of people / i am very good at listening carefully / at tracking what might land / at shaping myself around what will work."

#### Exceptions

A minority in several traditions gives the judgment to the person. Douglas Harding, who designed experiments for readers to do from a page, held that "you are the sole and final authority on you," Hübl told a participant "You know your process," and the Zen teacher Shin'ichi Hisamatsu taught that a person confirms their own awakening. In Zen the majority view, as Wikipedia summarizes it, is that "the risk of self-delusion is high."

#### Implication for the book

A book cannot distinguish one reader's state from another's, and four alternatives are available to it: printing the restraints a reader alone can use; stating the conditions under which it should be read, as the Kashmir Śaiva writer Kṣemarāja did when he addressed his summary of recognition to readers already touched by grace; giving the reader practices to use with other people, as the abridged edition does with Calling the Room, a practice in which the reader says aloud what is happening among the people present; and leaving the teaching of discernment to Book I.

### 6. Harm concentrates where a helper confirms whether a change is real {#p6}

**Where a helper has the authority to say whether a person's change is real, the documented harm concentrates; where the person keeps control of what is said and of the option to leave, it does not.**

#### Evidence

- The territories supplement found it in contemplative and clinical settings. An investigation that Rigpa, a Tibetan Buddhist organization, commissioned found, in Wikipedia's summary of it, that some students in its founder's inner circle "have been subjected to serious physical, sexual and emotional abuse by him." A student of the Zen teacher Joshu Sasaki reported, "Joshu Roshi told me I would never get enlightened if I thought about these things." The Zen teacher Bodhin Kjolhede recalls a teacher telling his disciples, "Just get Dharma transmission; once you get that, no one will be able to touch you." After a therapist's misconduct in one of its trials, MAPS wrote of its protections that "none of these measures were sufficient."
- The survey found that "Each of the four documented harms in the entries takes the content or the exit out of the person's hands," and that Open Space Technology's Law of Two Feet, which lets any participant leave any session, "is the explicit opposite, since it leaves the exit with each person" (section 13).
- The management supplement found consultation that is "power sharing on a pseudo-level" and accounts of self-direction in which a superior can withdraw the freedom granted (patterns 1 and 2).
- The catalog quoted the model's own warning: "'I am creating conditions for your authentic experience' can itself become an extraordinarily subtle form of authority" (the transcript, line 1805).

#### Exceptions

Confirmation also protects people. In a study of how Buddhist teachers assess students' distress, a teacher's judgment decided whether a student needed help, as when a practitioner was told, "No, it wasn't enlightenment because you were suffering too much."

#### Implication for the book

The book claims no authority to confirm anyone's recognition, and the vault describes it as auxiliary to the reader "rather than the source or the certifier" of the reader's recognition (`ops/orientation.md`). This removes the relationship in which these harms occurred, and it also removes the protection that confirmation gives. The book's description of itself could state both effects.

### 7. Whether a change lasts depends largely on the person's surroundings {#p7}

**Much of what looks like a person's own difficulty is produced by the structure around them, and a change lasts only where the person's surroundings allow it.**

#### Evidence

- The management supplement calls this the domain's "most consistent warning," found in the work of W. Edwards Deming, Christina Maslach, Fred and Merrelyn Emery, Isabel Menzies Lyth, and the blameless review of incidents: "much of what looks like a person's difficulty is produced by the structure the person works in" (sections 1 and 3.2). The corpus had stated it before the supplement was written: "you do not meditate your way out of a broken on-call rotation" (`walked` p. 13). Two meta-analyses of physician burnout measured the difference as one of size: changing the organization helped more, and work with the individual also helped.
- The territories supplement found it in studies of whether a change lasts. The psychologists Gregory Walton and David Yeager concluded that where a reader's context does not support the new way of thinking, "treatment effects are unlikely to persist." The neuroscientist Igor Branchi argues that raised plasticity in the brain acts "through permissive causality," with the person's context determining the direction of the change. A critique of the current emphasis on self-regulation states that "no amount of individual regulation will undo systemic harm." Several coaches turned toward social conditions in their later work: Singer asks "What can I do to help them pass gun laws or do whatever it is that needs to be done?", Hübl puts ending violence and reparation before group healing, and Colonna's book *Reunion* asks about "systems of oppression."
- The survey found that the vault's own research concentrates on the single person: "Of its 264 holdings, 100 work at the scale of one person alone. From three people outward it holds almost nothing" (section 1). Conflict, the future, and decisions are nearly absent from it.
- The vault before 26 September records the principle in a note: `notes/pathology belongs to conditions and capacity to persons.md`.

#### Exceptions

Work with the individual still helps, though less. A brief written intervention raised students' grades for three years, in surroundings that supported the new view.

#### Implication for the book

A book of inward practice cannot change its reader's surroundings. It can name the surroundings as a cause of difficulty, avoid blaming the reader for them, and direct the reader's attention to them. The vault's own research has the same limitation: it concentrates on the single person more than the evidence does.

### 8. Most of the work that determines a change happens before and after it {#p8}

**The moment of change is the part least under anyone's control; the work done before and after it determines whether it happens and whether it lasts, and the vault has the fewest notes on the time after.**

#### Evidence

- The territories supplement found it in the pointing-out traditions and in psychedelic care. A teacher's pointing-out instruction takes a moment, and the Tibetan teacher Patrul Rinpoche's instruction is that "until stability is attained" the practitioner keeps meditating. Orgyen Tobgyal Rinpoche, also a Tibetan teacher, says of more than a thousand people who received the instruction at once, "Yet I later met only a few there who truly recognized their own nature." Psychedelic guides put their effort into screening, building trust, and preparation before a session, and into integration after it, which Geoff Bathje and colleagues describe as a process of "making sense of, working through, translating, and processing" what happened.
- The management supplement records Schein's caution that unfreezing does not decide the direction of learning (pattern 3).
- The survey found the same sequence in rites of passage, which move a person "from the community into a time apart and back into the community in a new standing" (section 11).
- The catalog found that "Integration is the thinnest topic in the design and the return the thinnest stage in the vault," and that "What happens when nothing happens has no catalog" (finding 6).
- The vault before 26 September found that "Book II's return is the thinnest position in the whole graph" (`ops/scaffold.md`, section 14).
- The poems contain material for the return. Poems #1, #4, #7, and #16 each describe home, and poem #16 says "the home where people still say my name / like they mean it"; poem #12 repeats "beginning again" five times; and poem #16 ends with "returning weather."

#### Exceptions

In two recorded cases the moment of change is most of what the record contains: the author's 4am reading of a decade-old letter, and the Zen teacher Huineng's awakening on hearing a sūtra chanted. The records say little about what came after either moment, so neither case shows whether the change lasted without later work.

#### Implication for the book

Two of the arc's six phases, descent and integration, cover the time after the moment of change, and the vault has the fewest notes on them. The evidence indicates that the lasting part of a change happens in that time, and the author's poems contain material for writing it.

## Part Three. Accuracy of wording and attribution {#part3}

### 9. Quotations and attributions had changed from their sources {#p9}

**In the corpus, in the vault, and in the research tools, wording had changed from its source, and comparing it with the original text corrected it.**

#### Evidence

- The territories supplement found five cases. The corpus credits "State before story" to Tony Robbins, and the phrase appears in the writing of Jonny Miller, who teaches a course on the nervous system, while the therapist Deb Dana writes "Story follows state." The terms set and setting were in use by 1958, before Timothy Leary popularized them. A vault note says the coaches Gay and Kathlyn Hendricks have "zero occurrences" in the corpus, and *The Arc and the Gloss* names them on its first page. The sentence the vault's note on plasticity quotes does not appear in the paper it cites. And the widely repeated claim that mature trees send resources to their offspring through fungal networks "has no peer-reviewed, published evidence."
- The management supplement found that the tool its researchers used to fetch pages had "silently altered" quotations; its corrections, which also removed claims the pages did not support, numbered 21. It also found an argument that the three-step model of change credited to Kurt Lewin is not his.
- The catalog reported the finding of pull request #23 that in at least seven places the vault presents the model's words as the author's. The most repeated case is "The recognition didn’t require the mirror," which the provenance record marks as the AI's and which nine files quote without saying so (finding 2). The catalog also found that the vault's central terms, "sovereignty" and "locus of inquiry," are the model's, and that the author's were "sufficiency" and "locus of control" (finding 9).
- The pointing-out traditions describe the same problem in their own terms. An attendant who copied his teacher's raised finger conveyed nothing: "Enlightenment, which Gutei and the boy attained, has nothing to do with a finger." The checking questions of the Rinzai school of Zen, which test a student's first insight, became "a standardised set of questions and answers," and their answer books were published in 1916.
- The vault before 26 September records the mechanism: "statements shown in easier-to-read contrast are judged true above chance on presentation alone" (`notes/the felt sense of truth has a documented counterfeit catalogue.md`).

#### The author's rules for plain writing

The author gave the vault its rule against mannered prose on 25 September 2026, and the catalog read that rule as "the books' own ethic stated at the scale of a sentence" (finding 3). On 10 October 2026 he added rules for stating the conclusion first and against sentences shaped like maxims, and this page was rewritten under them. Poem #16 states a similar preference for the plain sentence: "because the naked sentence is not hard / you matter to me / i miss you / i want to stay in the thread."

#### Exceptions

Some of the checks confirmed the vault. The figures that the vault's note on resistance cites from a synthesis it had not read were found in the primary studies, and the corpus's sentence about a broken on-call rotation stated the structural warning before the management research was gathered.

#### Implication for the book

The book should mark whose words each quotation is, check each quotation against the primary text, and use plain sentences, because an easy-to-read sentence can produce a reader's agreement that feels like recognition and is not.

### 10. Many apparent agreements come from one shared lineage {#p10}

**Many agreements among teachers turned out to be one lineage counted several times, and the independent agreements are between fields that could not have influenced each other.**

#### Evidence

- The territories supplement traced one agreement after another to a shared teacher. Jonny Miller's account of unclenching is close to Hudson's, and Hudson teaches a masterclass in Miller's course and has interviewed him, "so their agreement counts once." The Conscious Leadership Group names Gay and Kathlyn Hendricks as its teachers and reproduces Katie's four questions. John Wineland adapts Deida's teaching. The meditation teacher Loch Kelly names Tulku Urgyen Rinpoche as a primary teacher. The teachers Greg Goode and Rupert Spira belong to the lineage of the Advaita teacher Atmananda Krishna Menon. Hübl's own website names Ken Wilber as an influence from the age of twenty. By contrast, catalysis, plant ecology, the Kagyu principle of medicine and poison, and randomized trials of self-help texts share no history, and their findings bear on the same few patterns.
- The survey stated the rule as its first safeguard: "Gathering is not agreement" (section 12).
- The catalog recorded the model's error at line 3114 of the transcript, "It is something that becomes increasingly trustworthy because you've encountered it from multiple independent loci," which treats agreement as evidence.
- The vault before 26 September applies the rule to the corpus itself: two of its documents were compiled from a third, "so agreement among those three counts once, not three times" (`CLAUDE.md`).

#### Implication for the book

The thinkers the book cites agree most independently where their fields could not have borrowed from one another, and least independently among the contemporary coaches, who learned from each other. The book could state, for each thinker it cites, whether the agreement is independent or comes through a shared teacher.

## Part Four. Three proposals for the manuscript {#part4}

**The three proposals below are interpretations of the ten patterns, and each decision in them is the author's.**

### Proposal 1. Book II could direct its reader to people the reader already knows {#c1}

**Book II could address the fact that it is read alone by directing the reader to people they already know, because in the evidence gathered an existing relationship, and not the presence of a person, produced the effect.**

Patterns 3, 5, and 6 describe what a person who is present does: signals safety, judges the other person's state, and exercises or declines the authority to confirm a change. Book II has no person present. The vault records a disagreement about this between the view that the book needs a second person present and the view that the book is sufficient on its own (`notes/the solitary form stands against seven witnesses who hold a second person constitutive.md`). The evidence gathered on 26 September supports a third option:

- A husband's hand reduced the brain's response to threat, and in a later study a stranger's hand had no measurable effect, so the effect depended on the relationship and not on the presence of a hand.
- In the vault's record, the one confirmed occurrence of the event the book promises "happened at 4am, reading a decade-old letter from a beloved friend, alone" (`CLAUDE.md`). The text that produced it was a letter from someone who knew the reader.
- Poem #16 makes the same distinction: "love will sit in a drawer and wait for you," while "current is the ongoing update of who you are in the people who know you," and "i had been mistaking love preserved / for presence ongoing."
- Where traditions that require a teacher taught at a distance, they maintained a relationship with the student. The Chan teacher Dahui Zonggao wrote his letters to students he knew, and the Dzogchen teacher Chögyal Namkhai Norbu taught by live webcast at a set hour. Before he trained as a coach, Colonna gave a young lawyer a book and asked him to come back: "Here, read this. And then get back to me."
- The abridged edition already gives this instruction: "Think of someone who truly sees you" (p. 7). The author gave a similar prompt at an event he led in Seattle: "Think of someone who loves you. Describe how they see you." (`provenance/provenance of the corpus round two.md`, line 100). The management supplement found the nearest published counterpart, the Reflected Best Self Exercise of 2005, in which a person collects written stories of themselves at their best from people who know them. The abridged edition also tells a reader in crisis to use "Calling the Room with the people who can be called (do not isolate)" (p. 45), and *The Whole Field Walked* describes "the book designed as one half of a pair, its loneliest passages built to be read toward someone" (p. 25).

The proposal is that the instruction "Think of someone who truly sees you" become one of Book II's main methods instead of one instruction among many.

The evidence is two laboratory studies, one event in the author's life, and the author's own practice as a facilitator. Whether remembering a person has the effect that holding their hand has was not checked; the research on attachment priming, which tests whether reminding a person of someone who cares for them changes their response, is where that check would start, and it has not been read. The proposal does not settle the vault's recorded disagreement; it adds a third option to it. Some teachers do write for strangers with effect: Harding designed his experiments to be done from a page.

### Proposal 2. The author's word and the model's word for the book's central subject may name different things {#c2}

**"Sufficiency," the author's word, may name what the fields agree on, and "sovereignty," the model's word, may name what they disagree on.**

The catalog found two sets of words for the book's central subject (finding 9). The author's "sufficiency" and "locus of control" ask whether a person is already enough and where they stand in their own life. The model's "sovereignty" and "locus of inquiry" ask about authorship and about the position from which a person inquires.

- Sufficiency matches what the fields agree on (pattern 1): the change already belongs to the person, and help supplies conditions. The abridged edition's front matter says "You already know everything in this book." Ramana Maharshi asked, "Does he who is Rama require the help of a mirror to know that he is Rama?" The coaching school named Aletheia, taught in Jonny Miller's course, asks, "What if nothing is missing?" The abridged edition ends, "For now, what you have is enough. Step in." The author's own sentence in the transcript is "the more that you allow that sufficiency to exist... it's one of the core conditions for being able to enter into this sort of reality to begin with" (line 446).
- Sovereignty matches what the fields disagree on (pattern 2): who interprets a change, who confirms it, and who decides when to leave.

The catalog asked the author whether his words or the model's name the book's central subject better. Under this reading, the question becomes which of the two the book should state first. This is an interpretation, and the decision is the author's.

### Proposal 3. The return needs more material, and the poems contain some of it {#c3}

**The return is where the evidence indicates that the lasting part of a change happens, and it is the part of the arc the vault has the fewest notes on.**

Patterns 7 and 8 both concern the return. Whether a change lasts depends on the surroundings a person returns to and on the work done after the moment of change, and the vault's account of the book's structure calls the return "the thinnest position in the whole graph." The author's poems contain material for it: home, "beginning again," "current," and "returning weather." Both of the corpus's endings are part of the return: "Welcome home" in *The Arc and the Gloss* and the descriptive documents, and "Step in." in the abridged edition.

## Limits of this page {#limits}
