# The shelf rule tells a session to keep the candidate list long, and the author has cut it short

Raised 2026-08-07 by the candidate-list pruning the author directed that day. Recorded here rather than in `notes/` because the subject is the vault's own working rule and not anything the book claims; the routing table in `CLAUDE.md` sends a place where the vault contradicts its own methodology to this directory.

## The contradiction

`ops/methodology/sharp questions.md` carries a section called the shelf rule, and that section says the vault deliberately keeps a long field of candidates. It supports the statement with a quotation from the candidate list itself: "the list is deliberately long — its sixty-odd entries are the field, not the task" (`ops/queue/candidates.md`). Its stated principle is that the rule is labeling and not pruning.

On 2026-08-07 the author directed the opposite act. He had the list cut from ninety-six unevaluated names to twenty, each kept only with a stated reason and the question in the vault that it bears on, and sixty-one names were declined with their reasons recorded in `ops/queue/declines.md`. The sentence the directive quotes no longer stands in the file it quotes, and the standing instruction the directive gives — that length is deliberate and pruning is not the rule — now runs against what the author asked for.

## What still holds, and what does not

The part of the shelf rule that survives is its distinction. A name with a topic gloss is still not work, work is still what has been sharpened into the five properties of a queue entry, and the two kinds are still never mixed in one list without the difference being visible. The pruning did not blur that line; it left twenty entries that are still shelf entries rather than sharpened readings, and it moved nine names that had already been sharpened out to `ops/reading queue.md` as pointers.

The part that does not survive is the licence to let the list grow without limit. What the pruning added to every kept entry is a warrant — the live tension, the open question, or the unopposed claim that the name bears on — and that is a bar the old rule explicitly did not impose.

## What would resolve this

The author decides whether the shelf rule is amended, and a methodology correction reaches `ops/methodology/` through /remember rather than through a session's own judgment. Two shapes are available and they differ in strength. The narrow amendment replaces the quoted sentence and states that a name enters the list only with the vault question it would answer written beside it, which is the rule the pruning actually ran. The wider amendment would also say what happens when the list grows again, since nothing currently stops a research round from adding thirty names with no warrant attached. The evidence for the narrow version is one directive on one day; the evidence for the wider version is the growth pattern that produced ninety-six names, which is stronger but has never been counted round by round.

Until one of those is made, this file is the record that a session reading `sharp questions.md` will find an instruction the author has overruled in practice.
