## How an entry reads

Each entry carries four parts. **Q** is the question, stated so the reading can fail it. **Stake** is what changes in the vault on each answer. **Address** is where to read, at page level where known. **Standing** is what the vault currently holds and how good it is — the grade the reading is testing.

## Tier 1 — the verification standard's defense

The felt shift is the book's single test for whether anything is true, the author judges it underdefended, and every entry here inherits the central finding of the Gendlin reading of 2026-08-05: his 1964 paper certifies *movement* (that the process carried forward), not *truth* (that a content is so), which narrows what introspection must deliver from describing inner states to noticing change against no-change.

1. **Nisbett and Wilson.** Q: does their confabulation result reach a standard that only asks a person to notice change, given their own carve-out for reports of present states? The question can fail in the standard's favor, and that outcome is worth as much as the other. Stake: if the carve-out protects the narrowed standard, the vault's first-ranked objection loses its canonical citation and the defense rests on keeping the standard narrow; if it does not, the objection stands with its best evidence and the book's exposure is confirmed. One load the corpus places on the instrument is reached either way: the body is seated as referee between the five theories of where suffering lives, which is causal adjudication, not a present-state report — that use is inside their range regardless. Address: "Telling More Than We Can Know," *Psychological Review* 84(3), 1977, especially the section distinguishing content reports from process reports. Standing: characterized from the mesh grid only, cosigned 65–75%, unread.

2. **Schwitzgebel.** Q: does his unreliability claim cover bodily and interoceptive report specifically, or is it drawn mostly from vision, imagery and inner speech? Stake: if it covers interoception, the narrowed certificate is attacked at its remaining premise and the standard needs a calibration story; if it does not, the standard's smallest form survives both canonical objections and the defense is writable. Secondary question with its own stake: whether anything in his work supports trainability, since Gendlin's own teaching papers are the book's nearest answer. Address: *Perplexities of Consciousness* (2011), the chapters on the unreliability of naive introspection. Standing: grid characterization, unread.

3. **Katz, reseated.** Q: does his constructivism — the report is trained by the tradition that produces it — apply to a *discrimination* (change against no-change) as strongly as to a *description*? Stake: if yes, the felt shift confirms the book's training rather than anything beyond it, which is the deepest available version of the fluency objection; if no, his objection lands on the book's prose and not on its instrument, and [[felt shift versus felt confirmation]] absorbs it. Address: "Language, Epistemology, and Mysticism" in *Mysticism and Philosophical Analysis* (1978). Standing: evaluated 2026-08-03 at survey depth for a different question; this question is new and unread.

4. **Gendlin, the remainder.** Q: three questions left open by the 1964 reading — does *Focusing* (1978) state the life-sentence the corpus quotes, and in what surrounding claim; does the outcome research support the corpus's story that focusing predicted change (the 1968 focusing-ability paper reported the ability largely stable, which is why the teaching method exists); and does *A Process Model* strengthen or qualify the movement-certificate of the 1964 footnote? Stake: the corpus tells the research story three times; whether that story survives its own literature decides how the book may cite its single standard. Address: *Focusing* (1978; the sentence is commonly placed in the early chapters); Gendlin, Beebe, Cassens, Klein and Oberlander, "Focusing ability in psychotherapy, personality and creativity" (1968); *A Process Model* (1997/2018), chapters I–II. Standing: the 1964 paper read whole (`archive/reading — gendlin, a theory of personality change (1964).md`); the rest unread.

The claim at [[the privation move is an independent arrival across five fields rather than a book coinage]] turns on one sentence: that each field defines its construct *by absence, in its own words*. Standing: correspondence proposed from general knowledge, both chairs marked unread.
