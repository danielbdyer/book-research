# ecology — the diagnostics

This recipe is for an ecology survey, the vault's name for a study that sorts a collection of the book's material by what each item does; it is not the book's own Ecology of Recognition, the name the corpus gives its orientation.

## The collection

The practice-model survey sorted the book's material by how facilitation runs. This survey sorts the other half: the question each model puts to a difficulty before anything is done about it. The book's own diagnostic asks what a state is made of and what is missing ([[the diagnosis contains the cure]], [[distress is a question about machinery not a verdict about character]]). Landmark asks what the hurting pays. Hudson asks what is being resisted. The Forum asks what is story and what happened. Wilber's psychograph asks which line is undeveloped. Ignatius asks which spirit is moving and where the course of the thoughts ends. McLaren asks each emotion its internal question. Robbins asks which element of the Master System is set wrong. The five theories of suffering are five diagnoses before they are five therapies ([[the transformation field divides into five theories of where suffering lives]]), and the "subsume or beat" call the coaches force on the book is a call about diagnoses.

## The sorting axis

The author's own diagnosis of his own event, in his words: "a fundamental clenching that didn't obscure the vision but just made it transfigured as not directly perceivable." That is a diagnosis by shape, not by lack, and the book's own founding case for its theory of suffering ([[the book's privative grammar runs four different engines under one word]]). His reported first move at the retreat was separating "the story from what's actually happening," which is the Forum's own question. The axis for every diagnostic: what does it presuppose trouble is — an absence, a presence that pays, a resistance, a false content, a clench, an undeveloped capacity, a spirit, a story — and would his own case answer its question?
