# ecology — the addressees

## The collection

The book's documents do not all speak to the same person. The first telling is written as letters to one friend. The abridged edition addresses a reader with a reading contract. The earliest edition speaks past a fourth wall. The reframe's two books split the addressee in two: Book I speaks to a facilitator and Book II to the one whose big experience did not hold. The last line addresses the reader so that it can be answered. The poems address a beloved, a friend, two cats, and, in the sixteenth, the architecture the sentence leaves. The vault holds fifteen notes on address and never sorts the addressees by what each is asked to do.

## The sorting axis

The author's own addressees, on the record: the friend the first telling is written to; the beloved friend whose decade-old letter he read at four in the morning; the collaborator he curated and vetoed; the reader he refuses to be an authority over; and, in the reflection, "one observer of many possible observers" who imagines another author in another part of the world choosing a different roster. The axis for every addressee: who is spoken to, what they are asked to do, what they are trusted to already have, and what would count as their answer.

## Provisional families (proposals, not filings)

By what is asked: addressees asked for attention (the abridged reader under the reading contract); asked for an answer (the reader of the last line; the poem's over-against); asked to do the work themselves (the reader-facilitator; the sovereign reader); asked to be changed (the one whose experience did not hold; the author as first participant); asked for nothing, and spoken to because they exist (the friend of the letters; the beloved; the cats). By what they are trusted to have: everything already, forgotten (the front matter's reader); a reliable other once (the reader the book can reach); a body that can countersign; a life the book is auxiliary to. The hypothesis to test is that the corpus's documents differ in addressee more than in doctrine, and that the executed draft's addressee is the one the author trusts least.

## Empty cells

The vault has no note on the addressee of the poems as a set. It has no account of the reader the book cannot reach, beyond the one without a reliable other. It has no note on what the two books ask of the same reader who reads both. And it has no account of the author as the addressee of his own poems, which the sixteenth poem may be.

## Tensions it would consolidate

The two books' two addressees against one reader. The letter to a friend against the book to a stranger. The reader asked to answer against the book that cannot hear the answer. And the author as first addressee against the sovereign reader who owes him nothing.

## The reading program, in the sharp-questions form

- Does Iser's implied reader do the work the book assigns to a sovereign reader, or is the implied reader a construct the text controls? Fails if the implied reader is the text's. Stake: whether the book's addressee has a literary precedent or is a new position. Address: *The Implied Reader* (1974), the introduction; *The Act of Reading* ch. 2. Grade: named from the digs; unread.
- Does Bakhtin's superaddressee appear in the vault's answerability material, and does it change who the last line is written to? Fails if the answerability record already holds it. Stake: a third addressee above the reader. Address: "The Problem of the Text" (1959–61), in *Speech Genres*; the existing answerability record. Grade: the triad is read; the superaddressee is not cited.
- Does the first telling name its friend, and is the friend the same one whose letter was read at four in the morning? Fails if the corpus keeps them separate. Stake: whether the book's native genre and its one confirmed sighting share an addressee. Address: `.corpus/the first telling.md`, the salutations; `provenance/`. Grade: the two are held in separate notes.

## Safeguards

The four in the index, and the personal-provenance directive: the addressees include real people in the author's life, and the record's distances are kept (`ops/methodology/personal provenance.md`).
