# Outside the design: vault material the design does not cover, and how the design would be extended to include it

**What this document is.** `reframe-map-and-system-2026-09-25.md` designs, from the transcript of the reframe conversation alone, a list of the kinds of material the two books need. This document describes the material in the research vault that the design does not cover, and how the design would be extended to include it. It answers the second of three questions the author asked on 2026-09-25, in his words: "where the existing material goes beyond or outside of the system, and how the system expands to receive it." The first companion document, `system-and-vault-collation-2026-09-25.md`, sorts the vault's existing material into the design. The third, `system-and-vault-left-to-populate-2026-09-25.md`, lists the places the design defines that the vault does not yet fill. All three draw on the same eight readers' reports, which are kept with the readers' instructions in `ops/system readings — 2026-09-25/` and cited here by label (O1 to O4, F1 to F4). The collation's Part 10 gives the key to the labels.

**The rule it applies.** The design has one rule for everything it contains: each kind of material, each field, and each connection must name which function of the books it serves, or it is not built. This document applies the same rule to the vault material outside the design. Material that serves one of the books' functions becomes an extension of the design. Material that serves none belongs to the vault's own tools and records, which is a finding and not a fault. Some vault material does not extend the design but shows that the design is wrong, often because the author has already decided the question; those corrections come first.

**Its status.** A session wrote this document. Every extension in it is a proposal at the level "proposed," the lowest of the six levels that record the author's own relation to a piece of material, and every decision is the author's.

**Names used.** The design's kinds of material are topics, pairs, practices, images, encounters, traditions and thinkers, comparisons, the author's views, writing techniques, drafted passages, and books. It records four things for every kind: the author's level, scale, quadrant, and source. It keeps two lists: the commitments, and the open questions. The collation's Part 2 defines each of these in one line, gives the earlier names some of them had, and defines the vault's own words used below. Three of the vault's words recur here. *Company* means thinkers who reached the same finding independently; the book cites them as others who noticed the same thing, not as evidence that it is true. A *nascent* note is a claim stated but not yet backed by a reading. The *decisions register* is the table of settled questions in `ops/decisions.md`.

## How this document is organized

1. The findings in brief.
2. Corrections: where the vault shows the design to be wrong, sorted by kind.
3. The material outside the design, area by area. For each area: what the material is; why the design cannot record it; what it does for the books; how the design would be extended; and one real example from the vault, written in the extended form.
4. What stays outside the design, and the four pieces of the vault's own tools that should be added to it.
5. The design with these additions, in one place.
6. Five steps for the vault's next stage.

## 1. The findings in brief

1. **The vault shows the design to be wrong in about twenty places, and in ten of them the author has already decided the question.** The most important corrections:
   - Book II follows the arc of the psychedelic experience, by the author's decision of 2026-08-16. The design gave both books the four movements.
   - The design calls writing techniques its largest omission. The vault once had a category for them, and the author removed it on 2026-08-09.
   - By the author's decision of 2026-08-05, the vault's `state:` field does not record his own relation to a piece of material.
   - The Sanskrit terms pair as contraction and expansion, with reflexive awareness kept separate, by the author's decision of 2026-08-20. This removes the basis for the design's reading of reflexive awareness and for one of its tables, and both are now removed.
   - The sentence the design used to state the aim of all its rules is the model's, not the letter's.

2. **One addition appears in all eight reports: a field recording the author's own relation to each piece of material, and a mark on every quotation saying whose words it is.** The vault records his decisions in eight separate places and never on the material they concern. Without that record, the model's words have been presented as the author's at least seven times.

3. **Three large areas of the books' material are outside the design, and each serves functions the design names.** The three are:
   - the theory of suffering (the concern the vault calls "contraction, not corruption," 48 notes);
   - verification, meaning how anyone knows that something happened, and the false versions of that knowledge;
   - sequence, meaning the order the author decided for Book II, the house whose rooms are the book's ideas, the named units, and a survey that sorts about sixty sequences by what moves a reader from one stage to the next.

   Four more areas need the design's kinds of material to be widened: the letter as a form and as an act, the roles a second person plays, the versions of the manuscript and who wrote each one, and the making of the book, including the poems that came before it.

4. **Most of the vault's own tools and records stay outside the design, and four of them are added to it.** They are the relations between versions of one passage in the drafts; the thirty directives, as commitments the vault itself keeps; the manuscript-readiness conditions, as a test on a drafted passage; and the vault's proposal for its own ending, as the procedure that the design's principle "the structure exists for the sake of encounters" lacks.

5. **The design should be added to the vault as one more reading of the whole vault, organized by what the books need, with two new fields on the notes themselves.** It should not be imposed as a schema that re-files the vault's 502 notes.

## 2. Corrections: where the vault shows the design to be wrong

### A. Where the author has already decided otherwise

1. **Book II follows the arc of the psychedelic experience.** On 2026-08-16 the author gave each book its own order: "this can be book 1 and book 2." Book I takes the reframe conversation's four movements, which end in the facilitator's withdrawal, and Book II takes the arc of the psychedelic experience, which rises to a peak (`ops/decisions.md`, the row "Which dramaturgy spines the manuscript"; `ops/methodology/the two-book architecture.md`). The earlier version of the design had answered the question of the manuscript's order with the four movements from the transcript, and it gave both books the same movements. The word "psychedelic" does not appear in the transcript at all (O4, O2, F2). The design's rows on this question, in its Part 8, are now corrected.

2. **A category for writing techniques was created and then removed.** The earlier version of the design called writing techniques the largest omission in the seven outlines. The vault created a category for them on 2026-08-01, under the rule "Record the mechanism, never the verdict" (`ops/methodology/craft evidence.md`). By 2026-08-08 it had eighteen notes of exactly the design's kind, each with a feature that could be checked in the written draft. The author removed them on 2026-08-09 ("Cut the craft-about-drafts notes too"), because "a research corpus studies the ideas rather than the manuscript's sentence technique" (`ops/decisions.md`). The notes can be recovered from commit `1efe050` (O4). The design's reason for wanting them, that a facilitator reading Book I learns from how the book itself is written, came after the removal and after the decision to write two books. Reopening the question is the author's decision: the decisions register says that reversing a row "is a /rethink proposal with the author's decision, never a session's own act" (F4).

3. **The `state:` field is not the author's level.** The limit the author set on 2026-08-05 reads: "`state:` **decouples.** It records how substantiated a note is: a maturity marker for the vault's own material, informing which pass to run next and nothing the book asserts" (`ops/methodology/vocabulary policy.md`). The design's author's level has to be a separate field. Treating "nascent" as "proposed" and "full" as "believed" would let a reading by a session raise the author's level (F4, F2, O1, O3).

4. **The vault's unit is the proposition, and the concept has its own place.** On 2026-08-13 the author settled the question of the vault's unit as "Both, allocated." The sentence stating one proposition is the unit of the notes, and the named concept is the unit of any list of concepts added later (`ops/decisions.md`). So the design's pairs are a way of deriving pairs from the vault's sentences, not a replacement for them (F4).

5. **The Sanskrit terms pair as contraction and expansion, and the author declined to join the terms into one cycle.** The decision of 2026-08-20 records that he "got confused with vimarsa/vikasa." Contraction (saṅkoca) pairs with expansion (vikāsa) as the two phases of the pulse, and reflexive awareness (vimarśa) is "a distinct element held on its own relevance." In his words, he is "not necessarily interested in pairing together vocabulary terms that don't necessarily go together just because" (`ops/decisions.md`; O1). This removes the basis for two things in the design, and both are now removed from it:
   - the reading in its Part 4 that "seeing the bubble is the reflexive move, vimarśa," which the vault already recorded as the model's elaboration;
   - the table in its Part 7 whose first column was the model's seven-step reading of the Śaiva terms.

6. **The worry about a false peak is already answered by the author's decision.** On 2026-08-20 he chose "peak resolves into return," which the decisions register explains as "the peak is an interior station, not the destination, which also answers the author's own false-peak hazard" (`ops/decisions.md`; O4). The design had treated the worry as raised once in the conversation and never returned to. The author's decision answers it.

7. **The four structures are four different kinds of structure, not one structure seen four times.** The decisions register reads the pulse, the five questions, the four movements, and the five nested levels as "four different kinds of structure at four altitudes (an operator, a method, a dramaturgy, an ontology)" (`ops/decisions.md`, the row "Does the book have one spine or the four the reframe offers?"). The directive that records the ruling adds that "shared vocabulary across them is evidence of common parentage, not of common structure" (`ops/methodology/the four kinds of structure.md`). The design's table that arranged the same material as one structure contradicted this directive, and it is now removed (O4).

8. **The quadrants are recorded as one way of reading the material, not as a field.** The decision of 2026-08-13 reads that "the four ontological roots, Wilber's four quadrants, and the plotted-lineage stack are registered lenses (research-frontier material), not re-seatings" (`ops/decisions.md`). A quadrant field on every piece of material would make a required part of the structure out of what the author recorded as one optional way of reading (F4).

9. **The author has already named Kashmir Śaivism as the tradition the book owes most.** His words of 2026-08-16 answer the design's open question of how fully the books state their basis in Kashmir Śaivism: "absolutely, it does seem to occupy that, and I think that's fine. It surprised me too" (quoted in `notes/the tradition the book owes most has no chair in its company.md`; F3).

10. **The book's standard for verification is the author's decision, which his own later question has reopened.** On 2026-08-04 he named the felt shift "the book's test for whether anything is true … one standard, not one doctrine among six" (`ops/decisions.md`). The design was built from a transcript in which the felt shift "is never author-ratified," and it has no place for this standard (F2). Question B3, one of the questions only the author can answer, now asks whether his standard is the felt shift or durability (`ops/practice-model ecology — candidate next steps.md`).

Line 3704 is inside the model's response that begins at line 3373. The vault had already recorded that "it is the model's articulation rather than the author's own words" (`notes/patriotism for oneself is the felt form of the auxiliary relation to a sovereign reader.md`). The author's own words are at line 770: "our relationship inspires patriotism in herself... And she did the same thing for me". The design now bases the aim on line 770 and keeps the model's sentence at the level "proposed" (O2).

2. **The question about form is the model's wording, which the author adopted.** The design's topic table marked the question for the topic Form as the author's. The vault's own learning arc makes a larger version of the same error: it attaches "the question of my life" to the master question, which is also the model's (7567) (O4, O1).

### C. Where the design's structure is wrong

1. **The design divides the topics differently from the vault, and the vault's division follows the primary material.** The design's fifteen topics come from the transcript. The vault's six doctrine concerns all appear in the author's first fifteen poems (`notes/all six doctrine concerns appear in the first fifteen poems.md`, marked full). By the vault's rule that the primary material governs, the six are the books' concerns, and most of the design's fifteen are a finer division inside them. Three of the six have no topic in the design: suffering, verification, and address (F2, O1, F1, O2).
2. **The design's "tradition or thinker" combines three things the vault keeps apart:** the standpoint (thirteen in `the-loci.md`), the thinker (64 thinker pages), and the reading record (47 in `archive/`). They have different fields and change at different times. The reading record is the record by which a claim moves from "nascent" to "full," and combining the three would lose that record (F2).
3. **Independence is a relation between two traditions or thinkers, not a property of one.** The vault tests it three ways (by dates, by a shared teacher, and by influence in both directions), dates each result, and names six kinds of descent (F3, O3, F4).
4. **No drafted passage in the vault is in the author's voice, so the design's definition leaves this kind of material empty.** Every version of the manuscript the vault contains was written by a collaborator or a session, and the one passage the author sent is not stored (O4, O2).
5. **The list of open questions is really four lists with different rules.** They are the tensions, the open questions, the clearing (the list of what the book's central commitments cannot account for), and the open corpus. A disagreement filed as a line in a table "silently loses its treatment tracking" (`CLAUDE.md`), meaning nobody follows its proposed handling any more. And a gap in what the book can account for, if it is filed as a tension, becomes one more disagreement to manage; the clearing exists to prevent that (`ops/clearing.md`; F3).
6. **A comparison can end in more than two ways.** Besides one of the author's views or an item on the list of open questions, it can produce:
   - a disagreement that must be handled in the writing;
   - a departure, where the book's own position is distinguished from the thinkers it resembles and "recorded as a departure and never cited as convergence" (`ops/decisions.md`, 2026-08-13);
   - a demotion to lineage, where the arrivals turn out not to be independent;
   - a counterexample kept on record (F3).
7. **What is left out can be recorded for the whole book as well as for each topic.** The clearing records it for the whole book, naming for each entry the central commitment that cannot account for it. The design records it only for each topic (F3).
8. **Keeping the poles unranked does not by itself answer the worry about a false peak.** Each pole has its own false version. For expansion it is the false peak; for contraction it is the racket, "a narrowing so habitual it no longer registers as a narrowing" (`notes/the pulse has a counterfeit at each phase, a false peak of expansion and a racket of forgotten contraction.md`; O1).
9. **A book's order can move steadily toward one pole.** The vault records that the arc "front-loads one pole and spends toward the other," meaning that it starts from one pole and moves toward the other, which goes against the vault's own rule that neither side of a polarity be given up for the other. The design's Book I ends in the facilitator's withdrawal, which is one pole. The commitment to unranked poles needs an open question: whether a book's order may resolve a polarity over the course of the book (O4).
10. **Prose that is easy to read can make a reader agree.** The commitment "Form directs attention, and the meaning is left to the reader" comes with the question "Is this device directing attention or supplying meaning?" The vault's felt-shift material records a third possibility: "statements shown in easier-to-read contrast are judged true above chance on presentation alone" (`notes/the felt sense of truth has a documented counterfeit catalogue.md`). The question needs a third alternative: "or producing agreement?" (F1).
11. **Telling the reader what recognition feels like teaches a false version of it.** "A reader taught this book's vocabulary and told what a shift feels like has been given a subculture's rules and its feedback" (`drafts/brief — the verification standard's defense.md`, §3). The commitment "Recognition belongs to the reader" needs a second question: does this passage tell the reader what the recognition will feel like? (F1)
12. **The nine commitments leave out three principles the author stated or agreed with.** `the-axioms.md` marks "Let constraint reveal what is essential" and "Collaborate, but keep the authorial center awake" as the author's own words, and "The author's experience is an offering, not an authority" as the model's words, which the author agreed with (F4).
13. **One of the questions the design asks of each forebear conflicts with the vault's stance.** The design asks of each forebear, "What did they mistake for the whole?" The vault's rule keeps a thinker's disagreement with the book "so the thinker survives being cited" (`CLAUDE.md`). The question should be kept as the books' own reading of the forebear, never as a verdict on them, and always paired with where the thinker disagrees with the book (O3).
14. **The record of where an image came from needs a fourth possible answer: the collaborator, writing with the author free to reject anything.** The images of the house, the orchard, and the gold thread were drafted within a working arrangement the author controlled (`notes/the figures precede the system by a year and the two were welded together later.md`; F1).
15. **The design marks too many practices as the model's alone.** The practices come from the corpus and from the author's own craft: "the book's practices are, at bottom, the craft of holding a room for people — scaled down to one" (first-telling, quoted in `notes/the book's practices are facilitation moves scaled to one reader.md`; F1).
16. **Where Book II names its method is not decided.** The design says Book II names its method at the end. The drafts of Book II's arc name the system in their second part, "The Architecture Revealed" (`ops/scaffold.md`), so that "naming lands as recognition rather than instruction" (`notes/book-structure.md`). How much of that part remains, now that the system has been ranked as secondary, is the author's decision (O4).
17. **Book II's premise, that a reader can practice alone, conflicts with what many of the book's own thinkers say.** "Roughly nineteen chairs with nothing else in common say *this cannot be done alone*" (`the-constellation.md`; a chair is the vault's word for a thinker's page). The vault's notes on discrimination, meaning the ability to tell a real change from a false one, were checked against primary texts and conclude that "a book read alone can state the whole of the restraint and none of the discrimination" (`notes/a solitary reader can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates.md`). Book II's statement of what it leaves out, and its promise to the reader, should both state this limit (O3, F1).
18. **The scale "people together" assumes the other party is a person.** The author's own record puts a sky, a bird's gaze, and two cats in the place of the second person (`ops/second person ecology.md`; O2).
19. **The design's field "first reader" cannot record the book's addressee.** The author named an addressee who is not a reader: "The book itself becomes me writing to the Third Thing in order to enact its outcomes through and with and upon myself" (`notes/the book is written to the relational field and enacted upon the author first.md`; O2).
20. **The design's level "inferred" conflicts with how the vault already uses the word.** The vault writes "Standing: inferred" on conclusions a session drew, and the file of derivations at the repository root marks its conclusions from first principles `[inferred]`. The design reserves the word for the author's own act (F2, O1, O2).

## 3. The material outside the design, area by area

### 1. The author's own relation to each piece of material, and whose words a sentence is

**What it is.** The vault records the author's decisions in eight places (F4):
- a thinker's role, which changes from "candidate" only by his decision;
- a tension's status, which becomes "resolved" only by his decision;
- placements (his assignments of material to a chapter), recorded only when he makes them;
- the 119 rows of the decisions register;
- dated citations of the form "author statement";
- the three levels of confidence he set on 2026-08-04;
- the standing of his reflection;
- the encounter record's rule that his turns in the conversation are primary material and the model's are works consulted.

It records none of these decisions on the material they concern. The provenance reconstructions mark whose words each sentence is ("[D] your words, [C] the AI’s, [A] hers as you transcribed," `provenance/provenance fourth addendum.md`, line 11), and those marks appear nowhere else. The consequence can be counted. The words of the model, or of an AI collaborator, are presented as the author's in at least seven places, and the third companion document lists each one with its path. The most important is a sentence about the 4am letter, "The recognition didn’t require the mirror," which is marked [C], the AI's, in its source and is quoted in nine files without the mark (O2, O1).

**Why the design cannot record it.** The design has the right levels and the right rule, that only the author raises the level. But it has no place to record the decision that raised the level of a piece of material, no place to say what convinced him, no mark for whose words a quotation is, and no level for a conclusion a session drew.

**What it does for the books.** It serves function 6, offering the author's own view as an example "with a statement of how certain he is of it." It makes it possible to apply the commitment "The author is the first participant," whose question ("Did this pass through the author's own encounter?") cannot be answered without the record. And it serves the principle that finding information may be delegated while authorship may not.

**How the design would be extended.**
- **The author's level becomes a field that only the author's decisions set.** It is a separate field from `state:` and never replaces it. Its values are:
  - "proposed," the default for everything a session, the corpus, the model, or a reading brought in;
  - "experienced," "inferred," "believed," "claimed," and "written";
  - "sent" and "answered," for drafted passages.
- **Every level above "proposed" carries a reference** to the dated decision that raised it: a row of the decisions register, a dated statement by the author, the reflection, or a placement. No session writes a level; a session writes only the reference to his decision (F3, F4).
- **An optional note records what convinced him** each time he raises a level: a felt shift, the view lasting over time, a reading, or a group session he facilitated (F1).
- **A separate mark, "derived," records a conclusion a session drew,** graded strict, plausible, or conjecture, as the file of derivations at the repository root already grades them. This keeps the word "inferred" for the author (F2).
- **Every quotation from the transcript or the provenance files carries a mark saying whose words it is:** the author, the model, the collaborator, or another named person. The transcript already carries this information in the headings that separate the author's prompts from the model's responses, and the provenance files carry it as [D], [C], and [A]. A quotation the author adopted but did not write carries both marks: "the model's words, adopted by the author" (O1).

**An example, written in the extended form.**
> **The author's view: the aim.**
> - *Statement:* "our relationship inspires patriotism in herself... And she did the same thing for me" (transcript line 770).
>   - *Whose words:* the author.
>   - *The author's level:* experienced.
>   - *Raised by:* his own turn in the conversation.
> - *A related wording:* "My participation with you makes it more possible for you to belong to yourself" (3704).
>   - *Whose words:* the model.
>   - *The author's level:* proposed.
> - *The letter's own wording:* "we each act as auxiliary for the other as we march through life with our own flags in hand."
>   - *Whose words:* the letter's writer, as the author transcribed them.
>   - *Source:* `notes/the book can only be auxiliary to a sovereign reader.md`.

### 2. Suffering: the concern the vault calls "contraction, not corruption"

**What it is.** The topic map `notes/contraction-not-corruption.md` gathers 48 notes, and the concern has its own entry in `the-concerns.md` and its own drafting document. The book's sentence for it is "Nothing in you is broken. Something in you is folded." (first-telling, quoted in `notes/suffering is contraction never corruption.md`). Its drafting document states an open question: "which suffering is which: a fold to open, a wound to be borne, or a condition to be changed." It also names a danger in the concern itself: the reading of suffering as contraction is "a mercy when it lifts a verdict off a person, and a cruelty when it is turned on a harm arranged outside them" (`drafts/contraction-not-corruption — a surface to write from.md`).

**Why the design cannot record it.** The word "suffer" does not appear in the design at all. Contraction appears only in one pair under the topic of recognition, and as one pole of a polarity (O1).

**What it does for the books.** It serves function 1, starting from what the reader already has ("nothing in you is broken" is a sentence that starts from the reader's present condition); function 2, through the design's own condition "permission not to solve the experience"; function 4, keeping the two poles unranked; and function 7, since the danger just quoted is what this concern leaves out if it is used carelessly.

**How the design would be extended.** Add a topic, Suffering, as one of the rows by which the books are organized (Part 2, item C1).

**An example** (compiled from the vault; at the level "proposed" until the author raises it):
> **Topic: Suffering.**
> - *Question:* which suffering is a fold to open, a wound to be borne, or a condition to be changed?
> - *What is at stake:* "Reading suffering as contraction rather than corruption is how the book keeps the sufferer the author of their own change" (`notes/contraction-not-corruption.md`).
> - *Book I form:* where a facilitator may help a person with what the book calls "a fold to open," and where reframing the suffering would be "a violence" (the drafting document's first question, not yet answered).
> - *Book II form:* self-facilitation "begins from what is happening rather than from what is wrong with the self" (a note with that title, not yet backed by a reading, in the model's words).
> - *What it leaves out:* the danger quoted above.
> - *The author's own words:* "even uncertain, I am tethered; never broken" (poem #4, quoted in `notes/all six doctrine concerns appear in the first fifteen poems.md`).

### 3. Verification: how anyone knows that something happened

**What it is.** Most of the material in the felt-shift concern is about verification rather than about inner life (F1). It asks how the book, its author, its reader, and each tradition the book cites can tell that something happened, what each sign shows, and how each sign can be faked. The concern contains:
- the standard, "nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned" (atlas §III, quoted in `notes/nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned.md`), which a reading of the primary text narrowed to "the body countersigns *movement*… not *truth*" (`drafts/felt-shift — a surface to write from.md`);
- a catalogue of false versions of the felt shift; Gendlin's four kinds of inward attention that are not focusing; and the Zen rule that a person cannot certify their own experience;
- the two strongest objections, from Nisbett and Wilson and from Schwitzgebel, both read in full;
- the notes on discrimination, checked against primary texts, which found that "every criterion a reader alone can apply declines to evaluate, and every discrimination proper routes to another person";
- about 10,000 words of drafts defending the standard.

At least twenty of the concern's 79 notes are about how a change is confirmed, or about how a confirmation can be faked (F1). Six readings of opposing thinkers have not yet been done (O3).

**Why the design cannot record it.** No kind of material, field, or connection in the design asks how anyone would know that something went right, or who could tell. A practice records "how it goes wrong"; the commitments record the ethical side ("Recognition belongs to the reader"); and the author's level records that the author believes something, but never on what evidence. The design was built from a transcript in which the felt shift "is never author-ratified" (`the-two-books.md`; F2).

**What it does for the books.** It serves function 2 (arranging the conditions for recognition, one of which is "safety with enough friction to notice what was hidden before"); function 3 (a way of confirming a change is what keeps the book's direction of attention from turning into a verdict about the reader); and function 8 (the book's promise, including what it will not do for its reader). It also serves Book II's authority, which comes from embodied realization, and Book I, whose reader is exactly the second person whom the notes on discrimination say a reader needs.

**How the design would be extended.**
- **A new field records how one would know a practice or passage worked** (F1 calls it a certificate). Its parts are:
  - the sign: at the moment, over a period of time, by what follows, by a person's conduct, or through another person;
  - who can check it: the person at the time, the person later, another person who has been through it, or a clinician;
  - its false version, and who catches it;
  - whether a page can provide it.
- **A possible commitment is worded as the primary text narrows it:** *the felt shift shows that a process moved forward, not that a content is true* (Gendlin 1964, fn. 9). The question to ask of a draft: does this passage or practice produce a step that changes what can be said next? (O3, F2)
- **A new connection records that a tradition or thinker disputes a commitment.** The thinkers who argue against the standard are recorded this way, with the readings that answer each one recorded as the commitment's defense (O3).
- Question B3 goes on the list of open questions until the author answers it.

**An example** (F1):
> **Practice: Focusing.**
> - *The invitation:* the six steps, from locating "the felt sense of something in the body without analyzing it" to waiting.
> - *What it lets the reader notice:* the pair "sheer emotion / felt sense."
> - *The tradition it comes from:* Gendlin, read in full on 2026-08-28.
> - *How one would know it worked:*
>   - the sign at the moment is that what the person is attending to moves (Gendlin's "referent movement"); the sign over days is a step after which "the meanings and symbolizations one formulates are different";
>   - the person can apply the practice's restraint alone and at the time, but telling a real shift from a false one takes a listener;
>   - the false version is a fluent "yes" that "arrives fast," "cannot be re-contacted as a body-sense," and "has nothing new when revisited" (`notes/gendlin's focusing protocol already guards against the counterfeit shift.md`), which is caught by returning to the feeling and by checking again over several days;
>   - a page can print the safeguards, but it cannot do the telling apart.
> - *The author's encounters with it:* none recorded.

### 4. Sequence: the order of Book II, and the vault's account of order

**What it is.** This is the material behind the author's decision that Book II follows the arc of the psychedelic experience (O4):
- the six phases (preparation, onset, deepening, peak, descent, and integration), which only the sources that describe the book name;
- five named parts;
- fifteen named units in order;
- four readings of the arc that run alongside it (the treatment calls them overlays);
- the house of nine rooms;
- the plan of how intense each part should be;
- two alternative orders, the straight passage through the arc and the "concentric spirals."

The vault also has its own tools for thinking about order:
- the four kinds of structure (`ops/methodology/the four kinds of structure.md`);
- a survey that sorts about sixty sequences into fourteen types, by what moves a reader to the next stage and what marks the end (`ops/arc ecology.md`);
- the order in which the author wants things revealed, in his words: "I can't reveal something early that I didn't know until much later without breaking the thing that makes it work" (`ops/methodology/the book's current shape.md`).

**Why the design cannot record it.** The design's record for a book has one field for its "movement and developmental steps." It has no kind of material for a sequence, no difference between a developmental sequence and the dramatic order of a book, and nothing on what moves a reader from one stage to the next, how a high point is handled, or what marks the end. None of its commitments says anything about order.

**What it does for the books.** It serves function 1 (the front matter's "You already know everything in this book. You've just forgotten. Reading is remembering."); functions 2 to 4, through the pacing; function 7 ("You can put the book down now," abridged p. 47); and function 8.

**How the design would be extended.**
- **The design gains a new kind of material, the sequence.** Its fields are these (O4):
  - *kind:* a repeated operation, a method, the dramatic order of a book, or an account of what exists, following the vault's directive on the four kinds of structure;
  - *book*;
  - *stages:* the named units in order, with who named them;
  - *what moves the reader from one stage to the next*;
  - *how it handles its high point:* in one place, spread across the book, or passed through without being the destination;
  - *its ending:* whether it completes or repeats;
  - *what marks the end*;
  - *the author's level*.
- Each book gets two fields, its dramatic order and its developmental sequence, and each is a sequence.
- Three new connections are added: *a sequence puts drafted passages in order*; *a sequence moves from one side of a pair to the other across its stages*; *one passage introduces something that a later passage resolves*.
- A possible commitment, in the author's own terms, is that *the book reveals things in the order the author learned them*. The question to ask of a draft: does this passage state something, at its place in the book, that the author learned only at a later point? (O4)
- The design's table that arranged four structures as one is replaced by the four kinds of structure (Part 2, item A7).

**An example** (O4):
> **Sequence: Book II's dramatic order.**
> - *Kind:* the dramatic order of a book. *Book:* Book II (the author's decision, 2026-08-16).
> - *Stages:* preparation → onset → deepening → peak → descent → integration, named only at atlas §X.1, walked p. 21, and first-telling p. 3. The drafts name parts instead: The Clearing Opens, The Architecture Revealed, The Relational Field, The Break, The Return with Gifts.
> - *What moves the reader on:* the pacing of the prose ("density, tempo, and sentence-length are respiratory instruments," walked X), judged by asking "is she ready for it here, in sequence."
> - *High point:* passed through rather than treated as the destination, by the author's decision "peak resolves into return."
> - *Ending:* undecided between repeating and completing (the fourth disagreement recorded in the survey of the arc).
> - *What marks the end:* "welcome home" in the treatment and in the sources that describe the book, against "Step in." in the fully written draft (`ops/chapter concordance.md`).
> - *The author's level:* the phases are the corpus's. Whether they describe what happens to a reader, or how the book supports the reader through it, is item 3 of `ops/decision sitting — 2026-09-03.md`, the document gathering every open question only the author can answer.

### 5. The letter: a form, an act, and a concern of its own

### 6. The roles a second person plays: Book I's list of what a facilitator does

**What it is.** `ops/second person ecology.md` sorts about seventy examples of second persons, from the traditions, from clinical practice, from the corpus, and from the author's own record, by the job each one does:
- the former, who shapes a person over time;
- the gatekeeper, who decides what a person receives and when;
- the certifier, who says afterward whether what happened was the real thing;
- the receiver, who takes a person's own valuing without overruling it;
- the interpolator, who adds content to intensify what a person is acting out;
- the enforcer, who assigns an act and holds the person to account for it;
- the answerer, who completes a word by replying;
- the companion, who does nothing but is there;
- four roles the survey added: the giver, the summoner, the survivor, and an arrangement that is not a person.

For each job the survey records whether it helps a recognition happen or acts on one that has happened, what can perform it (a person present, a text, an object, and so on), and which combinations have no example. Its most direct sentence is: "The book kept the receiver and discarded the certifier, the gatekeeper, and the former from every tradition it honours. That is a design decision the vault has not recorded as one" (O2).

**Why the design cannot record it.** Nothing in the design records what a second person does. An encounter's "who" names a person, and a practice's scale counts people. Neither can say that a receiver helps a recognition happen while a certifier judges it, or that a text can act as a companion but not as a certifier. The design's record for a book cannot say which roles a book supplies and which it declines.

**What it does for the books.** It serves Book I directly, since these are the jobs a facilitator can take on, each with its own way of going wrong. It serves function 7, since the roles a book cannot perform are part of what it leaves out, and function 8, since the roles it declines belong in its promise to the reader.

**How the design would be extended.** The design could gain a new kind of material, the **role**, with these fields:
- the job;
- whether it helps a recognition happen or acts on one;
- whether it evaluates the person;
- what can perform it (a person present, someone who responds, a text, the world, an object, or a memory);
- how it goes wrong;
- its examples, by thinker or tradition;
- its form in Book I and its form in Book II.

Its connections would be: *a role is filled in an encounter*; *a book supplies or declines a role*; and *a tradition or thinker names a role*. If a new kind of material is more than the books need, the smaller change is to give each book two fields: the roles it supplies and the roles it declines (O2).

**An example** (O2):
> **Role: the receiver.**
> - *Job:* takes a person's own valuing without overruling it.
> - *Helps a recognition happen:* yes, in Gendlin 1964, Rogers 1957, and Winnicott 1958.
> - *Evaluates:* no.
> - *Can be performed by:* someone who responds, not necessarily a person in the room. The working alliance (the bond between client and therapist) is about as strong in written and other mediated therapy (r = .275) as face to face (r = .278).
> - *How it goes wrong:* by supplying content the person had not yet reached.
> - *Book I:* the facilitator's central job. *Book II:* declined by the book, in its own words.
> - *The author's level:* proposed. The author's question S9 asks whether keeping this role was a decision.

**What it is.** `ops/chapter concordance.md`, the table comparing the drafts chapter by chapter, follows fifteen named units across four documents. **Why the design cannot record it.** The design's drafted passage has a book and a possible position. It has no idea of several versions of one element, no record of who wrote a version, and no connection between versions. **What it does for the books.** It serves the manuscript itself: with the table, drafting a unit costs "one lookup rather than a re-reading of seven documents" (the table's stated purpose, O4).

### 8. The making of the book: dated stages, who wrote what, and the poems that came first

**What it is.** The provenance files and about twenty notes record the book's origin as a series of dated stages, and they mark who wrote each sentence (F4). The stages are these:
- the poems: a lifetime of them, fifteen of which came before the book;
- the first images (the "seed"): 9 April 2025;
- the triad, worked out in another project: 8 December 2025;
- the author's first formal contact with the tradition the triad is named for: 19–22 March 2026;
- the joining of the images to the system: July 2026;
- the corpus, compiled in one month;
- the reframe conversation: 12 August 2026.

The direction of influence is settled: "the author said the plain thing first, in his own words, about his own life … and the scholarship arrived afterward and dressed it" (`CLAUDE.md`). The corpus is an earlier version of the book, written by a collaborator while the author chose and arranged the material (O1). The poems are primary material: "for the fifteen poems that predate the book, a phrase shared with the corpus originates in the poem" (`CLAUDE.md`). In the author's words, they are "a part of the idiosyncrasies of the book for me, as a part of my offering, for now" (`ops/methodology/source standing.md`).

**Why the design cannot record it.** Four of the design's kinds of material each record part of it, and none records the whole. An encounter needs the author's note, but the notes in these records are reconstructions attributed to others. An author's view needs what the author still believed after comparison, but the poems came before any comparison and, by the author's decision, are not used as evidence for doctrine. An image needs a picture, but "the figures … appear nowhere in the collection" of poems (`archive/source — the poems.md`). A source needs an address, but the design's source field cannot say that a source came before the book, or who wrote a sentence.

**What it does for the books.** It serves function 6: the honest account of the book's origin "doubles as the book's invitation" (`notes/the book's honest origin story is noticing first and finding company after.md`), because it shows the book's offer happening in the author's own life. It serves function 1. And it serves the commitment "The author is the first participant," whose question can be answered only against this record.

**How the design would be extended.**
- The source field gains a **stage** (the poems, the first images, the ontology project, the retreat, the corpus, the reframe conversation, a reading, or a draft) and a **writer** (the author, the collaborator, another named person, or a session).
- Encounters widen to include **events in the making of the book**, with a field for the **account** of the event: in the author's words, reconstructed from works consulted, or a session's inference. The author's level applies to the account separately from the event (O4).
- Every kind of material gains a field for **where it was first said**: the earliest dated statement of it in the author's life or poems. A new connection, **first said in**, links a phrase in the corpus or the book to that earliest statement (O1, F2).
- The corpus is recorded as an **earlier draft**: chosen and arranged by the author, written by a collaborator, and kept at the level "proposed" until the author adopts a sentence as his own (O1).

> - *When:* 8 December 2025. *Where and who:* the author's separate ontology project, [D].
> - *Conditions:* three months before his first formal contact with the tradition the triad is named for (Hridaya Northwest, 19–22 March 2026).

### 9. Sources, recorded in full: the standing of each source, independence, and how deeply a source was read

**What it is.** The vault records the standing of each source (a description of the book, a draft of it, primary material, or a work consulted); a citation form for readings (`Gendlin 1964, fn. 13`); the rule that three documents compiled from one earlier document count as one; the six kinds of descent; the shared-teacher test; and the rule that, where two documents disagree about why something was done, the one written closer in time to the event is trusted. Grades record how deeply a source was read: Grade A, Grade B, "verified at web depth," "corpus-relay only," or "from memory." Fourteen reading records name a grade, and the reading list carries 32 marks for secondary reading and 8 for primary (O3). The vault has measured what goes wrong when only an address is recorded: thirteen notes claiming a correspondence quoted the corpus and only paraphrased the thinker, and looked fully supported because "the page is full of anchored quotations from one side" (`ops/decisions.md`; `ops/methodology/both halves of a correspondence.md`; F4).

**Why the design cannot record it.** The design's source field gives every quotation an address. It says nothing about what kind of document the address points to, how deeply that document was read, or how two sources are related.

**What it does for the books.** It serves function 5, bringing in the thinkers who noticed the same thing without merging them, and the commitment "A difference is understood before a likeness is claimed."

**How the design would be extended.** The source field becomes a small record: the address; the source's standing; who wrote it; its stage; a grade for how deeply it was read (O3); and, where two sources agree, whether they reached their agreement independently. The vault's existing `state:` field, with its `privation:` entry naming what is missing, measures the same thing for a whole note. It should be named plainly as the measure of reading and kept apart from the author's level: a note can be fully backed by readings and still be at the level "proposed," and the author's own words can be at the level "believed" while the note recording them is marked nascent (O1).

**An example** (F4):
> **Sources, for a claim that cites the atlas, the walked document, and the treatment (`arc-gloss`).**
> - *Standing:* a description of the book, a description of the book, and a draft.
> - *Relation:* compiled from one another. "Where the atlas, the walked document, and the Arc and the Gloss agree, the vault is reading one document three times."
> - *Consequence:* "A claim citing all three anchors is not thereby better supported than a claim citing one" (`notes/the atlas was compiled from the arc and gloss treatment.md`).

### 10. The thinkers, recorded more fully: what each contributes, whom each descends from, where the book uses them, what readers bring to their names, and their disagreements among themselves

**What it is.**
- What each thinker contributes to the book's argument (`load:`), recorded on every thinker page: a precedent for 17 thinkers, an independent arrival at the same finding for 13, the book's thesis for 12, company for 9, an opponent for 7, and a name for a practice the book already uses for 6.
- The six kinds of descent between thinkers.
- The role a thinker plays for the book (`seat:`): patron of a chapter (quoted at its opening), a thinker quoted in the margin, part of the lineage listed at the back, recommended reading, or a referral to other help.
- The list of precedents: earlier attempts at the book's project, kept for what became of them.
- Heidegger's page, which records, at the author's instruction, what an informed reader associates with his name (his membership in the Nazi party and the antisemitic passages in his notebooks), together with the thinkers in the book's company who responded to him, such as Paul Celan and Hannah Arendt.
- Thinker pages that record a tradition's own internal argument, such as Menkiti and Gyekye on personhood in Ubuntu (O3).

**Why the design cannot record it.**
- The design has one connection from a tradition or thinker to a topic: the thinker says something about the topic. But an opponent disputes the book, a precedent matters for what happened to it, and a thinker who names an existing practice gives an established name to something the book already does.
- The vault's own `load:` field, which takes one value, fails for the same reason. Vervaeke contributes in three ways, and Wilber's recommendation reads "thesis-bearing for the lattice on the claim note that carries it, precedent here" (O3). What a thinker contributes depends on the topic.
- The design's record for a tradition or thinker has no field for where a book uses them, for what a reader brings to their name, or for the disagreements inside a tradition.

**What it does for the books.** It serves function 5, since the company of thinkers must not be "mistaken for the argument"; function 7, since the opponents say what the book's framework hides; and function 8, since referring the reader to other help and giving honest credit are part of the book's promise.

**How the design would be extended.**
- **A tradition or thinker contributes to a topic, or to a commitment,** in one of six ways: as the thesis, an independent arrival, company, a name for an existing practice, a precedent, or an opponent. There is one entry for each thinker and topic, and the contribution field keeps its three rules: it decides what to read next and never ranks evidence; the author makes the assignment; and company is not a lesser status.
- **A tradition or thinker descends from another.** The fields are the kind of descent (one of the six), how the contact happened, and the evidence, with a page-level address.
- **A book uses a tradition or thinker at a position:** an epigraph, the margin, the main text, the back matter, the reading list, or a referral. This is recorded once for each book, and placing the thinker stays the author's decision.
- A tradition or thinker gains new fields: **what a reader brings to this name**, with the thinkers who responded to it; **what happened to it**, for precedents; and **its internal argument**.
- The design's single record is split into the vault's three levels: the standpoint, the thinker, and the reading record (Part 2, item C2).

**An example** (O3):
> **Tradition or thinker: John Vervaeke, recorded as three connections.**
> - Vervaeke contributes to the topic *Meaning* as the thesis: relevance realization is "the criterion of the cognitive" (his 2012 paper, read at Grade A).
> - Vervaeke contributes to the topic *Participation* as company, not as an independent arrival: he "roots participatory knowing in Heidegger."
> - Vervaeke disputes the aim, with his wisdom test: "Does this actually improve the agent's capacity to navigate reality?" (transcript line 2316).

### 12. The lists of open items

**What it is.** The vault keeps four lists, each with its own rules (F3):
- **the tensions**, each with two sides, a status, and a proposed way of handling it, and resolved only by the author;
- **the open questions**, kept open as "questions whose openness does work";
- **`ops/clearing.md`**, the clearing, the list of what the book's central commitments cannot account for, which admits an item because it does not fit and requires each entry to name "which commitment of the center cannot hold it";
- **the open corpus**, subjects the corpus named and never developed.

Alongside them are:
- the document of questions for the author (`ops/decision sitting — 2026-09-03.md`), which sorts 22 items by who can settle each one;
- the reading list's 94 questions, each stated so that a reading could answer it either way, with what is at stake, an address, what it builds on, and a grade of the current evidence;
- the list of names not yet worked on, with the rule that "an entry that is a name plus a topic gloss is a shelf entry, and a shelf entry is not work" (O3);
- three competing explanations of why a big experience did not last, "held side by side and deliberately not merged, because they imply different books" (`notes/recognition.md`; O1).

**Why the design cannot record it.** The design's list of open questions keeps unresolved questions and choices visible. It has no status, no proposed handling, no rule about whose decision closes an item, no statement of what would close it, no grade of the evidence, no place for what the whole book cannot account for, and no place for choices whose alternatives would each produce a different book.

**What it does for the books.** It serves function 7, saying what the book's framework hides; function 4, since a proposed handling of a choice is how a page keeps both sides present; and the design's own account of how the work proceeds.

**How the design would be extended.** The list of open questions becomes six lists:
- **choices between two positions:** the two sides, quoted with addresses; what changes under each; status; proposed handling; whose decision resolves it; and a grade of the evidence;
- **open questions:** each stated so that it could be answered either way, with what would close it (a reading, the author, drafting, an extraction from the corpus, or the transcript), what is at stake, an address, what it builds on, and a grade;
- **what the whole book leaves out,** kept under the rules of `ops/clearing.md`: an entry leaves the list when a second, independent arrival is found, and a disagreement may be recorded without giving the opposing thinker a role in the book;
- **questions only the author can answer,** in the form of the document that gathers them;
- **proposed names** that are not yet questions;
- **choices that decide the book's overall structure:** alternatives kept separate, each with the book it would produce.

**An example** (F3):
> **A choice between two positions: the reader alone, against the thinkers who say a second person is required.**
> - *First position:* seven thinkers who hold that the inner life needs a second person (the vault calls them the seven witnesses). One of them, al-Qushayrī, writes: "The tree that has grown on its own without a gardener will bring forth foliage, but will never bring any fruit" (Knysh 2007, p. 406).
> - *Second position:* the book's own form, a letter read alone, and the one recorded occurrence of its event, which happened with "no mirror present, no reply possible" (`provenance/provenance fourth addendum.md`).
> - *What changes:* "the book's scope and form, the seventh joint of the scaffold … and the relational-field surface" (`ops/decision sitting — 2026-09-03.md`, item 1). The scaffold is `ops/scaffold.md`, the book's structure as derived from the vault's notes, and a joint is one of seven points it lists where the vault's readings of the book's structure differ or the material is thin; at four of them, this one included, the author has to decide.
> - *Status:* live. *Proposed handling:* "the book adds no second chair; it holds that recognition is already a with, so solitude was never alone — a candidate drawn from the author's reflection, not his decision." ("Second chair" is the vault's phrase for a second person present with the reader.)
> - *Whose decision:* the author's. *Grade of the evidence:* "five of seven at primary; two unread."

### 14. Practices, recorded more fully

**What it is.** `ops/practice-model ecology.md` sorts about seventy models of practice into eleven structural types. **What it does for the books.** It serves functions 2, 3, and 4, and Book I's material on the practice of facilitation.

- **how one would know it worked** (area 3);

## 6. Five steps for the vault's next stage

Five steps follow from the three documents. Each changes the vault's structure, so each is the author's to decide. Each is chosen so that the vault becomes simpler to use, not larger.

1. **Record the author's level on the material itself, and mark whose words every quotation is.** This is one field and one mark. It stops the model's words from being presented as the author's, and it makes the author's columns in the topic table calculable.
