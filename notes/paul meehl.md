# paul meehl

Paul Meehl spent a career on why theories in soft psychology accumulate without ever being decided between. His diagnosis has two parts. Everything in psychology correlates weakly with everything else — the crud factor — so a theory that predicts merely that a difference will be nonzero is predicting something that is true almost regardless of the theory. And a theory that makes no risky prediction, one that could have come out otherwise and would have embarrassed it, has not been tested at all no matter how many results are consistent with it. His measure of a theory's seriousness is what it forbids.

He was not a hostile outsider. He was a clinical psychologist who believed the field could do better and thought its methods were preventing it.

## Why the sort wants him

The book's compositional account holds that difficulties are compounds of a small set of elements, that the formula names the trouble and its remedy together, and that the scheme applies across the range of human distress ([[distress is a question about machinery not a verdict about character]]). A scheme with that reach is exactly what Meehl's crux is designed to test: what configuration of a person's life would the scheme be unable to describe?

The vault previously held one adversary for the compositional account — Richard Biernacki's charge that a scheme's builder finds the scheme everywhere, which is historical and about method. Meehl is the formal version of the same charge, and the grid pairs them for that reason (`mesh grid: against C5`). The bloc reading of the coaches supplies the third and different attack, aimed at content rather than at method ([[the coaches are a bloc that competes with the compositional scheme]]).

## What he would refuse

Derived rather than sourced; no primary work has been read in this vault.

Meehl's own commitments cut against the book more broadly than the crux does, and this should be recorded rather than trimmed. He was among the strongest advocates of statistical over clinical judgment — his 1954 book showed that simple formulas routinely outperform trained clinicians' holistic impressions, and he never retreated from it. A book whose verification standard is a practitioner's felt sense is proposing the thing he spent forty years documenting the failure of.

He would also refuse the demotion as a defence. The author's decision of 2026-08-04 makes the schemes instruments rather than architecture, and gaps or overlaps in them are explicitly never work. That answers a completeness objection; it does not answer Meehl's, which is about risk rather than coverage — an instrument that cannot come out wrong is not made safer by being called an instrument.

## What the evaluation still requires

The 1978 paper, with one question that decides how much of him is usable: whether his crux applies to a scheme offered as a way of seeing rather than as a predictive theory. There is a real answer available in either direction, and the honest version of this chair is that the book may be outside his target entirely — in which case the finding is that the compositional account is not the kind of thing that can be wrong, which is worth knowing and is not obviously a compliment.
