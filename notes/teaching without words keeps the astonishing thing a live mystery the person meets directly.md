# teaching without words keeps the astonishing thing a live mystery the person meets directly

The reader gains the recognition that the helper's silence — the refusal to explain the astonishing thing, to gloss the moment, to name what is happening — is itself a gift, because an experience left unexplained stays alive and the person meets it firsthand. Over-explanation would hand the reader a summary in place of the encounter. The helper who withholds the interpretation protects the "whoa," and the reader keeps the numinous intact by being trusted to meet it without a caption. Its use to the writing is a positive craft of reticence through the arc's onset, peak, and descent, exactly where the "whoa" must be kept.

## Substantiation

- Laozi, ch. 2: the sage "practices the teaching that is without words" (行不言之教) — the deepest instruction is not spoken, so the learner meets the thing rather than the account of it. The finger pointing at the moon (Zen): the teacher points and then must get out of the way; to keep explaining the finger is to make the student miss the moon. Heidegger, Discourse on Thinking: releasement pairs with "openness to the mystery" (*die Offenheit für das Geheimnis*), holding the concealed *as* mystery rather than dissolving it into explanation.
- Refusals kept: Zen refuses the mystification error as sharply as the over-explanation one — the silence is not obscurantism, and the finger is a real, precise pointer; Heidegger's openness is a disciplined holding-open, not a refusal to think.
- Standing: nascent; the ch. 2 phrase and "openness to the mystery" (Anderson/Freund) are queued.
