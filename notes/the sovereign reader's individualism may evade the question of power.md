# the sovereign reader's individualism may evade the question of power

The conversation seats Paulo Freire and bell hooks at the point where the book's refusal to colonize the reader becomes politically serious, and it presses a question the book's individualism may not answer. Freire's problem is who gets to name reality and who is reduced to an object inside someone else's epistemology — a question about power, about who materially holds the authority to name. The book takes the word colonize from that politics of liberation and applies it to a single reader's inner experience: do not replace the reader's meaning with the author's. The tension is that the book's answer is sovereignty, and sovereignty as the book states it is an individual capacity — my authorship, my recognition, my sufficiency — which can hold the epistemic half of Freire's question while leaving the structural half untouched. A reader with no material power to name their world is still, in the book's terms, sovereign over their own recognition. Whether that is the book's honest scope or an evasion of the power question is the blade Freire holds to it, and the conversation states plainly that the framework must be able to interrogate itself on exactly this point.
