# felt shift versus felt confirmation

The space-around survey records two limits on the book's verification standard, and recommends the book hold both rather than defend against them. First, the click can lie: processing-fluency research shows that ease of processing feels like truth — familiar, rhythmic, well-formed sentences produce a bodily yes that is about the sentence rather than the world. In a well-written book, some confirmations will be responses to the prose. The survey names the needed distinction: felt shift versus felt confirmation. Second, access is unevenly distributed: interoception research shows the inner signal varies widely in clarity and is often muted precisely where protective structure is thickest. For some readers the test returns nothing, and the survey's calibration note (drawn from Somatic Experiencing) is titration — small approaches, touch and retreat — with an explicit sentence of allowance for the reader the test does not yet work for.

The tension binds this vault as well as the book, and [[methods]] records the operational consequence: a well-formed note can read as true because it is well-formed, and the vault's verification checks structure and provenance while the somatic certification remains the author's.

## Substantiation

- Space-around, "Around the instrument," carries both counterweights and ranks them in its closing priority list as the additions that make the book's other claims more credible.

- Existing treatments in the book's architecture: the plain-prose passes (a counterfeit check by contrast) and the relational field as a second check on solitary readings.
