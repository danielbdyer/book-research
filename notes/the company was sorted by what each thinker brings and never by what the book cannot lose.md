# the company was sorted by what each thinker brings and never by what the book cannot lose

The corpus gathers the book's company into five groups and introduces each by what it carries in — practice technology, a model of emotion, a mechanism, a structural warrant, a referral network — explicitly not by what it holds true ([[the book's company of thinkers is grouped by contribution rather than by doctrine]]). That sort has a blind spot, and the author named it on 2026-08-05 on receiving the mesh grid: "I think the 29 thinkers were the wrong lens all along. This actually helps sharpen the focus of the theses of the book."

The twenty-nine are the largest of the five groups, the builders of enterable structure (atlas §IX.4). Sorting by contribution puts them in one room because each supplies a way of building enterable structure, and by that test the room is correct. What the test cannot see is that the room contains both the floor and the furniture. Eugene Gendlin is in it, and the felt shift is his; the author's own statement of 2026-08-04 makes the felt shift the book's single verification standard with everything else running downstream of it ([[the verification standard rests on one name and that name has no chair]]). Christopher Alexander is in it, and structure-preserving transformation is arguably the book's whole edit-ethic ([[structure-preserving transformation is a method-bearing claim rather than a directional one]]). Brian Eno is in it. The taxonomy has no way to say that the book survives losing one of those three and does not survive losing another.

The correction is not a replacement. A taxonomy of contribution answers a real question and is kept; a second axis is added beside it answering the other one — what happens to the book if this thinker leaves. The two are orthogonal, and the directive that installs the second is `ops/methodology/thesis-bearing and company.md`.

## Substantiation

- The author's words are quoted from his decision of 2026-08-05, recorded in `ops/decisions.md` and in the directive. The cosign of the instrument that produced the finding is partial and stated as such — "I co-sign it gently but it's about 65–75% co-signed — we're still evolving but I think it's a smart move" — so this note records the lens correction, which he states flatly, and not the individual reassignments, which he does not.
- The grid states the same defect as a property of rosters generally: "Most rosters are built from the base up and then mistaken for an argument" (`mesh grid: how to read the pyramid`). The vault's roster was built the other way — top-down from the corpus's own five rooms — and arrived at the same place, which suggests the defect belongs to sorting by contribution rather than to any particular assembly order.
- The twenty-nine are named at atlas §IX.4 and reproduced in `notes/interlocutors.md`: twenty-eight practitioners plus the book itself, which holds the axis position of selfhood cohering as container.
- What makes this a finding about the book rather than about the vault is that the corpus's own sort is the one being corrected. The five rooms are the corpus's arrangement (arc-gloss: the five rooms), and the vault adopted it. The correction therefore lands on the book's own description of its company, which is why it is a claim here rather than an operational note.
- One thing the correction does not license, recorded because the grid says it and the temptation is real: the company row is not a lesser standing. "This tier is not a problem and does not need pruning. It is the book's company… Its only failure mode is being mistaken for the argument" (`mesh grid: tier 5`). The second axis exists so that mistake stops being available in both directions.
