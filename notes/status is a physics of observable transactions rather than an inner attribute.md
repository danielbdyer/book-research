# status is a physics of observable transactions rather than an inner attribute

Keith Johnstone taught that status is something a person does and not something a person is: in every exchange the two parties run a seesaw in which one's rise is the other's fall, and the movement is legible from outside in eye contact, stillness or fidget, the claiming of space, and the position of the feet. His instruction to a player is to play status just above or just below a partner, which makes the otherwise invisible traffic between people operable and teachable. The account is deflationary by design. It reduces relation to adjustable transactions that can be watched and altered, with no inner mystery underneath, and it is this deflationary character that lets the reader learn to see status even where they cannot run one with a book.

The finding fills a gap the vault records on Johnstone's own chair, where the status material is noted as having no equivalent anywhere in the book: the corpus has a rich account of what happens inside a held container and nothing on the continuous, involuntary status transaction also happening in it. As a device the status frame ports to a book as analysis but not as practice, because a reader can be taught to read the seesaw yet has no partner on the page to transact with. Its failure mode, recorded separately, is that the same physics stripped of its liberatory frame becomes a manual for domination.
