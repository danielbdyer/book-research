# the maneuvers that change a person's frame require a live protected setting a book is not

Bateson describes how a change at the frame-generating level is actually induced, and the description rules a solitary book out of the work. The maneuvers confront a person's premises, make him act against them, demonstrate their contradiction, and induce caricature — and then seal every escape, so the loopholes a person would slip through are blocked one by one. The mechanism is an engineered set of contraries with exit closed, and Bateson stipulates it be run in "a controlled and protected setting," because the same double bind that can free a person can injure him. A book read alone supplies none of that container: it cannot watch for the reader's evasions, cannot block a loophole in real time, and cannot hold a protective frame around a person it has destabilized. The most powerful frame-changing method the region offers is therefore the least portable, needing exactly the live, responsive, protected setting the page is not.

The verdict is a bound on the method rather than a claim that the book can do nothing. A book can arrange contraries and invite a reader into them; what it cannot do is seal the escapes safely, which is the part of the method that does the frame-changing and the part that requires a person in the room.

## Substantiation

- The maneuvers and the protected-setting requirement are drawn from Bateson's 1971 essay, opened at primary depth by transmission dig 4; the clinical corpus in which the method was practised was not read, so the claim enters nascent and its deepening sits with the Bateson readings in `ops/reading queue.md`.
- The safety condition is not incidental: the failure Bateson names for an unprotected attempt at this level is the self-dissolution that psychiatry sometimes labels psychotic, which is why the setting must hold and why a book cannot be trusted to run the maneuvers alone.
- Held under `ops/methodology/authorial stance.md`: the finding bounds a device the book might reach for, and does not claim the book fails at anything it does not attempt.
