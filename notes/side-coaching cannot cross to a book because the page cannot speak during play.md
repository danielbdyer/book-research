# side-coaching cannot cross to a book because the page cannot speak during play

Viola Spolin's side-coaching is instruction a coach calls out during play without stopping it, so the player stays in the present moment and the correction arrives without a judgment that would break the flow. The dig finds the device the least portable of the lineage to a book read alone. Its essence is a live, timed, responsive intervention: the coach sees the moment and speaks into it while the play continues. A page can carry none of that, because it cannot speak during the reader's activity and cannot see the moment it would correct, and a margin note aimed at a reader mid-practice stops the very play it means to guide rather than accompanying it.

The finding bears directly on the book's own apparatus, which the corpus equips with inhabited margins that form a second book. Side-coaching is the closest theatre analogue to what those margins attempt, and the dig's verdict is that the analogue does not hold: the margin can annotate before or after an activity but cannot coach inside it. Side-coaching also carries its own failure mode even in the room, sliding into what the sources call the language of authority, so that the nagging correction reinstalls the approval Spolin's method exists to withdraw.
