# a durable perspective is a recognition whose value persists across contexts

The author names the thing he is after "a durable perspective," and the conversation unfolds the name into a plain criterion: a recognition counts as durable when it keeps disclosing value as it is carried across contexts, traditions, relationships, and practices. Durability on this account is not certainty. The test is not whether a recognition can be metaphysically proven, nor whether some tradition asserts it, but whether it continues to help a person see, participate, and act more truthfully when they meet reality from somewhere else. Because the criterion is survival-across-movement rather than demonstration, it functions as a signal of worth and not a proof of truth — the same standing the vault keeps for the book's convergences, which are held as company rather than corroboration. Its practical use is to separate a genuine resonance from a projection: a perspective that seems to hold everywhere only because its differences were flattened has not survived a crossing, while one that keeps disclosing value while the differences remain intact has.

The name is the author's coinage and is held as a candidate rather than a settled term; what the note asserts is the concern beneath it, that cross-contextual resilience is the mark the research trusts. The perspective is acquired rather than declared: it is what remains after the difference between traditions has been preserved, not selected in advance from a menu of ideas.
