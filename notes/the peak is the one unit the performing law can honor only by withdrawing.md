# the peak is the one unit the performing law can honor only by withdrawing

Two of the book's laws contradict each other at exactly one unit. The first law holds that every structural device makes the reading enact what the text explains, and it specifies pacing that is "most destabilizing at the peak." The second is the refusal both of the book's foundations share, Heidegger's account of truth as unconcealment and the Pratyabhijñā school's recognition, which holds that the breakthrough cannot be manufactured: "An apparatus can prepare; it cannot cause." A peak that performed itself would therefore be an engineered epiphany, and this is the one unit where obeying the first law breaks the second.

The corpus's staging already answers the contradiction, without stating the rule it is following. At the peak "the prose form itself thins; the margin voices go silent; and 'the sentence that eleven chapters have been finishing finally completes at a mirror'" (walked X), which hands the completion to the reader and withdraws the apparatus at the moment it would otherwise be working hardest. Recording that as a claim turns the peak's staging from a fact about style into a requirement of doctrine that any future draft inherits, because the more engineered the peak reads, the more it claims the power both foundations deny, so withdrawal is not one option among several but the only performance the law permits there.
