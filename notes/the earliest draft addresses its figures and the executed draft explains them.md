# the earliest draft addresses its figures and the executed draft explains them

The author names the earliest draft's one written chapter his high-water mark of voice, and the difference between that voice and the executed draft's reduces to a single grammatical operation. The mythic draft addresses its textual objects as present company: "Hello, ink. Thank you for coming. We see you not merely as medium, but as witness" (mythic-os: page 9); "The ink nodded" (page 12); "Page 6 is waiting with its palms open" (page 10). The executed draft keeps the same figure and converts address to exposition under a simile hedge: "as though the page were a kind of patient listener" (abridged ch2), with only two residual animations surviving unhedged ("the book wants to commend," ch8 and ch15).

The change from one draft to the other is a single operation on the sentence, and it runs in both directions, which is what makes it usable by a writer rather than merely descriptive of two documents. A drafting session that wants the earliest draft's voice back has an exact instruction: remove the simile hedge and speak to the object directly, so that a sentence about how the page is as though listening becomes a sentence addressed to the page. A session that prefers the executed draft's register can see exactly what that register cost, which is the standing of the ink and the page as company present in the room.

## Substantiation

- Fourteen unhedged animate instances were verified in the mythic extraction, against one hedged instance and two residual unhedged instances in the abridged; the specimens above are quoted from those extractions.
- Anyone can check this by counting sentences in which a textual object — ink, page, story, chapter — takes an animate verb with no hedge attached. The two drafts sit at opposite ends of that count.
- The earliest draft's single completed chapter is the document the author named his high-water mark of voice on submission, and the rest of that edition's description sits with it ([[source — the mythic operating system]]). The figure both drafts are handling — prose that reads as received by a patient listener rather than deposited by an author — carries the book's claim that a third presence is at work between writer and page ([[the ink that listens is the relational field at the scale of a page]]).
