# in cloud the body's certainty about up is confidently and fatally wrong

Instrument pilots are trained on a documented fact: in cloud, with no visual horizon, the vestibular sense of which way is up becomes confidently and fatally wrong, and the whole discipline of instrument flight is the practiced override of that bodily certainty by an external reference. The panel marked this as the one resonance on its list that tests the chain rather than accompanying it. It describes a domain where the body's countersignature kills, where its confidence peaks exactly where it is most wrong, and where the remedy is an instrument plus a trained cross-check rather than closer attention to the body. The test it puts to the book's bodily verification standard ([[nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned]]) is a boundary question the standard's defense has not been made to answer: for which class of judgments is the body competent, and what distinguishes that class from the vestibular class where it is not? It lands beside the live question the author has named, whether bodily verification can bear the weight the book puts on it.

The vault's readings answered part of the question and left part open, so the test is partly met rather than defeated: the body is competent at reporting that a sensation is present now and unreliable at reporting what caused a response ([[the confabulation finding exempts current sensations and targets reports of causes]]), while the interoception evidence keeps even the present-sensation exemption contested ([[attending to the body is not the same as reading it accurately]]).
