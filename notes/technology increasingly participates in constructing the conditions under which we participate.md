# technology increasingly participates in constructing the conditions under which we participate

The claim raises the contemporary attention problem above the level of distraction. The environments a person moves through increasingly compete to determine what they notice, what they desire, what they fear, what they value, what they remember, and what they believe matters — and these are the conditions that decide how, and whether, the person participates at all. So the consequential claim is not that technology distracts a person but that it participates in constructing the conditions of their participation, one level beneath any single act of attention. This makes the attention crisis ecological rather than a failure of individual willpower, and it is the widest frame of the technology material, the point at which it meets the book's diagnosis of the attention economy. The claim is the conversation's model-assisted synthesis, held as candidate and kept nascent until the writing reaches for it.

## Substantiation

- The conversation raises the claim above distraction: the issue "isn't merely" that "Technology distracts us," it is that "Technology increasingly participates in the construction of the conditions under which we participate," which it calls "a much more consequential claim" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: the ecology of attention).
- It lists what the environments compete to determine — "what we notice; what we desire; what we fear; what we value; what we remember; what we believe matters" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: the ecology of attention).
