# a partnership can be deliberately entered and deliberately left

The gesture asserts that partnerships have deliberate thresholds at both ends, and that leaving one well is a practice rather than a failure. The vault does not hold this. The relational material treats the field as a standing condition to be tended, and its account of endings is grief — the severing of a Sanctuary, treated as loss to be metabolised. An ending that is chosen and designed is a different object, and the corpus has no name for it.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- The word decouple carries a different sense everywhere else in the corpus. Every occurrence outside the earliest contents refers to the three loops releasing at the peak, as at atlas §V and throughout `abridged ch11`. The relational sense appears once, in the contents entry.
- The nearest held material runs the other way: the relational teaching is that most relationships break at withdrawal, and that the practical work of love is "the practice of staying" (abridged ch8).
- The chemistry has the components — Sever exists as a primitive, and forgiveness is recorded as an ordering of Sever and Release — but no molecule for a chosen ending.

## What would close it

Closing it means deciding whether a designed ending is a molecule the catalogue lacks or a case of grief already covered.
