# an interaction science term earns its place by its worked mechanism rather than its aura

The interaction sciences hand the book a vocabulary that is dangerous exactly because it sounds profound. Structural coupling, autopoiesis, and participatory sense-making are terms that sound significant apart from their content, which is the property Adorno's critique of a jargon of authenticity was built to detect. A book that cites them for that sound while leaving the causal chain unstated has, in the dig's phrase, produced "jargon in a lab coat" — the machinery of proof displayed without the proof. The discipline the borrowing requires is therefore exact: carry each term as a worked mechanism, stating what perturbs what and to what measured effect, or drop the word. A scientific term earns its place in the book only when its mechanism is spelled out, never when it is cited for the standing it lends.

The rule is a constraint on the book's own honesty rather than an attack from outside. The same digs that recover the mechanisms record that the mechanisms lose the first person at the book's altitude, so a term invoked without its chain imports the prestige and none of the limits the chain would also carry.
