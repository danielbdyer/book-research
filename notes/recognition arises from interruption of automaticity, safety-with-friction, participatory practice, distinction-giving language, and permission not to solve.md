# recognition arises from interruption of automaticity, safety-with-friction, participatory practice, distinction-giving language, and permission not to solve

The book names five recurring conditions under which recognition tends to occur, and it is careful about their status: they are patterns that keep appearing rather than a fixed procedure, and they are cultivated rather than caused. The five are an interruption of automaticity, in which something breaks the usual frame; safety carrying enough friction that what was hidden can be noticed; participatory practice rather than ideas alone, so the thing is met in experience; language that supplies distinctions, such as the difference between a story and an observation; and permission not to solve the experience, so that a contraction met with curiosity is not treated as a failure to be fixed. Held together, the five describe an ecology of recognition — conditions that reduce the noise so insight has room — rather than a machine that manufactures insight on demand.
