# the executed draft scripts the reader's recognition in the reader's own first person

The book's promised event — recognition arriving — is rehearsed on the page in the reader's own grammatical person. Immediately after a naming, the prose supplies the sentence the reader would say: "The name comes now as relief, not as instruction. Oh — that is what I have been doing" (abridged ch4). "Oh — I am stuck because I cannot Sever this connection that no longer serves me" (abridged ch5). At the practice chapters the same form carries the discrimination the standard requires: "I am angry. Not I should be angry because. Just I am angry" (abridged ch14).

These scripted sentences bear directly on the book's test for whether anything is true, which is whether the reader's own body registers a shift. Each scripted sentence shows what an arrived recognition sounds like from inside: it comes as relief rather than as an instruction to carry out, and it states a plain discrimination rather than an explanation of why the discrimination holds. That is as close as the executed prose comes to teaching a reader the difference between a real shift and the bodily yes that well-formed sentences can produce on their own.

## Substantiation

- Seven sites were verified in the extracted text, among them abridged ch4 twice, ch5 twice, and ch14; the specimens above are quoted from that extraction.
- Anyone can check this by finding every "Oh —" in the draft: each opens a first-person sentence belonging to the reader rather than to the author, placed immediately after a naming. An "Oh —" doing anything else is the exception to look for.
- The problem these sentences work against is recorded in the corpus's own survey of the book's surroundings, which reports that familiar, rhythmic, well-formed prose produces a bodily yes about the sentence rather than about the world ([[felt shift versus felt confirmation]]). The script's preference for a bare discrimination over an explanation runs in the same direction as Gendlin's own definition, which certifies a symbolization by the movement it produces in the process rather than by any match between the words and a fact ([[the felt shift certifies carrying forward rather than correspondence]]).
