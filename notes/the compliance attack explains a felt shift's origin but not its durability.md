# the compliance attack explains a felt shift's origin but not its durability

The compliance case is strongest against the felt shift's origin and has no account of its persistence, and the boundary is the attack's own. Orne's strongest form predicts that a demand-produced effect vanishes once the demand is removed, which means the one thing an instructed faker cannot fake is a durable, out-of-situation, third-party-verified change. Two bodies of evidence sit outside the demand field and the attack cannot generate them. The recorded casualties of high-demand transformational rooms — roughly nineteen percent enduring harm among encounter-group participants (Lieberman and Yalom), extended to the large-group awareness trainings — are behavioral outcomes demand does not produce; a wish to please the instructor manufactures a positive report, not a lasting injury. And distress reported against the gradient of a setting that demands benefit (Britton's work on adverse contemplative experience) runs the wrong way for pure compliance, which cannot generate a report that contradicts what the room wants. So the attack removes the felt shift's standing as self-sufficient evidence of transmission without touching the existence of durable change measured outside the room.

The honest closing is that the record cannot yet rule how much durable, externally verified change this genre produces, because the studies that would settle it — active controls, blinding, delayed follow-up administered by someone who did not induce the effect — have mostly not been run. The claim is a boundary on the attack, not a defense of efficacy.

## Substantiation

- The dig records the boundary as the remainder that survives the attack unmodified: the attack explains origin, not durability, and the casualties and against-the-gradient distress are "exactly what demand cannot produce" (transmission dig 6: REMAINDER). This note carries that boundary and its honest limit — the settling studies are absent.
- Kirsch cuts both ways here in a way that reinforces the boundary: response-expectancy effects register as real behavioral and physiological change, so "produced by expectancy" does not entail "unreal." That reading is homed to the counterfeit tension and is not re-argued here ([[felt shift versus felt confirmation]]); this note uses only its consequence, that a manufactured origin does not settle reality or durability.
- The depth is thin and stated: dig 6 read no primary here, so the durability question rests on scholarship and is marked `nascent`. Whether response-expectancy effects persist after the inducing situation is removed is the decisive queued reading (dig 6, READINGS TO QUEUE item 3); the est/LGAT adverse-effects record and Britton's taxonomy are queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
- The consequence for the book is a division of evidence: the felt shift certifies movement in the room, and the claim that a change is durable and real routes to a check the room cannot supply — which is the treatment the compliance tension carries.
