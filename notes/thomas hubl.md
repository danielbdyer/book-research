# thomas hübl

Thomas Hübl teaches a practice addressed to trauma held collectively and across generations rather than in one person's history. His central structural claim is that a rupture which was never processed does not leave damage behind so much as a gap: a place where something should have developed and did not, an absence in the field rather than a wound in it. That absence is what transmits — descendants inherit not the event but the unmetabolized space around it, which shows up as an inability rather than an injury.

He is currently one of eleven contemporary teachers in the corpus's survey, in the sub-position it calls the Externalizers, seated for collective trauma as inherited weather (atlas §IX.5).

## The correspondence

The book's own account of what is wrong with a person is that nothing is broken: there is privation, an absence where something should be, which is why the diagnosis names the remedy at the same time it names the trouble ([[suffering is contraction never corruption]]). The book's diagnostic sentence for a compound case runs *this is obligation privated of sanctuary* — the difficulty identified by naming what is not there.

That is Hübl's shape, and he got to it through trauma work with no Kashmir Śaiva metaphysics under him. The mesh grid: "Same shape, different road, no shared teacher. This is a genuine found correspondence and it is the strongest thing the coaches thread contributes" (`mesh grid: the coaches`). The finding is at [[what was never metabolized lives on as absence and hubl reached it through inherited trauma]].

What his current placement cannot record is exactly this. Filing him among the eleven says what he talks about. It has no way to say that he independently reached the concept the book's whole diagnostic apparatus is built on, which is a different order of fact.

## What he would refuse

Derived rather than sourced; no primary work has been read in this vault. The refusal that bears hardest is about who does the metabolizing. Hübl's practice is collective by design — the absence is held and processed in a group, with a facilitator, over time, and his argument is that individual work cannot reach what was never individual in the first place. A book read alone is proposing to address, in solitude, the thing he says only company can metabolize.

That objection lands on the book's form rather than on its concept, which makes it more serious rather than less. The vault already holds the finding that the book's native genre is a letter read alone ([[the book's native genre is the letter from a friend read alone]]), and Hübl's position is that some of what the book addresses cannot be reached there.

He would also, on his own terms, refuse the compression his position receives in the corpus. He is summarized in one clause as inherited weather; his account is a full theory of transmission with a practice attached, and the survey's two lines are the corpus's compression of him rather than his statement.

## What the evaluation still requires

The primary reading, with three questions: whether the absence in his account is structurally the same as privation or merely described in the same words; whether he holds the collective requirement as strict or as a matter of degree; and how his account relates to the position the vault carries as the material voice, since inherited trauma and structural arrangement are both external locations for suffering but not the same one (hartmut rosa, andrea jain).
