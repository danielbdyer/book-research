# the kashmir shaiva tradition's five principles assemble into one circuit

The reframe conversation gathers the source tradition's operators into a single recurring circuit. Read by their plain functions, the five are illumination (prakāśa, the light in which anything appears), reflexive awareness (vimarśa, awareness recognizing itself), the pulse (spanda, the vibration of consciousness), contraction and expansion (saṅkoca and vikāsa, the two movements of that pulse), and recognition (pratyabhijñā, the seeing of what was already so). The conversation compresses "an astonishing amount" of itself into one pattern running through them, from a bounded vantage through encounter and reflexive noticing to recognition and renewal, then back to a new vantage — a loop rather than a line.

The circuit is offered as a resonance, not an identity. The conversation states the limit in its own voice: the pattern holds "not as a claim that the Sanskrit concepts literally map one-to-one onto the whole model" but "as a resonant generative grammar." The five-term ordering named here — illumination, reflexive awareness, pulse, contraction and expansion, recognition — is the reframe's arrangement of operators the vault already holds separately, and it is kept as a candidate grammar under the vault's rule that no single scheme becomes sovereign over what it organizes.
