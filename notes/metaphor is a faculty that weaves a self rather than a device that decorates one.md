# metaphor is a faculty that weaves a self rather than a device that decorates one

The gesture asserts that metaphor is a faculty of self-construction: a person weaves an identity by the figures they reach for, and memory is metaphoric before it is propositional. The vault does not hold this. Every treatment of metaphor in the corpus is a treatment of the book's own image system — the eight paired figures the atlas records at §X.5 — which is a claim about how the book works on a reader, not about how a reader works on themselves. The two are close enough to be confused and are not the same proposition.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- Metaphor appears in six of the seven sources, and in each of them it names the book's device rather than the reader's faculty. The image system is inventoried at atlas §X.5 and carried in [[figure glossary]].
- The gesture's own statement is its subtitle alone, "Metaphor, Memory, and Self-Weaving" (mythic-os: contents). The written chapter comes nearest at `mythic-os: page 12B`, where the thread figure is said to have become the reader rather than to describe them: "You were never just holding it. You were becoming it."
- The nearest thing the vault holds is the recognition doctrine, in which naming converts bondage into instrument. Whether metaphor is a special case of that or a separate faculty is exactly what is unexplored.

## What would close it

Closing it means deciding whether the book has a theory of the reader's own figurative life, or only a set of figures it uses on them.
