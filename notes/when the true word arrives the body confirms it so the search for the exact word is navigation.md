# when the true word arrives the body confirms it so the search for the exact word is navigation

There is a moment every honestly-speaking person knows: a word is tried and it is almost right, and then the true one lands and something in the chest settles, a small click of rightness no cleverness can fake. Truth has a felt receipt. A person is not left to judge the true word by argument alone; the body gives its confirmation, quietly, when the saying finally fits the sense. This is why the search for the exact word is not fussiness but navigation, and the "yes" that arrives with the right word is aliveness reporting that the saying has touched the thing itself.

## Substantiation

- Two accounts couple the word to the body. Gendlin, read at the primary text: when a word, phrase, or image "fits exactly" it provides a "handle," and "the whole felt sense stirs just slightly and eases a little. This is a signal, as if it said: 'This is right'" — while a word that does not fit gives nothing (Focusing, chs. 4–5). The later-Heidegger pole is still owed: reading Stefan George's line "let no thing be where the word breaks off," Heidegger holds that the word first lets the thing be present rather than labeling a thing already there, so the true word's arrival is an event ("The Nature of Language," On the Way to Language; from memory, grounding owed); and Abhinavagupta's camatkāra, the savored flash when word and real coincide, keeps company (from memory, grounding owed). Cited by address.
- Guard kept, and load-bearing: this sits one inch from the register's own caution that a reader's bodily assent can respond to fluent prose rather than to a true claim. Gendlin's specificity rides inside the claim — the click is a specific easing, not the warmth of a phrase one happens to like, and fluency is not the felt shift. Heidegger, for his part, will not reduce the word's power to a feeling; the coming-into-presence is the point.
- Standing: privated — the Gendlin pole read at the primary text (Focusing chs. 4–5; the record is in `archive/`); the later-Heidegger and Abhinavagupta poles owed. This one touches the making of the book, where a word is perfected until the body says yes.
