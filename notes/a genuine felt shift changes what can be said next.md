# a genuine felt shift changes what can be said next

The defense the felt-shift standard needs was written by its own source, in the philosophical work almost nobody reads behind the popular one. Gendlin's technical name for the genuine article is *referent movement*, and his definition makes it a step rather than a feeling: "not only a change but a directly experienced 'give' or 'movement' which feels right and welcome. Its tremendous importance lies in the fact that after such a referent movement (even very small), the implicit meanings are now different. The 'scenery,' as it were, which one confronts, changes" — and, decisively, "after referent movement, the meanings and symbolizations one formulates are different. The relevant considerations are different" (A Theory of Personality Change, 1964). The mechanism beneath it is *carrying forward*: "symbols (or events) occur to interact with already implicitly functioning aspects of ongoing experiencing," and "to explicate is to carry forward a bodily felt process" (same work). A genuine shift is generative — something new becomes sayable that was not sayable before.

This converts the counterfeit problem from a threat into a diagnostic, because the counterfeit fails exactly this test. Bodily assent produced by fluent prose is agreement with a sentence, and agreement leaves the sayable where it was: nothing new can be said next, only the sentence said back. Gendlin describes the unmoved state directly, in the passage about talking at oneself: "one has recited all the good reasons… the same unchanged felt referent is still there… From this lack of referent movement, one knows that nothing has really changed" — and without the movement, "what is said is 'merely' talk, 'merely' intellectualization… 'merely' reporting" (1964). The difference between a shift and a shiver is therefore checkable in the minutes after: did the scenery change, or did the reader just feel good about a sentence? Can they now say something about their own situation that they could not say before the page, or only repeat the page?

Two boundary stones from the same texts keep the claim honest. The shift is not an emotion — "A felt sense is not an emotion," and emotions can "'skip' the incomplete implicit meanings… like an animal whose response to hunger is to bite itself in the leg" (Focusing; 1964) — so intensity of feeling is no evidence at all. And a genuine shift can arrive with worse content and still release: "Logically, you should feel worse. Yet you don't… your body feels better, more free, released" (Focusing) — which cleanly separates the shift from pleasure, the counterfeit's native register.

## Substantiation

- The step-language is consistent from the 1964 formulation to the late one: "'Implying' may lead to an actual occurring, which could 'carry the implying forward.' This term 'carrying forward' is the relationship between what now occurs and what 'was implied'" (A New Way of Thinking, 2014).
- Insight is demoted to by-product in the primary text — "insight and better understanding are the results, the by-products, of this process" (1964) — which blocks the fluent reader's favorite counterfeit: understanding acquired, nothing carried forward.
- Access limits recorded: A Process Model's full text was not accessible this round, and several Gendlin Online Library pages returned errors; the claims above rest on the verified 1964 paper, the full Focusing text, and the 2014 essay.
