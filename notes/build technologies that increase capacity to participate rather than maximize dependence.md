# build technologies that increase capacity to participate rather than maximize dependence

The claim turns the book's participation criterion into a directive for making technology. A design ethic follows from the philosophy: a technology should be built to increase the user's capacity to participate rather than to maximize the user's dependence on the technology. Increased participation and maximized dependence are the two ends a design can be aimed at, and the ethic chooses the first against the second. The conversation frames this as a civilizational extension of the book's philosophy, which places it in the book's forward, technology-facing material rather than at its center. The same directive is what the conversation turns on the vault's own system when it asks whether the ontology increases the author's capacity to participate in the work rather than whether it is elegant. The claim is the conversation's model-assisted synthesis, held as candidate and kept nascent until the writing reaches for it.
