# the improv method franchised into a product reinstalls the approval it was built to remove

Spolin's games were designed to withdraw the evaluating gaze so that doing could replace performing-for. The dig records what became of the method once it was franchised into commercial theatre and corporate applied-improv training: it produced its opposite. Spontaneity, made a reproducible product, became a technique to be delivered on schedule; the accepting move Johnstone named yes-and hardened into a management cliche; and approval, the exact force Spolin removed, re-entered the room as the laugh a scene now plays for. The failure is not a corruption imported from outside but the method turning into its own inversion under the pressure to be taught, sold, and repeated.

The finding matters to the book as a warning about its own reproduction. The book's practice curriculum descends from this facilitation craft, and the case shows a craft for un-selfconscious doing becoming, at scale, a product that reinstalls self-conscious performance. It rhymes with two propositions the vault already holds: that a technique detached from the ontology beneath it degrades into a move performed on people, and that a spontaneous social form decays the moment it is organized into a repeatable system.
