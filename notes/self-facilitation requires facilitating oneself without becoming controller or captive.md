# self-facilitation requires facilitating oneself without becoming controller or captive

The reframe conversation specifies self-facilitation as a stance held between two failures. The first failure is control: the person takes command of themselves as an object to be directed, which reinstates the management frame self-facilitation refuses and forecloses the becoming it exists to allow. The second failure is captivity: the person is taken over by whatever arises and surrenders the point from which they participate, which abandons authorship altogether. Self-facilitation requires facilitating oneself without either — remaining authorially present while not seizing command — so that the person stays the author of their becoming without controlling it. This restates the reframe's central question, whether a person can be deeply influenced without being dispossessed of themselves, at the scale of a person's relation to their own arising, recorded at [[the deepest question is whether i can be deeply influenced without becoming dispossessed of myself]]; the controller loses the influence and the captive loses the self, and self-facilitation is the posture that keeps both.
