# deceiving a reader into the truth requires knowing his destination, a warrant a book without authority has renounced

The maieutic deception is not portable to a relation of equals. "Deceiving into the truth" requires the author to know the destination in advance and to steer the reader there covertly; the deception is benevolent but it is still manipulation, and its only warrant is the author's certainty of where the reader must arrive. A facilitator writing without authority, who does not claim to know the reader's destination, has no such warrant, so the same device that is licensed for Kierkegaard is unlicensed for a participatory project. Stated at full strength: indirect communication as built is asymmetric and manipulative by design, and its ethics rest on a religious certainty a participatory book has renounced — which means a project can keep the machine or keep the humility, but not both unexamined. The finding rests on secondary commentary, the primaries unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
