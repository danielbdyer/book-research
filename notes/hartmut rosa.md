# hartmut rosa

Hartmut Rosa is a sociologist whose diagnosis of modernity runs: social acceleration produces alienation — a world gone mute, with the points of contact between self and world frozen. His name for the alternative is resonance: a mode of relation in which self and world reach and change each other. The clause that matters most for the book is his insistence that resonance is constitutively uncontrollable — it cannot be manufactured, purchased, or guaranteed, only prepared for. The space-around survey ranks this "first in this entire survey for sheer fit." He is not yet seated; this note is the candidate file.

The fit is with the book's closing image of the tuning fork. The survey states the connection in its own words: "the book can build every condition for resonance and still cannot promise it, because resonance that could be promised would not be resonance," and it judges that this admission, "made once and plainly, would be the most trust-building sentence in the book" (space-around: around the honesty). Rosa is the completed sociological theory standing behind that sentence.
