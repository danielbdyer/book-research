# a vocabulary can assist recognition but cannot own it

A vocabulary can assist a person toward their own recognition, and it cannot own that recognition or stand as its authority. The reframe conversation reaches this as the answer to a question it poses — who gets to determine what another person's experience means — and the answer is a refusal: no one, merely because they hold a better vocabulary. The claim guards the book's whole relation to its reader. The book supplies words that may help a reader recognize something already theirs, and the moment those words are taken to license authority over what the reader recognizes, the assistance has turned into appropriation. It is the book's naming discipline stated from the reader's side: a name helps, and a name does not decide.

## Substantiation

The question and its answer, in the conversation's words under the heading "The Central Political/Ethical Question": "Who gets to determine what another person's experience means? ... No one should be given that authority merely because they possess a better vocabulary" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, line 6911). The distinction is then stated as a pair: "A vocabulary can assist recognition. It cannot own it" (lines 6921–6923).
