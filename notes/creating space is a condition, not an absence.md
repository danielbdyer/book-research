# creating space is a condition, not an absence

The reframe conversation corrects a likely misreading of facilitative restraint. When a facilitator declines to answer, slows down, asks a better question, tolerates silence, refuses premature interpretation, or allows contradiction, this is not the absence of facilitation but a specific condition being created. Space is something made rather than something left over. The conversation draws a paradox from this: because created space is itself an intervention, the most skillful facilitation can be the facilitation that appears to do the least — a deliberate act a superficial account would mistake for passivity.

## Substantiation

- The reframe conversation states that "Space is not absence. Space is a condition," and lists the ways a facilitator makes it (not answering, slowing down, asking a better question, tolerating silence, refusing premature interpretation, allowing contradiction). It states the paradox that follows: "Sometimes the most skillful facilitation is the facilitation that appears to do the least" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: creating space).
