# the stance is specified as facets held together rather than steps taken in order

The executed draft names six components of the Stance and marks their relation explicitly: "The Stance has the following components, which are not steps but facets" (abridged ch13). The distinction is load-bearing for a practice curriculum, because a sequence can be performed in order and reported as done, while a set of simultaneous conditions can only be occupied or not.

The six, with the instruction each carries. **Available** — reachable and unblocked rather than performing receptivity: "if something asks for you, you can show up." **Discerning** — open but not indiscriminately, able to tell what to receive and what to decline. **Held but not holding** — internally gathered without gripping. **Becoming and being** — in motion and at rest at once, with the motion arising from the rest. **Felt and revealing** — in contact with one's own felt sense and willing to let it become visible, without leading with the revelation. **Present without agenda** — not trying to fix, advise, console, perform, or impress: "you are here for what is here. If something needs to be done, you will do it. The doing arises from the presence, not the other way around" (abridged ch13).

The draft supplies a recognition test in place of a completion test, and states it as a list of negations rather than as a signal to look for: the Stance "is not trying. It is not performing. It is not waiting to speak. It is not assessing. It is not impressing. It is not even quite listening, in the sense of listening for. It is being there for what is there." The unit's practice instruction is thirty seconds long and asks the practitioner to notice a change rather than to reach a state: "no agenda. No fixing. Just here. Notice what changes when you stop trying to make something happen."

## Substantiation

- Abridged ch13 is the only source that enumerates the six; the treatment names three of them in passing — "presence without agenda; available, discerning, held-not-holding" — and adds two figures the executed prose drops, Scharmer's presencing and the Hollow Bone (arc-gloss: ch13). Atlas §VII.1 seats the Stance at the head of the practice curriculum without listing components.
- Two of the six are stated in the vocabulary of the container anatomy rather than in plain terms — "the Knife is operating, even in the receptivity"; "the Vessel is intact; the Vessel is not clenched" — using the corner names both prose drafts carry and the founding sources veto ([[the prose drafts use the vetoed triad names]]).
- The executed draft names one external source inside the unit, Scharmer's presencing at the bottom of the U-curve, and describes it as "one face of this" rather than as the thing itself (abridged ch13).
- The facets are given without a verification condition. The corpus's general standard would supply one — the felt shift — but the unit does not invoke it, and the thirty-second practice asks the reader to notice a change without saying what change counts.
