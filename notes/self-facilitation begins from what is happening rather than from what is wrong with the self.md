# self-facilitation begins from what is happening rather than from what is wrong with the self

The reframe conversation gives self-facilitation its opening move and states it as a substitution of one question for another. The practice begins from the question of what is happening — a perception of what is actually present — rather than from the question of what is wrong, which presumes a fault to be located and corrected. Beneath the substitution sits a prior refusal: the self is not a project, so it is not a thing to be fixed toward a specification, and the question of what is wrong imports exactly the project frame the practice declines. The claim carries the book's theory of suffering inward, where the book already refuses the corruption reading and holds that nothing in a person is broken, recorded at [[suffering is contraction never corruption]]; asking what is wrong reinstalls the broken-thing assumption, and asking what is happening does not. What follows is that self-facilitation's first act is perceptual rather than corrective, and it belongs with the book's account of change as an uncovering of what is present rather than an addition of what is missing.
