# the basic posture is describable as dissociation and the harm literature is unread

The book's central posture is held but not holding, available and discerning: the gaze outward with attention resting inward. A dozen traditions are reported converging on it ([[unrelated traditions converge on presence without agenda while remaining available to act]]). The same description, read clinically, is a symptom. Depersonalization and derealization are characterized by exactly an observing self standing at a slight remove from experience, and that literature treats the observer's position as something to be treated rather than attained.

Beside it sits an empirical body the book has more direct exposure to: Willoughby Britton's research on the adverse effects of contemplative practice, which documents deadening, flattened affect, and detachment as outcomes rather than as failures of technique. The mesh grid's assessment is the sharpest sentence it writes about the book: "For a book that opens reconsolidation windows and writes an Afterglow law, the harm literature is not optional. Its absence is currently the book's most defensible criticism" (`mesh grid: against C3`).

The exposure is specific rather than general. A book that describes the multi-step arc of a psychedelic experience without a substance, walks a reader through deliberate destabilization at the peak, and prescribes a practice curriculum is making the kind of proposal the adverse-effects literature exists to evaluate. That the book postulates no efficacy is the author's recorded stance and it is genuinely load-bearing here — the vault does not claim this works — but declining to claim benefit is not the same as having considered harm, and the two are easy to run together.

## Substantiation

- Stated on the grid's authority and `privated`: neither Britton's work nor the depersonalization literature has been read in this vault, and Britton has no note here. The reading is fourth in the ranked holes at `scripts/queries/lineage-sort.sh`, where the grid calls it "the easiest to fix."
- The posture the objection aims at is recorded at [[unrelated traditions converge on presence without agenda while remaining available to act]], where a dozen vocabularies orbit one description and no source records a cost.
- The vault holds one adjacent finding that cuts the same way from inside the book's own foundations: both traditions the book stands on refuse the manufactured epiphany ([[both poles of the convergence refuse the engineered epiphany]]). A posture produced by an apparatus is, in their terms, the apparatus working. That is a doctrinal objection to producing the state; the harm literature is an empirical objection to what the produced state does afterward, and they are not the same argument.
- The grid pairs this hole with a second one and the pairing is the useful part: the same clinical literature that documents contemplative harm also supplies the diagnostic for telling a reader in dissolution from a reader in regression ([[the book cannot tell dissolution from regression and neither can its reader]]). One reading pass would populate both, which is why the grid's revised map raises the priority of both together.
- What holding this tension does not require, recorded because the stance governs: the vault is not thereby claiming the practice causes harm, any more than it claims the practice works. `ops/methodology/authorial stance.md` rules both out. What is claimed here is narrower and is about the book's completeness rather than its efficacy — a proposal of this shape has a standing literature addressed to it, and the book has not met it.
