# every locus is potentially revelatory and necessarily partial — partial truths become dangerous mistaken for total truths

The reframe conversation states the deepest constraint on its ecology as a pair. Every locus is potentially revelatory: a standpoint can be illuminating, generative, developmentally apt, empirically useful, or spiritually profound. Every locus is also necessarily partial: whatever it discloses, it does not exhaust the phenomenon. The danger the conversation names is specific — a partial truth becomes dangerous when it is mistaken for a total one, when a standpoint that discloses something real is taken to disclose everything. The constraint is stronger than a tolerance that says everything is equally true, because it keeps discernment: a standpoint can be judged illuminating and still be held as incomplete, which is what lets two loci be entertained at once without either being crowned or dismissed.

The claim is the working form of the book's refusal to let any tradition become the master account. It converts that refusal from a prohibition into a practice: enter each field carefully enough to find both what becomes visible there and what disappears from view.

## Substantiation

- The pair, in the conversation's words: "Every locus is potentially revelatory and necessarily partial" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, line 2934; restated as the ecology's "deepest constraint" at line 4165).
- The danger and its source: the formulation is credited to Wilber's four-quadrant model, whose "great contribution" the conversation names as the insistence that "partial truths become dangerous when mistaken for total truths" (line 2924). The conversation prefers it to the weaker "Everything is equally true" because it "allows genuine discernment" — a perspective can be "illuminating," "generative," "developmentally appropriate," "empirically useful," "spiritually profound," and still "not exhaust the phenomenon" (lines 2930-2948).
- Standing: reached in the conversation's shared portion as an axiom of the ecology; held as candidate material and kept nascent until the writing reaches for it.
