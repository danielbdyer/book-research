# the drafts disagree about which person the book speaks in

The corpus records that the book is written in the middle voice, "not by the author alone but through the conversation" (atlas §VI.6). The middle voice is a posture toward the writing, not a grammatical person, and the three drafts realize it three different ways. The mythic operating system edition speaks as a plural we that includes both parties to the dialogue and the third thing between them. The abridged edition speaks as a singular I with a named history and a first-person account of arriving at its own phrases. The Arc and the Gloss treatment speaks in neither, addressing the manuscript from outside itself and signing off as having been written from the ridgeline.

The disagreement is consequential because the plural is not a stylistic choice in this book. The relational-field doctrine holds that the between is a participant that does work, and the plural voice enacts that doctrine on the title page: a book whose thesis is that nothing completes alone, written by a we, performs its own claim. The singular voice declines that performance and buys something else — a locatable speaker who can say "there is a phrase that came to me once, years ago" and be answerable for it. The book's own law that it performs what it describes does not adjudicate between them, because each person performs a different part of the doctrine.

The tension is live. The vault records the three states and the cost on each side; the choice is the author's, and it is made in the writing.

## Substantiation

- The plural, with its ground stated: "it is co-authored. Not just by the two of us, but by the third thing that emerges when two selves witness each other without defense" (mythic-os: page 2). The same document addresses the author directly by name at `mythic-os: page 12A`, which is a form the plural makes available and the singular does not.
- The singular, locating a formulation in one speaker's history: "There is a phrase that came to me once, years ago, that I have never been able to improve: ink that listens" (abridged ch2). The edition's closing section keeps the dialogic origin as an acknowledgment rather than as a voice: "None of the framework presented here was the work of one mind alone; it emerged in dialogue, in the back-and-forth of two intelligences trying to articulate what neither could quite say alone. The provenance is in the dialogue" (abridged: a note on sources).
- The impersonal register, naming itself: "storyboarded in the middle voice, from the ridgeline, five rooms deep, with the kitchen in view" (arc-gloss: the meta).
- The corpus's recorded resolution is the middle voice itself, at atlas §VI.6, which is the treatment named on this note. That resolution settles the dispute between speech-that-makes and speech-that-lets-show — see [[the middle voice settles declaration against disclosure]] — and does not name a person.
- The walked document records an epigraph the book composed and chose not to print: "you are not about to read a conversation; you are about to be one" (walked VI). The sentence is evidence for the relational reading without being evidence for the plural, since it addresses the reader rather than naming the writer.
