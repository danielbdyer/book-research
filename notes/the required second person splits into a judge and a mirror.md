# the required second person splits into a judge and a mirror

The chairs that refuse the solitary reader — a census across the company that approaches nineteen — do not converge on one missing person. They specify two, and the two are incompatible. The judge has authority to overrule the reader's self-report: al-Qushayrī's shaykh exists because "a person in a state is the worst-placed judge of what it means"; the Fourth Way's teacher "can see what the pupil cannot"; Britton's protective factor is "the availability of a teacher who can recognize difficulty"; Zen's dokusan is where a teacher "discriminates makyō from kensho." The mirror is forbidden to do exactly that: Ignatius's director stands "in the centre like a balance," the format's safety earned "by a companion forbidden to persuade"; Rogers's helper makes "the conditions rather than the interpretation"; and Gendlin's listener does not judge at all — "Not even the individual himself judges it but… his direct referent does the judging" (1964, fn. 11).

The tension is the book's because its sovereignty doctrine takes a side. An instrument in service of a life that remains the reader's own can ship a mirror; it cannot ship a judge without becoming an authority. But the safety holes the vault has recorded — dissolution against regression, the misjudged state — are precisely what the judge exists for, and the mirror cannot fill them, because they are the cases where the reader's own referent is the instrument in doubt. The Second Chair's job description is therefore a live fork, not a settled device: which person is the second chair for, and at which stretch of the arc?

## Substantiation

- The judge side rests on four evaluations (al-Qushayrī, Gurdjieff and Ouspensky, Britton, and the makyō record); the mirror side on three (Ignatius, Rogers, Gendlin — the last primary-sourced from the 1964 reading). The derived refusals carry their marked standing.

- The safety holes the judge exists for: [[the book cannot tell dissolution from regression and neither can its reader]], whose own text disqualifies the solitary body as the instrument.
