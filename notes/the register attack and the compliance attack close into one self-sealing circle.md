# the register attack and the compliance attack close into one self-sealing circle

The two attacks the adversary chamber seats are not independent; they interlock into a single loop that needs no true content to keep running. The register attack supplies the first turn: haloed prose raises the reader's expectation that a shift belongs here (aura raises expectancy). The compliance mechanism supplies the second: the expectancy manufactures the experience, self-confirmingly, so the shift arrives whether or not the content did (Kirsch). The situation supplies the third: the high-demand frame secures the report and suppresses its disconfirmation, so the shift is reported and its absence is not (Orne, Britton). And the report supplies the return: taken as proof of depth, it recharges the aura and deepens the next reader's expectancy. Aura, then expectancy, then experience, then report, then aura again — a circle that is self-sealing because each stage produces the input the next stage needs.

The finding that makes this dangerous rather than merely elegant is where the circle turns. The felt-shift standard was installed to protect the reader *against* mere assent, and it is the exact bearing the loop rotates on, because it instructs the reader to seek an inner change and to trust that change as verification. The book's central safeguard is, at full strength, the mechanism the objection needs.

## Substantiation

- The dig states the loop once and names it the chamber's central artifact: aura raises expectancy (Adorno), expectancy manufactures the experience (Kirsch), demand secures the report (Orne, Britton), the report recharges the aura (transmission dig 6: ARTIFACT D). This note reproduces the four-stage structure and the finding that the felt-shift standard is the bearing it turns on.
- This claim is distinct from the isomorph that the two attacks name one defect. That the register attack's non-falsifiable interiority and the compliance attack's demand characteristic are the same defect reached from two disciplines is a static correspondence, homed at [[both attacks on the standard blame the absence of correction by another person]]; this note asserts the dynamic — that the two, coupled, form a feedback loop — which is a different proposition about the same pair.
- The depth is thin and stated: none of Adorno, Kirsch, Orne, or Britton was read at the primary in dig 6, so the loop stands on scholarship and is marked `nascent`. Each stage's primary reading is queued (dig 6, READINGS TO QUEUE, items 1–4).
- The loop has a documented exit, recorded separately: it accounts for the shift's origin and not its durability, and an external, delayed, other-administered check is the one input the circle cannot generate from inside itself ([[the compliance attack explains a felt shift's origin but not its durability]]).
