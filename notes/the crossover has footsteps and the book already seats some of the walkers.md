# the crossover has footsteps and the book already seats some of the walkers

The author has named what may be the book's core thesis, and this note records his words first: "these thinkers give illumination to a particular aspect of human reality that I think is underdeveloped" (author statement, 2026-08-03). The aspect, as the vault understands it from everything held so far: the deepest human change is a disclosure of what is already present, not an acquisition and not a repair. He asked who else stands around this crossover — who is for it, who is against it, who develops it, and whose footsteps he walks in. This is the survey from the vault's knowledge; each name that matters is queued for a real evaluation in `ops/queue/candidates.md`.

**The footsteps.** The nearest single precedent is Henry Corbin — Heidegger's first French translator, who carried the hermeneutics of unveiling from Freiburg into Persian illuminationist Sufism and said so plainly in "From Heidegger to Suhrawardī." He is the one figure on record who walked the author's exact line, from aletheia into an Eastern tradition of light and disclosure — with the instructive difference that Corbin went esoteric where this book goes householder. Behind him: J. L. Mehta, the founding scholar of the India–Heidegger encounter; John Caputo, whose study of the mystical element in Heidegger established that Gelassenheit is Meister Eckhart's own word — meaning the book's Eckhart seat already touches Heidegger's deepest borrowing; and the Kyoto School, where Nishida (already seated in the book's company) founded the tradition, Nishitani studied under Heidegger himself, and Ueda Shizuteru wrote the Eckhart–Zen comparison. Reinhard May's study of Heidegger's East Asian sources argues the traffic ran both ways. Deeper still stand the West's own remembering-doctrines — Plato's anamnesis, Augustine's memoria, the "you were within me and I was outside" of Confessions X — and Heidegger's Parmenides lectures on lēthē are the hinge where he touches them.

**The developers.** The recognition school now has a serious philosophical literature: Torella's edition of Utpaladeva and Isabelle Ratié's studies of self and other in Pratyabhijñā; David Peter Lawrence, who carries recognition into Western argumentative form; Muller-Ortega and Dyczkowski on Abhinavagupta and vibration; Loriliai Biernacki, who brings Abhinavagupta into contemporary panentheism and new materialism; Christopher Wallis as the scholar-practitioner bridge. On the phenomenology side, comparative work pairing Michel Henry's self-manifestation with vimarśa is beginning to exist. No one on record has put Heidegger and Kashmir Śaivism together at book length for a general reader; at that specific crossing, the field is open, and the author appears to be first.

**The against.** Steven Katz and the constructivists: there is no unmediated experience, so convergence claims are artifacts of description — the scholarly form of the sampling caution the vault already holds. Thomas Sheehan: aletheia deflated to sense-making, the mystical Heidegger refused. Adorno: the jargon of authenticity — the prosecution already seated among the book's critics of the age. Andrea Jain and the appropriation critics: the extraction economy around Indian contemplative goods, which the author's stewardship line answers from inside. And Ferrer, already held in the vault: different practices may reach genuinely different ends.

**The finding.** The book's company already contains the crossover's friends, witnesses, and enemies — Nishida, Eckhart, Celan, Arendt, Adorno, Loy, Weil. The road the author is being asked to build runs mostly through chairs he has already filled. What remains is to introduce his guests to one another.
