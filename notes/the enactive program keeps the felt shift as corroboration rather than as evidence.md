# the enactive program keeps the felt shift as corroboration rather than as evidence

Among the interaction sciences, participatory sense-making treats the felt report in a way the book could learn from rather than fear. Its evidence is relational and dynamical: coordination is read through relative phase, recurrence, and the signature response of the coupling to disturbance — De Jaegher and Di Paolo note that "interactive factors affecting coordination may be uncovered by their signature response to controlled perturbation methods." Within that program the felt report — a participant's sense that something shifted — survives as corroborating phenomenology and is explicitly not made the evidence. This is a third settlement between the two the vault already holds: Maturana's frame demotes the felt shift to nothing trans-observer ([[on the autopoietic account the felt shift certifies nothing beyond the one who feels it]]), while the book elevates it to the final authority; De Jaegher and Di Paolo keep it as a real but subordinate witness, corroborating an evidence that lives in the measurable dynamics of the coupling.

The model matters because it is constructive. It shows the felt shift need not be either the whole proof or worthless — a book could keep the somatic report as corroboration while letting an external, relational check carry the evidential weight the report cannot bear alone.
