# the contemporary attention crisis is ecological, not merely psychological, technical, cultural, or spiritual

The reframe conversation reads the contemporary crisis the book diagnoses — the technological fragmentation of attention, relevance, relationship, and meaning — as ecological in kind. The crisis is not merely a psychological problem, nor merely a technical, cultural, or spiritual one; each of those names it from a single standpoint and so discloses only part of it. Naming it ecological locates it across all of them at once: it is a disturbance in the whole field of conditions under which participation becomes more or less possible, not a fault inside any one region. The claim is what gives the book's civilizational concern its scale — the crisis is sized to the field rather than to the person, which is why it calls for a book addressed to conditions rather than only to individuals.

The reading is an application of the ecology model to a diagnosis. The same insistence that every standpoint is partial, turned on the attention crisis, forbids reducing it to any one discipline's account and reads the crisis instead as the fragmentation of a whole ecology of attention, relevance, relationship, and meaning.

## Substantiation

- The fourfold refusal, in the conversation's words: technological fragmentation "isn't merely" "a psychological problem," "Nor merely" "a technical problem," "Nor merely" "a cultural problem," "Nor merely" "a spiritual problem" — "It is ecological" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, lines 5434-5450).
- The scale it sets, stated as the civilizational thesis: "We are building increasingly powerful systems for mediating attention, relevance, relationship, and meaning without necessarily cultivating the corresponding human capacities for participation, discernment, recognition, and sovereignty" (line 5458).
- Standing: reached in the conversation's shared portion, using Wilber's four-world model as the structure that makes the diagnosis concrete. It is held as candidate material and kept nascent until the writing reaches for it; whether a civilizational book is written from it is a horizon the author holds open, not a commitment recorded here.
