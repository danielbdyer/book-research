# the facilitated room's documented origin set its standard as social adaptability rather than felt transformation

Spolin studied under Neva Boyd at Hull House between 1923 and 1926, and the dig records that her method descends whole from Boyd's settlement-house recreation program, where traditional games were used as democratic pedagogy for immigrant children who shared no common language. The consequence the dig draws is a question about the standard. The facilitated room's documented birth certificate is a social-group-work document, and its original measure was social adaptability among people learning to act together, not the felt, bodily transformation of a single person that the book adopts as its verification floor. The facilitation lineage the book's practice curriculum inherits may therefore not underwrite, at its origin, the standard the book asks it to bear.

The finding is a provenance check on the book rather than a settled result. It leaves open whether Boyd herself claimed a transformation of the player or only socialization and adaptation, and the answer decides whether the lineage the book stands in holds a felt-shift standard at its root or acquires one only later, downstream of its beginnings.
