# how much ontology preserves the ecology without killing the pulse

The conversation names, as the design version of its deepest question, how much ontology is enough to preserve the ecology without killing the pulse. Ontology here means the fixed structure of entity-types a system of record commits to; the ecology means the living field of movements and relationships the inquiry actually is; the pulse means the felt aliveness, the "Aha" the author would not lose to over-structure. The question is the record-design form of the balance that runs through facilitation and book design alike: enough structure to make the work fluent, not so much that it determines what emerges. The conversation offers its only answer as a proportion rather than a number — enough constraint to support fluency, not enough to determine emergence — and the author supplies the felt test for when the balance tips, the moment the model draws attention to itself instead of quietly helping him notice. It stays open because the right amount is discovered case by case in use, never fixed in advance.
