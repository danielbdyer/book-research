# the process is part of the product when it changes the participant

The process is part of the product when the process changes the participant, which is the conversation's rule for deciding what may be delegated without loss. Writing a book, on this account, is not merely producing a book, since the writing changes the writer, organizes attention, forces discrimination, and turns experience into articulation. Where the doing works that change in the doer, the artifact and the transformation are not separable, so handing the whole activity to a machine keeps the output and discards the part that mattered. The rule is conditional rather than absolute, binding only where the process changes the participant, which is what makes it a test rather than a general prohibition on delegation.

## Substantiation

- The conversation lists what writing does to the writer — it "changes the writer," "organizes attention," "forces discrimination," and "transforms experience into articulation" — and concludes that "delegating writing entirely may preserve the artifact while destroying the transformation" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: LXVIII. the transformational value of doing).
- It states the rule as a general principle: "The process is part of the product when the process changes the participant."
