# the third thing has a mechanism in participatory sense-making

The book asserts that something happens between two people that neither supplied — a field that does work, a third thing present in the room ([[the field between two people does work]]). The vault has held that claim with a clinical bearer and, since 2026-08-05, a proposed ontological one ([[the relational vocabulary's ontology is buber's rather than benjamin's]]). What it has not had is a mechanism.

De Jaegher and Di Paolo's enactivist account of participatory sense-making, now read at the primary text, names one. The interaction process between two people can "take on a form of autonomy," becoming "a level of analysis not reducible, in general, to individual behaviours" (2007, p. 492). Meaning is generated in the coordinated rhythm of the exchange rather than transmitted between two already-formed minds: participatory sense-making is "the coordination of intentional activity in interaction, whereby individual sense-making processes are affected and new domains of social sense-making can be generated that were not available to each individual on her own" (p. 497). The account runs on a spectrum from mere orientation of individual sense-making up to joint acts that can only be completed socially — an infant's offered fork "needs the taking in order to be completed" (2008 chapter, p. 10).

The reading corrected the vault's own phrasing at two points. The construct is a "level of analysis," where the vault had carried "level of organization," and the sentence the vault had quoted as the authors' summary — "a defensible mechanism for 'the third thing': shared meaning that is neither speaker's nor hearer's but the couple's" — is a gloss the vault imported from the standing-wave paper, not the authors'. They never use "third thing," and they guard against exactly that reification.

## Substantiation

- Read at the primary texts (the 2007 journal article and the 2008 chapter, both read in full 2026-08-30). The core claim is verbatim: the interaction process "can take on a form of autonomy" and constitutes "a level of analysis not reducible, in general, to individual behaviours" (2007, p. 492). Social interaction itself is defined as "the regulated coupling between at least two autonomous agents, where the regulation is aimed at aspects of the coupling itself" (p. 493).
- The refusal, kept and now precise at the text: the irreducible level is a *process*, not the presence the book's figurative language implies. The authors prefer "mutual modulation... so as not to imply a self-sufficiency of the social domain that obscures the interplay between the autonomy of the interaction process and that of the interactors" (p. 504), and they require the interactors' own autonomy to be preserved (pp. 492–493). Where they do write that the process "emerges as an entity" (p. 492), the entity is operational and "typically fleeting" — a precarious operational closure with no existence apart from the interacting. So the mechanism licenses "meaning made between," not a substantival third thing in the room.
- Why this counts as an isomorph rather than support. Enactivist cognitive science reached the irreducible interaction process from autonomy and dynamical systems, with no contact with either pole of the book's founding convergence ([[the convergence's independence fails where three of its names share one teacher]]); under `ops/methodology/authorial stance.md` the arrival is company rather than corroboration. One qualification the reading adds: the account is not free of phenomenology writ large — the authors lean on Merleau-Ponty and align with "the phenomenological insight of the alterity of the other" (p. 504) — so it is independent of the book's two *founding poles*, not of phenomenology entirely.
- The authors' own illustrations, kept in place of the vault's: two people stepping side to side in a corridor, unable to pass, the coordination no longer "the sum of the individual's actions" (p. 493); playing charades, where the meaning is worked out in the doing (pp. 500–501); the infant's offered fork that needs the taking to be complete (2008, p. 10). The ensemble-tempo illustration the vault had used is the standing-wave paper's gloss on the entrainment literature (Kelso; Zamm et al.), not De Jaegher and Di Paolo's, and is kept only as that.
- The sharpest empirical case, read at the paper (p. 502): in the perceptual-crossing paradigm, when "the movement of the attached lure and the other partner are indistinguishable, recognition still occurs, because both participants are looking for each other," so "the sense-making that solves the task only happens at the level of the collective dynamics" — the dyad discriminates what neither individual can. The study is Auvray, Lenay and Stewart (2009; the paper cites the 2006 conference version); its double-video companion is at [[infants distinguish a live exchange from a recording of the same person]].
- The refusal that reaches the book's solitary form, now the most mechanically exact of its many arrivals: participatory sense-making requires live mutual regulation, so even a short delay deforms it (the Ruhleder and Jordan video-conferencing case, pp. 497–498) and a medium that cannot adjust breaks it outright. A text does not adjust. Their later work does not exempt reading but re-models it: *Linguistic Bodies* (2018) extends participatory sense-making into "languaging" and treats solitary reading as a "virtual dialogue" in which the absent producer is animated as an agent, the utterance remaining "a dialogic act... producer and audience" — which extends the participation law into reading rather than freeing reading from it, consonant with the book's own "whatever transforms, the reader did it."
- The resonance material has a measured arm, held as standing-wave-relayed company: synchrony has a documented social consequence — people acting in synchrony cooperate more afterward, "even in situations requiring personal sacrifice," and "positive emotions need not be generated for synchrony to foster cooperation" (Wiltermuth and Heath 2009, reaching the vault through the standing-wave paper) — which cuts against reading the relational field as fundamentally about warmth. Hartmut Rosa's uncontrollability clause is the sociological neighbour.
