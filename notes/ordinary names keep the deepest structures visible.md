# ordinary names keep the deepest structures visible

When the three structures lost their mythic names — Knife, Thread, Vessel — for Wall, Door, Floor, the corpus drew a doctrine from the veto rather than a preference: "the deepest structures should wear the most ordinary clothes" (atlas §II). The mechanism is stated in the walked document: "If you name the fundamental structures of experience after ritual objects, the reader files them under mythology and walks past the actual walls of the actual room she is sitting in. Name them Wall, Door, Floor, and she cannot leave the building without touching the teaching" (walked II). The walked document generalizes the point: "The rename enacts the Householder Axiom at the level of vocabulary" — the book's refusal of renunciation, applied to words, so that the vocabulary itself asks for no departure from the ordinary.

The doctrine is available wherever the manuscript names something: it argues against reintroducing mythic register at the level of terms, and it explains why the book's Sanskrit is carried mostly unnamed — the front-matter promise is printed "without its Sanskrit name" (atlas §I) — while its working nouns stay at the height of a hardware store.

## Substantiation

- Atlas §II: "Formerly Knife / Thread / Vessel — vetoed by the author: 'nothing hides like the always-there.' The rename is itself a doctrine: the deepest structures should wear the most ordinary clothes (≈ Householder Axiom)."
- The provenance qualification stays attached: the sentence the veto is remembered by — "nothing hides like the always-there" — is dated to three nights before the veto and to a different subject, so the doctrine is a later distillation rather than the reason given at the time.
