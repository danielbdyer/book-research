# the solo practice reaches the felt shift and the primitives then surface by themselves

The book names one solo practice as foundational, Gendlin's Focusing, and hands it to the reader whole "because it fits in a paragraph and the book lives in the doing" (walked VII). The protocol is six moves: locate the felt sense of something in the body without analyzing it; attend to its quality; let words or images arise from inside the sense rather than imposed on it; check each against the sense; release what does not match; and wait. "When one matches, you will feel it — the felt shift, a loosening, a small step" (walked VII).

The claim that makes Focusing the curriculum's foundation rather than one practice among many is what the book says happens next. "And then, the book observes, the primitives surface by themselves: oh — this is something I need to Release. This Commitment has hardened into a cage" (walked VII). The practice is offered as the bridge from raw felt experience to the book's own alphabet: the felt sense, once attended, presents itself already sorted into the twelve verbs, so the vocabulary is claimed to be read off one's own weather rather than applied to it. The corpus compresses this to a maxim it attributes across traditions — "To name truly is half of the medicine" — and marks the convergence: "every contemplative tradition found this in its own vocabulary, and Gendlin found it with clinical tape recorders" (walked VII).

## Substantiation

- **The six-move protocol and the felt-shift endpoint** are quoted from walked VII above; the atlas states the same sequence compactly as "Locate the felt sense in the body ⟿ attend without analyzing ⟿ let words/images arise from inside it ⟿ check them against the sense ⟿ release what doesn't match ⟿ wait ⟿ the felt shift" (atlas §VII).
- **The distinct proposition of this note** is Focusing's role in the practice curriculum and its bridge to the primitives, which the verification-facing notes do not carry: [[felt shift versus felt confirmation]] and [[a genuine felt shift changes what can be said next]] concern whether the felt shift certifies anything, and [[gendlin's focusing protocol already guards against the counterfeit shift]] concerns the protocol's defense against a false positive; this note concerns the practice as the curriculum's foundation and the claim that the primitives are self-presenting after the shift.
- **The self-presenting claim is the book's, and it is strong**: that the twelve primitives "surface by themselves" asserts the analytic vocabulary is discovered in the felt sense rather than projected onto it, which is the same move the recognition doctrine makes about the reclaimed word — the name was already there. It is stated by the corpus and not independently verified here.
- **The reading is done for the felt shift and undone for the practice**: Gendlin's *A Theory of Personality Change* has a reading record in `archive/`, cited as the felt shift's source; his account of Focusing as a taught practice, in *Focusing* (1978), is not read, so the protocol is held as the corpus hands it. His seat is [[eugene gendlin]].
