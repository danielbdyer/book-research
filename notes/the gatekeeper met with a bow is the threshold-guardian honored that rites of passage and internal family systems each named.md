# the gatekeeper met with a bow is the threshold-guardian honored that rites of passage and internal family systems each named

This gives the reader a way to be tender toward the part of themselves that guards too hard. The structure that walls you in was a bodyguard once; it took its post for a reason, in a real hour, and it does not need to be defeated for you to pass — it needs to be met, bowed to, thanked, and it will usually relax. The reader who has ever softened toward their own defensiveness and felt it stand down has practiced something whole traditions ritualized: the guardian at the threshold is honored, and the passage opens. Nothing in you is your enemy; even the guard is on your side. Its use to the writing is to reframe the peak's terror for the reader as a guardian afraid of a dissolution that is not actually coming.
