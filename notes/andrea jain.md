# andrea jain

Andrea Jain studies the global spirituality economy, and her named mechanism is "gestural subversion": commodified spirituality acknowledges the harms of the system it lives inside and subverts them in gesture only, so that even the subversion is colonized. Her analysis targets therapeutic spirituality that heals the broken person rather than the system that broke them — and her challenge is structural rather than personal, which is what makes it the sharpest available form of the question the author's stewardship line must answer. Sincerity is not a defense, because gestural subversion is what sincere actors do inside the economy she describes.

The refusal kept: she would refuse the framing of Indian contemplative goods as apolitical inner technology at all. Any book that walks a Western reader into the valley is, on her account, operating inside the appropriation economy and owes its answer from there, not from outside. The vault holds the beginning of an answer in the seat found for the structural position — that some suffering lives in economic and institutional arrangement, with Rosa as its candidate — and Jain is the test of whether that answer is load-bearing or gestural.

The recommendation rests on that survey depth, not on a reading.
