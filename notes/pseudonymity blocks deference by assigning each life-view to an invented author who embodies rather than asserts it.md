# pseudonymity blocks deference by assigning each life-view to an invented author who embodies rather than asserts it

Pseudonymity is a device for removing the author's authority from the reader's path. Kierkegaard gives each life-view to an invented author — Victor Eremita, Johannes de Silentio, Climacus, Anti-Climacus — who embodies the position rather than asserting it, and in the appended "First and Last Declaration" he disclaims authorship and asks that nothing be attributed to him but to the pseudonym. The device blocks two postures at once: the reader's deference to Kierkegaard-the-authority, and the collapse of a position into what the author happens to believe. Its cost is that the author forfeits credit, coherence, and the ability to be quoted as himself, becoming the author of the authors, present only as absence. For a testimony that must be trusted the device is only partly available, since a book resting on a witness's own life cannot fully disown its first person; recording it keeps the option and its price visible.
