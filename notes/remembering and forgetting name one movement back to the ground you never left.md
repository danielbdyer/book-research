# remembering and forgetting name one movement back to the ground you never left

The book's founding word is aletheia, un-forgetting — and here is its strangest company: a tradition that reaches the same ground by the opposite verb, calling the work forgetting. The gift to the reader is that the destination does not depend on which door you use. Whether your temperament reaches home by remembering — gathering back what was lost — or by forgetting — letting fall the self and its distinctions until only the ground remains — you arrive at the same place, the always-present you never actually departed. Its use to the writing is to turn an apparent embarrassment for a book built on un-forgetting into a deepening, and to welcome readers whose path is subtractive rather than mnemonic.

## Substantiation

- Heidegger: *a-lēthē*, truth as the un-forgetting of Being drawn out of oblivion. Zhuangzi: "sitting in forgetfulness" (*zuowang*) — "I let my limbs and body fall away, dismiss hearing and sight, leave form behind and part with knowledge, and become one with the Great Thoroughfare" (Zhuangzi ch. 6; to verify) — the forgetting itself the return to the Dao, paired with "returning is the movement of the Dao" (DDJ 40).
- First-principles bridge: if the obstacle to the already-present is a false overlay, then removing it can be described either as *remembering* what the overlay hid or as *forgetting* the overlay itself — one event, narrated from the two things it changes. The verbs disagree only about which object they take.
- Standing: a reach; the Zhuangzi passage and the Heidegger etymology are solid, but the identification of the two verbs as one move is structural synthesis, to be stated as illumination rather than wordplay; a clean *zuowang* translation is queued.
