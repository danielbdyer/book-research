# facilitation is a technology of relationship whose success is increased capacity for participation, not efficient production of an output

The reframe conversation offers a formulation for Book I: facilitation is a technology of relationship. It keeps the word technology while stripping its industrial measure. Unlike a technology judged by how efficiently it produces a predefined output, facilitation succeeds when it increases the participant's capacity for awareness, agency, discernment, participation, and authorship. The measure is therefore what happens to the participant's capacity, not what the facilitator produces. The conversation flags this as a bridge to its later treatment of artificial intelligence, where the same test — whether a technology increases or decreases the capacity to participate — is applied to a tool of a different kind.

## Substantiation

- The reframe conversation names the formulation and its inverted measure: "Facilitation is a technology of relationship. But unlike industrial technology, its success is not measured by how efficiently it produces a predefined output. It is successful when it increases the participant's capacity for: awareness; agency; discernment; participation; and authorship" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: facilitation as an ethical technology).
