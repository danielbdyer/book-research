# thisness is the grain of the container anatomy

The anatomy's first structure already contains the concern: the Wall is what "makes a this possible" (atlas §II). The corpus names the lineage of that *this* and holds it as "thisness as the Triad's grain" (atlas §II.4) — the structures are never in service of containment in general but of a particular, unrepeatable one: this person, this room, this moment. The lineage runs in three steps. Duns Scotus coined haecceitas for the unrepeatable individuality of a particular thing. Gerard Manley Hopkins carried it into inscape — the inner form of a thing — and instress, "the strike that form makes on a beholder" (walked II). Deleuze carried it to the event: in the walked document's words, "the thisness not of an object but of a moment."

The lineage does quiet work in two other places the vault already holds. The tuning-fork image carries Hopkins inside it — instress is what a striking surface receives, so the book's image for itself is this lineage's Victorian step made into a reading relation. And the sister project takes its name from the lineage's first step: the Haecceity Graph, the same ontology run in software, whose portability the corpus counts as evidence for the anatomy.
