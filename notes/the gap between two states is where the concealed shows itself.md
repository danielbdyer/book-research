# the gap between two states is where the concealed shows itself

The teaching, as the corpus carries it from the Vijñāna Bhairava: the junction between two states — "between two breaths, two thoughts, waking and sleep" — is the seam "where the god stands unclothed" (atlas §IX.6c). The tradition's name for the junction is sandhi. The claim beneath the figure is precise: disclosure does not happen inside a state but at the crossing between states, so the gap is not dead time between the things that matter — it is the site where what the states conceal can show.

The book takes the teaching as architecture rather than allusion. Its interludes are placed at the junctions between Parts — "the interludes are placed at the sandhis" (atlas §IX.6c) — and its cusp figure carries the same term. The walked document then extends the teaching to the book's own ending: "the trembling between a sentence spoken and the answer arriving" is read as "sandhi mid-crossing" (walked IX). That extension is what a drafting session would reach for. The book's last line is written so that it can be answered, and this teaching names what the interval after such a line is: not silence, not failure to land, but the junction itself — which bears directly on how the ending's wait, and the manuscript's own unsent condition, can be honestly described.

- Walked IX carries the interlude placement and the extension: the book "reads the trembling between a sentence spoken and the answer arriving as sandhi mid-crossing."
