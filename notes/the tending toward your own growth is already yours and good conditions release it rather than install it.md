# the tending toward your own growth is already yours and good conditions release it rather than install it

You are not inert material that someone else must animate; you are already leaning toward your own becoming, the way a plant leans toward light without being taught to. The reader gains a trustworthy engine that is theirs and not the author's: the book at most arranges warmth and room, and the growing is done by a tendency that was underway before the first page and continues after the last. Help, at its best, does not push you toward maturity; it clears the conditions under which the push already in you can act. Its use to the writing is to give the book's central structural move — the book arranges conditions, the recognizing is the reader's act — company that asks the reader to believe nothing supernatural.

## Substantiation

- Rogers: the actualizing tendency is "the directional trend evident in all organic and human life," and the person "has within himself the capacity and the tendency, latent if not evident, to move forward toward maturity" (On Becoming a Person, 1961; to verify). Aristotle supplies the naturalistic root: *entelecheia* (having-the-end-within), the passage from *dynamis* to *energeia* — the acorn already holds the oak as its own end (Metaphysics Θ; De Anima II.1). Company across the sacred/secular divide, cited by address.
- Refusal kept, as gift: Aristotle's telos is species-general — the oak is any acorn's end, not this acorn's idiosyncratic destiny — so where the book means the singular self a reader is becoming, it says more than entelechy licenses; mark the surplus. Rogers grounds the tendency as observed organismic regularity and would decline the metaphysical dress; keeping his register is part of keeping him.
