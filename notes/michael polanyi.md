# michael polanyi

Michael Polanyi was a working physical chemist — X-ray crystallography, reaction kinetics — who turned to philosophy in middle life because he thought the official account of how science works did not match how he had watched it work. His argument is that all knowing involves the personal participation of the knower, that this participation is not a defect to be eliminated but the condition of knowing anything, and that a great deal of what a competent person knows cannot be stated: we can know more than we can tell.

His structural claim is the one that matters here. Attention has two terms. A person attends *from* a set of particulars they have made part of themselves — the feel of a probe, the words of a sentence, the muscles of a hand — *to* the thing those particulars disclose. He calls the first term subsidiary and the second focal, and the act of making particulars one's own he calls indwelling. Turn attention onto the subsidiary particulars and the focal meaning dissolves; a pianist who watches their fingers stops playing.

## Why he holds two positions rather than one

The mesh grid's placement is a different function, and it is the larger of the two. The book's first law is that the instrument of knowing is not separable from what it knows, and every thinker the corpus reports arriving at it is a phenomenologist, a contemplative or a facilitator. Polanyi got there from crystallography with no phenomenological debt, which is the property the sort finds the whole company lacking ([[the convergence's independence fails where three of its names share one teacher]]). ## What he would refuse

Derived rather than sourced; no primary work has been read here. Polanyi's argument is a *defense* of science, and the refusal follows from that. He held that personal participation is what makes objective knowledge achievable — that the scientist's commitment is answerable to a reality that will show itself in unexpected ways — and he wrote against the reduction of knowledge to subjective preference as explicitly as against the reduction of it to formal procedure. A use of him that treats the knower's involvement as dissolving accountability to what is known inverts his position.

He would also, on the same ground, refuse a symmetric reading of the participation law. Indwelling is asymmetric: the knower is changed by dwelling in the particulars, and the crystal is not changed by being known. The book's law says you come back changed by the emergence and often implies the emergence is changed too. That gap is where a careful reading would either find a real convergence or find that two different claims have been sharing a sentence.

## What the evaluation still requires

*Personal Knowledge* is long and the relevant chapters are on skills, indwelling and commitment. Three things to settle: whether he claims for tacit knowing anything like a verification function, or only a discovery function; how his account of commitment handles the objection that participation immunizes a position against disagreement, which is the hole next to this one ([[the participation law has no opponent and its oldest objection is that participation is contamination]]); and whether the subsidiary-focal structure genuinely matches the book's posture or merely rhymes with it.
