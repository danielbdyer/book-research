# a chapter opens by restating what is built before adding to it

From the architecture's beginning onward, every unit of the executed draft opens with an inventory of what is already built: "We have built, by now, an alphabet (the primitives) and a chemistry (the molecules). But there is one more layer" (abridged ch7); "We arrive now at the praxis. The architecture has been laid. The mystical detour has been taken" (abridged ch13). Twelve of the fifteen units open this way. The three that open cold are exactly the phenomenological entries — the chapters that begin inside an experience rather than inside the argument — and the split falls precisely at the architecture's edge.

This is the executed counterpart of the earliest draft's spatial device: where the mythic draft kept the reader located in a walkable house ([[the voice keeps the reader located in a walkable space]]), the execution keeps the reader located in the argument. Location survived the rewrite; the space it referred to changed.

## Substantiation

- All fifteen unit openings checked in the extracted text: twelve carry the inventory, three open cold, and the three are the phenomenological entries; the ch7 and ch13 specimens are quoted above.
- Checkable: read each unit's first three sentences — the inventory names already-built material before new material, and a cold open outside the phenomenological entries is the exception to find.
- The relation between the two location devices is a concordance-grade fact about the same element across draft states: the device was revised, not dropped.
