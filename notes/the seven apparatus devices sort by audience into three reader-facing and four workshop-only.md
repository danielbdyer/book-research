# the seven apparatus devices sort by audience into three reader-facing and four workshop-only

The corpus specifies seven apparatus devices, and the offering sorts them by who sees them. Four can do their whole work in the workshop and never print: the per-chapter dial recording the ratio of evocative to informative prose, the eight-question checklist every chapter must pass, the planting ledger tracking each planted image to its payoff, and the escrow bookkeeping of promises made early and kept. Three are reader-facing: the inhabited margins, the verso column, and the interludes. The sort halves the cost of a fully treated chapter, since only three of the seven have to appear on a page, and it guards a named regression, because a visible apparatus of clever devices would rebuild the intellectual-pleasure engine the system's demotion removed.

The route is atlas §X.6, read device by device with one question: does this work if the reader never sees it? An answer of yes moves the device off the printed page and into production tooling, where its absence from a draft is not evidence that the design was dropped.

- The regression the sort guards against is the appreciating posture at [[beauty recruits the wrong posture]]: a visible engine of clever devices recruits the reader toward pleasure in the machinery, the posture the demotion was meant to remove.
