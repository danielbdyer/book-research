# trained introspection is the repair the standard's sharpest opponent named and left open

The book teaches a practice and then asks the practised reader to verify by the body. The obvious defense of that arrangement is that practice makes the reading reliable, and the obvious worry is that the defense is convenient. It turns out the defense was named by the opponent rather than by the book, in the same paper that carries the attack. Schwitzgebel writes: "Perhaps the right kind of learning, practice, or care could largely shield us from error — an interesting possibility that merits exploration! — but I see as yet no robust scientific support for such hopes" (2008, p. 259).

Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions. The objection is not that trained introspection is impossible, so the book's arrangement is not ruled out in advance by the strongest published attack on it. And the condition Schwitzgebel sets is evidential rather than philosophical, so the book cannot meet it by argument; it can only be met by results of a kind he specifies.

He also says where he had looked. His footnote names classical introspective training in his own 2004 work, careful interviewing about randomly sampled experiences in Hurlburt and Schwitzgebel 2007, Schooler and Schreiber 2004 on the state of the science, and one line that dates the whole condition: "Very recently, there has been some promising work on meditation: see Lutz, Dunne, and Davidson 2007" (p. 259 n. 21). His verdict is from 2008. Anything that has arrived since is evidence he had not seen, which turns a standing objection into a research question with an address.

## Substantiation

- The quotation and the footnote are on page 259 of the 2008 paper, read complete on 2026-08-07; the record is `archive/reading — schwitzgebel, the unreliability of naive introspection (2008).md`.
- One later result the vault already holds fits the description he gave. Petitmengin and colleagues replicated the standard unawareness finding and then found that subjects guided by an elicitation interview detected the manipulation in 80 percent of cases (*Consciousness and Cognition* 22, 2013), a citation verified at web depth on 2026-08-06 and recorded at [[introspective access is the felt shift's undefended premise]]. That paper has not been read here, and whether it meets his standard is exactly what its reading would settle.
- The book's own practice curriculum is what would have to do the training, and its verification conditions have never been stated per practice; that pass is queued in `ops/queue/extractions.md` and has not run.
- The claim asserts what Schwitzgebel wrote and what follows from it. It does not assert that training works, and the vault does not postulate efficacy anywhere, per `ops/methodology/authorial stance.md`.
