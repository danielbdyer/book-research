# the transactional program has not dislodged the efferent default and cannot tell an evoked aesthetic reading from a performed one

Rosenblatt's transactional pedagogy is a decades-long program built to do a version of what the book wants — to move readers off the efferent default and into the lived aesthetic event — and it is also a failure report on uptake. Under testing the efferent default has not been dislodged; students continue to analyse literature efferently, and the aesthetic stance is more often reported than evoked. The setting compounds the doubt: the classroom is a high-demand environment, so a question like "what did you live through" invites the wanted answer rather than a true report, and the record cannot rule whether a reported aesthetic reading was evoked or merely performed to satisfy the instruction.

The finding is a caution the book must carry because the book proposes to do by prose, read alone, what a century of designed instruction has struggled to do in the room. If a pedagogy explicitly aimed at the shift under-delivers and cannot certify its own successes against the demand of its setting, a book that asks the reader to check their own experience inherits the same two problems: the shift may not fire, and the reader's report of it may be compliance.

## Substantiation

- The dig recorded the uptake failure at secondary depth [S], from Rosenblatt's own account of the persistent "efferent reading of literature" and the observation that the aesthetic stance is reported more often than evoked (`transmission dig 2, 2026-08-13`, Failures).
