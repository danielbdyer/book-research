# kenneth burke

Burke's claim is one sentence and it is not about any particular vocabulary: any terminology is a *reflection* of reality, and by being a selection of reality it necessarily functions also as a *deflection* of reality. Terms direct attention, and attention directed is attention withheld. He pairs it with a second idea from the same argument — *trained incapacity*, the state in which a person's very competence with a vocabulary makes them unable to see what the vocabulary does not name.

The corpus states the application itself: "Whatever the twelve reveal, they also hide — an alphabet of operations will under-see whatever isn't an operation (givenness, grace, sheer quality)" (space-around: around the alphabet). That is precise and it names three things the book cares about a great deal. The book's own center is a disclosure that is not done — recognition of what was already there — and an alphabet of verbs is the least hospitable possible notation for anything received rather than performed.

**What he would refuse.** The escape the corpus reaches for. The survey answers its own objection immediately: "The book actually has an answer already — Level Zero is anatomy, not operation, and the Clearing is no verb at all." Burke would not accept that, because *anatomy* and *Clearing* are also terms, selecting and deflecting on their own account, and adding a layer that names the unnamed does not exit the screen — it widens it. His position has no outside, which is what makes it durable and also what limits it: an objection that applies to every vocabulary equally cannot tell the book which vocabulary to prefer.

**What he gives.** The proper form of an objection the book half-states and never lands. And a lever the vault has not used: if every terminology deflects, then the honest move is to say *what this one deflects*, in the book, in the chapter that introduces it. That is a writable paragraph rather than a research program, and it is a stronger answer than any completeness claim would be — it concedes the screen and tells the reader where to look around it.

**The load, and what happens without him.** With `adversary`: he is the objection that does not go away if Wierzbicka's test is passed. Without him the book's only defense of its vocabulary is that the vocabulary is complete, which is a claim about the set; Burke's charge is about what having a set does to a reader, and no set answers it. The assignment is the vault's, made 2026-08-05, and it has not been tested against the primary essay.

**One half of this correspondence is quoted and the other is not.** The survey's application of his objection is quoted above. Burke's own formulation is stated here in the vault's words from secondary knowledge, and the essay has not been read in this vault. The address is "Terministic Screens" in *Language as Symbolic Action*.

## What the reading has to settle

Two questions, in the order that decides the most.

- Whether Burke's own answer to his objection — the "dramatistic" pentad, itself a five-term screen he defends as more capacious rather than as neutral — is a model the book could adopt, since that would turn the objection into a method.
- Whether *trained incapacity* bears on the book's defining reader, the one who has learned a fluent integration vocabulary and uses it to avoid feeling anything all the way through, which is the same shape and would make Burke relevant to the felt-shift region as well as this one.
