# a ritual can be designed to hold a self in place

The gesture asserts that a person can build ritual structures that hold them steady — anchors, in its word — and that this is design work rather than discipline. The vault does not hold this. Its account of steadiness is the Stance, which is explicitly not a structure but a point of return, and the returning is the practice. An anchor is a different proposal: something built once that goes on holding without being returned to. The two accounts may be complementary or may be alternatives, and nothing in the corpus decides.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- Sovereignty appears four times in the earliest contents, twice in the treatment, once in the abridged edition, and zero times in the atlas, the walked document, the first telling, and the survey. The concept entered through the drafts and never reached the descriptive corpus.
- The held account is at [[the stance is a point of return not a state]], whose whole force is that steadiness is not maintained but recovered: "You fall out; you return; the returning is the practice" (abridged ch13).
- The container anatomy would predict that an anchor is a container like any other, with a boundary, a permeability, and a duration. That prediction has not been tested against any ritual object.

## What would close it

Closing it means deciding whether steadiness is built or returned to, which the corpus currently answers only one way.
