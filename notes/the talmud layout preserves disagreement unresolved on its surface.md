# the talmud layout preserves disagreement unresolved on its surface

The Talmud page does not resolve the disagreements it displays. Competing readings coexist on one leaf, and the layout adjudicates no winner between them; the reader inherits a contested text rather than a settled one. The tradition names the disagreement it preserves *makhloket*, and it licenses the coexistence with the maxim that of two contradicting schools "these and these are the words of the living God" (b. Eruvin 13b). The protection the arrangement buys is against premature closure: a reader cannot mistake one voice for the verdict, because the page never issues one.

This is the surface property a single-answer book gives up. A page that stages a dispute keeps the dispute available to the reader as something to enter; a page that resolves the dispute hands the reader a conclusion and removes the work. The cost of the held-open form is that it supplies no answer and requires a community willing to live with an unclosed question, which is why the property does not survive being extracted into a private reference (treated at [[subtracting the community from the talmud page converts it from staging disagreement to looking one up]]).

## Substantiation

- The *eilu va-eilu* maxim is at b. Eruvin 13b, named at scholarly depth [S] in `transmission dig 3, 2026-08-13` (§I); the Talmud primary was not opened at this location in the dig, and the reading stays `state: nascent`.
- The quoted phrase "these and these are the words of the living God" is the tradition's own gloss on the preserved disagreement; it is reproduced once, short, and attributed to its locus.
- The book's own posture toward unresolved tension — held rather than adjudicated — is the reason this precedent bears; the vault records its doctrinal tensions as tracked rather than resolved, so a page that prints a dispute without a verdict is a formal ancestor of that stance.
