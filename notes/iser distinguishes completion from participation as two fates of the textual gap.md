# iser distinguishes completion from participation as two fates of the textual gap

Iser locates the literary work not in the text and not in the reader but in their convergence, the virtual dimension the reader supplies by filling the text's gaps, and he splits that gap-filling into two fates. In what he calls traditional texts the filling is "more or less unconscious" and builds a consistent world-picture, which is completion. In modern texts the gap is exploited deliberately, so fragmentary that the reader's attention is occupied with the search for connections and is thereby made aware of the reader's own capacity for providing them; the text turns back onto the reader's preconceptions, which is participation. The distinction is the region's most exact mechanism for the difference the book cares about, between a reading that confirms the reader and a reading that examines the reader.

The split is load-bearing for the book because participation, not completion, is the fate that could recruit enactment over appreciation: it moves attention off the built world and onto the reader's own apparatus. The book's candidate use of the mechanism therefore depends on holding a reader in participation without the reader sliding back into completion.
