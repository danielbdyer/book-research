# one interlude the reader overhears as a letter can carry the letter genre's pull

The book's native genre leans toward the letter, and the offering holds that the genre's pull does not require the whole book to become one. A single late interlude could be an actual letter the reader overhears rather than receives, and overhearing sits closer to the one confirmed sighting of the book's promised event — a man alone with a letter addressed to who he used to be — than direct address does. The device uses the third person-position the vault already holds, where the writer speaks with his whole voice to a Thou who is not the reader while the reader receives everything and is the target of nothing, and it sits at a junction between two states, where the corpus already places its interludes, without touching what the poems and the margins are for.

This is the interlude-scale realization of the letter's pull, recorded beside the correspondence pick that makes the whole book letters rather than in place of it: where a book is not otherwise epistolary, one overheard letter would carry the genre's answerable pull at a single seam.
