# suffering in an intense state is the intensity multiplied by the resistance

The same neurobiology produces either a transcendent experience or a psychological catastrophe, and the synthesis received 2026-08-05 names the variable that decides between them. Of people surveyed about their most difficult psilocybin experience, 39% rated it among the five most challenging experiences of their lifetime and 11% put themselves or others at risk of physical harm — while 84% of the same participants reported benefiting from it and 34% rated it among their five most meaningful. Difficulty and benefit are not opposed in the data; degree of difficulty was positively associated with personal meaning and with enduring wellbeing.

What separates the outcomes is what the person does with the intensity. Among participants who worked through a difficult experience, 84.5% found "trying to let go, accept or surrender to the experience" helpful and 77.3% found "trying to observe my mind and not fight it" effective. The synthesis states the pattern as a formula: suffering equals intensity multiplied by resistance. Intensity alone is not the cause; it becomes suffering when it meets resistance.

The book already holds this in its own terms and in an image rather than a formula: the threshold figure is met with a bow and never a fight ([[the gatekeeper is met with a bow never a fight]]). The synthesis's own phrasing closes the loop — "the gatekeeper fighting the clearing generates most of the suffering; the clearing itself is neutral territory."

## Substantiation

- `privated`, and the reason matters here more than usual: no primary study behind these figures has been read in this vault. The percentages are the synthesis's report of the challenging-experiences survey literature and the study on resolving difficult experiences, and they need checking at source before manuscript use.
- The finding is stated here in plain terms rather than as a property of the three loops, per the treatment recorded at a synthesis organized by the schemes offers to promote them back to architecture. The synthesis attributes the mechanism to loop release; what was measured is self-reported difficulty, self-reported benefit, and the reported helpfulness of surrender.
- The formula's shape is the book's own compositional idiom — two named quantities and an operator — which is a reason for caution rather than encouragement. A finding that arrives already looking like the book's diagnostic formulas is the easiest kind to adopt without examining, and the compositional scheme is exactly what the author demoted. What the vault takes here is the claim that resistance is the multiplier, not the notation.
- One thing the finding sharpens and one it does not. It sharpens the corpus's fork between surrender and will, because it puts empirical weight on the surrender pole at the specific moment of high intensity — which is a narrower claim than the fork is about, and does not settle it ([[surrender and will are adjudicated from the body rather than from doctrine]]). It does not resolve the voluntarist objection, since Robbins's position is about a person's ordinary state rather than about what to do inside an overwhelming one ([[the middle voice is only earned against the voluntarist pole at full strength]]).
- The most useful corollary is one the formula does not state. If resistance is the multiplier, then anything that reduces resistance reduces suffering at constant intensity — and having a framework for what is happening is one of the things the same literature reports reduces it ([[a framework for what is happening is itself a protective factor]]). That is where the book's own position in this territory actually sits.
