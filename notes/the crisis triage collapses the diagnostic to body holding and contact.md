# the crisis triage collapses the diagnostic to body holding and contact

The book's diagnostic normally runs four reads, one per structural layer ([[the diagnostic method reads each structural layer with its own question]]). For acute distress — "a loss, a betrayal, an unexpected illness, a sudden recognition" — the abridged edition specifies that the method deliberately shrinks. "The diagnostic move, in crisis, is not analytic precision; it is the basic question: am I in my body, am I held, am I in I–Thou with someone? If yes to all three, the system can survive almost anything. If no, the system needs those three before anything else" (abridged ch15).

Three things about the reduction are specified rather than implied. The precise diagnostic is **suspended, not merely simplified**: "The Molecular Look may be deferred — there is no time, yet, for molecular precision," so the triage replaces the chemistry rather than compressing it. The three questions are **an ordering of need**, not a description — a no to any one names the thing to restore before the others can matter, so the triage is a prescription in the form of a checklist. And the practice load changes shape: "The Kata for crisis is fewer, more often — not a complex sequence, but the basic moves repeated," with the named moves being Anchor (return to body), Sanctuary (a place that holds), and Calling the Room ("do not isolate"). The three questions and the three moves are the same three concerns — body, holding, contact — read once as diagnosis and once as medicine.

The triage also carries the arc's own reading of what a crisis is: "this is a Composting that arrived faster than expected, and the work is to let it compost without forcing it back to Full" (abridged ch15), which keeps the metabolic layer present even while the molecular layer is deferred.

## Substantiation

- **The three questions and the "before anything else" ordering** are quoted verbatim above from abridged ch15 (Case 3 — Crisis, pp. 44–45).
- **The three concerns map onto the book's three loops and its container law**: body is the temporal-somatic loop, holding is Sanctuary (the container's protective condition), and I–Thou is the relational field's minimum — so the triage is the whole architecture reduced to its survival floor rather than a separate doctrine.
- **The verification condition is built into the triage's grammar**: each question is answerable only from the body ("you may be barely in your body"), which is the praxis stratum's standing rule — "verifiable by felt-shift, or it doesn't count" (atlas §VII) — operating where there is no time for anything slower.
- **What the corpus does not specify**: the triage names no test for when acute distress has passed and the four-move diagnostic may resume, which is the same gap the practice forms carry ([[the practice forms specify when to begin and not how to tell one is finished]]) appearing again at the crisis end of the curriculum.
