# the requirement that the book's author be trusted and disappear restates kierkegaard's authorial problem

The book asks its author to be trusted and then to disappear, so the reader does the transforming and no authority stands between the reader and the work. Transmission dig 1 reports that this requirement is not merely analogous to Søren Kierkegaard's project but is that project restated: his entire authorship is engineered so the reader cannot lean on the author's standing and must instead appropriate the truth himself. The consequence for a drafting session is that the book's medium-problem has a named precedent who built an explicit inventory of devices for it and located their breaking points himself, so the book inherits both a toolkit and a record of where the toolkit failed. The finding rests on secondary scholarship; the dig could not open Kierkegaard's copyrighted primaries, and the readings that would raise it above report are queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.

## Substantiation

- Dig 1 states the identity directly: commissioning question two, "an author who must be trusted and must disappear," "is not analogous to Kierkegaard's project; it *is* his project, restated" (transmission dig 1, 2026-08-13, F1).
- The dig's basis is the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on Kierkegaard, section on indirect communication [S]. The four primary sites the dig names for the doctrine — the *Concluding Unscientific Postscript*, the 1847 lectures on communication, *Practice in Christianity*, and *The Point of View* — are unopened here and named in dig 1's readings-to-queue for `ops/reading queue.md`.
