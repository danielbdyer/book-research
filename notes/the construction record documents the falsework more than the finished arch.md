# the construction record documents the falsework more than the finished arch

A masonry arch cannot carry itself while it is being built. A wooden frame, called the falsework or the centering, holds every stone until the keystone closes the ring; the centering is then struck out, and the arch takes its own weight and settles a small distance into its own line of thrust. The panel drew a twofold resonance from this. First, because a construction record documents construction, the record of the book's making is largely a record of its falsework, which is one concrete reason the corpus's page-count over-represents the system the author demoted ([[the system is scaffolding rather than the centerpiece]]). Second, a struck arch settles, so the book's shape after the schemes are demoted should be expected to differ slightly from the shape the scaffolding held, and that small movement is a sign of soundness rather than of loss.

This resonance accompanies the demotion rather than testing it: the struck arch is company for how a made thing carries its own weight once its supports are gone, not evidence that the demotion is correct.
