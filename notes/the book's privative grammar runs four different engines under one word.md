# the book's privative grammar runs four different engines under one word

The book "runs on a single privative grammar, and it never says so" — recognition un-forgets, suffering unfolds, the reclaimed *psychedelic* un-conceals (author statement, 2026-08-10, recorded at [[the book reads deep change as uncovering rather than addition]]). The survey of the vault's practice-models found that the one word is carrying four engines, and that they do not move a person the same way. A subtractive privation removes what was added: Advaita's *neti neti*, the Daoist "does less every day," the apophatic stripping of predicates, and the operational form of the book's own Privation Principle, "supply the missing element." A contractive privation un-clenches a fullness that was never lost: the three malas, which "though uncontracted, appear contracted," the fold that is learned rather than repaired, the protector met with a bow, and the welcome that lets a feeling complete. A never-formed gap is an absence where something should have developed and did not: what a family never metabolized, living on in a descendant as an inability rather than an injury. And an anti-privation is a suffering diagnosed by a presence: the Racket, "Complaint + Payoff, both fixed," the one additive formula in a catalogue of subtractions.

The four are not one schematic, and treating them as one is what makes the book's grammar strain. "Supply the missing element" reads as an addition against the book's claim that deep change is uncovering; the strain is already recorded, and it resolves once the subtractive and the contractive engines are told apart, because the contractive one supplies nothing and removes nothing. The author's own account of recognition belongs to the contractive branch and to no other — a clench "that didn't obscure the vision but just made it transfigured as not directly perceivable" has a substratum and a form and no *steresis* in Aristotle's sense, nothing missing and nothing lost — which is the survey's pointing and not the reflection's assertion, held under the reflection's own standing that it "grounds no doctrinal inference." What it changes, if the author owns it, is the verb the grammar is unified under: un-clenching rather than un-covering, which [[aletheia and pratyabhijna part on whether un-seeing is concealment or contraction]] had already reached from the traditions' side.

## Substantiation

- The subtractive branch is read at its own texts: Aristotle's *steresis* is "a lack of what a thing would naturally have," a directional absence, and he "nowhere says the named absence prescribes its own restoration" ([[aristotle on privation]]); the Daodejing's "in the pursuit of the way one does less every day" (ch. 48) and Śaṅkara's *neti neti* as the removal of "the obstructions produced by ignorance" are recorded at [[there is a homeward path that goes by subtraction, letting go of what was added]] and [[advaita reaches the uncovering move through neti neti and an ever-attained freedom]], with the standing rule that the traditions "disagree on what remains after the subtraction" and their destinations must not be merged.
- The contractive branch is read at the primary Sanskrit: the malas as *saṅkoca*, contractions of intact powers, at Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam sūtra 9 ([[the triads privations map the three malas corner for corner]]); the fold that "you learn" rather than repair ([[suffering is contraction never corruption]]); the productive reading of contraction, which [[the productive reading of contraction is the book's revaluation rather than the tradition's doctrine]] marks as the book's departure from classical Trika, its classical side untested.
- The never-formed branch is Hübl's, unread, and Winnicott's: "an absence in the field rather than a wound in it," inherited as "an inability rather than an injury" ([[what was never metabolized lives on as absence and hubl reached it through inherited trauma]]); the contraction doctrine already flags this case as "a fold with no fold-maker inside the person carrying it."
- The anti-privation is the Racket, "a suffering diagnosed by a *presence* — the payoff is there and is doing work," kept as the counter-case the privative reading cannot reach ([[the privation move is an independent arrival across five fields rather than a book coinage]]; [[the racket is the attractor state with a salary]]).
- Three regions of the survey placed the author's account in the contractive branch without contact with each other: the book's own scaffolding (his account fits the uncovering generalization and strains against supply-the-missing-element), the source traditions (a substratum, a form, no *steresis*), and the coaches (only Hudson's welcome is contractive; the see-through camp is subtractive; Robbins replaces rather than un-clenches; Hübl is the third branch). The survey is `ops/practice-model ecology.md`, section 4; whether the reflection carries the revaluation is the author's to say.
- What this does not establish: that the four engines never co-occur in one practice, or that a tradition holding one cannot also hold another; the Daodejing subtracts and also lets, and Advaita negates predicates while its freedom is "never produced."
