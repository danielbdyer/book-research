# the compliance attack taken to full strength demands the second-person check rather than dissolving it

The compliance-and-aura attack, taken to its own logical end, demands the external second-person check it appeared to remove rather than dissolving it. That attack is the book's most threatening objection, a self-sealing circle in which haloed prose raises the reader's expectancy, the expectancy manufactures the felt shift, the demand of the setting secures its report, and the report recharges the prose's aura. Two features of the attack turn it, at full strength, back toward a verifier outside the reader. First, the strongest form of the demand-characteristic case predicts that an effect produced by demand vanishes once the demand is removed, so the one outcome a compliant or self-suggesting reader cannot manufacture is a durable change verified out of the situation by a third party; the discriminating instrument the attack implies is therefore an external, delayed, other-administered check. Second, the simulating-subject test the attack wields — instructed fakers passed before an assessor blind to which is which — needs a role the solitary reader cannot occupy, because a reader is at once the subject undergoing the shift and the assessor judging it. The attack does not touch the second chair; it demands one.

The consequence is that the second-person check is what survives the adversary chamber. The external verifier the contemplative traditions already built — the study partner who can reject a reading, the teacher who checks a claimed insight — is not weakened by the strongest attack but ratified by it, because the attack's two mechanisms each end at the same missing person. The chamber removes the felt shift's standing as evidence on its own and, in the same motion, requires the check that would restore it.
