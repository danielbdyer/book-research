# a genuine transformation registers first as loss rather than as gain

A genuine change in a reader is first registered as loss rather than as acquisition, and two reader-response theories that share no method arrive at that same location for it. The proposition matters to the book's verification standard because it predicts the felt signature of a real shift: a reader who reports only enrichment, only having gained something, has appreciated the text rather than been changed by it, while the reader who has changed reports something closer to bereavement. This rhymes with the book's own reading of deep change as a privative — an undoing rather than an addition — and it supplies that reading with a felt marker on the reader's side.

The two arrivals agree on the site and part on what follows it. Iser's loss is a stage inside a re-orientation that builds capacity; Barthes's is dissolution, asocial and unsayable, that builds nothing. The convergence is therefore established at the location of genuine change, not at its aftermath, and the note is held at that width.

## Substantiation

- Wolfgang Iser's 1972 essay, opened directly by the dig [P], reaches this point at page 296, where he reproduces Shaw's line that learning "always feels at first as if you had lost something" and glosses the reading event as one in which "something happens to us." The dig recorded the page and the quotation at primary depth.
- Roland Barthes defines the transformative reading, jouissance, as one that "imposes a state of loss" and unsettles the reader's assumptions, reached here through secondary reproduction rather than the primary text [S]. The primary reading of *The Pleasure of the Text* (1973) remains undone and is registered among dig 2's readings to queue (`transmission dig 2, 2026-08-13`, "Readings to queue"), bound for `ops/reading queue.md`, with the question whether Barthes ever grants jouissance any durability.
- The independence is what gives the convergence its weight: the two strata are mutually hostile in method, one phenomenological and one post-structuralist, and neither cites the other for the claim. Under `ops/methodology/authorial stance.md` this is company rather than corroboration, and the vault does not rank it as stronger evidence than any other arrival.
