# the householder road is priced long by every witness who grants it

The claim that deep change is available inside ordinary life has one adversary who attacks whether it is possible at all, Simone Weil, and it has witnesses who grant that it is possible; those witnesses converge on how long it takes. Ignatius's Annotation 19 adaptation "runs about nine months… the performing text in ordinary life is not the shorter option, it is the longer one." Al-Qushayrī holds that stations "are acquired by the sweat of one's brow" (Knysh 2007, p. 78). Maslow's plateau is "built by long living." Mehta writes that "the way to what is closest to us is the longest way back." Kornfield's record of what happens after the ecstasy is that "nobody did the laundry alone," across years. Dewey's growth is "unspectacular, cumulative, and made of ordinary experiences well connected." Christopher Alexander adds a structural warning, that "discontinuous change is the mechanism by which living structure is destroyed."

The convergence leaves the book's doctrine untouched, because every one of these witnesses grants that an ordinary Tuesday can be the site of the work. What it presses on is the book's form. The book's arc is written to be traversed in a single reading, which gives it the shape of a peak, and these seven witnesses jointly say that change inside ordinary life does not have that shape, taking months to decades instead. The book's own material on what follows a big experience states what happens after a peak; this convergence states how long a stretch of time the book's held container would have to be built for, and that is a design pressure the author's commitment to the one-reading arc has to hold or to answer.

## Substantiation

- The seven positions are quoted from the evaluations written for those thinkers. Ignatius's is anchored to the Annotations read at his evaluation on 2026-08-02, and Kornfield's and Maslow's rest on works about them; the remaining four are marked on their notes as derived rather than read in the thinker's own work, and this claim carries that mixed standing forward.
- The nearest existing claim is [[what the big experience leaves behind depends on what follows it]], which reports the clinical finding that peak experiences fade on a decay curve and that lasting change tracks the completeness of the experience and the practice that follows it. That claim says how long the aftermath runs; this one says how long the road itself runs.
- The one-reading arc is the author's live commitment and nothing here proposes changing it. What this claim records is how long the book's own company says the road takes.

**A counterpoint arrived on 2026-08-06, and the word "every" in this claim fails as written.** A measured literature documents deep change in ordinary lives that is sudden and durable. The quantum-change program (Miller and C'de Baca, *Quantum Change*, Guilford 2001 — "ordinary lives" is the subtitle's own setting) reports vivid, permanent transformations, with a ten-year follow-up finding them sustained (C'de Baca and Wilbourne, *Journal of Clinical Psychology* 60(5), 2004), and the sudden-gains literature (Tang and DeRubeis, *JCCP* 67(6), 1999; meta-analyzed at g = 0.62 in Aderka et al., *JCCP* 80(1), 2012) finds large between-session steps that carry half of a patient's total improvement and hold at follow-up. The claim's honest form is therefore "every witness assembled so far," and the convergence about duration is a fact about the company that has been gathered rather than about the field. Two further facts are kept alongside the counterpoint. The quantum changes arrived unbidden, described as "a bolt from the blue", which makes the same witnesses company for the refusal of the engineered epiphany even as they break this claim's universality. And the program's method, which recruited by newspaper advertisement and relied on retrospective self-report, has to survive a reading before the counterpoint hardens; the question that can fail is proposed in `ops/reading queue.md`, the list of readings each stated as a question with a stake and a page-level address. These findings were checked against published literature in a web research round, and the record of that round is `archive/research — counterpoint round background.md`, which holds the citations and what each was checked for.
