# one person can turn another toward their own light without dimming either

Being helped to recognize yourself does not make you the helper's dependent. The light was already yours; another person only turned you toward it, and a turning takes nothing and adds nothing to the flame — it was going to be your fire either way. The reader gains permission to be genuinely helped without fear of being colonized: love and teaching, at their truest, expand the authorship of the one they reach, because they wake a light rather than lend one. Its use to the writing is to weld the recognition doctrine to the book's core of love and answering, answering the master question's hardest word — remaining the author of what you become while changed *by* another — structurally.

## Substantiation

- Quakerism (George Fox): "there is that of God in every one," and the practice of "answering that of God" in another — the Inner Light already present in each person, so ministry only turns them toward what they already carry and needs no mediator (Fox's Epistles and Journal; attributed, to verify). This is the plain seventeenth-century English arrival of the book's third core move: love that makes the beloved more themselves.
- First-principles, for "without dimming either": if what passes between two people were a finite content transferred, helping would deplete the helper and indebt the helped; but if the helper only occasions a recognition the other performs on their own already-present light, the transaction has no ledger. Rogers keeps company — the conditions that release another's actualizing tendency cost the helper no actualizing of their own.
- Standing: nascent; a real Fox locus is queued, and the derivation is held so it does not over-claim.
