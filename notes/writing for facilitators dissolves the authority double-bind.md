# writing for facilitators dissolves the authority double-bind

The author proposes addressing the first draft exclusively to facilitators — same subject matter, same scope, but a readership that already understands at the outset what the book is doing — and observes that the move shifts the duty to prove authority into a facilitation of facilitation rather than an authorial transformation that must first be unbound from a general reader's expectations. The conversation confirms that this dissolves a tension it had been treating as intrinsic to the work. A general readership forces the book into a double movement: establishing enough authority that the reader trusts the terrain, and relinquishing authority so the reader can discover their own relationship to it, two impulses that pull against one another. A reader who is already a facilitator, or is becoming capable of facilitating, does not need the book to establish that an encounter cannot simply be transmitted, so the book can speak as one facilitator helping another become more capable of creating conditions rather than as an authority delivering an experience.

## Substantiation

- The author's proposal, quoted from the conversation of 2026-08-13: "What if the first draft of the book was specifically written with all of this in mind but whose readership was exclusively facilitators themselves realizing this at the outset — same topic area, no adjustment in scope... the duty to prove authority on the topics shifts into just a facilitation of facilitation rather than an authorial transformation dynamic that has to be first unbound from the general reader's expectations. That could remove a core tension."
- The double-bind it dissolves, quoted from the response: a general readership requires the book to both "Establish enough authority that the reader trusts the terrain" and "Relinquish authority so the reader can discover their own relationship to it," and "Those impulses pull against one another."
- The reframed contract, quoted from the same passage: to a facilitator the book can say "I'm not here to give you the experience. I'm here to help you become more capable of creating conditions in which experience can happen — while remaining aware that you cannot control what happens," and "The facilitator becomes the first participant, but not necessarily the audience in the same sense."
- A blind play-lineage excavation named a theatrical mechanism that performs the same double-bind in a single act rather than dissolving it by readership, now read at the primary text: Johnstone's teacher who takes the failure onto himself — "I play low status physically but my actual status is going up, since only a very confident and experienced person would put the blame for failure on himself" (*Impro*, p. 29) — at once establishes trust and lowers his played authority. The reading sharpened the caveat that matters for the book: Johnstone's device is thoroughly embodied and live (he sits on the floor; the students "almost certainly start sliding off their chairs"), so it ports to a disembodied author only as a *structural analogue* — building the book so the reader's not-getting-it is the book's fault rather than the reader's — not as Johnstone's actual mechanism, which needs a body performing low status in real time. The remainder stays: it relocates demand rather than removing it, and the reader's resulting trust can itself read as an aura-and-expectancy effect (*Impro*, p. 29; transmission dig 7, 2026-08-13).
