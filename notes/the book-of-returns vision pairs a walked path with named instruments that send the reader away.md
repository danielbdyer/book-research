# the book-of-returns vision pairs a walked path with named instruments that send the reader away

The forest panel's second vision proposed the book as a pocket book sewn to lie flat, in two unequal parts. The first part is a thin continuous account of one walked path; the second is a large gathering of short named instruments, each a page or less, each ending with a stopping instruction and a verification condition in the reader's own body, none numbered and none claiming to complete the set. That standing is the one the author gave the Vijñāna Bhairava comparison — ways in rather than a map of the territory. The book opens on the ordinary Tuesday after the retreat and ends with the crisis floor's three questions printed alone on the last page. The reader is instructed to put the book down, repeatedly, and the book states plainly that being put down is its success condition.

This vision stays a recorded candidate, a road not taken beside the correspondence the author chose. It marks its assumptions as losable bets: the pronoun splits, first person in the account and a neutral imperative in the instruments; the page-turn engine is abandoned for a return engine, which is itself a bet that the tier-three question the book asks is the wrong one; the poems do not appear; and the verification standard bears weight one small claim at a time rather than all at once. Its wager is that a reader will come back to a book that keeps sending them away, and the panel stated the cost without softening it: no book it could name has survived on a return engine alone. What the vision makes urgent, whatever the author does with it, is a set of registered absences — the per-practice verification conditions the forms never supply, the crisis floor, and ripening held as permission.
