# gregory bateson

Gregory Bateson was an anthropologist and systems thinker whose work on communication, learning, and pathology anticipated much of what the book builds. The space-around survey calls him "possibly the largest single missing grandfather in the whole edifice" and places him first in its priority list. He is not yet seated; this note is the candidate file.

The case for a seat rests on three specific correspondences. First, his double bind — contradictory demands issued at two logical levels, with escape and comment both forbidden — is the ancestor of the book's Racket diagnostic, and it adds something the Racket lacks: an account of how such structures get installed as the only available solution to an impossible situation, which converts blame into history. Second, his levels of learning give the book's folds a formal ladder: learning within a context; learning the context itself (which is what a fold is — accumulated context-learning, also called character); and the rare, destabilizing change in that second-level learning, which is where the book's reframe formally lives. Third, his ecology of mind — the insistence that the unit of survival is organism-plus-environment — supplies grounding for the book's structural voice, which holds that some suffering lives in circumstances rather than selves.

The survey records no case against a seat, and the evaluation has not yet been run against his primary texts. The open question for the evaluation is where he sits: the survey's correspondences touch three different seats (the speech-act lineage's diagnostic, the coaches' theories of suffering, and the relational-field material), so the seat recommendation is itself part of the evaluation.

## What the evaluation requires

Read the double bind papers and the learning-levels material in Steps to an Ecology of Mind; verify the three correspondences against the primary text rather than the survey's summary; write the recommendation. This work has not been done.
