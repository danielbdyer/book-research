# the locus of control is better named the locus of inquiry

The author brought a recurring motif to the conversation, which he named the locus of control, and the exchange reinterpreted what that locus locates. On the reinterpretation the locus is not a position from which one settles which account of reality is correct; it is the place from which one participates, and the question it opens is what that place makes possible. The move relocates the term from control over the truth to the standpoint of inquiry — hence the locus is better understood as a locus of inquiry than as a locus of control. The generative question the exchange arrives at is not "which account is right?" but "from which locus am I participating, and what does that locus make possible?", and the conversation observes that the locus can move — a person can participate from an experiential, a dialogical, or a facilitative standpoint, each disclosing something the others do not.

The claim matters to the book because it converts a question that forces rivalry between frameworks into one that puts them to work. Asking which ontology is correct makes the frameworks compete; asking from which locus one is participating makes each framework a place from which some part of reality becomes intelligible, which is the posture the book takes toward the several traditions it draws on.
