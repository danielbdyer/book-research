# abram's more-than-human makes the animate world a participant in the inner life

The book's relational field is human-only, the witnesses it seats being human minds. David Abram's "more-than-human" is the independent arrival that presses on that limit: perception is inherently participatory and reciprocal with an animate world, so the sensing body is always already in exchange with land, air, and animals, and inner life is co-constituted by an outer living world that takes part in it. A reading confirms it. It is the exact phrase the clearing reaches for, and it pairs with Buber's tree as the non-human's independent arrival beside its chain-link. The corpus already names Abram on its recommended-reading list, so this grows a name the book already carries.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the field does work and is the book's deepest claim, modeled on human recognition (walked VIII; [[the field between two people does work]]).
- Abram's half is now shown, from his own statements and reliable sources (the reading record is `archive/reading — abram and kimmerer on the more-than-human (1996 and 2013).md`): he coined "the more-than-human world," his premise is "the fluid, participatory nature of perception, and the reciprocity between our senses and the sensuous earth," and "we are human only in contact, and conviviality, with what is not human." Kimmerer's grammar of animacy is the independent company: "These beings are not 'it,' they are our relatives," and indigenous languages "use the same words to address the living world as we use for our family." Read via the authors' statements and reliable sources, not the full books.
- The refusal is kept. Abram's participation is sensory, pre-reflective, and outdoor — reached through the body's coupling with the land — and it is skeptical of the interiorizing move that lifts experience out of the sensuous world, which lands squarely on a book of inner life read alone indoors. To admit it, the book must let the structure of inner life open onto an animate outer world it does not currently model.
