# abram's more-than-human makes the animate world a participant in the inner life

The book's relational field is human-only, the witnesses it seats being human minds. David Abram's "more-than-human" is the independent arrival that presses on that limit: perception is inherently participatory and reciprocal with an animate world, so the sensing body is always already in exchange with land, air, and animals, and inner life is co-constituted by an outer living world that takes part in it. It is the exact phrase the clearing reaches for, and it pairs with Buber's tree as the non-human's independent arrival beside its chain-link. The corpus already names Abram on its recommended-reading list, so this grows a name the book already carries. The reading is not done, so the claim is privated.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the field does work and is the book's deepest claim, modeled on human recognition (walked VIII; [[the field between two people does work]]).
- Abram's half is named, not shown: the participatory-perception argument is in *The Spell of the Sensuous* (1996), which draws Merleau-Ponty's "flesh of the world" into ecology, with Robin Wall Kimmerer's "Learning the Grammar of Animacy" in *Braiding Sweetgrass* (2013) as its independent company. To verify at the text; not quoted from memory. Abram is on the recommended-reading roster with no note; Kimmerer is seated among container-makers for the wrong reason.
- The refusal is kept. Abram's participation is sensory, pre-reflective, and outdoor — reached through the body's coupling with the land — and it is skeptical of the interiorizing move that lifts experience out of the sensuous world, which lands squarely on a book of inner life read alone indoors. To admit it, the book must let the structure of inner life open onto an animate outer world it does not currently model.
