# lectio divina is a four-rung reading protocol in which each rung sends the reader to the next

Guigo II's *Scala Claustralium*, "The Ladder of Monks" (c. 1150), sets out a reading practice as four sequenced rungs. *Lectio* is the careful study of the text with the whole attention; *meditatio* is the mind's action to investigate the hidden truth; *oratio* is devoted attending to God; and *contemplatio* is the mind lifted above itself. The rungs are not a menu of options. Each is described as powerless to obtain its own object and therefore as handing the reader on: reading is the foundation and gives the matter for meditation, meditation finds the treasure but cannot obtain it and so sends the reader to prayer, and prayer opens toward the contemplation that is the practice's end. The dig compresses Guigo's own formula: reading seeks, meditation finds, prayer asks, contemplation tastes.

The claim about the form is that its sequence is what guards it. A reading that stops at the first rung is, in Guigo's figure, food taken into the mouth and never chewed — reading-as-information, which the protocol exists to prevent by refusing to let the first rung be the whole. The book's own reading contract asks for the same refusal at the smaller scale of a single sitting, instructing the reader to slow down and to treat the urge to move on as information rather than as permission.
