# participatory stewardship is the capacity beneath both books

The reframe conversation proposes the name "participatory stewardship" for the capacity it places beneath both books. The claim is that facilitation of another, the work of Book I, and self-facilitation, the work of Book II, are two expressions of one underlying capacity, which the conversation states plainly as "the capacity to remain authorially present while participating in a reality one does not completely control." The name marks that capacity and is not the capacity itself; in plain terms the concern is keeping authorship of one's own participation inside a field one shares and does not govern. The conversation sets the capacity against six things it is not — control, surrender, optimization, passivity, autonomy, and dependence — and judges the formulation "closer to the philosophical center of the project than 'facilitation' itself."

The name is held as a candidate under the vault's naming policy: it is attributed to the conversation once and not carried as settled vocabulary, and the concern it marks is stated in plain terms wherever it is used. Because the formulation is the conversation's model-elaborated proposal rather than the author's own words, the claim is kept nascent, and it stands as one of several candidate names for the project's center rather than a chosen one.

## Substantiation

- The unification and the name, in the conversation's words: "facilitation and self-facilitation are manifestations of the same underlying capacity," which "might provisionally be called: participatory stewardship."
- The capacity in plain terms: "the capacity to remain authorially present while participating in a reality one does not completely control."
- Its weighting against facilitation: the formulation "may actually be closer to the philosophical center of the project than 'facilitation' itself."
- Naming standing: "participatory stewardship" is one of several candidate center-names the conversation offered without choosing among them, held here under the vault's naming policy and stated plainly as authorship kept within shared, uncontrolled participation.
