# the book's honest origin story is noticing first and finding company after

The provenance work ran for five rounds, and every dated case came out the same way. *Kairos rituals* is the author's coinage of March 2025, fifteen months before a split clock was read as kairos. *Gravitational wells* organized his flourishing practices in October 2024, two years before an atlas took the image as its architecture. The reclamation of *psychedelic* was performed on his own life on New Year's Day 2026, months before the corpus formalized it. *Automatic yet fully attended* is his sentence about facilitation from January 2026, which the survey later matched to the classical wu wei debate as though it were doctrine. The youngness line is a poem's ending. The participation law was lived in a room in 2017, nine years before it was assembled into a roll-call. The pattern held every single time it was tested: the author said the plain thing first, in his own words, about his own life, and the scholarship arrived afterward and dressed it.

The consequence is an origin story, and it is smaller and stronger than the one the corpus tells. The corpus narrates convergence discovered — traditions agreeing, unanimity found, the framework confirmed. The story the record supports is: **a man noticed some things, and then found out that other people had noticed them too.** That telling asks for nothing. It claims no unanimity, needs no independence argument, and survives every flag the provenance work raised, because company is not corroboration and this telling never says it is. It is the authorial stance — gesture at the crossovers, hold no evidentiary remarks — with its feet on dated ground.

And it doubles as the book's invitation, which is why a drafting session would reach for it. The reader the book addresses has also been noticing things — on runs, in kitchens, in the middle of hard nights — without knowing the noticings had company. A book whose origin story is *I noticed, then found company* is performing its own offer on page one: what happened to the author reading the traditions is what the book proposes to be for the reader. The finding of company is the corpus's own recognition doctrine at the scale of authorship — *this is that* — and telling it plainly models the event the book exists to occasion.
