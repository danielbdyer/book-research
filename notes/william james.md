# william james

William James built the method this book's stance re-invents. The *Varieties* holds transformation reports as observations — "religious feelings and religious impulses… recorded in literature produced by articulate and fully self-conscious men" — and splits every evaluation into two judgments that must not contaminate each other: the existential judgment (what is it, how did it come about) and the spiritual judgment (what is it worth, now that it is here), with worth settled "by their fruits ye shall know them, not by their roots" (Varieties, lecture I). That is the authorial stance with a century of standing: gesture at the phenomena, hold no evidentiary remarks about their cause, judge nothing by its origin.

Two of his findings bear directly. His four marks of the mystical state include transiency, and his paragraph on it names the book's promise in 1902: "Mystical states cannot be sustained for long… half an hour, or at most an hour or two, seems to be the limit beyond which they fade into the light of common day" — with the generous clause the promise also needs: "when they recur it is recognized; and from one recurrence to another it is susceptible of continuous development in what is felt as inner richness and importance" (Varieties, lectures XVI–XVII). Fading is constitutive, and recurrence is cumulative — *it didn't hold* and *it can still be built on*, in one paragraph. And his verdict on authority is the sovereignty doctrine exactly: mystical states "usually are, and have the right to be, absolutely authoritative over the individuals to whom they come," but "no authority emanates from them which should make it a duty for those who stand outside of them to accept their revelations uncritically" — the reader's experience binds the reader and licenses no claim on anyone else, which is the book's convergence-as-company rule and its auxiliary relation to a sovereign reader, stated as one sentence in 1902.

The refusal worth keeping is his test itself: James verifies by fruits, and the felt shift is a root. His test for what an experience is worth looks at the life that follows it — conduct, character, what the experience made — never at the feeling's intensity or its bodily signature. Held whole, this refuses the book's verification standard as sufficient: a shift in the sternum is root-side evidence, and James would ask what fruit it bore by spring. The book's own promise ("it didn't hold") concedes his point, which is what makes the refusal company rather than damage.

A second work is now read at the primary text, and it seats James in the felt-shift concern as well as the stance. *The Principles of Psychology* (1890) draws the distinction the implicit-knowing notes turn on: "There are two kinds of knowledge broadly and practically distinguishable: we may call them respectively knowledge of acquaintance and knowledge-about" (I:221) — acquaintance a knowing had *through* contact and unable to be conveyed in words ("I cannot impart acquaintance with them to any one who has not already made it himself"), against the propositional knowing accumulated *about* a thing. James footnotes the distinction to John Grote (*Exploratio Philosophica*, 1865), so it is his self-flagged debt rather than his coinage. And "The Stream of Thought" names the felt penumbra the felt-sense notes lean on: every image "steeped and dyed in the free water that flows round it," consciousness "constituted of feelings of tendency, often so vague that we are unable to name them at all," a "psychic overtone, suffusion, or fringe" (I:254–258). One bound the reading fixed: James ranks acquaintance the *barer* knowing, not the richer one (I:259), so he grounds knowing-*through* as a distinct kind of knowing, and the "know more than you can tell" surplus stays Polanyi's phrasing rather than his.
