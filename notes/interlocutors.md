# interlocutors

This map organizes the people the book thinks with. The corpus's rule for them, stated in its own words: "secrets are for the story, never for the sources" — every thinker is named on the page at the moment their idea appears. The vault's version of that rule is the sources field on every claim.

Each interlocutor note records the seat the thinker holds and the single sentence that justifies it (the corpus calls this the clause that earns the chair). Candidates arrive through [[research-frontier]] and are evaluated against the admission bar recorded in [[methods]]. A candidate evaluation ends in a recommendation; the seating decision belongs to the author.

## Seat taxonomy

The seats are functional. The corpus's narrative names for them are recorded in [[figure glossary]].

| Seat | Concern |
|------|---------|
| `facilitation` | The relational-practice technology: witnessing, group protocols, Circling. |
| `emotion-work` | Emotions treated as messengers carrying information; the practice of channeling. |
| `speech-acts` | Language as mechanism: performative speech, declaration versus disclosure. |
| `container-makers` | Builders of enterable structure across different media; the evidence base for the container anatomy. |
| `coaches` | The contemporary transformation landscape, organized by five theories of where suffering lives; the book's referral network. |
| `source-tradition` | The Trika (Kashmir Śaiva) tradition: root texts and their authors. |
| `reference-lineage` | Philosophical grounding hosted in the back matter rather than the main narrative. |
| `margin-voices` | The annotating hands that live in the book's margins. |
| `chapter-patron` | The epigraph seat at the door of each chapter. |
| `recommended-reading` | The curated reading list. |
| `candidate` | Under evaluation; not yet seated. |
| `declined` | Evaluated and not seated; the reason is retained. |

## Notes in this map

- [[david loy]] — seated, reference-lineage. His account of constitutive lack independently confirms the book's first contraction.
- [[gregory bateson]] — candidate. The survey calls him possibly the largest missing ancestor of the whole framework.
- [[internal family systems]] — candidate. The clinical model closest to the book's ethic; the book needs a stated position relative to it.
- [[hartmut rosa]] — candidate. His sociology of resonance supplies a completed theory for the book's closing image.
- [[ignatius of loyola]] — candidate. The Spiritual Exercises are a four-century-old precedent for the book's central design gamble.

## Roster recorded in the corpus

Notes are created for these thinkers when existing or new claims need them; until then this roster locates them. Facilitation: the Authentic Relating and Circling lineages. Emotion-work: Karla McLaren. Speech-acts: Austin, Erhard, Flores, Dreyfus, with Heidegger anchoring the group and one warning the corpus records in its own words: a declared self can be "worn as costume" — transformation performed rather than undergone. Container-makers, twenty-nine in the corpus's count: Alexander, Meadows, Akomolafe, Gendlin, McGilchrist, Carse, Whyte, O'Donohue, Oliver, Berry, brown, Parker, Scharmer, Palmer, Kimmerer, Oxman, Matuschak, Appleton, Brander, Hudson, Jeong Kwan, Le Guin, Eno, Brand, hooks, Freire, Rohr, Tippett — with the axis position (selfhood cohering as container) held by the book itself. Coaches, eleven figures across five theories of suffering: Crone, Katie, and Singer (believed thought); Hudson (resisted feeling); Robbins (disempowered state); the Conscious Leadership Group and Colonna (disowned complicity); Wineland and Deida (closed body or withheld gift); expanded by the corpus with Frankl, Weil, and Camus (suffering inhabited), Lacan (the refusal), and Hübl plus the structural voice (suffering located outside the self). Source-tradition: Vasugupta, Kṣemarāja, Utpaladeva, Abhinavagupta, Bhartṛhari. Reference-lineage: Loy, Nishida, Eckhart, Nāgārjuna, Benjamin, Winnicott, Rosenberg, Bakhtin, Rosenstock-Huessy, Arendt, Celan, Dōgen, Kabir, Weil, Zhuangzi, Suzuki, Wilber, Hyde, Varela, Bohm, van Gennep and Turner, the Talmudic study tradition, Adorno, Han, Rieff, Illich, Rancière, Murdoch, Rogers, Heschel, Gilbert. Margin-voices: Julian of Norwich, Heraclitus, Kṣemarāja, Lao Tzu, a modern pencil revealed late in the book, and a doubting hand added by the revision program. Chapter-patrons, in spine order: Rilke, Alexander, Buber, Heraclitus, Lao Tzu, Gendlin with McLaren, Meadows, McGilchrist, hooks, with later seats rotating. Recommended-reading: Abram, Li-Young Lee, Gilbert, the Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam, Alexander's Book Four, Hyde's The Gift, the Zhuangzi, and Loy's applied writings.

## Adjacent maps
