# donald schön

Donald Schön's subject was how competent professionals actually work, as against how the professions describe themselves as working. His finding was that skilled practice runs on knowing-in-action — judgment carried in the doing, not derivable from the rules the profession teaches — and that the practitioner is continuously in conversation with a situation that talks back, reframing the problem as they work rather than solving a problem given in advance.

His image for the stakes is the one the grid singles out. On the high hard ground, problems are well-formed and technical rationality solves them; in the swampy lowlands lie the problems of greatest human concern, which are messy, resist technique, and are where the practitioner has to work anyway. The choice between them is not a choice between rigour and sloppiness but between relevance and irrelevance.

## Why the sort wants him

The book's first law says the knower is inside what they know, and Schön reached a working version of it from professional education — a discipline with no contact with either pole of the founding convergence ([[the convergence's independence fails where three of its names share one teacher]]). Practitioner pedagogy is one of three disciplines the sort finds near-empty across every load.

The grid's own note is that the swampy-lowlands image is "the de-management pass, drawn in 1983 by a management theorist" (`mesh grid: tier 3`) — which matters because the book's argument against technique-driven transformation is made from inside a contemplative register, and here is the same argument made from inside the management literature it would be objecting to.

## What he would refuse

Derived rather than sourced; no primary work has been read in this vault.

Schön would refuse the dissolution of expertise. His practitioner is highly trained, works inside a discipline with standards, and reflects in action *on a body of technique they have already mastered* — knowing-in-action is what expertise becomes, not an alternative to acquiring it. A book addressed to a reader working alone, with no discipline and no standards to reflect against, is using half his structure.

He would also refuse the interior turn. His conversation is with a material, a client, a design problem; the situation that talks back is outside the practitioner. Reading it as a description of a person in conversation with their own experience is a transposition he did not make.

## What the evaluation still requires

*The Reflective Practitioner*, with one question that decides the seat: whether knowing-in-action is a claim about the structure of knowing, which would make it an arrival at the book's law, or a claim about professional competence specifically, which would make it a useful parallel and nothing more.
