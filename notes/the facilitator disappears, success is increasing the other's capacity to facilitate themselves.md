# the facilitator disappears, success is increasing the other's capacity to facilitate themselves

The reframe conversation places a paradox at the close of Book I: the best facilitator eventually becomes less necessary. As the participant grows more capable of noticing, discerning, relating, participating, and authoring, dependency on the facilitator decreases. Success is therefore defined against the facilitator's own indispensability — the facilitator succeeds by increasing the other's capacity to facilitate themselves. The claim is also a structural hinge. Staged as the epilogue in which the facilitator disappears, it mirrors and opens Book II, which turns the same facilitation inward toward a practitioner facilitating their own becoming. The disappearance is the dramaturgy by which Book I performs its own ethic and hands the reader across to the self-facilitation material.
