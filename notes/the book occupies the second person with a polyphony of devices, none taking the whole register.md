# the book occupies the second person with a polyphony of devices, none taking the whole register

The book's architecture assumed a second person the medium cannot supply — a study-partner, a judge, a mirror — and the author's disposition is that no single device is asked to become that person. Across both the facilitator book and the self-facilitation book, the second person is occupied by a polyphony of devices rather than by one second voice running the whole register: a second voice is "one such way to occupy the third space," alongside a poetic symbol or experiment, or a passage that shifts perspective, one of many. The author reached the disposition after the margins question moved off its original hinge — from "should the book run a second voice?" to whether a second voice can do its work without the second person the architecture assumed — and his answer keeps the second voice as one member of a plurality and refuses to let any device totalize. On his account a plurality of partial devices is "more honest" than a single device asked to be the absent partner, because it claims less.

## Substantiation

- The author states the disposition in his own words: the second voice is "one such way to occupy the third space and it doesn't have to take up the entire register of the entire book. It can be a poetic symbol or experiment or part of the book as it shifts perspectives or something like that. One of many, a polyphony, sounds more honest across the facilitator and the self-facilitation" (author statement, 2026-08-16, open-decisions queue, B1).
- The cross-dig synthesis records where the margins decision moved: after Dig III the question is "a decision about whether the book can manufacture the absent second person, not about whether to run a second voice" (transmission dig 0, 2026-08-13, §5.4, verdict i). The author's polyphony answers that question by distributing the second-person function across devices rather than restoring the person in one.
- Dig III's warning is kept beside the disposition and bounds it: every margin architecture that ran for centuries sat inside a binding community with living authority, and the one margin device that recruits enactment rather than collection — jakugo — is load-bearing on its checker and decays to a phrase-book without it (transmission dig 3, 2026-08-13). A polyphony of devices therefore does not restore the second person; it lets no one device claim to be the partner the medium is short.
