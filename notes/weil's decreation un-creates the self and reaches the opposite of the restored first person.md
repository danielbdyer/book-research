# weil's decreation un-creates the self and reaches the opposite of the restored first person

Simone Weil is already the book's seated adversary, the renunciation that keeps the ordinary-life commitment honest ([[weil is seated as the opponent the plumbing deserves]]). Her concept of *decreation* is the unmined mechanism of that opposition: to make something created pass into the uncreated, to un-create the "I" so that God can occupy the place it vacates, grounded in God's own self-renunciation. It is the same make-room-by-self-withdrawal move as *tzimtzum*, *kenosis*, and the *fana* pole — Weil draws them together herself — but it reaches the opposite conclusion. The "I" is the obstacle to be un-created permanently, where the book's fullness contracts and then opens back into fullness, the first person restored. So the withdrawal family splits along the book's own householder-versus-renunciation fork, and decreation is the sharpest statement of the far side. A reading of *Gravity and Grace* confirms it at the text.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the first person is the road to selflessness rather than its obstacle, restored on the last page ([[the fullness of the first person is the road to selflessness rather than its obstacle]]), and Weil is seated against the plumbing (atlas §XI; [[weil is seated as the opponent the plumbing deserves]]).
- Decreation's half is now shown, from *Gravity and Grace* in the Crawford translation (the reading record is `archive/reading — weil, gravity and grace on decreation (1947).md`). The definition, from "Decreation": "Decreation: to make something created pass into the uncreated," marked off from "Destruction: to make something created pass into nothingness. A blameworthy substitute for decreation." The grounding, from "Renunciation": "Imitation of God's renunciation in creation. In a sense God renounces being everything. We should renounce being something. That is our only good." So the "I" passes back into the uncreated, in imitation of a prior divine self-withdrawal — the same make-room-by-contraction shape *tzimtzum* names. Read via a reproduction of the "Decreation" material confirmed at two independent sources, not the French or a scholarly critical edition.
- The refusal is the finding. Decreation opposes the restored first person outright, and its room is made *for God* rather than for a life: the decreated self becomes a co-creator with God — "We participate in the creation of the world by decreating ourselves" — a cosmic and theological destination the book's register does not share. So it stays adversary, and its value is to give that adversary a precise, quotable concept and to expose the fork the whole withdrawal family divides on. The formulation once flagged as unconfirmable is the co-creator line just quoted, now confirmed at the text and usable with its translation provenance (Crawford, 1963).
