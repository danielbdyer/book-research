# a kept tension can be mapped rather than only held

The gesture asserts that a paradox can be mapped — that there is a procedure for holding two opposed things in view without resolving them or being paralysed by them. The vault does not hold this, and the absence is conspicuous, because the corpus is built on kept tensions. The atlas registers nine fault lines and eight assessment tensions, each with a status and a treatment under trial, and every one of those treatments is an authorial move made in the writing. None is a move the reader can make. If the gesture is right, the reader needs the same capacity the author is exercising, and the book never teaches it.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- The atlas's tension policy is that a strain is "kept as a feature, never resolved by fiat" (atlas §0), which states the posture and not the method.
- Paradox appears four times in the space-around survey and once each in the other sources; the survey's uses are in its counterweight material rather than as a proposed method.
- The nearest executed material is the polarity teaching, which holds that tension is home and is to be inhabited (abridged ch9). That is a disposition rather than a procedure, and the gesture names a procedure.

## What would close it

Closing it means producing the reader-facing move that corresponds to what the author does when a tension is kept.
