# the executed draft introduces each thinker by the duration of their labor

The convergence of thinkers, which the author has named one of the book's two centerpieces, has a sentence-level formula in the executed draft. A thinker arrives with the duration of their labor stated first, and the finding is then compressed into one sentence: "Christopher Alexander spent his career trying to name this. He came to the conclusion, late in his life, that it could not be named. He kept trying anyway" (abridged ch1). "Eugene Gendlin's life work, distilled into a single sentence, was this: what is split off, not felt, remains the same… He arrived at this through decades of empirical work" (abridged ch5). Where a thinker limited their own finding, that limit is kept inside the introduction rather than dropped from it: Alexander's late conclusion that the quality could not be named is his refusal, and it stands in the same breath as what he found.

The duration clause does two things the convergence needs. It shows that the finding was arrived at across a working life rather than simply asserted, which is the difference between a person joining a conversation and a citation propped up to settle one. And because the reader has just been told the finding took a career, the one-sentence version of it reads as a life's work boiled down rather than as a slogan.

## Substantiation

- Seven introductions were verified as carrying the duration clause — Heidegger, Alexander, Buber, Gendlin, Meadows, Berry, and Oliver, each at their own chapter in the extracted text; the Alexander and Gendlin specimens are quoted above.
- Anyone can check this by listing the draft's in-text thinker introductions and counting those in which a duration clause ("spent his career," "life work," "the better part of a life") precedes a one-sentence finding. An introduction built any other way is the exception to look for.
- The corpus states the design law this formula carries out: the book's thinkers are to be held as a company the reader gradually joins rather than as a bibliography consulted, so that the reader feels admitted to a live argument rather than handed a settled canon ([[the company is joined by the reader rather than consulted]]). Saying how long someone worked is what makes that person someone at the table rather than an authority cited in a footnote.
