# the book points at the external check it cannot administer, the finger rather than the moon

Dig VI dissolved the choice between practitioner testimony and academic citation by showing each fails alone, and left standing a third requirement neither supplies — an external, delayed, other-administered check, a felt result that can fail out of the room, later, in front of someone who did not induce it. The author accepts the requirement and states the book's relation to it: supplying the check is "not exclusively the book's responsibility to be the moon but the finger pointing towards it as long as it admits it as such." The book does not administer the external check; it points at the check and admits, in the same breath, that it cannot administer it. This applies to the specific check Dig VI isolated the respect the author already stated as a core trust — that the book must respect it cannot verify the reader has done the work — so the pointing is honest rather than evasive: a finger that admits it is a finger names the moon it is not.

## Substantiation

- The author states the disposition in his own words: the third requirement "is valid, and yet it's not exclusively the book's responsibility to be the moon but the finger pointing towards it as long as it admits it as such" (author statement, 2026-08-16, open-decisions queue, B2).
- The cross-dig synthesis records the requirement the author is answering: the decision "is not testimony-versus-citation," and "what the strata leave standing is a third requirement neither option supplies — an external, delayed, other-administered check," so "the book earns trust not by choosing testimony or citation but by carrying a felt result that can *fail* out of the room, later, in front of someone else" (transmission dig 0, 2026-08-13, §5.4, verdict ii).
- Dig VI names what that external check is in the traditions' own terms — "exactly the second person Dig III's traditions installed (chavruta, dokusan, an abbot's *discretio*)" (transmission dig 6, 2026-08-13) — which is the person the book points at and cannot be.
