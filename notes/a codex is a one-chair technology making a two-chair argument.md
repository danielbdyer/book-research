# a codex is a one-chair technology making a two-chair argument

The book's deepest claim concerns the field between two people; a printed book is read by one. The corpus calls this its structural wound and declines to soften it: if witnessing is the completion mechanism that solitary insight cannot supply, then the medium delivering that claim is structurally short one chair.

The treatment under trial is the Second Chair: designing the book as one half of a pair — passages built to be read toward someone, with the Talmudic practice of paired study (chavruta) as the working ancestor. The corpus keeps the tension open on purpose, since the treatment's sufficiency is an empirical question about readers rather than a design fact. The space-around survey adds the strongest historical precedent: Ignatius concluded the Spiritual Exercises could not complete alone and built the companion into the form.

## Substantiation

- Atlas §XI.1d records the tension and treatment; §VIII introduces the underlying conflict with the sentence "the book's deepest claim, and its most exposed flank."
- First-telling, "The between," sets up the bind for the reader ("which puts a book — an object read alone — in an honest bind") and defers the answer to the design.
