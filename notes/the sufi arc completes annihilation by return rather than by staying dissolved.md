# the sufi arc completes annihilation by return rather than by staying dissolved

The book walks an arc whose peak is completed by its descent and integration, and its householder axiom holds that the ordinary Tuesday is where the work lands ([[the tuesday is the temple]]; [[the householder road is priced long by every witness who grants it]]). The Sufi structure of *fana* and *baqa*, in al-Junayd's "sobriety" doctrine, reaches the same finding: the self is annihilated (*fana*) and then subsists and returns transformed (*baqa*), and the return to full, responsible life in the world is "the very proof and completion" of the annihilation rather than an afterthought. Annihilation-and-return is one structure, which is an independent arrival at the book's claim that the peak is finished by the return. The reading is not done, so the claim is privated.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the arc concentrates a peak and then restores the ground for integration (atlas §I), and the ordinary-life commitment is the householder axiom.
- The Sufi half is named, not shown: the annihilation-then-subsistence structure is in al-Junayd's *Rasa'il*, especially the *Kitab al-Fana*, and in the *fana*/*baqa* chapter of al-Qushayri's *Risala* — which is distinct from the state-and-station chapter the vault already cites at [[al-qushayri]]. Both are to verify at the text; the structure is an unmined part of a tradition already seated for a different finding.
- The refusal is kept. Annihilation is *in God* and subsistence is *by God's* attributes, a theological destination the book's psychological register cannot assume; and al-Junayd requires the whole movement to be legally and communally grounded, so the self-authorizing solo version is exactly what the tradition guards against — the same refusal al-Qushayri's seated note already carries, that a shaykh is required.
