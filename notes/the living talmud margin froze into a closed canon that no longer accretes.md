# the living talmud margin froze into a closed canon that no longer accretes

The Talmud margin was once a growing thing and later stopped growing. For centuries the page accreted commentary — Rashi, then the Tosafists, then the further apparatus of the outer rings — as an argument that successive generations extended. The Tosafot layer closed around the fourteenth century, and the standardization of the page in the Vilna edition fixed the arrangement that the printing froze. What had been a living dispute became a monument: the page still displays the accumulated voices, but no new voice joins them in the canonical layout. The margin that had been a place where the tradition kept arguing became a place where the tradition's arguments are preserved.

The finding is the cost that shadows the strength recorded at [[the talmud page forces the eye through a fixed route and the route is its pedagogy]]. The same standardization that gave a dispersed community one shared coordinate system also closed the canon, and a fixed page cannot accrete. For a book that would build inhabited margins as a second, living text, the Talmud page is both the precedent and the warning: the form that best preserves an argument across centuries is the form that stops the argument from continuing.
