# the two books mirror each other on every shared concern

The reframe conversation finds a structural symmetry between the two books: each concern the books share appears in both, treated from the two loci at once. The conversation tabulates ten such pairings — sovereignty (preserve another's, cultivate one's own), awareness (facilitate noticing, practice noticing), relationship (hold the relational field, relate to self and world), participation (facilitate participation, enter participation), authorship (do not author another, author oneself), meaning (facilitate meaning-making, participate in meaning-making), attention (protect the field, protect one's own attention), technology (facilitate responsibly, use sovereignly), transformation (create conditions, practice becoming), and integration (hold complexity, integrate experience). The pairing follows from the locus distinction: because the two books differ in where practice is aimed rather than in what it concerns, every concern recurs on both sides, once as something done for another and once as something done for oneself.

The vault holds the ten-row table as a candidate rendering rather than a fixed table of contents, since the conversation produced several architectures on demand; what is durable is the mirror relation itself, which the locus split entails. The conversation reports the table as "one of the strongest structural discoveries available to the project."

## Substantiation

- The conversation presents the symmetry as a table under the heading it titles the two books as a mirror, pairing each shared concern's Book I and Book II treatment. Representative rows: "Sovereignty — Preserve another's — Cultivate one's own," "Authorship — Don't author another — Author oneself," "Attention — Protect the field — Protect one's attention."
- The conversation's own weighting: "This is one of the strongest structural discoveries available to the project."
