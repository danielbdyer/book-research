# sovereignty is participation in what the system means, not control over the system

The reframe conversation states the "true unit" of sovereignty as a distinction between control and participation. Sovereignty is not measured by how much of a system a person operates directly; it is measured by whether the person's participation in what the system means stays alive. The conversation draws the counterintuitive pair that follows: a person can delegate enormous amounts of the doing and remain sovereign, and a person can do everything themselves and be profoundly unsovereign. What decides the case is neither the quantity of delegation nor the quantity of labor but whether participation in the meaning of the activity remains the person's own.

The claim matters to the book because the book is written by an author using a system — an artificial intelligence and a knowledge graph — to help make it, and the author's worry is whether that use costs him authorship. This distinction answers the worry: delegating production is compatible with sovereignty so long as the author keeps participating in what the production means, and the danger is not delegation as such but a participation that has gone dead.
