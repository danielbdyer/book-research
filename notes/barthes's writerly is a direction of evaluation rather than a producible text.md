# barthes's writerly is a direction of evaluation rather than a producible text

Barthes sets the goal of literary work as making the reader "no longer a consumer, but a producer of the text," and opposes the writerly text, which the reader writes, to the readerly text, which positions the reader as the receiver of a fixed meaning. Yet the writerly turns out to be a value and not a thing: because it can only be written and not spoken about, there is nothing to exhibit of it and it cannot be found as an object, so *S/Z* abandons the search for a writerly text and instead reads, slowly, a classic readerly one, Balzac's *Sarrasine*. The theorist of the reader-as-producer therefore could display no writerly text and demonstrated his method on a readerly one. The writerly is asymptotic: it names a direction in which a reading can be evaluated, not an artifact a book can be built to be.

The finding is load-bearing against a design aim the book might hold. A project that wants "writerly effects at scale" — many readers made producers by one text — is chasing exactly what Barthes located as a limit-case, a value approached and never instantiated. The book can orient by the writerly, grading itself by how much of a producer it makes of its reader, but it cannot expect to manufacture the writerly as an outcome.
