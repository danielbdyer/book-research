# nihility may be a station the contraction scheme cannot represent

The book's account of what is wrong with a person is that nothing is wrong: there is contraction, a protective narrowing that persists after its cause, and the work is releasing it rather than repairing damage ([[suffering is contraction never corruption]]). The scheme is complete by design — every difficulty is a configuration of what is present, and the diagnosis names the remedy.

Keiji Nishitani describes a condition that may not fit. Nihility is not a contraction of something present but the ground giving way: the standpoint from which beings, including the self, are disclosed as groundless, and which is passed *through* rather than released. It is a station in his account rather than an obstruction, and the field of emptiness lies on its far side rather than beneath it.

The mesh grid flags this as the one name it cannot place, and treats the unplaceability as the finding: "If nihility is a station the current physics cannot represent — if the Floor's absence is a real condition distinct from contraction — then he is Tier 1 and forces a structural revision. If it turns out to be a variant of Loy's lack, he is Tier 2. The only way to know is to read him and let it push. Flagged as the highest-variance name in the whole set" (`mesh grid: tier 1`).

## Substantiation

- Recorded as an open question and `privated`: *Religion and Nothingness* has not been read in this vault. Nishitani appears here through the crossover research of 2026-08-03, which established his study under Heidegger in Freiburg and seated him within [[the kyoto school]] rather than on his own.
- The alternative the grid names is already held here. David Loy's constitutive lack is seated as independently confirming the book's first contraction (david loy), and if nihility reduces to that, the book loses nothing and gains a second voice for a position it already has.
- A distinction the Madhyamaka reading (2026-08-28) sharpens without settling. Nāgārjuna's *śūnyatā* is explicitly *not* a nihilistic void — it is dependent origination and the middle way ("all is possible when emptiness is possible," MMK 24.14; the two truths, 24.8–10), and the Mādhyamikas "avoid both extreme views, the existence of svabhāva as well as its nihilistic opposite" (Westerhoff, SEP). So if Nishitani's "nihility" is a real station, it is not simply Madhyamaka emptiness misread as a void; and reputable secondary sources suggest Nishitani himself treats nihility (relative nothingness) as a station passed *through* toward *śūnyatā* (his Essay 3, "Nihility and Śūnyatā"), never citing Nāgārjuna directly there. This sharpens the fork rather than closing it — nihility may be Nishitani's own distinct station rather than a variant of Loy's lack or a misreading of emptiness — and only *Religion and Nothingness* itself will settle it. The Madhyamaka reading record is `archive/reading — nāgārjuna, mūlamadhyamakakārikā (c. 150 ce).md`.
- What makes the other branch expensive rather than merely interesting: the book's scheme treats every difficulty as configuration, which is what lets the diagnosis contain the cure. A condition that is not a configuration of anything has no formula, and a reader in it would be handed an instrument that returns nothing — which is the same failure the corpus already records for readers whose somatic signal is muted (felt shift versus felt confirmation), arriving by a different route.
- The question also bears on the vault's own metabolic vocabulary. The `state:` enum takes privation as a stage — incomplete in a specific named way — and a condition that is not a privation of any particular thing would have no state to occupy, which is a small structural echo of the same gap.
- The reading is the only way to settle it, and the grid says so; the vault does not decide it in advance in either direction. What is recorded here is the fork and what each branch would cost, which is what an open question is for.
