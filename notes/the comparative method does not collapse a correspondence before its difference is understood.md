# the comparative method does not collapse a correspondence before its difference is understood

The reframe conversation states a discipline for comparing traditions to one another: when two of them appear to say the same thing, the comparison holds the apparent agreement open until the difference between them has been understood, and only then names whatever resonance survives. The move runs in an order — a shared concern is noticed, the difference is marked, the resonance is named, the remainder that resists synthesis is kept, and the author's own synthesis is made last — and its single governing rule is that no step collapses the correspondence into sameness before the difference has been done justice. The danger it guards against is what the conversation calls "premature correspondence": the reward the mind takes from declaring that several traditions are all pointing at one thing, when sometimes the difference between them is where the insight actually lives. The discipline protects the author's authorship, because the residue that survives a preserved difference is where the conversation locates it, and it protects the traditions, because each is left able to disagree rather than flattened until it agrees.

This claim is the content form of the vault's own both-halves rule (`ops/methodology/both halves of a correspondence.md`), which requires a note asserting a correspondence to show both sides rather than quote one and impute the rest. It bears directly on the book's convergence of thinkers, which the corpus reports as a unanimity: a comparison that collapses correspondences early is the mechanism by which an assembled agreement is mistaken for an independent one.

## Substantiation

The conversation makes the discipline explicit as a research rule: "Never collapse a correspondence until the difference has been adequately understood" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, line 3344), and states the reason beside it: "sometimes the *difference* is where the philosophical gold is" (line 3340). It gives the crossing its steps in sequence — "I've seen something like this before" (line 3156), then "But it's *not* the same" (line 3160), then "What survives the difference?" (line 3164), and then "That residue is where your authorship lives" (line 3166). The criterion the rule serves is stated as a way "to distinguish **resonance** from **projection**" (line 3194): a resonance found only by flattening vocabularies until traditions agree is projection, while a resonance that survives their remaining genuinely different is discovery. The conversation closes the point on the durability side: what the crossing yields "should be what remains **after difference has been preserved**, not what remains after difference has been erased" (line 3346).
