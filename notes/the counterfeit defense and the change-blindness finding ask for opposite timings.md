# the counterfeit defense and the change-blindness finding ask for opposite timings

The vault holds two defenses of the felt shift, and the second primary reading showed that they instruct a reader to do opposite things.

The first defense answers the fluent counterfeit. A well-written page can produce a bodily yes that responds to the prose rather than to the claim, so the vault's repair is to stop trusting the moment and check across time instead: a genuine shift changes what can be said next, and that is visible over days rather than in the instant ([[a genuine felt shift changes what can be said next]]). The instruction to a reader is to wait and check later.

The second defense now runs the other way. Nisbett and Wilson's account of when self-report goes wrong puts distance first among the causes: "Perhaps chief among the circumstances that should decrease accuracy in self-report is a separation in time between the report and the actual occurrence of the process" (1977, p. 251), and they note that the studies they review questioned subjects "immediately after its occurrence, often within seconds" (p. 251). Their two change-blindness studies are the case in point, because both asked people to compare a present state against a state remembered from one or two weeks earlier, and both found the comparison failing completely ([[a large change in evaluation can happen without being experienced as change]]). The instruction that follows from this evidence is to check now, while the thing is present.

So the book cannot simply tell a reader to verify by the body without saying when. Checking immediately exposes the reader to the fluent counterfeit, which is the failure the book's own honesty section already names. Checking later exposes the reader to reconstructing the earlier state from the present one, which is the failure the 1977 studies document and which felt, to the students in them, like certainty rather than doubt.

The candidate treatment separates what each check is for, and it comes from reading the two findings against each other rather than from either alone. The immediate comparison certifies only that something is present and moving now, which is the report the same paper exempts as a private fact known with near certainty ([[the confabulation finding exempts current sensations and targets reports of causes]]). The across-days check then asks a different question, and the difference is the whole proposal: not "did you feel different back then," which requires the memory that failed, but "what can you say now, or do now, that you could not before," which is an outward difference a person can check against their own recent conduct. Gendlin's own criterion is already stated that way — the shift certifies that the process carried forward — so the treatment asks the book to instruct the second check in carrying-forward terms rather than in remembered-feeling terms. Whether that holds is untested, and the design decision belongs to the author.
