# a recorded reason drifts toward doctrine with distance from the decision

The provenance reconstruction traces one decision's stated reason through three recordings and finds it changing shape as it moves away from the event. The decision is the retirement of the triad's first corner names — Knife, Thread, Vessel — after four chapters had been built on them.

**Minutes after, in the revision headnote**, the reason is three practical problems: the first name required constant defusing, so retiring it means "the redemption tax is refunded (nothing to defuse)"; the second collided with an existing figure, so "the namespace collision dissolves (the gold thread stands alone, uncontested)"; and together they forced an extra step of translation, so "the translation layer vanishes — body → house → word, no hardware store between" (provenance reconstruction §5, quoting [C] 7 Jul 2026).

**Five days later** the reason has become a principle: the names were retired because "tools imply a wielder standing outside the work, and the ontology has no outside."

**Three weeks later** it is doctrine: the names were "vivid, mythic, and wrong, and the author vetoed them with a sentence that is itself a piece of the book's doctrine: nothing hides like the always-there."

The last version is the one that reads best and is furthest from what happened. It adds the detail that completes the case: "nothing hides like the always-there" is the author's own line, from three nights *before* the veto and about a different subject, retroactively installed as the veto's reason (provenance reconstruction §5).

## Substantiation

- The author's own recorded words nearest the decision are about weighting rather than naming: "the prominence of the triad feels a little perched or weighted in anxiety or something — like it's a secret to be revealed. It's more like it's the thing that was always there and it's just seeing them really fucking clearly now — Ken Wilber style rather than sci-fi fiction" (provenance reconstruction §3, quoting [D] 7 Jul 2026). The verbatim veto sentence is not in the retrieved record at all.
- The finding generalizes into a reading instruction for the corpus, because the documents sit at different distances from the events they report. The treatment was written in the drafting thread itself; the atlas and the walked document were assembled three weeks later from those threads. Where the two disagree about *why* something was done, this case shows which direction the drift runs, and the earlier statement is the one closer to the decision.
- The same pattern appears once more without a veto attached: the drift in the thinker count from twenty-nine to thirty-two was reframed as "a rotation… The wobble in the number is the proof it's alive," which the reconstruction marks as "an inconsistency recast as evidence. Elegant; not evidence" (provenance reconstruction §3; provenance reconstruction: flagged overstatements).
- This does not establish that the later reasons are false. It establishes that they are later, that they were written by a different hand than the decision, and that the corpus preserves no marker distinguishing a reason given at the time from one composed afterward.
