# the participation gap relapses into completion when the reader consolidates the disruption into a deeper consistency

Iser's participation gap works only while it stays unresolved, and it relapses into completion the moment the reader re-forms the disruption into a new and deeper consistency. A blank that cannot be filled into a consistent world-picture holds the reader's attention on the reader's own preconceptions; but the reader who reads the blank as irony, or as any higher-order gestalt, has resolved it after all, and consistency-building resumes one level up. The device that guards against completion therefore carries completion as its own failure mode, and the guard is bounded on the other side too: a gap that overstrains the reader produces boredom rather than participation.

The finding bears directly on the book because the participation gap is its most portable candidate device for recruiting enactment over appreciation. If the mechanism relapses whenever the reader consolidates the disruption, then a book that installs such gaps cannot assume they keep working; the reader's drive toward a consistent reading will, given time, re-absorb the disruption into a deeper order.

## Substantiation

- Iser's 1972 essay, opened directly by the dig [P], bounds the effect between "boredom and overstrain" at page 280 and treats illusion-breaking as productive only while it is not re-consolidated; at page 289 he calls an uninterrupted building-up of illusions "a suspect, if not downright dangerous, process," and at page 294 he shows a disruption re-formed as a new gestalt. The dig recorded the pages at primary depth.
- The relapse is the reason the dig files the participation gap as portable-but-unstable rather than as a settled solution, and it is the honest boundary any book-side use of the device inherits (`transmission dig 2, 2026-08-13`, Devices).
