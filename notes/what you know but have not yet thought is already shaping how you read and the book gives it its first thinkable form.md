# what you know but have not yet thought is already shaping how you read and the book gives it its first thinkable form

The reader gains contact with a knowing that has been organizing their life from beneath thought — a knowing carried in how they relate, what moves them, what they reach for — and the book offers it, perhaps for the first time, a form it can be thought in. What the reader gains is not new information but the thinkability of something long known and never held: the wordless competence at the base of their character rising into a shape they can finally turn over. It arrives as recognition precisely because it was always known; the book only lends it its first sayable form. Its use to the writing is to reach the book's deepest register — recognition arriving for something never once consciously held.

## Substantiation

- Bollas (The Shadow of the Object, 1987), pole still owed: "the unthought known" — "there is in each of us a fundamental split between what we think we know and what we know but may never be able to think," a knowing lodged in our idiom and earliest, preverbal experience, real and operative long before it is available to thought (from memory, grounding owed). Gendlin, read at the primary text, supplies the bridge: the implicit is "our more intricate experiencing," and giving it a thinkable form is carrying-forward — "no concept … ever equals experiencing, -- but may carry it forward" (ECM Preface, 1997) — so the split Bollas names is the very gap across which a symbol carries the felt sense.
- Refusal kept: Bollas holds that some of the unthought known "may never" be thought — the point is not total explication but respect for an idiom that exceeds what can be thought of it.
- Standing: privated — the Gendlin bridge read at the primary text (ECM Preface, 1997; the record is in `archive/`); the Bollas pole still owed. Keep a clean line between the *ongoing* unfolding of the unthought (this lane) and the *momentary* shift of recognition, so the two are companions and not the same claim wearing two coats.
