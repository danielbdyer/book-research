# learning to learn is a distinct level and not more of the same learning

The book's kind of change alters the frame that organizes experience rather than adding content inside it, and Gregory Bateson gives that distinction a formal spine. He stratifies learning into levels that are distinct logical types in Russell's sense: Learning I corrects a response within a fixed set of alternatives, and Learning II is a change in the process of Learning I — a corrective change in the set of alternatives itself, or in how experience is punctuated. The step between the levels is categorical rather than quantitative, because a change in the process of learning is a higher logical type than the learning it changes. Bateson locates the self at the second level — "Selfhood is a product or aggregate of Learning II" — so the frame a person learns in order to stop re-deciding is where character forms. Learning III, the change in the process of Learning II, is the altitude the book's arc arrives at, and Bateson marks it as rare and sometimes dangerous.

One consequence bounds how the book may borrow the ladder. Because the levels are logical types rather than grades of one skill, they do not compose a trainable career ladder, and a scheme that sorted practitioners into learning-levels would read a logical distinction as a ranking of persons.

## Substantiation

- The definitions are quoted from Bateson's standalone 1971 essay, opened at primary depth by transmission dig 4; the vault's own reading of Bateson is not yet made, so the claim enters nascent.
- Bateson gives the lower levels an operational measure and the highest none: Learning II carries an external signature, in that a subject needs fewer trials on each successive problem of the same type, while Learning III has no such measure — which is why the book's deepest altitude has no third-person instrument and rests on the felt shift alone.
- Held as company rather than corroboration under `ops/methodology/authorial stance.md`: Bateson formalizes a distinction the book already draws, and formalization is not evidence that the distinction is true.
