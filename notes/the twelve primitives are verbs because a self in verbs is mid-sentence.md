# the twelve primitives are verbs because a self in verbs is mid-sentence

The book's second layer is an alphabet of twelve basic operations of inner life, and every one is a verb: Create, Dissolve, Bind, Sever, Maintain, Release (three make/unmake pairs corresponding to the three container structures), plus Transform, Attend, Express, Contain, Commit, and Defer. The grammatical choice carries the claim, which the first-telling states directly: "a self described in nouns can be finished, filed, concluded. A self described in verbs is always mid-sentence" (first-telling: the offering). The corpus attributes the stake to Bakhtin's concept of unfinalizability and treats the parts of speech as protection against description hardening into verdict.

The set is claimed to be closed, and the corpus reports the test was actually run: every candidate thirteenth verb proposed reduced to a combination of the twelve. Closure is what qualifies the set as an alphabet — a small, complete basis rather than an open-ended list.

## Substantiation

- Walked III lists all twelve with their pairings and glosses, and records the falsifiability drill.
- First-telling, "The offering," carries the nouns-versus-verbs argument in reader-facing form and deliberately withholds the full set.
- Atlas §III records one reflexive fact the corpus flags for the author: the book itself is built almost entirely from one primitive, Express. That fact has its own tension note, [[the whole volume is wagered on express]].
- Space-around queues Wierzbicka's semantic primes as the empirical sparring partner for any claim that the alphabet is found rather than invented, and Whitehead as the completed metaphysics behind the verbs-only commitment.
