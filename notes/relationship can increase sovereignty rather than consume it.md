# relationship can increase sovereignty rather than consume it

The reframe conversation makes a claim about the direction of exchange between relationship and agency: participation that goes well does not subtract from a person's sovereignty but can add to it. The conversation states this against the common assumption that influence and dependence necessarily cost a person some of their self-determination, so that every gain in connection is read as a loss in independence. Its positive form is that relationship can increase a person's capacity to belong to themselves — the more fully someone participates with another, the more authorship of their own life they may come to hold rather than less. The claim inverts the zero-sum picture in which connection and sovereignty trade against each other.

For the book this is the premise beneath the reader relation. A book that influences its reader can, on this claim, leave the reader more sovereign than before rather than diminished, which is the outcome the author's facilitation craft already aims at when it treats the field as something that returns a person to their own authorship.
