# ripeness is not refrigeration

The corpus distinguishes two fates for finished material. An archive preserves: work is stored, intact, revisable, and inert. Ripening ends differently: mature work is released to where it can no longer be revised. The rule this note takes as its title is recorded in the walked document as a working note directed at the praxis chapters and at the author's own desk, where fifteen years of poems and seventeen chapters sit preserved.

The rule binds this vault directly, and [[methods]] adopts it as the output rule: a research system stores by default, so its success measure has to be what leaves it for the manuscript, not what accumulates in it.

## Substantiation

- Walked V records the rule and its target: "the archive preserves; the orchard ripens," applied to the author's accumulated finished work.
- Atlas §V.3 attaches it to the metabolic arc (nascent, privated, full, flourishing, composting), whose endpoint treats endings as inputs to the next season rather than as loss.
- The question of which finished work gets released is part of the tension recorded at [[seventeen chapters rest at madhyama]].
