# the book asks only for attention until it has given the reader something to act with

The book has a working rule of practice pedagogy that it never states: an instruction may ask for attention anywhere in the arc, and may ask for an action only after the reader has been given something to do it with. Everywhere up to and including the Stance, the closing instruction directs attention to a condition already present in the reader and declines to ask for more — "Just notice. You do not have to fix anything yet" at the Generative Triad, "Notice without changing anything. The noticing is the practice" at the Alphabet. Only at the two praxis units, the Kata and the Engineer's Eye, does an instruction assign a task with a horizon: "Choose one. Try it today. Tomorrow, try another," and "Tomorrow, try the Stack Scan once." Those are the only two forward-assigning instructions in the book, and they arrive exactly where the arc stops presenting the architecture and begins issuing practice — the point the treatment marks as where "invitations become instructions" (arc-gloss: part v).

The rule matters because a prompt that assigned a task before the practice material would borrow authority the arc has not yet earned. A reader told to "try" something before being handed a form to try has been asked to act on nothing. The rule is checkable on any new unit: read its closing instruction and ask whether the reader could comply without leaving the page. If the instruction asks for action, the practice it acts on must already be on the page.

## Substantiation

- The two forward-assigning instructions are `abridged ch14` (the Kata, "Choose one. Try it today. Tomorrow, try another") and the Engineer's Eye ("Tomorrow, try the Stack Scan once"). Every instruction before them asks only for present attention, and the full set by unit runs from "Pause. Let this settle. There is no hurry" through "Right now: thirty seconds. No agenda" at the Stance.
- The rule bears directly on a tension the atlas records: the book's forward momentum works against the stopping its practices require, treated with thresholds that demand action before the text continues (atlas §XI.1b). The executed draft's closing instructions are all skippable, which leaves that treatment untried — so the pedagogy rule and the untreated tension are two views of the same fact, that the book withholds the demand for action until late and then makes it gently.
- The rule is a claim about how the book teaches practice rather than about its sentence craft. It is checkable against the corpus and transferable to any unit the author writes next, which is what distinguishes it from an observation about the prose that carried it.
