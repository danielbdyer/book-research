# grief cannot complete alone

The book's doctrine, carried in all four source documents and attributed to Karla McLaren: grief is the one emotion that cannot complete in solitude — it structurally requires witness and ceremony. The first-telling specifies what the requirement means: "not advice, not fixing, but another nervous system present while the thing moves through." The first-telling grounds the claim anthropologically, reporting that funeral traditions arrived at the requirement long before psychology did, and that the gathering, the ceremony, and the food serve it (first-telling: the between).

This claim serves a distinct function in each document: it is the catalyst inside the grief formula (chemistry), the clearest case for the relational field's reality (doctrine), and the strongest instance of the completion mechanism the book claims solitary insight cannot supply. The first-telling adds a distinction the vault preserves: "a witness is not a commentator. Being seen is a different event from being explained. Only one of them completes anything" (first-telling: the between).

At McLaren's own text the attribution splits. The ceremony is hers; the witness is the book's. "Your grief process can be undertaken individually, or in a community or family group," she writes, and the solitary route is sufficient with the body as its agent: "Your body is a brilliant mourner, and if you trust it, it will convey you into the river of tears and bring you back out safe again." What she requires is ritual — a container, not a person: a shrine "so that you can create a container for your mourning," something in it that can be released so the ritual can end, and "a secluded physical space where you can work through your grief in your own time"; community is an explicit option inside the ritual, "Inviting community into your grief ritual." And the communal grief ritual the corpus generalizes from her she credits by name to Sobonfu Somé and the Dagara — "This grief ritual lasted for two full days, but in Sobonfu's village, they last for three." So the claim stands as the book's doctrine, grounded on the first-telling's anthropology and on the book's own chemistry, and its attribution is corrected: in the vault's six-relation scheme the witness clause is a mis-addressed credit (the book's own hardening, credited to McLaren) and the ritual is a self-flagged debt (McLaren's, credited by her to Somé) ([[the apparatus descends from the settlement the book positions itself against]]).

## Substantiation

- Atlas §IX.2 records the finding and its structural role: it underwrites the relational-field section.
- Walked VIII restates it and reports the book's staging of the requirement in a canning scene: some preservations cannot be done alone.
- First-telling, "The between": the funeral-tradition grounding and the witness/commentator distinction.
- McLaren's own sentences, read 2026-09-02 at her site: "Your grief process can be undertaken individually, or in a community or family group" ("Healing rituals for grief"); "Your body is a brilliant mourner" ("Grief: The deep river of the soul"); the shrine, the disposable object, the secluded space, and the invitation to community as an option ("Healing rituals for grief"). Her genuine relational claim is about a grief-impaired culture rather than a structural requirement: "When we see someone actively dropping into grief and mourning, we often turn away… And that's the real death – of compassion, of community, of feeling, and of understanding."
- The Somé credit, in her words: "With my husband Tino, I had the great good fortune to participate in a number of indigenous West African grief rituals with the late, great Sobonfu Somé, who was a ritual keeper from the ancient Dagara tribe." The first-telling's anthropological grounding is therefore specific rather than general, and the specific ground is a named teacher and tradition the corpus does not print.
- The space-around survey proposes Tronick's still-face experiment as the laboratory evidence "a skeptic cannot wave away" (space-around: around the between), and reports its companion finding: flourishing pairs are distinguished by their rate of repair rather than by attunement. Tronick has not been researched for this project; he is on the pending-evaluation list in `ops/queue/candidates.md`.
- What the correction leaves open: with McLaren's witness withdrawn, the claim that grief structurally requires a second person has no outside witness of its own beyond Somé's tradition at one remove. That is a reading question for the relational field, and it bears on every note that leans on this one as the book's strongest relational case.
