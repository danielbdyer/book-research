# grief cannot complete alone

Karla McLaren's finding: grief is the one emotion that cannot complete in solitude — it structurally requires witness and ceremony. The first-telling specifies what the requirement means: "not advice, not fixing, but another nervous system present while the thing moves through." The first-telling grounds the claim anthropologically, reporting that funeral traditions arrived at the requirement long before psychology did, and that the gathering, the ceremony, and the food serve it (first-telling: the between).

This claim appears in all four source documents and serves a distinct function in each: it is the catalyst inside the grief formula (chemistry), the clearest case for the relational field's reality (doctrine), and the strongest instance of the completion mechanism the book claims solitary insight cannot supply. The first-telling adds a distinction the vault preserves: "a witness is not a commentator. Being seen is a different event from being explained. Only one of them completes anything" (first-telling: the between).

## Substantiation

- Atlas §IX.2 records the finding and its structural role: it underwrites the relational-field section.
- Walked VIII restates it and reports the book's staging of the requirement in a canning scene: some preservations cannot be done alone.
- First-telling, "The between": the funeral-tradition grounding and the witness/commentator distinction.
- The space-around survey proposes Tronick's still-face experiment as the laboratory evidence "a skeptic cannot wave away" (space-around: around the between), and reports its companion finding: flourishing pairs are distinguished by their rate of repair rather than by attunement. Tronick has not been researched for this project; he is on the pending-evaluation list in `ops/queue/candidates.md`.
