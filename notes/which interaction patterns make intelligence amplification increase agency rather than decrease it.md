# which interaction patterns make intelligence amplification increase agency rather than decrease it

The conversation names, as potentially a major contribution of the project, the question of which interaction patterns cause intelligence amplification to increase a person's agency rather than decrease it. Its premise is that the difference between an amplifier of agency and a replacement of it is not in the technology but in the relationship and the architecture of the exchange: the same tool can ask better questions, expose blind spots, and hold complexity, or it can do the person's thinking for them. The question asks for the patterns, the repeatable shapes of interaction, that reliably fall on the first side. It stays open because it is empirical and unsettled — the conversation offers the frame, the tool as interlocutor rather than oracle, and marks the patterns themselves as the contribution still to be worked out.
