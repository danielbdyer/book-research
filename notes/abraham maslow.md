# abraham maslow

*Peak experience* is Maslow's term. He introduced it to name the moments of unitive, self-validating, time-suspending experience he found reported by people he considered healthy rather than ill, and he built an argument on it: that such experiences are ordinary equipment of a functioning person rather than the property of mystics, and that religions are the institutionalized residue of one person's peak reported to people who had not had one.

Then he corrected himself. In his last years — the 1970 preface to *Religions, Values, and Peak-Experiences*, and the essays gathered posthumously in *The Farther Reaches of Human Nature* — he named a second thing, the **plateau experience**: serene rather than climactic, sustained rather than visiting, noetic and voluntary, achieved by long effort rather than granted. And he said plainly that the plateau is the more valuable of the two, that peak-seeking had become a distraction his own vocabulary had licensed, and that the culture forming around the term was chasing the wrong object.

**That is this book's argument, made by the man who supplied its vocabulary.** The executed draft uses *peak experience* as a technical term nine times and states its thesis in the same register: "The peak experience is not the work. The peak experience is the opening. The work is what you do with the opening" (abridged ch12). To say it, the corpus reaches for the Sufi distinction between *aḥwāl* and *maqāmāt* — states that visit against stations that are built — which it credits. Maslow's own version of the same correction is uncited, and no source in `sources/` mentions him at all.

**What he would refuse.** The hierarchy of needs is not in this book and he would ask where it went: his account makes peak and plateau available at the top of a prepotency ladder, after safety and belonging, which is a structural claim about who gets to have these experiences that the book's householder axiom does not engage. He would also refuse the reading of the plateau as an achievement of technique — his description is of something that arrives after long living and cannot be arranged, which is the same refusal the book's own anti-instrumentality doctrine makes and would therefore be an ally's objection rather than an opponent's.

**The liability that travels with him.** Maslow is the founding figure of the human-potential movement, which is the field the corpus diagnoses as having a structural inability to hold suffering as external ("the American transformation canon's radical interiority of cause," walked IX). Citing him makes the book's descent from that field visible. The vault's reading is that this is a gain rather than a cost, and it is stated as the vault's rather than as a finding: a book that names its parents and says where they went wrong is in a stronger position than one that appears to have no parents ([[the apparatus descends from the settlement the book positions itself against]]).

**The evaluation is from secondary knowledge.** What the reading has to settle: whether the plateau in Maslow's own words is the same thing the book calls integration, or a different object that merely occupies the same slot; whether his late writing supplies any mechanism, which the book badly wants and the *aḥwāl*/*maqāmāt* framing does not give; and whether the prepotency hierarchy is separable from the peak/plateau material or comes attached.
