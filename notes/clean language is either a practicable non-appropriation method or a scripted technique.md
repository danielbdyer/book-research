# clean language is either a practicable non-appropriation method or a scripted technique

The conversation proposes David Grove's Clean Language as a near-direct technical ancestor of the book's facilitation ethic, and the same proposal opens a tension. Clean Language minimizes the facilitator's contamination of a participant's experiential world, and its governing principle — constrain the form of the inquiry while leaving its content radically unconstrained — is the book's own insight that constraint produces fluency while preserving surprise. That is the method's promise: a practicable, teachable way to facilitate without appropriating, which is the book's first axiom made operable. The tension is that Clean Language is a script, a small set of stock question-forms, and the book subordinates technique to ontology, admitting techniques only last and warning that technique without an ontology beneath it becomes manipulation. Taken as a set of moves detached from the stance that grounds them, the same method becomes the "hundred-and-one techniques" mechanization the book refuses. Whether Clean Language enters as craft or as the thing the book warns against depends entirely on whether its ontology travels with it.

## Substantiation

- reframe conversation, 2026-08-13 (transcript lines 1689–1712): Grove's approaches are "explicitly concerned with minimizing the facilitator's contamination of the participant's experiential world," and the design principle is stated, "The facilitator can constrain the form of inquiry while remaining radically unconstrained about its content," which the model reads as "almost exactly your recent insight about constraint creating fluency while preserving surprise."
- reframe conversation, 2026-08-13 (transcript lines 15575–15577): the book's own ordering, "Only now should techniques enter. Because technique without ontology becomes manipulation," which places any method like Clean Language beneath the stance rather than beside it.
- The book's arrival at the same forbiddance is held at [[technique without ontology becomes manipulation]]; the ethic the method would serve is [[the other is not your project]], and the standard it must meet is [[facilitation is a technology of relationship whose success is increased capacity for participation, not efficient production of an output]].
- The treatment under trial is to admit Clean Language as a candidate craft held under the ontology — taken for its constraint-form, the structure that fixes the form and frees the content, and refused as a standalone script a facilitator could run without the stance. The line between a form that protects the participant and a script that mechanizes the facilitator is exactly what the book has not yet drawn, so the status is live.
