# sufficiency is the capacity to remain present without needing the next input to complete oneself

The reframe conversation defines sufficiency as a capacity rather than a possession or a renunciation. Against a condition that keeps a person reaching for the next input to feel complete, sufficiency is the capacity to remain present without that next input — to stay with what is here rather than requiring an addition to be whole. The conversation is careful that the counter-movement is not renunciation, not a refusal of desire or a stripping-away; it is the ability to encounter "this is enough for now" without collapsing into passivity. Sufficiency so defined is not having enough things but needing no further thing in order to be oneself, which is why it can be present in the midst of activity and absent in the midst of plenty.

The claim matters to the book because it completes, at the scale of a person's relation to input, the single move the book runs everywhere else. Recognition un-forgets, the theory of suffering un-folds a fullness already present, and the reclaimed word un-conceals; sufficiency un-needs, treating wholeness as already given rather than as one input away. Read this way sufficiency is a fourth instance of the book's uncovering-rather-than-addition grammar — the doctrine that the book adds nothing to a person and only removes what obscures what is already there.
