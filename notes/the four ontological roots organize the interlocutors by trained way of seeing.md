# the four ontological roots organize the interlocutors by trained way of seeing

The reframe conversation lays out four "ontological roots," each a distinct way of entering reality rather than a doctrine about what reality contains. Root A, Vervaeke, holds "Reality as an arena of relevance realization and participatory knowing." Root B, Pratyabhijñā and Spanda, holds "Reality as dynamic self-aware consciousness whose nature is recognized rather than acquired." Root C, phenomenology, holds "Experience as the primary site of inquiry." Root D, dialogical or participatory inquiry, holds "Knowledge emerges through relationship rather than unilateral observation" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, lines 2376-2419). Each root is a trained way of seeing — a cultivated way of noticing, in the same sense the conversation gives a tradition ([[a tradition is a cultivated locus of recognition — a trained way of noticing — not merely doctrine]]) — and the roster of thinkers sorts by which way of seeing each was trained in.

The claim is that these four roots are held as a lens over the vault's existing `discipline:` axis, not as a fifth taxonomy competing to govern the roster. The reconciliation register settles this at Fork 23: of the four schemes that could organize the cast — the vault's `seat`/`load`/`discipline` axes, the four ontological roots, Wilber's four quadrants, and the plotted-lineage stack — none governs. Only the operational axes ask the load question, the question of what the book cannot afford to lose, so only they re-seat anyone; the roots, the quadrants, and the stack are registered lenses that add a way of looking without moving a chair. Holding the roots as a lens is what keeps them from becoming a proprietary taxonomy the book would then have to defend, and it is the same move the vault makes for Wilber's quadrants, kept as a coordinate system rather than an architecture ([[wilber's four quadrants are a coordinate system, not an architecture]]).

Because the roots are a lens over the discipline axis, they map onto its existing values rather than replacing them. Root A (cognitive science) maps onto `empirical-science`, the value the vault already carries for the enactivist cognitive scientists de Jaegher and Di Paolo and now for Vervaeke ([[john vervaeke]]). Root B (recognition, the operators) maps onto `indic-and-buddhist-primary`, where Kṣemarāja and Utpaladeva are seated. Root C (phenomenology) maps onto `continental-philosophy`. Root D is the one root the existing axis does not cleanly hold: its members run from Buber, filed under continental philosophy, to de Jaegher and Di Paolo under empirical science, so the register notes it needs a dialogical or practitioner-pedagogy column the schema does not yet supply. The mapping is a reading aid, not a schema change; the discipline axis stays as it is.

Root C is a named-but-empty slot the vault must fill from its own roster. The conversation names phenomenology as a root but adds no phenomenologist of its own, unlike Root A, which it populates richly with Vervaeke. The vault fills the slot: Heidegger is already seated, Merleau-Ponty supplies a phenomenology of the lived body ([[maurice merleau-ponty]]), and Michel Henry is queued as a possible phenomenological arrival at the reflexive dimension of awareness. The emptiness is therefore the conversation's contribution to the root, not the vault's; naming the slot is what turns it into a reading question.

## Substantiation

- The four roots and their one-line characterizations are laid out at reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, lines 2376-2419; the facilitation question the conversation attaches to them — "What practices and conditions allow these different modes of inquiry to be entered without forcing the participant into the facilitator's ontology?" (line 2423) — is why the roots function as ways-in rather than as a hierarchy of what exists.
- The lens-not-taxonomy status, and the mapping onto `discipline:` (A to cognitive science, B to source tradition, C to phenomenology, D to a new dialogical or practitioner-pedagogy column), are the reconciliation register's Fork 23 recommendation; the register's destination for the decision is an `ops/decisions.md` row plus a research-frontier note, which is why this note joins the research-frontier map.
- The `discipline:` values the roots map onto are the ones in use: `empirical-science` for de Jaegher and Di Paolo, `indic-and-buddhist-primary` for Kṣemarāja and Utpaladeva, `continental-philosophy` for Heidegger, Buber, Henry, and Merleau-Ponty.
- Standing: reached in the conversation's shared portion and held as candidate material under the rule that every name is a candidate until the author seats it; Fork 23 is unadjudicated, so the note is nascent and awaits the author's decision on whether the lens is registered as recommended.
