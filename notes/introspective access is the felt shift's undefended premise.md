# introspective access is the felt shift's undefended premise

The book's test for whether anything is true is that the body countersigns it. That test presupposes something it does not argue for: that a person can tell, from the inside, what is happening in them. The corpus already records two limits on the standard — fluent prose can manufacture the confirmation, and the signal is faintest in the readers carrying the most protective structure ([[felt shift versus felt confirmation]]) — but both are limits on the *reliability of the reading under conditions*. Neither attacks the premise that there is a reading to be had.

There is a literature that does, and no part of it is in this vault. The mesh grid names three entry points and ranks the hole first among nine: "The claim doing the most work has the least defense… Until this cell is populated, 'the body countersigns' is an assertion wearing the costume of a standard" (`mesh grid: against C4`).

The three are of different kinds and the difference matters. Nisbett and Wilson's 1977 paper argues that people confabulate reports of their own mental processes and lack privileged access to the causes of their own behaviour — an attack on knowing *why*. Eric Schwitzgebel's work argues that introspection is unreliable even about current experience, not merely about its causes — an attack on knowing *what*, which is the one aimed at where the book actually stands, since the felt shift is a report about a present bodily state rather than about a cause. Steven Katz's constructivism attacks from a third side: the felt shift is trained by the tradition that produces it, so its countersignature confirms the training rather than the truth. Katz already has an interlocutor note in this vault, evaluated 2026-08-03, and he is seated nowhere in particular; the grid's correction is that he belongs against this claim specifically rather than floating as a general skeptic.

**Held within the ecology.** The book is one locus in an ecology of participation and stands auxiliary to a sovereign reader, not the sovereign source of the reader's recognition ([[the book is one locus in an ecology of participation, not the sovereign source of the reader's recognition]]), and it tends the conditions for the reader's own facilitation rather than producing or certifying a transformation ([[the book tends conditions for the reader's own facilitation of recognition rather than facilitating transformation]]; [[the book must respect that it cannot verify the reader has done the work]]). On that ground the introspection objection bears on the reliability of one instrument the reader-facilitator uses in their own self-facilitation ([[self-facilitation is creating conditions for one's own becoming, not self-management, optimization, or analysis]]) rather than on a warrant the book holds to verify the reader's outcome, because the book holds no such warrant. The candidate treatment recorded above — narrowing what the shift certifies to shrink the introspective demand — therefore reads as a guardrail on that instrument's dependability rather than as the book's defense of a standard its authority rests on; the objection, the treatment, and the unread readings all stay exactly as they are, their standing changed and their content untouched, on the move at [[the ecology frame transcends and includes the verification frame rather than replacing it]].

## Substantiation

- This note is stated on the grid's authority and is `privated` by the vault's own convention: none of the three primary works has been read here, and the descriptions above are the grid's characterizations rather than the vault's verification. Filling it is the first item in the reading queue and in the torch.
- The load the claim carries is the author's own, stated 2026-08-04: the felt shift is the book's single verification criterion and "everything else runs downstream of it." He judges it currently to have "little defense," which makes this hole his assessment as well as the grid's.
- The tension stays `live`, and it gained a candidate treatment on 2026-08-05 from the least expected direction: the standard's own founding paper. Gendlin's 1964 definition makes the shift certify that the process moved, not that a content is true, and his negative criterion — "From this lack of referent movement, one knows that nothing has really changed" — asks a person only to notice change against no-change, not to describe their inner states accurately. A discrimination is a weaker introspective demand than a description, so the standard as its author stated it has a smaller attack surface than the standard as the book uses it ([[the felt shift certifies carrying forward rather than correspondence]]). This narrows the target; whether the narrowed target survives Schwitzgebel is exactly what his reading now has to settle, and it is unread.
- One reason this tension is harder than a literature pass, carried from `ops/methodology/the book's current shape.md`: the book's second reader type is fluent in integration vocabulary and uses that fluency to avoid feeling, which makes the author's own articulacy a hazard. If the standard's failure mode and the book's defining reader are the same thing, then a defense of the standard has to survive being written in the book's own prose.
- What would break if the objection holds is worth stating so the stakes are not vague: not the book's claims about what is true, but its account of how a reader would know. A reader who cannot trust the countersignature is left with the argument, and the author has explicitly said the book does not verify there.

**The blind repeat pass armed both sides of this tension with published material (2026-08-06, round three).** On the standard's side, the naked-assertion framing is false in the literature: the felt-shift construct was operationalized as the Experiencing Scale in 1969 (Klein, Mathieu, Gendlin, and Kiesler, Wisconsin Psychiatric Institute), and a meta-analysis of ten studies with 406 clients finds client experiencing predicts symptom improvement at r = −.19 (Pascual-Leone and Yeryomenko, *Psychotherapy Research*, 2017) — modest and correlational, and still a published empirical check where the naked-assertion framing said none existed. The Nisbett-and-Wilson strike also has a published answer from inside the introspective-training lineage: Petitmengin and colleagues replicated the unawareness finding and then showed that subjects guided by elicitation interview detected the manipulation in 80 percent of cases (*Consciousness and Cognition* 22, 2013). On the attack's side, the pass confirmed that the sharper enemy is Schwitzgebel, because Nisbett and Wilson's verified core concerns reports about the causes of behavior, not reports of current experience — with one exception kept at full force: when a felt shift certifies that a practice caused a change, the report is causal attribution, and there the 1977 paper bites squarely. The three readings stay queued; what changed is that the standard's defense and the attack on it each now hold published material. Verified at web depth; the trace is `archive/research — counterpoint round background.md`, round three.

**The first of the three attacks was read at its own text on 2026-08-07, and it split in two.** Nisbett and Wilson 1977 was read complete, and the half the vault expected holds: the authors name current sensations among the private facts a person knows "with near certainty," and they aim their finding at reports of "higher order, inference-based responses" — so a report that something is present in the body now sits on the safe side of their own line, and the exemption is stated in their voice rather than inferred ([[the confabulation finding exempts current sensations and targets reports of causes]]). The half the vault did not expect fails. Pages 235 to 237 carry a section titled "Awareness of the Existence of a Change Process," reporting two studies in which people whose evaluations had moved sharply reported no experience of having moved at all, and noticing change is precisely what the candidate treatment above narrows the standard down to ([[a large change in evaluation can happen without being experienced as change]]). The narrowing therefore does not clear the objection; it relocates it. Two further findings came with the reading: the paper's account of when self-report degrades puts distance in time first, which collides with the vault's across-days defense against the fluent counterfeit ([[the counterfeit defense and the change-blindness finding ask for opposite timings]]), and the paper reaches Katz's own premise from laboratory evidence a year before his essay ([[the trained-vocabulary objection arrives independently from social psychology]]). The paper says nothing about whether introspective accuracy can be trained, so Petitmengin's result contradicts none of it. The full record is `archive/reading — nisbett and wilson, telling more than we can know (1977).md`.

**The second of the three attacks was read at its own text the same day, and it answered in the hard direction.** Schwitzgebel's 2008 paper was read complete, and the queued question — whether his unreliability claim reaches bodily and interoceptive report or is drawn mostly from vision and imagery — comes back against the standard. His first extended case is emotion, and the features he says elude us are the bodily ones: viscerality, bodily arousal, and location in the interior of the head and body (2008, p. 249). He names the present-tense exemption that Nisbett and Wilson's carve-out permits and rejects it — "We may concede the past to the skeptic, but not the present" — calling the claim "blatantly unrealistic" (pp. 250–251). The only reports he treats as secure are a vivid canonical pain and a bright foveal colour, and a felt shift is neither ([[the case against introspection is strongest where the book's standard stands]]). So the two canonical attacks do not overlap and together they cover both halves of what the book asks a reader to do: one attacks the report of a cause, the other attacks the report of a present bodily state.

**Two things came out of the same paper that improve the tension's position rather than worsen it.** Schwitzgebel names the repair himself — "Perhaps the right kind of learning, practice, or care could largely shield us from error — an interesting possibility that merits exploration! — but I see as yet no robust scientific support for such hopes" (p. 259) — which turns the defense from an argument the book must win into evidence the book must find, with a 2008 date on the verdict ([[trained introspection is the repair the standard's sharpest opponent named and left open]]). And his diagnosis of why introspective confidence persists is the same one Nisbett and Wilson give, that nothing ever corrects a person's reading of themselves, which is an argument for the Second Chair arriving from the standard's two sharpest opponents ([[both attacks on the standard blame the absence of correction by another person]]). The full record is `archive/reading — schwitzgebel, the unreliability of naive introspection (2008).md`.

A blind excavation of the interaction sciences adds a mechanism distinct from the unreliability attacks above: Polanyi's from–to structure states the connoisseur problem as attentional physics, so a reader who turns focal attention onto her own felt shift may abolish it — the watching effaces the joint meaning it attends to. The warning is bounded, since it is a performance-moment rule and deliberate self-conscious attention is how skill is built in the learning phase (transmission dig 4, 2026-08-13).
