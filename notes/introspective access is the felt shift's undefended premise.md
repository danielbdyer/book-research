# introspective access is the felt shift's undefended premise

The book's test for whether anything is true is that the body countersigns it. That test presupposes something it does not argue for: that a person can tell, from the inside, what is happening in them. The corpus already records two limits on the standard — fluent prose can manufacture the confirmation, and the signal is faintest in the readers carrying the most protective structure ([[felt shift versus felt confirmation]]) — but both are limits on the *reliability of the reading under conditions*. Neither attacks the premise that there is a reading to be had.

There is a literature that does, and no part of it is in this vault. The mesh grid names three entry points and ranks the hole first among nine: "The claim doing the most work has the least defense… Until this cell is populated, 'the body countersigns' is an assertion wearing the costume of a standard" (`mesh grid: against C4`).

The three are of different kinds and the difference matters. Nisbett and Wilson's 1977 paper argues that people confabulate reports of their own mental processes and lack privileged access to the causes of their own behaviour — an attack on knowing *why*. Eric Schwitzgebel's work argues that introspection is unreliable even about current experience, not merely about its causes — an attack on knowing *what*, which is the one aimed at where the book actually stands, since the felt shift is a report about a present bodily state rather than about a cause. Steven Katz's constructivism attacks from a third side: the felt shift is trained by the tradition that produces it, so its countersignature confirms the training rather than the truth. Katz already has an interlocutor note in this vault, evaluated 2026-08-03, and he is seated nowhere in particular; the grid's correction is that he belongs against this claim specifically rather than floating as a general skeptic.

## Substantiation

- This note is stated on the grid's authority and is `privated` by the vault's own convention: none of the three primary works has been read here, and the descriptions above are the grid's characterizations rather than the vault's verification. Filling it is the first item in `ops/lineage sort.md` and the first item in the torch.
- The load the claim carries is the author's own, stated 2026-08-04: the felt shift is the book's single verification criterion and "everything else runs downstream of it." He judges it currently to have "little defense," which makes this hole his assessment as well as the grid's.
- The tension is filed `live` with no treatment, and that is accurate rather than a placeholder: the vault has nothing to try yet, because the objection has not been read.
- One reason this tension is harder than a literature pass, carried from `ops/methodology/the book's current shape.md`: the book's second reader type is fluent in integration vocabulary and uses that fluency to avoid feeling, which makes the author's own articulacy a hazard. If the standard's failure mode and the book's defining reader are the same thing, then a defense of the standard has to survive being written in the book's own prose.
- What would break if the objection holds is worth stating so the stakes are not vague: not the book's claims about what is true, but its account of how a reader would know. A reader who cannot trust the countersignature is left with the argument, and the author has explicitly said the book does not verify there.
