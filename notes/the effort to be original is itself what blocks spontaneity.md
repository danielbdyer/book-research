# the effort to be original is itself what blocks spontaneity

Johnstone instructed his students to be obvious, or to be average, against their instinct to be original. His finding is that originality is a trap and the effort to reach it is itself the block: a player who reaches for the clever, invented response is producing fear wearing wit, while the first average response that arrives unbidden is both freer and, in practice, the more interesting one. Removing the pressure to be interesting therefore removes the pressure that produces the blockage, so spontaneity is recovered not by trying harder to be spontaneous but by dropping the demand to perform.

The finding bears on the book at the register of its own prose. The book's beautiful sentences risk two coupled effects the vault already tracks — recruiting a connoisseur's posture in the reader and manufacturing a felt assent to fluency rather than to a claim — and Johnstone names the maker's side of the same pressure: the drive to be interesting that blocks the plain, true move. His instruction is recorded on his own chair as the clause that justifies his seat, and whether it is a technique the book could use or a position it would have to argue for is the open question that chair leaves.
