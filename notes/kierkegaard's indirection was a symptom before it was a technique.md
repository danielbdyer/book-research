# kierkegaard's indirection was a symptom before it was a technique

Indirect communication is a symptom before it is a technique. The method is soaked in personal suffering — melancholy, the severed engagement to Regine Olsen, the "thorn in the flesh" — and Kierkegaard's inability to be direct was constitutional rather than chosen, so the method partly rationalizes a wound. The consequence for anyone borrowing the devices is a caution: a practitioner who adopts indirection as neat craft inherits a device whose origin was pathology and risks aestheticizing an incapacity, dressing an inability to speak plainly as a sophisticated refusal to do so. The point does not discredit the devices, which work whatever their origin, but it bars treating them as pure technique detached from the condition that produced them. The finding rests on secondary and biographical scholarship, the primaries unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
