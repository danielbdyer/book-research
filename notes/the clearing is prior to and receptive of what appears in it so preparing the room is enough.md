# the clearing is prior to and receptive of what appears in it so preparing the room is enough

The reader is relieved of the largest burden — manufacturing the insight. The clearing does not make the light; it lets the light in. Heidegger's finding is that appearing presupposes an open region already there for it, receptive and prior; your work is to prepare and to wait in an active, attentive way, not to force the thing to show. What you gain is real relief and a truer posture — you cannot produce recognition, you were never supposed to, you make the room and the concealed un-conceals. Its use to the writing is to convert the book's foundational aletheia from a doctrine the reader is told into an instruction for what the prose must *be*: make a clearing, do not make the appearing.

## Substantiation

- Heidegger: light can stream into the clearing and play there, but it never first creates the clearing; it presupposes it; the clearing is the open region that grants the possibility of unconcealment (*aletheia*) ("The End of Philosophy and the Task of Thinking," 1964). The Pratyabhijñā side, independently: liberation as recognition of what never left produces nothing — the real was always present, and the "event" is only its being recognized. The book's own law arrives independently — "the book performs what it describes": a book that means to let recognition happen must itself be a clearing, a prepared receptive room, rather than an argument that pushes.
- Refusal kept, his sharpest: the clearing is granted (*Ereignis*, the event of appropriation); we do not command it, and the preparing is *Gelassenheit*, active waiting and not a technique. The gift is real and the control is not; both are kept.
- Standing: nascent; both halves are already owed to the foundation, and the 1964 passage is queued.
