# reflection that returns authorship differs from reflection that takes it

The reframe conversation defines reflection not as repeating a participant's words but as offering something back so the participant can see their own experience differently. It then splits reflection by what the form does to authorship. A provisional offering, phrased as "I wonder if...," returns authorship to the participant, who stays free to accept, revise, or reject it. A declarative naming, phrased as "What you're doing is...," risks taking authorship, since it settles the participant's experience from outside. The difference lies not in the content offered but in whether the phrasing leaves the participant the author of the conclusion. It is the ethic of non-appropriation made operable at the scale of a single sentence.

## Substantiation

- The reframe conversation states that reflection "is offering something back in a way that allows the participant to see their own experience differently," and requires that "reflection must remain provisional." It draws the distinction by example: "'I wonder if...' is fundamentally different from: 'What you're doing is...' The former returns authorship. The latter risks taking it" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: reflection).
