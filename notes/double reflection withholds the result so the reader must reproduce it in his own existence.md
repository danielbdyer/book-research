# double reflection withholds the result so the reader must reproduce it in his own existence

Double reflection is the central mechanism of indirect communication. In the *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* the pseudonym Johannes Climacus separates a first reflection, which gets a thought into adequate objective expression, from a second reflection, the communicator's own inward relation to what is expressed, and builds the form so the receiver must redouble that inwardness in his own existence rather than receive a finished conclusion. The form deliberately withholds the result, so the reader cannot take the point over at second hand but has to re-produce it inwardly; the communication is thereby isolated, throwing the receiver back on himself instead of gathering him into agreement. The cost falls on the author: because appropriation happens invisibly inside the receiver, the author loses every external sign that it occurred, which is the same unverifiability the book names as its own core trust. The device description rests on secondary scholarship; the *Postscript* is unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
