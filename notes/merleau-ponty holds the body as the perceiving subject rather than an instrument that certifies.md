# merleau-ponty holds the body as the perceiving subject rather than an instrument that certifies

The book makes the felt shift its test of truth, so the body enters the center as an instrument that certifies whether a change occurred ([[nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned]]). The clearing names the cost: the body's reality that certifies nothing has no standing when the body's job is to be a test. Merleau-Ponty is the philosopher of exactly that body. His *corps propre* is not an object I have or a tool I use but the perceiving subject itself, the medium through which there is a world at all — the body is constitutive of perception rather than external to it. A reading via the primary's page citations confirms it. He fills the embodied blind spot by refusing the very commitment that created it, which makes him company for the embodied and a standing refusal of the verification standard at once.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the felt shift is the verification standard, and the body is assigned the deciding role (atlas §III; [[nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned]]).
- Merleau-Ponty's half is now shown, from the Stanford Encyclopedia's Merleau-Ponty entry citing the primary by pagination (the reading record is `archive/reading — merleau-ponty, phenomenology of perception on the body-subject (1945).md`): the body is "not external to perception but constitutive of it — the perceiving subject itself," "One's own body is in the world just as the heart is in the organism" (PP: 245/209), and the two-hands reversibility and the flesh (*la chair*, "a prototype of Being," V&I: 179/136) make it ontologically fundamental rather than derived from consciousness. Read via the encyclopedia, not the primary; the French and the verbatim text await copyright editions.
- The refusal is the finding. His body-subject refuses both the body-as-object and the body-as-instrument, including the felt-shift-as-test framing, so he does not shore up the verification standard but stands against it — which is why he fills the blind spot rather than the map. His independence is partial: he shares phenomenological method with Husserl and Heidegger, but the lived-body analysis is precisely where he departs from Heidegger's bodiless Dasein.
