# merleau-ponty holds the body as the perceiving subject rather than an instrument that certifies

The book makes the felt shift its test of truth, so the body enters the center as an instrument that certifies whether a change occurred ([[nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned]]). The clearing names the cost: the body's reality that certifies nothing has no standing when the body's job is to be a test. Merleau-Ponty is the philosopher of exactly that body. His *corps propre* is not an object I have or a tool I use but the perceiving subject itself, the medium through which there is a world at all — "I am my body," prior to any body that could be measured or made a test. He fills the embodied blind spot by refusing the very commitment that created it, which makes him company for the embodied and a standing refusal of the verification standard at once. The reading is not done, so the claim is privated.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the felt shift is the verification standard, and the body is assigned the deciding role (atlas §III; [[nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned]]).
- Merleau-Ponty's half is named, not shown: the body-subject is in *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945), Part One, "The Body" (the reversibility of the two hands touching), and the flesh in *The Visible and the Invisible* (1964), ch. 4, "The Intertwining — The Chiasm," which he left unfinished at his death. To verify at the text; not quoted from memory. The vault shelved him under the relational field, where de Jaegher and Di Paolo already hold the enactivist side, so the re-file is from the relational field, where he is redundant, to the embodied, where nothing is seated.
- The refusal is the finding. His body-subject refuses both the body-as-object and the body-as-instrument, including the felt-shift-as-test framing, so he does not shore up the verification standard but stands against it — which is why he fills the blind spot rather than the map. His independence is partial: he shares phenomenological method with Husserl and Heidegger, but the lived-body analysis is precisely where he departs from Heidegger's bodiless Dasein.
