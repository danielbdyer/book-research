# a tradition is a cultivated locus of recognition — a trained way of noticing — not merely doctrine

The reframe conversation reinterprets what a tradition is. A tradition is not merely a doctrine, a vocabulary, or a belief system; it is a cultivated locus of recognition — a standpoint that has trained a particular way of noticing. On this account a tradition carries distinctions, practices, metaphors, attention structures, methods, transformations, histories, ontological assumptions, and characteristic blind spots, and to enter it is to learn to see through a trained way of seeing rather than to adopt a set of propositions. The claim follows from the conversation's richer sense of a locus: a standpoint is not only a place reality is disclosed from but a way of noticing that has been cultivated over time.

The reinterpretation sets how the book holds the traditions in its company. If a tradition is a trained way of noticing, then the book's task is not to extract its doctrines and reconcile them but to enter each way of seeing carefully enough to find what becomes visible there — which is why the traditions function as lenses rather than as ontological categories to be ranked.
