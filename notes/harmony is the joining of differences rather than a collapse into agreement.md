# harmony is the joining of differences rather than a collapse into agreement

The reader gains a way to think and move with others that costs them none of their own clarity — in fact it sharpens it. You do not have to choose between belonging to the conversation and keeping your own mind; the good conversation is the one where a common understanding forms *and* every voice stays distinctly itself, the way a chord is more than a unison. Meeting of minds is not the erasure of the two minds. Its use to the writing is to replace "getting on the same page" with the older, stronger finding that the richest shared meaning *requires* the differences it holds.

## Substantiation

- Confucius, Analects 13.23: "the exemplary person harmonizes but is not the same (*hé ér bù tóng*); the small person is the same but does not harmonize" — harmony (*hé*) is the concord of things that remain different, like a soup or a piece of music; and 6.30, "wishing to establish himself, he establishes others." Bohm, On Dialogue: *dia-logos*, "meaning flowing through," makes a shared "pool of meaning," yet the discipline is suspension of assumptions in view, not surrender of perception, and dialogue "seeks no agreement." Merleau-Ponty is a third witness (dialogue as "a single fabric," each keeping a standpoint); the Quaker meeting lives it as practice.
- Refusal kept: Confucian harmony is embedded in role and ritual (*lǐ*), which the book does not import; Bohm insists dialogue is not negotiation or consensus. Independent (5th c. BCE, a 20th-c. physicist).
- Standing: nascent; the *hé ér bù tóng* and "pool of meaning" phrasings are queued so they do not blur into a generic "listening well."
