# harmony is the joining of differences rather than a collapse into agreement

The reader gains a way to think and move with others that costs them none of their own clarity — in fact it sharpens it. You do not have to choose between belonging to the conversation and keeping your own mind; the good conversation is the one where a common understanding forms *and* every voice stays distinctly itself, the way a chord is more than a unison. Meeting of minds is not the erasure of the two minds. Its use to the writing is to replace "getting on the same page" with the older, stronger finding that the richest shared meaning *requires* the differences it holds.

## Substantiation

- Bohm grounds his pole at the primary text: dialogue is *dia-logos* — "*dia* means 'through' — it doesn't mean two" — so the image "is of a stream of meaning flowing among and through us and between us … out of which will emerge some new understanding … something creative" (Bohm, On Dialogue); the shared meaning forms by suspending assumptions rather than surrendering perception — "suspend those assumptions, so that you neither carry them out nor suppress them … You simply see what they mean — not only your own, but the other people's as well. We are not trying to change anybody's opinion" — reached "without coming to any conclusions or judgements." The Confucius pole is still owed: Analects 13.23, "the exemplary person harmonizes but is not the same (*hé ér bù tóng*); the small person is the same but does not harmonize," and 6.30, "wishing to establish himself, he establishes others" (from memory, grounding owed). Merleau-Ponty is a third witness (dialogue as "a single fabric," each keeping a standpoint, unread); the Quaker meeting lives it as practice.
- Refusal kept, and Bohm's now grounded: Confucian harmony is embedded in role and ritual (*lǐ*), which the book does not import; Bohm insists dialogue is not consensus or negotiation — not discussion, "a Ping-Pong game … the object of the game is to win," so "we are not playing a game against each other but with each other" (On Dialogue). Independent (5th c. BCE, a 20th-c. physicist).
- Standing: privated — the Bohm pole read at the primary text (On Dialogue); the Confucius *hé ér bù tóng* and the Merleau-Ponty witness still owed, kept so they do not blur into a generic "listening well."
