# the executed draft announces a register change before performing it

The executed draft never changes register unannounced. Before the deepening: "We are about to go deeper, and the prose will get stranger. You may want to read what follows in a single sitting" (abridged ch10, closing). Entering the peak: "This chapter will move, deliberately, more slowly than the others. The prose will loop… If you find yourself disoriented, you are reading correctly. Stay." (abridged ch11).

A reader who becomes disoriented partway through the peak chapter would ordinarily take that as a sign of failing to follow the book, and the announcement removes that reading in advance. Because the text has already said the prose will loop and that disorientation is the correct response to it, the disorientation the reader then feels confirms that the chapter is doing what it said it would do. The earliest draft handles the same problem in the opposite order, naming a difficult move immediately after making it, in the reader's own likely words, so that the reader's suspicion is met rather than avoided ([[the voice names the move it has just made]]); that note also records that the after-the-fact form appears nowhere in the executed arc. Each draft tells the reader what the prose is doing, the earliest one just after the prose does it and the executed one just before.

## Substantiation

- Five sites were verified in the extracted text: the front matter, the prologue, the close of ch10, and two places in ch11; the specimens above are quoted from that extraction.
- Anyone can check this by looking at what precedes each shift of pace or register in the draft, where a sentence describing the coming shift stands. A register change that arrives unannounced is the exception to look for.
- The pacing these sentences manage is the arc's own. The corpus states as the book's single design law that the reading is built to enact the process the text describes, which includes a pacing that is grounded early and most destabilizing at the peak ([[the book performs what it describes]]).
