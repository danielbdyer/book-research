# the unfolding of the center is planted half-erased and finished at a mirror

The book carries its deepest practice formula as a planting rather than a citation. The formula is Kṣemarāja's, madhya-vikāsāt, in the corpus's gloss "from the unfolding of the center comes the bliss of consciousness," and the atlas records both of its sites in one line: "the half-erased word beside the unlabeled room; the sentence eleven chapters finish at the mirror" (atlas §IX.6c). Walked IX confirms the pair as one design: the formula "appears in the book as a half-erased word beside an unlabeled room, and as the sentence eleven chapters finally finish at a mirror."

The design fact is that the formula is neither quoted nor explained where it first appears — it is planted illegibly and paid off at the distance of half the book. This is the planting-ledger apparatus running on the source tradition's own material: the plant-and-payoff device the corpus specifies for images, here applied to a Sanskrit sentence, so that the reader's eventual completion of it is an instance of the front-matter promise rather than an act of instruction — the sentence is met as something already seen and not yet read.
