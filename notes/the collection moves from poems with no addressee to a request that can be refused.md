# the collection moves from poems with no addressee to a request that can be refused

Read end to end rather than mined for phrases, the collection has one movement, and it is a movement in grammar. Early poems have no one to speak to. Five of the first ten address no second person at all: the romance of poem two is narrated in the third person, with the speaker absent from his own scene; poem five makes him a bird; poem ten has neither an *I* nor a *you*. When a second person does stabilize it is the speaker himself, addressed in poem eleven for a whole poem — "inveterate being, you are young / in your ways" (poems #11). A named person arrives only at thirteen: "when i was with you, Kristen" (poems #13).

The last poem completes the movement by making the address public, plural, and refusable. It is spoken to the people he loves, out loud — "so this is me at the microphone telling you" — and what it says is a question rather than a report: "the courage now is — hey. would you want this too? / and then the trembling / the trembling before the answer" (poems #16). The trembling is the content of the finding. An address that can be answered is an address that can be declined, and the collection takes sixteen poems to reach one.

What makes this a movement rather than a sequence is that the poems keep naming the difficulty themselves. The same book that cannot find an addressee also says why: "i don't care that it's 3am / and i'm writing love poetry to nobody / in particular" (poems #12) — an address offered and withdrawn in one line.

## Substantiation

- The distribution is countable and is the claim's evidence: poems two, five, eight, nine, and ten carry no second person; poem eleven's second person is the speaker; poems thirteen through sixteen carry a real, present addressee.
- One typographic detail supports the reading and is recorded as this note's own observation. Poem sixteen is written throughout in lowercase *i*, with exactly two exceptions, both in the homecoming passage: "the place home before I knew the word home" and "and my body — when I heard my name said like that" (poems #16). The capital appears only where the speaker is being named by other people.
- The collection's own emblem for a completed address is a shared noticing, and it is carried from one poem to another: "we saw it because / you saw it and you shared it to me" (poems #12) returns in the last poem as a definition — "current is look at this bird" (poems #16).
