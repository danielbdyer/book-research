# subtracting the community from the talmud page converts it from staging disagreement to looking one up

The clearest case of a margin architecture failing under subtraction is the modern elucidated Talmud. The fully-elucidated translations — the ArtScroll and Steinsaltz editions — fold Rashi's commentary, the Tosafot glosses, and the outer apparatus into one continuous, navigable answer. What the traditional page kept separate and unresolved becomes a single settled exposition a reader can follow alone. Three things drop out in the folding: the eye's forced route through the voices in order, which had been the pedagogy; the study partner the page presupposed, who enacted the disagreement rather than reading it; and the held, unadjudicated dispute, which is replaced by a resolution. A page built to stage a disagreement becomes a book for looking one up.

The finding is the concrete mechanism beneath the general verdict that the margin architectures do not port to solitude. It shows subtraction happening in a real artifact rather than as a thought experiment: the same content, made usable by a solitary reader, is no longer the instrument it was. The elucidated edition is more accessible and less demanding, and it is precisely by removing the demand that it removes the staging. For a book weighing whether to hand its reader a resolved second voice or an unresolved one, the elucidated Talmud is the record of what resolution costs.
