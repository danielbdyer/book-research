# seasonal and weekly form can be templated

The gesture asserts that the shapes of a life can be templated — that seasons, sabbaths, and deliberate bookends are forms which can be written down and reused. The vault holds the doctrine that life is seasonal and holds no form. The metabolic arc names five phases and is a description of what happens to living patterns; it is not something a reader enacts on a calendar. A template is the missing artefact between the two.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- The seasonal doctrine is at atlas §V.3, whose five phases the vault has adopted as the `state:` vocabulary on claims. Its own image is the orchard, and its rule is that composting is not death.
- Season and sabbath together appear across most of the corpus, but as figures for the arc rather than as units of practice; the treatment's register specification for the ripening material is that "the prose slows like September" (arc-gloss: ch10), which is a prose instruction.
- The corpus's one bookend device is the pair of epigraphs, Rilke opening and Eliot closing, which is the book bookending itself rather than a reader bookending anything.

## What would close it

Closing it means producing one reusable form and testing whether the arc survives being put on a calendar.
