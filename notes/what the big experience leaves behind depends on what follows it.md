# what the big experience leaves behind depends on what follows it

The book's premise — the reader already had the big experience and it didn't hold — is the psychedelic research literature's own documented finding, not a rhetorical frame. The afterglow fades on a decay curve the literature has named and measured (Majić, Schmidt & Gallinat 2015, on peak experiences and the afterglow phenomenon). Whether anything persists past the curve tracks two things the peak itself does not supply. In the study that first showed a lasting personality change from psilocybin, increases in openness persisted at roughly fourteen months *only* in participants whose experience met criteria for a complete mystical experience; without it, openness returned to baseline (MacLean, Johnson & Griffiths 2011). And in the later prospective work, enduring positive change at six months was determined by the type of experience *and the rates of ongoing meditation and spiritual practice afterward* — not by dose (Griffiths et al. 2018). The peak is neither sufficient nor self-completing: what it leaves behind depends on what follows it.

This is the strongest outside grounding the shifted promise has. "It didn't hold" is what the decay curve says; "here's what to do about that" is what the persistence predictors say — the after is built from practice and context, which is the phase the book actually addresses. James's transiency mark said the structure a century before the instruments existed: fading is constitutive of the states, "but when they recur it is recognized; and from one recurrence to another it is susceptible of continuous development" — the fading and the buildability in one paragraph ([[william james]]).

The claim's limits are kept in it. These are studies of substance-occasioned experiences, and the book's psychedelic is a movement, not a molecule — the literature grounds the premise about *big experiences* generally only by analogy the vault does not press. And nothing here postulates the book's own efficacy; the finding is about what predicted persistence in clinical cohorts, cited as the field's answer to a question the book shares.
