# the primary layer states its own limit on atomization

The book's system vocabulary decomposes experience into twelve basic operations and into formulas built from them. The poems sit beneath the seven corpus documents as the primary layer, which governs the corpus where the two conflict, and they carry a ruling of their own about how far such decomposition may go. Poem #7: "that elements may not be split / into particles beyond their matter." Poem #11, on the unsolved equation: "the equation has no solution as / the variables continue to churn // when / there is a lovely elegance in the way that / it all works out sometimes isn't there?" The word "particles" appears nowhere in the seven corpus documents, and the corpus's equations — the molecule formulas — are built to solve.

Read under the rule that a phrase shared between one of the first fifteen poems and the corpus originates in the poem, the poet set this boundary before the system was built: analysis is licensed down to the level at which matter still holds together, and an equation may work out without having a solution. On 2026-08-04 the author decided that the book's conceptual schemes hold the status of an instrument for seeing with rather than of the architecture the book is wrapped around, so that gaps and overlaps in those schemes are not defects and are never work. That decision lands where the poems already stood, which makes this claim a precedent for the decision in the author's own earlier writing rather than a fresh argument for it.
