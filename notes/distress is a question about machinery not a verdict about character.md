# distress is a question about machinery not a verdict about character

The book's title names its engine model: a self runs on three loops — a bodily loop (rhythm, appetite, timing), an identity loop (who one takes oneself to be and how that self expresses), and a continuity loop (the thread of meaning that makes yesterday's and tomorrow's self the same project). The corpus notes the model's older form: the Trika tradition's three faces of divine power (will, knowledge, action), inseparable when a person is running well.

The diagnostic move is the question "which cylinder?" — distress stops functioning as a verdict about character and becomes a question about which loop is misfiring. The answer routes: chapter 7 sorts the book's eleven contemporary teachers by which loop their theory of suffering actually treats, converting the company into a referral network. When the teachers' doctrines conflict, the book seats the body as referee, case by case; no doctrine adjudicates.

## Substantiation

- First-telling, "The engine": the three loops and the map of five theories of where suffering lives, presented with their disagreements intact.
- Atlas §V: the loop-to-Śakti correspondence, the referral-network docking at chapter 7, and the body-as-tiebreaker rule (cross-linked to the tensions kept live in §XI.2).
- Walked V records the two additions the book makes to the five theories: suffering carried with meaning (Frankl) and suffering located in structures, where "the cure for a structure is structural."
- The space-around survey identifies a missing voice in the book's map of where suffering lives: biological causes — thyroid, anemia, sleep apnea, medication effects — where no amount of inner work substitutes for a blood panel. The survey marks this as a safety matter, not an enrichment. Drafting that addition has not been done; it is on the pending-evaluation list in `ops/queue/candidates.md`.
