# the guild is seated by contribution rather than by doctrine

The corpus gathers the book's company into five rooms, and the treatment introduces each room by naming what it carries in rather than what it holds true. The facilitation room "brings the technology" — Authentic Relating's protocols and Circling, the witnessing craft the author practices by hand. McLaren's room "brings the address" — every emotion a distinct messenger carrying a gift and a question. The Landmark–Heidegger speech-act room "brings the mechanism, and one warning." The container-makers' room "brings the structural why" — twenty-nine builders each making a way-of-being enterable in one medium. The coaches' room "brings the referral network and the honest fault lines" (arc-gloss: the five rooms).

The organizing principle has a consequence the corpus states directly: because a room is admitted for its contribution, the rooms are not required to agree, and the disagreements are kept rather than smoothed. The treatment records this as an instruction for the Lineage unit: "the roster's fault lines are features — the reader should feel they've joined a living argument, not a settled canon" (arc-gloss: front matter). The same section carries the unit's jewel in the corpus's own words: "you are not reading about these people; you are pulling up a chair."

A second consequence is that one figure can hold two seats without contradiction. Joe Hudson sits in the container-makers' room for emotional fluency and in the coaches' room as the allowers' camp, "seated twice in this guild" (walked IX); the corpus marks the double seat rather than choosing one.

## Substantiation

- Arc-gloss, "the five rooms," is the primary statement; the atlas seats the same five at §IX.1 through §IX.5 under the same contribution headings — Room One "(the technology)", Room Two "(the address)", Room Three "(the mechanism, and one warning)", Room Four "(the structural why)", Room Five "(the referral network, and the honest fault lines)".
- The atlas records three cross-cutting seating charts over the same company — "by medium / by theory of suffering / by level of the word" (atlas §IX preamble) — plus the valley and the porch. The charts sort by contribution, by position, and by level; none sorts by agreement.
- The rule that governs how the company is used in the prose is the atlas's Generosity Policy: "secrets are for the story, never for the sources — every elder named on sight, on the page, diegetically" (atlas §IX preamble).
- The treatment's warning clause belongs to the speech-act room alone and is recorded as part of its contribution rather than as an objection to it: the standing danger of "declaration-as-vestment — the declared self worn as costume" (atlas §IX.3).
