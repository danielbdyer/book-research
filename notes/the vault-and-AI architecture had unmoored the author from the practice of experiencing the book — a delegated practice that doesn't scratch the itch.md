# the vault-and-AI architecture had unmoored the author from the practice of experiencing the book — a delegated practice that doesn't scratch the itch

The author states, in his own words, the diagnosis that occasions the whole reframe. The research codebase he has been building with AI collaborators had "gotten off track," conflating distinct upper ontologies with one another; and beyond that confusion he names a deeper cost. The practice had "fundamentally... unmoored me from the practice of experience around what it's like to write this book." The work of building and delegating to the system had become a substitute for the lived experience of writing, and that substitute "doesn't really scratch the itch of... the whole reason why the book is important to me in the first place." The delegated practice produces artifacts without returning him to the experience the book exists to hold.

The diagnosis is the lived instance of a distinction the book itself draws — that information about an experience is not participation in it — turned on the making of the book. It is not a rejection of the tools; the same exchange holds that they are "scaffolding, not the dwelling itself." What it identifies is a direction that had inverted: the apparatus had come to stand where the lived practice should, so that the thinking got done while, in the collaborator's echo, "the body doesn't get to recognize." This is the highest-value diagnostic of the conversation, because every subsequent design rule — the system's purpose, the direction of flow, the central place of the encounter — is an answer to it.
