# seventeen chapters rest at madhyama

Madhyamā, the third of the corpus's four levels of speech, names the fully formed sentence that has not been spoken. The corpus applies the term to its own condition: seventeen trial chapters exist, fully formed and sequenced, and none has been released. The walked document states what that condition amounts to — the chapters are "perfectible forever, and therefore safe, and therefore not yet real" (walked VI, applied at XI) — and the atlas ranks this tension above the others because it concerns whether the book exists at all (§XI.1f).

The treatment on record is specific: one pilot chapter, treated fully against every revision pass, sent to two real readers. Two readers constitute release — the chapter can no longer be revised for them and can therefore be answered by them. The corpus frames this as its own doctrine applied at home rather than as an external deadline.

This vault's relationship to the tension is stated in [[methods]]: the vault is measured by what it contributes toward this move, and vault growth that displaces it is the failure the periodic /rethink review is required to ask about.

## Substantiation

- Atlas §XI.1f records the tension and the recommended move.
- Walked XI carries the fuller statement, including the count (fifteen years of poems, seventeen chapters) and the framing of release as the doctrine walked to its own door.
- The doctrine itself is recorded at [[a word at madhyama can be perfected forever a word at vaikhari can be answered]]; the anti-preservation rule at [[ripeness is not refrigeration]].
- The drafts admitted on 2026-08-01 supply a fact that narrows the tension without altering it. A complete prose pass at the arc exists, recorded at [[the whole arc has already been drafted once in prose]], so the unreleased state is not explained by unfinished writing. The treatment above is unchanged, because what it requires is a reader rather than a draft.
