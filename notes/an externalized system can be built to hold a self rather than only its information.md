# an externalized system can be built to hold a self rather than only its information

The gesture asserts that an externalized system — the thing the culture calls a second brain — can be designed to be soul-aligned, which in the book's own vocabulary would mean designed as a container with a boundary, a permeability, and a duration. The vault does not hold this, and the omission is structural rather than accidental: the container anatomy claims to describe every container in any medium, and the corpus tests that claim only on rooms, dyads, circles, and the book itself. A built information system would be the anatomy's hardest case, because it is the one container whose contents are explicitly not the person.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- The gesture exists as a contents entry and a reader profile. The earliest edition names among its intended readers "Creators of inner architectures and Second Brains" (mythic-os: book overview), an audience the arc's front matter does not address.
- The phrase appears once in seven documents. No descriptive source treats externalized systems at all.
- The claim it would test is at [[the triad is the anatomy of every container]], which rests on twenty-nine builders working in twenty-nine media, none of them a personal information system.

## What would close it

Closing it means running the container anatomy against a built system and reporting whether the three structures are found or forced.
