# the householder axiom depends on the participation law being satisfiable within ordinary life

The book holds two doctrines that have not been composed. The Householder Axiom names ordinary life the full site of the work — "the Tuesday is the temple; no renunciation is required" (walked I; [[the tuesday is the temple]]). The participation law holds that nothing changes from the audience, so transformation requires participation inside a held container with a witness ([[nothing transforms from the audience]]; [[transformation is enacted participation inside a held container]]). Set side by side, the first depends silently on the second: if ordinary life is the full site, then ordinary life must itself supply the container and the witness the participation law requires, or the axiom names a site the law has already called insufficient.

The dependency has a known boundary case and a known bridge, both already in the vault. The boundary is grief, the one place the corpus says the Tuesday cannot work solo — grief "cannot complete alone" and structurally requires a witness ([[grief cannot complete alone]]), which is exactly a demand the participation law makes that a solitary household week may not meet. The bridge is the book's own practice doctrine: the practices are the author's room-holding craft scaled to a room of one, so the reader ends as their own container ([[the book's practices are facilitation moves scaled to one reader]]). That thesis is what would satisfy the dependency from inside ordinary life, and it sits exactly on this seam.

So the axiom is not refuted; it is shown to rest on a condition it never states, and the condition is where the book's hardest structural question lives — what participation can mean for a reader holding a printed page alone. Whether the Tuesday can supply container and witness within ordinary life is the same question the facilitation-scaled-to-one thesis answers and the solitary-reader tension keeps open.

## Substantiation

- The two doctrines are quoted at their notes with locations: the axiom at walked I ("the Tuesday is the temple; no renunciation is required; the grid stays on"), the law at atlas §IX.3 ("the lineage's one unanimous law: nothing transforms from the audience"). The composition of the two is the vault's, stated here for the first time, per the panel's connector entry (forest panel, 2026-08-08).
- The boundary case is the corpus's own: grief is carried in all four source documents as the emotion that cannot complete without witness and ceremony (atlas §IX.2; [[grief cannot complete alone]]), which is where the participation law's demand exceeds what a solitary household week supplies.
- The bridge is the corpus's design law for the praxis section, stated in its own words — "the book's practices are, at bottom, the craft of holding a room for people — scaled down to one" (first-telling: the practice) — and it is the material that would let ordinary life meet the participation law's requirement without leaving the household.
- Held as a dependency rather than a tension. The composed relation reveals a condition the axiom rests on, not a contradiction that changes what the book is; whether the split is doctrinal enough to earn a tension is the author's judgment, and the facilitation thesis and the one-chair tension already carry the live form of the question.
