# the alliance holds its size when the exchange is mediated and written

The book's hardest structural problem is that its deepest claim needs two people while a book is read by one, and the sharpest version of the objection is mechanical: coordination requires two systems adjusting to each other in time, and a text does not adjust. The first measured evidence bearing on that objection sits inside the alliance meta-analysis, in its separate treatment of therapy delivered over the internet.

The expectation going in was that the relation would weaken. The authors record it: "It has been repeatedly hypothesized that the alliance is probably less important in Internet-based therapy than in standard face-to-face therapies." What they found across 23 independent samples and 1,178 clients was an association of r = .275 (95% CI [.205, .344], p < .0001), against r = .278 for face-to-face therapy — and the internet effect sizes were more homogeneous than the larger set rather than noisier. Their own statement of it: "Whatever aspects of the alliance are captured in Internet therapies, the alliance appears to relate to outcome, in a quantitative sense, similarly to face-to-face psychotherapy." Most of these treatments are internet-delivered cognitive behavioural therapy, with the alliance rated by the client in the first weeks of a course conducted largely in writing.

What this rules out is narrow and worth having exactly. It rules out the claim that a working relationship must be face-to-face and synchronous to do its work. It does not show that a book can carry one. Every case counted here has a person on the other end who reads what the client wrote and answers it, so the finding narrows the distance between the book's form and the evidence without closing it. The remaining gap is precisely the book's own: a letter expects an answer and a published book cannot give one, which is why the answerable last line and the pilot's two readers are the parts of the design this evidence bears on most directly.

Their claim concerns real-time coupling, and internet-delivered therapy is asynchronous in most of these studies, so the finding meets the objection rather than sidestepping it.
- The reading queue already carried this as the drafting question for the book's genre, in the entry asking whether the enactivist extension into language covers asynchronous addressed exchange. That entry stands, because a measured association is not an account of the mechanism.
- One limit belongs to the claim and cannot be argued away. The alliance measured in these studies is between two people, one of whom is responding. Nothing here measures a reader alone with a finished text.
