# allowing one's own sufficiency is a recursive condition for entering this reality

The author states, in his own words, that allowing one's own sufficiency is among the core conditions for entering the reality the work opens onto, and that the condition is recursive — the more the sufficiency is allowed, the more the entering becomes possible, which in turn allows more of the sufficiency. He puts it as a turn the work makes toward the people doing it, the participants and facilitators in co-creation: that each of them already has a way of recognizing the world that is genuinely useful and has its own merit, by the mere suchness of who they are, and that allowing that sufficiency to exist is what makes entry possible. He attaches a guard in the same breath — the allowing is done without making too much of a self-importance of it — so that sufficiency is a permission rather than a new claim to standing.

The claim matters to the book because it names sufficiency not as a comfort offered at the end but as a precondition at the threshold: a person cannot enter the reality the book describes while withholding from themselves the permission to be enough as they are. Stated as a loop, it also explains why the book can only invite and not install the condition — the allowing has to be done by the person, and each allowing makes the next one more possible.
