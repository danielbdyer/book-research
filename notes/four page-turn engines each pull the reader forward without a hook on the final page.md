# four page-turn engines each pull the reader forward without a hook on the final page

Four forward engines can pull a reader through a unit while its close stays restful, and each is keyed to a device the corpus already designed. A promise made early and visibly not yet kept is itself a forward engine, drawn from the escrow of kept promises. The inhabited margins form a second book with its own plot, so a reader can rest in the body text and still turn the page toward the margin's subplot. A planted fragment held until it is completed keeps attention until the payoff, the mechanism the planting ledger runs; its outside name is the Zeigarnik effect — interrupted tasks persist in memory where completed ones release (1927, unread here). And the letter's engine pulls the reader toward the place where answering becomes possible.

All four are compatible with restful closes, which is what qualifies them for a book whose units must end in narrative slack, and the working test for all four is the pilot unit rather than any further argument.

## Substantiation

- The offering is recorded at the forest panel of 2026-08-08, panel three, eighth entry.
- The devices the engines are keyed to are the escrow, the margins, and the planting ledger at atlas §X.6, most of them unbuilt in any draft ([[the drafts execute one of the seven designed apparatus devices]]).
- The planted-fragment engine has one recorded instance in the extracted material, a formula planted half-erased and finished late ([[the unfolding of the center is planted half-erased and finished at a mirror]]).
- The Zeigarnik name is kept as a label rather than a citation: the panel's own note marks the reading as stood down, since the pilot unit tests the page-turn candidates directly.
