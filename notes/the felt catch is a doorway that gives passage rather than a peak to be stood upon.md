# the felt catch is a doorway that gives passage rather than a peak to be stood upon

The felt catch the book calls the "whoa" — the bodily give when something long known is suddenly seen — is a doorway rather than a summit. It gives passage: the give means a way has opened, so a reader can trust the sensation without gripping it, prolonging it, or trying to manufacture its return. Held this way, the catch stops being a peak to re-climb and becomes a threshold to walk through, which relieves the exhausting project of chasing the feeling itself. A door is for passing through, not for framing.

## Substantiation

- Two traditions with no historical contact figure the event of aliveness as a flash that opens the eye onto a world. In Kashmir Śaivism the pulse is ocular, now read at the primary Sanskrit — "yasyonmeṣanimeṣābhyāṃ jagataḥ pralayodayau," Śiva's *unmeṣa* and *nimeṣa*, the opening and closing of the eyes by which the world's arising and dissolution occur (Spanda Kārikā I.1; Singh: "by whose mere opening and shutting of the eye-lids there is the appearance and dissolution of the world"). The later-Heidegger pole is still owed: he names the same as the Einblitz, the "in-flashing" of being ("Die Kehre"), and reads the event of appropriation through *er-äugen*, "to bring before the eyes" ("The Principle of Identity"; from memory, grounding owed). The convergence is company rather than proof, each half cited by address.
- Refusal kept: Heidegger refuses to let the flash become a possession — "das Ereignis ereignet," the event only appropriates and cannot be grasped as a thing in time or repeated on command. The door is real; it is not merchandise.
- Standing: privated — the Spanda pole read at the primary Sanskrit (Spanda Kārikā I.1, unmeṣa/nimeṣa; the record is in `archive/`); the two later-Heidegger essays owed. The eye-flash rhyme is company, not proof, and one scholarly crux is kept open on purpose: which eye-movement maps to arising versus dissolution is contested (Singh and Kṣemarāja read *unmeṣa*↔arising; Lakshmanjoo the reverse), so the note stays uncommitted on that pairing.
