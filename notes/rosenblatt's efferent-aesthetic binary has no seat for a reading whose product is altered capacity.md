# rosenblatt's efferent-aesthetic binary has no seat for a reading whose product is altered capacity

Rosenblatt's continuum is defined entirely by where the reader's attention falls during the transaction, and that definition leaves the book's own standard without a place. The aesthetic pole terminates in the savouring of the lived-through event; the efferent pole gathers what is carried away after. Anything that happens after the reading — a changed capacity to act, a re-formed way of meeting a situation — she re-files as efferent residue rather than as a further kind of reading. So the stance the book most wants to name, a reading whose product is enactment, gets no seat: the experiential slot is spent on the aesthetic savouring and the consequential slot is efferent. Her framework implies an event-ontology, reading as something done and the poem as something that happens, yet forecloses an enactive stance in the book's sense.

The finding sets a decision for the book. A book whose standard is lived capacity needs a term Rosenblatt's binary excludes, and whether that exclusion is her omission or her considered denial is exactly what a primary reading must settle, because it decides whether the book extends her transactional theory or breaks with it.
