# the facilitator can supply meaning where the participant might have discovered it, so do not solve what the participant has not yet had the opportunity to encounter

The reframe conversation identifies a subtle failure available to a capable facilitator: supplying meaning the participant might have discovered for themselves. A compelling interpretation can be accepted precisely because it is compelling, and in being accepted it forecloses the participant's own encounter. The conversation raises this into an ethical constraint on intervention — the facilitator must not solve what the participant has not yet had the opportunity to encounter. The constraint is about timing and ownership rather than about withholding help in general: the harm is pre-empting a discovery, not offering support once the encounter has been had.

## Substantiation

- The reframe conversation states that "A powerful facilitator can easily colonize meaning" by offering an interpretation such as "What you're really experiencing is...," which the participant "may accept because it is compelling." It names what has gone wrong: "The facilitator has supplied meaning where the participant might have discovered meaning," and sets the constraint in imperative form: "Do not solve what the participant has not yet had the opportunity to encounter" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: the ethics of non-appropriation).
