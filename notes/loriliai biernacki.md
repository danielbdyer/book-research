# loriliai biernacki

Loriliai Biernacki's *The Matter of Wonder* (Oxford, 2023) takes Abhinavagupta's late commentary as a live philosophical resource for the present-day consciousness-and-matter debate — on her reading, everything carries an innate capacity for sentience even where nothing is actually sentient — and her operative term is wonder, from Abhinavagupta's camatkāra. She is the proof that the crossover is a current scholarly project and not a closed historical episode: a university press published the eleventh-century Kashmiri into the New Materialism debate the year before this vault existed.

The refusal kept: her destination is metaphysics — a model for contemporary theory and environmental thought — not practice. The living developer develops the crossover toward theory, which means the lane this book occupies, the practice verified in the body, stays unoccupied even by the field's most current work. Her camatkāra is also worth the book's attention on its own: the tradition's own word for wonder as a mark of recognition.
