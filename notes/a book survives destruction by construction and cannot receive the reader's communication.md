# a book survives destruction by construction and cannot receive the reader's communication

Winnicott's account of what turns an object into something a person can use has two conditions on the object's side, and a book stands in opposite relations to them. The first condition is survival, and he defines it negatively and narrowly: "It is important that 'survive', in this context, means 'not retaliate'" (Winnicott 1969, p. 360). The risk he treats as gravest is not the object's disappearance but its change of manner — "even the actual death of the analyst is not as bad as the development in the analyst of a change of attitude towards retaliation" (pp. 360–361) — and he puts the whole weight of the word destruction on that fallibility: "The word 'destruction' is needed, not because of the baby's impulse to destroy, but because of the object's liability not to survive, which also means to suffer change in quality, in attitude" (p. 361). A printed book has no attitude that can change after printing. It is also, on his other requirement, unambiguously qualified: an object that is to be used "must necessarily be real in the sense of being part of shared reality, not a bundle of projections" (p. 357), and a book written by another person, sitting in the world, is exactly that. A reader can attack a book with everything they have and the book will be there tomorrow, unchanged and unretaliating. On the survival condition a codex is close to an ideal object.

The second condition is the one it fails, and the failure is total rather than partial. Winnicott's sequence is a dialogue with a listener in it: "The subject says to the object: 'I destroyed you', and the object is there to receive the communication. From now on the subject says: 'Hullo object!' 'I destroyed you'. 'I love you'. 'You have value for me because of your survival of my destruction of you'" (p. 359). Being there to receive is not decoration on the survival; it is how the subject learns that the object survived. A book cannot register that it was attacked, cannot be found still standing by anyone but the reader's own report, and returns nothing. Whatever survival the reader observes is a survival the reader has to certify alone, which is the same position the verification standard is in when a reader checks the body with nobody in the room.

The design consequence is narrow and it is not a verdict on any book. A text can be built so that the receiving position exists somewhere other than in the text — a reader asked to write an answer has made a place for the communication, and a reader who is actually answered has an object that received it. The vault's measured evidence points the same way from a different direction, since every case in which a written and mediated exchange keeps the effect of a working alliance has a live person on the other end who replies. What Winnicott adds is that the missing piece is not warmth or attention but a receiver for one specific message, which is the message that the reader attacked the thing and the thing is still there.

## Substantiation

- The reading is recorded at `archive/reading — winnicott, the use of an object and relating through identifications (1969).md`, from the Collected Works text of the paper, read complete.
- Nothing in the paper concerns books, texts, or any object that is not a person, so the two-condition split above is a reading of his conditions rather than a distinction he draws. He never asks what happens when an object cannot fail to survive, and the question does not arise inside a clinical argument.
- The companion finding from the same reading, which disqualifies the book writing its own refusal, is at [[a refusal the book writes for itself fails the warrant the design cites]]. The two are separate claims because they fall on opposite sides of the same paper: one is about where the destruction comes from, this one is about what the object has to do with it.
- The measured evidence that agrees from outside is at [[the alliance holds its size when the exchange is mediated and written]], whose limit is stated in the same terms — every case has a live responder.
