# the felt shift's external check can be a resisting world, not only another person

The vault frames the check that would tell a genuine felt shift from its counterfeit as a second person: the second chair, the judge and the mirror, the partner who corrects a reading the self cannot correct alone ([[the second chair is the verification standard's own requirement]]). Matthew Crawford locates the same corrective function somewhere else, in the resistance of a world. A craft, on his account, submits the practitioner to hard external constraints — the engine runs or it does not — and those constraints tell the truth more reliably than the voices in one's own head. He names the physical constraint that offloads a choice onto the world a jig, and holds that a person individuates by submitting to it rather than by consulting an inner buffer. The finding this yields for the book is that the missing external check has two forms rather than one: a correcting person, and a resisting world. Both are standards outside the felt sense that the felt sense cannot supply to itself, and neither is the room's own approval.

The remainder is severe for a book. A book is a weak jig. Reading resists almost nothing — a page does not refuse a false move the way a mis-assembled engine refuses to start — so the world-as-verifier is close to exactly what the page cannot furnish. This widens the solitary-reader problem rather than relieving it: where the second chair named one absent corrective, a person, Crawford names a second absent corrective, a resisting world, and the codex supplies neither ([[a codex is a one-chair technology making a two-chair argument]]).
