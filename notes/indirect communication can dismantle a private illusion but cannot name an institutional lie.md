# indirect communication can dismantle a private illusion but cannot name an institutional lie

When Bishop Mynster died in January 1854, Kierkegaard dropped the entire apparatus of indirection and attacked the Danish state church directly — the pamphlets of *The Moment* used named targets, plain polemic, no pseudonym, and no revocation. The abandonment marks a limit in the method rather than a change of heart: indirect communication can dismantle an illusion held by an individual, but it cannot name an institutional lie or force a public decision, because a maieutic that refuses authority cannot stand up and assert that the official church is not Christianity. The register indirection could not enter is the collective and the institutional, which is precisely the register a participatory, plural book lives in. A book addressed to a shared world therefore cannot rely on the indirect devices for the part of its work that is public and collective. The finding rests on secondary scholarship, the primaries unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
