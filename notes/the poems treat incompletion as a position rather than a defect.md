# the poems treat incompletion as a position rather than a defect

The collection's most consistent formal habit is that its poems do not close. They stop on dashes — "and he is soon ready to go–" (poems #5), "I still need a lot these days–" (poems #13) — on typographic marks that are not punctuation, on deflations after high rhetoric ("well I want to see," poems #3), and in one case on an open parenthesis that is never closed (poems #6). This is not carelessness in a few places; it is the collection's default, and the poems say so about themselves.

Poem seven states the position as a theory of endings: "good endings aren't really endings / they're just ways of being sure / that what you're over is like / a bird flying over – yes, it's gone / but does it really cease to exist / or can we just permit it / to find residency outside of our whole" (poems #7). Poem two ends by naming incompletion as the achievement rather than the shortfall — the love it describes is "ineffable, innocent, and beautifully incomplete" (poems #2). Poem six puts the poem's unfinished state and the life's in a single parenthesis: "i do not have a resolution yet (to this poem or to life either for that matter)" (poems #6).

The position has one counter-instance, and it is the collection's hinge. Poem fourteen ends on an intention to end — "and i'm almost there. i just need to say goodbye" (poems #14) — which is a direct reversal of poem seven's "there's a reason I don't like / to say goodbye" (poems #7). A body of work organized against closing contains, near its end, one poem that decides to close.

## Substantiation

- The formal evidence is a count rather than an impression: of sixteen poems, one ends in an aphorism (poems #8), one ends on an intention to conclude (poems #14), and the rest trail, dissolve, deflate, or stop mid-gesture. The final words of the collection are an imperative cut off: "listen—." (poems #16).
- Poem eleven opens with a compositional rule that explains the habit from inside: "(only write from the now / right about now lest / you leave behind a part of / yourself in a place you cannot see)" (poems #11).
- Poem twelve declines its own genre twice in eight lines — "the book that doesn't need to be written" and the love poetry written "to nobody / in particular" (poems #12).
- The limit is stated rather than argued past: these are unedited personal papers, carrying uncorrected misspellings, and a reader could call the open endings a symptom instead of a stance. What makes the stance reading available is that three poems articulate it in propositions rather than only performing it.
