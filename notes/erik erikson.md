# erik erikson

Erikson's eight-stage account of the life cycle put a developmental task at each stage and a characteristic failure beside it, and its fifth stage — identity against role confusion — gave the twentieth century two of its most durable terms: *identity crisis*, which he coined, and the *psychosocial moratorium*, a sanctioned period in which a young person is permitted not to have settled on who they are. His claim is that identity is not discovered once but repeatedly negotiated between a person and what their society will recognize, which makes it a relational and historical achievement rather than an inner fact.

The book's identity loop runs on that vocabulary without naming it, and one of its formulas runs on the measurement Erikson's frame made possible. `Foreclosure = Commitment − Defer` is James Marcia's identity status, and Marcia's four statuses are an operationalization of Erikson's fifth stage — Marcia's contribution was to cross Erikson's two dimensions and make them interviewable ([[james marcia]]). Neither name appears in any of the eight documents, and *Erikson* returns zero.

**What he would refuse.** The privacy of the whole operation. Erikson's identity is confirmed by a community — a person's sense of who they are has to be recognized by others to hold, which is why he wrote about it historically and biographically rather than as a psychology of the interior. A reader working out their own configuration from a printed formula is doing half of what he described. He would also refuse the loop's timelessness: his stages are age-graded and his moratorium is a feature of a particular society's arrangements for the young, which is a structural claim about the conditions of identity work that the book's engine does not carry.

**The load, and why he sits behind Marcia rather than beside him.** With `precedent`. The construct the book actually states is Marcia's, and the attribution debt is Marcia's; Erikson is the frame that makes the construct intelligible, and he supplies something Marcia does not — the reason the absence of exploration matters, which is that identity has to be negotiated rather than assigned. One clause. This is the honest size of it, and it is smaller than the other finds in this group.

**Neither half of this correspondence is quoted.** The book's identity-loop material is described rather than quoted here, and Erikson's stage is stated from secondary knowledge in the vault's own words. Nothing of his has been read in this vault. The address is *Identity: Youth and Crisis*, chapter three. Of the group this is the smallest claim and the loosest evidence, which is consistent: one clause is what it asks for.

## What the reading has to settle

Two questions, in the order that decides the most.

- Whether Erikson's own account of foreclosure — he discusses premature identity closure directly — is stated as a privation, which would put him in the convergence at [[the privation move is an independent arrival across five fields rather than a book coinage]].
- Whether the moratorium has anything to say to the book's Defer, which would make the correspondence a construct rather than a frame.
