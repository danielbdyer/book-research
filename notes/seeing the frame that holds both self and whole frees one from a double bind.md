# seeing the frame that holds both self and whole frees one from a double bind

The author names this the texture that "spawned the whole thing." Experience offers two movements — identifying as the particular self, and identifying with something larger of which the self is one expression — and the insight is that neither is ranked beneath the other. What frees is not choosing the larger identification over the smaller but seeing the encompassing frame that holds both: in the author's own figure, "popping out of that bubble, observing that that is a bubble that contains the two parts." Seen that way, the two identifications stop being a forced choice and become the ordinary rhythm of experience, so a person is released from the double bind of having to be either the separate self or the dissolved whole. The author records the freedom as durable — once seen, the access stays — and states the reader's version of it as an invitation to "give yourself the liberty of relating to reality in more of this way."

The author holds the insight without a lineage claim. He connects it to the tradition's non-dual seeing, to the racket a person tells themselves, and back toward Heidegger's aletheia, but explicitly declines to draw the connection tight — "I don't claim a straight lineage there at all" — offering it instead as a recurring human movement, one more set of "fingers pointing to the moon." Held here as primary material in the author's voice, at the specificity the record supplies.
