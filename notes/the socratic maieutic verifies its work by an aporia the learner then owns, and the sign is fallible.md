# the socratic maieutic verifies its work by an aporia the learner then owns, and the sign is fallible

The oldest ancestor of the book's felt-shift standard is Socratic midwifery, and it is checkable by a felt shift, but fallibly. In the *Theaetetus* and *Meno* Socrates, barren himself, delivers the interlocutor's offspring and tests whether it is a genuine child or, in his phrase, a mere image and a lie; the observable sign is aporia — the *Meno*'s torpedo-fish numbing, a felt state of perplexity — after which the interlocutor recognizes the answer as his own, which is recollection. So the shift is felt, and it is the interlocutor's rather than the teacher's, which is exactly the structure the book's verification claims. But Socrates admits the standard is fallible: the wind-egg can pass for a true child, and the aporia can be produced by being cornered before an audience rather than by insight. The book inherits both the test and its oldest admission of unreliability. The finding rests on memory of the dialogues; the primary reading is queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.

## Substantiation

- Dig 1 records the in-situ standard from the *Theaetetus* (148e–151d) and *Meno* (80–86) [S]: the midwife tests whether the offspring is "a genuine child or 'a mere image and a lie,'" the sign being aporia followed by the interlocutor recognizing the answer as his own (transmission dig 1, 2026-08-13, felt-shift audit).
- The dig marks the fallibility from Socrates' own admission — the wind-egg passes for a true child and aporia can come from being cornered — and queues the reading with the question of what Socrates accepts as evidence of a genuine birth (`ops/reading queue.md`; dig 1's readings-to-queue).
