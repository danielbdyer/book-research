# the cusp is the back door to the stance

The cusp carries the present-tense demand that cannot be prepared for: the weight is heavy or it is not, the sentence is mid-air or it is not. Because the managing mind arrives too late at a cusp, what is left standing there is the Stance — which makes the cusp the back door to a posture that resists direct cultivation.

## Substantiation

- Arc-gloss, the image-system: "The Cusp ↔ the present-tense demand the inner manager cannot prepare for ↔ sandhi, the junction; the back door to the Stance. Why participation works when insight has stalled: the cusp is where the cathedral-making mind arrives too late."
- The treatment applies it twice: the embedded poem "arrives too fast and too whole for the analytical reader to prepare a stance; the manager can't set the room for it. The gate is a cusp" (arc-gloss: ch1), and as instruction — "When cultivation stalls, arrange a cusp" (arc-gloss: ch13). The corpus places the interludes at the same junction term, sandhi (atlas §X.4c). The teaching this figure carries is recorded on its own at [[the gap between two states is where the concealed shows itself]]: the tradition holds that what is concealed shows itself at the crossing between two states rather than inside either one, which is why arranging a cusp is a usable instruction rather than a metaphor.
- The figure is second-edition material; the executed draft's Dead Stop is the nearest executed form, which the treatment reads as "a manufactured cusp" (arc-gloss: ch14).
