# you are already free and the work removes a misunderstanding rather than adding an attainment

Freedom, on this reading, is not a state reached at the end of a long acquisition but a fact about you now, obscured only by a mistaken idea of yourself. The reader gains the lightest possible starting point: nothing to build, nothing to earn, only something to stop overlooking. Where most self-improvement adds — more discipline, more insight, more practice — this says the essential move is subtractive, the quiet removal of a single misapprehension, after which what remains was never actually missing. Its use to the writing is to isolate the ontological form of the thesis and to stage the book's most important preserved disagreement as a live seam.

## Substantiation

- Advaita (Śaṅkara): the Self already is Brahman, already unbound; bondage is superimposition (adhyāsa) resting on ignorance (avidyā), and liberation dissolves the ignorance rather than attaining anything — *tat tvam asi*, "that thou art" (Chāndogya 6.8.7); Gauḍapāda's *ajātivāda*, "none is bound, none aspires, none is liberated" (Māṇḍūkya-kārikā 2.32). Dzogchen: the nature of mind is *ka dag*, primordially pure, so the path improves nothing and only recognizes. All attributed, to verify.
- Refusal kept, and it is essential rather than a blemish: Advaita and Buddhism agree the work is un-forgetting and disagree, irreconcilably, about *what* was forgotten. Advaita recognizes a permanent Self; Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka refuses to reify anything recognized (anātman; all things empty of svabhāva). The book must not resolve this into a bland "they all point to the same thing" — they point to the same *move*, stop mistaking, and to opposite *findings*. Keeping the disagreement is what lets each survive being quoted.
- Standing: nascent; the doctrines are firm, the kārikā translations queued.
