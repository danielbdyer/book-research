# you are already free and the work removes a misunderstanding rather than adding an attainment

Freedom, on this reading, is not a state reached at the end of a long acquisition but a fact about you now, obscured only by a mistaken idea of yourself. The reader gains the lightest possible starting point: nothing to build, nothing to earn, only something to stop overlooking. Where most self-improvement adds — more discipline, more insight, more practice — this says the essential move is subtractive, the quiet removal of a single misapprehension, after which what remains was never actually missing. Its use to the writing is to isolate the ontological form of the thesis and to stage the book's most important preserved disagreement as a live seam.

## Substantiation

- Advaita (Śaṅkara), the ever-attained-freedom pole read: the Self already is Brahman, bondage is superimposition (adhyāsa) resting on ignorance (avidyā), and liberation dissolves the ignorance rather than attaining anything — *tat tvam asi*, "that thou art" (Chāndogya 6.8.7); Gauḍapāda's *ajātivāda*, "none is bound, none aspires, none is liberated" (Māṇḍūkya-kārikā 2.32); the arrival is grounded at [[advaita reaches the uncovering move through neti neti and an ever-attained freedom]]. Dzogchen, now read: original purity is said of the essence of phenomena rather than narrowly of the mind — "Because phenomena are originally pure in essence and spontaneously present by their very nature, they are free of the limitations of the four alternatives" (Longchenpa, *The Precious Treasury of the Way of Abiding*, trans. Barron, Source Verses p. 46); "Original purity in its essence has never existed as anything; rather, its nature, like that of space, is primordially pure" (pp. 47–48) — and the path adds nothing: "Within the ultimate heart essence — awareness as such — there is nothing to improve upon, so positive actions bring no benefit. There is nothing to deteriorate, so negative actions inflict no injury" (pp. 7–8).
- Refusal kept, and it is essential rather than a blemish — now grounded at the primary Sanskrit on the Madhyamaka side. Advaita and Buddhism agree the work is un-forgetting and disagree, irreconcilably, about *what* was forgotten. Advaita recognizes a permanent Self; Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka refuses to reify anything — no self ("the self not existing, how will there be what belongs to a self?," MMK 18.2), all things empty of *svabhāva* (MMK 15.1–2), and even emptiness not to be held as a view ("those for whom emptiness is a view have been called incurable," *asādhya*, MMK 13.8), refusing even "no-self" as a positive doctrine ("neither self nor nonself," MMK 18.6). The disagreement is the traditions' own — Śaṅkara refutes the emptiness doctrine as "contradicted by all means of right knowledge" (Brahmasūtrabhāṣya II.2.31), and Advaitins were charged in return as "crypto-Buddhists." Both reach "nothing is attained" (Advaita because the Self already is; Nāgārjuna because there is no ground to gain — "no distinction whatsoever between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa," MMK 25.19), so they point to the same *move* and to opposite *findings*. The book must not resolve this into a bland "they all point to the same thing"; keeping the disagreement is what lets each survive being quoted.
- Standing: full — the Advaita and Madhyamaka poles both read at the primary texts (the record is `archive/reading — nāgārjuna, mūlamadhyamakakārikā (c. 150 ce).md`; the Advaita arrival at [[advaita reaches the uncovering move through neti neti and an ever-attained freedom]]), and the Dzogchen *ka dag* pole read 2026-09-02 at Barron's translation of Longchenpa (the record is `archive/reading — the seamless mirror (henry, longchenpa, śaṅkara on self-knowing).md`).
- A caution the third voice brings with it. Longchenpa's route is not the Advaitin one. Advaita removes an *avidyā* that genuinely superimposed; Longchenpa denies there was ever any obscuration in the essence to remove — "has never existed as anything" — which is nearer the author's own "only unregarded" and his "fundamental clenching that didn't obscure the vision" (`reflections/on recognition (2026-08-24)`) than to Śaṅkara, and is a second, independent instance of the contraction-not-concealment seam the reflection already found. Three convergent voices on "nothing is attained," then, and three different accounts of what was in the way.
