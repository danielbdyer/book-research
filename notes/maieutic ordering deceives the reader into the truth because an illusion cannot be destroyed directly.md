# maieutic ordering deceives the reader into the truth because an illusion cannot be destroyed directly

Maieutic ordering is a device that works at the scale of a whole corpus rather than a single sentence. Kierkegaard sequences the authorship so it meets the reader inside his aesthetic illusion — beginning where the reader already stands, as in *Either/Or* — and moves him toward the religious, the signed upbuilding discourses walking beside the pseudonymous works; he calls this "deceiving into the truth," on the principle that an illusion can never be destroyed directly but only approached from within. The device blocks the defensive resistance a direct religious address provokes in someone who believes he is already there. Its cost is a covert asymmetry: the method requires the author to know the destination in advance and to withhold it, an arrangement Kierkegaard later named a demonic temptation. The finding rests on secondary commentary, *The Point of View* being unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
