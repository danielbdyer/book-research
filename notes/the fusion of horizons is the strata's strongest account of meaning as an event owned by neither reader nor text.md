# the fusion of horizons is the strata's strongest account of meaning as an event owned by neither reader nor text

Gadamer's fusion of horizons is the reader-response region's strongest account of meaning as an event between, and it arrives at the structure the book names the Third Entity from the side of reading. The text contributes its horizon, a tradition-laden claim and the question to which it stands as an answer; the reader contributes prejudices and fore-understanding, a historically formed consciousness. In the encounter neither remains unaffected, and what results belongs to neither party: the fused horizon is the subject-matter itself, a new context of meaning owned by the dialogue rather than by either side. Meaning is thereby an event between, not a transfer from text to reader — which is the book's own deepest claim, that two in genuine contact generate a third thing neither contains, reached here for the specific case of a reader with a text.

The correspondence is worth the book because it supplies a reading-native precedent for a doctrine the book otherwise states about two people in a room. It comes at a price the book must also inherit: if meaning is completed only within tradition, the model has no standpoint from which to tell a binding truth-claim from an inherited authority in its clothes, which is the fracture the next note holds.
