# the abridged edition compresses the opening poem rather than reproducing it

Both prose drafts place a poem at the heart of the first chapter, and the Arc and the Gloss treatment specifies that position as the chapter's peak and as a gate. The two drafts put different texts there. The mythic operating system edition prints "Time Is Not Linear" entire — roughly a hundred lines of verse, moving through ages thirty-three, fifty-four, forty-two, and back to thirty-two. The abridged edition prints five lines of prose at the same position, keeping the title's proposition and the chapter's three images and discarding the biographical sequence.

The compression is a worked instance of a treatment the corpus records as pending. The atlas's first assessment tension holds that the book's prose recruits an appreciating posture, so that the reader collects sentences while the practices go undone, and it names the treatment under trial Plain Water: stretches of deliberately plain prose. The abridged edition applies that treatment to the book's most lyrical single element, at its most exposed position, and the reduced version is the one that carries the chapter's argument rather than the poet's history. The vault records this as evidence that the treatment has been trialled once, at a scale large enough to judge.

## Substantiation

- The full text runs at `mythic-os: page 9` under the heading "An Untitled Poem (Time Is Not Linear)," opening "When I finally learned how to be, / I was thirty three" and closing "soon I'll be thirty two."
- The compressed text is the whole of the poem's appearance in the abridged edition: "Time is not linear. It loops. It folds. It arrives in the shape of an afternoon you thought you had forgotten and turns out to have been keeping the whole time like a candle in a drawer. / I did not become this person. I was always this person. I was only standing in the wrong room. The room had a low ceiling. I made myself small. Now there is no ceiling and I am still here, and the smallness has nowhere to go, and it falls off in pieces, and the pieces feed the orchard, and the orchard does not ask if it is good fruit. / It is only the fruit. That is what fruit does" (abridged ch1).
- The position is specified in the treatment as load-bearing: "Embed the first poem — 'Time Is Not Linear' — at the chapter's heart as its peak: the book's first psychedelic moment, eight pages in; also a gate (readers who can't receive the poem may not be ready, and that's okay)" (arc-gloss: ch1). The same passage adds a reading of why the position works, which the compression does not disturb: the poem "arrives too fast and too whole for the analytical reader to prepare a stance."
- The treatment the compression instances is recorded at atlas §XI.1a and carried in this vault at [[beauty recruits the wrong posture]].
- The compressed version keeps the chapter's three images — the low-ceilinged room, the smallness falling off, the orchard — and so does the work the treatment names as the poem's job; what it drops is the material that made the poem a life rather than an argument.
