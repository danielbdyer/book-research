# writing to facilitators turns the one-chair reading into training for the second chair

The reframe splits the work and grounds Book I in tending conditions for the reader's own facilitation of recognition. Book I is addressed to the facilitator, and its endpoint is a craft the reader is being brought into: the facilitator "becomes a steward of conditions," able to say "I helped create a field in which something became possible" rather than that they caused a change (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, line 16020), and the facilitator "succeeds by increasing the other's capacity to facilitate themselves" (lines 16038–16050). When the book is written to a facilitator the solitary reading is re-posed. The reader in one chair is positioned as a facilitator-in-training, rehearsing the moves the book describes — stewarding conditions ([[facilitation is stewardship of conditions]]), tending a field that "does work" ([[the field between two people does work]]), and disappearing into the other's grown capacity ([[the facilitator disappears, success is increasing the other's capacity to facilitate themselves]]). So the one-chair reading takes on a purpose it did not have as a mere solitary read, becoming training for the second chair, the capacity to hold a field for another.

This re-nests the book's structural wound rather than dissolving it. The corpus's hardest tension about the medium stands, since a printed book read alone cannot furnish the live second person its deepest claim requires ([[a codex is a one-chair technology making a two-chair argument]]), and writing to facilitators supplies no partner and does not claim to. What the address changes is what the empty chair is for. Under the old frame the missing chair was the reading's defect; under the new ground it is the reading's subject — the very seat the facilitator-reader is being trained to occupy. The tension names what the medium lacks, this note names what the one-chair reading is for, and the two run together without either being softened.
