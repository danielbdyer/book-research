# ignatius of loyola

Ignatius of Loyola's Spiritual Exercises (published 1548) are, in the space-around survey's judgment, the closest historical precedent for the book's entire design — and the project has never named them. He is not yet seated; this note is the candidate file.

The parallels are specific. The Exercises are a book that is not a book but a staged experience; organized in phased weeks rather than chapters; paced to a deliberate arc of consolation and desolation; and full of thresholds at which the reader must stop and do — compose the place, engage the senses, examine the movements of the soul. Most pointed of all: Ignatius concluded the Exercises could not complete alone and designed them to be undergone with a companion — a sixteenth-century solution to the exact problem the book registers as its one-chair tension, solved by building the second chair into the form. The Exercises' rules for discernment of spirits — telling which inner movements to trust — are also a documented ancestor of the book's somatic verification concern, with four centuries of field notes.

The survey's summary: the Exercises prove the performing book is a genre with a long safety record. What the book would take from the precedent is craft-intelligence, not doctrine.

## What the evaluation requires

Read the Exercises' structure (the weeks, the gates, the director's role); map each parallel against the book's corresponding device; note where the precedent's failures are documented; recommend a seat (the reference lineage is the likely fit). This work has not been done.
