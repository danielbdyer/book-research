# ignatius of loyola

Ignatius of Loyola's Spiritual Exercises (published 1548) are, in the space-around survey's judgment, the closest historical precedent for the book's entire design, and the survey notes the project had never named them. He is not yet seated; this note is the candidate file.

The parallels are specific, and the survey states them in its own words: the Exercises are "a book that is not a book but a staged experience; organized in phased weeks rather than chapters; paced to a deliberate arc of consolation and desolation; full of thresholds where the reader must stop and do" (space-around: around how it's built). The most pointed parallel, also quoted: the Exercises were "designed to be undergone with a companion, because Ignatius concluded it could not complete alone" — a sixteenth-century solution to the exact problem the book registers as its one-chair tension. The Exercises' rules for discernment of spirits — telling which inner movements to trust — are also a documented ancestor of the book's somatic verification concern, with four centuries of field notes.

The survey's summary, quoted: "the Exercises are proof that the performing-book is not an experiment but a genre with a long safety record" (space-around: around how it's built). What the book would take from the precedent is craft knowledge rather than doctrine.

## What the evaluation requires

Read the Exercises' structure (the weeks, the gates, the director's role); map each parallel against the book's corresponding device; note where the precedent's failures are documented; recommend a seat (the reference lineage is the likely fit). This work has not been done.
