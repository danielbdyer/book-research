# kierkegaard departs from the socratic occasion that the book's facilitation model rests on

The book's facilitator arranges conditions under which a reader may recognize something and claims no more, which is the Socratic occasion restated. In *Philosophical Fragments* Kierkegaard reconstructs the Socratic account: because learning is recollection, the learner already has the truth, so the teacher collapses to an occasion — a prompt that triggers the learner's own recollecting — and is in principle replaceable and forgettable, a midwife who wants no adherents and takes nothing for himself. This is the classical statement of facilitation as an epistemic position. Kierkegaard does not refine it; he exits it. His counter-hypothesis is that the learner may lack not only the truth but the very condition for recognizing it, in which case a mere occasion is not enough and the teacher must supply both the truth and the condition, which no human teacher can do. The fault line matters to the book: its facilitator-as-occasion model is Socratic, and the tradition's sharpest thinker on the medium stands on the far side of the line, holding that where the decisive help is needed no facilitator can give it. The dig reports this sharp and unresolved; the finding rests on secondary commentary, *Fragments* being unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.

## Substantiation

- Dig 1 reconstructs the occasion in ten steps from Storm quoting *Philosophical Fragments* chapter one (Hong p. 14) [S], including that "the teacher is only an occasion, whoever he may be, even if he is a god" (transmission dig 1, 2026-08-13, F4).
- The dig states the departure as a position, not a refinement: the occasion-teacher "is the exact classical statement of facilitation as an epistemic position," and Kierkegaard's alternative "needs an agent who can install what the learner structurally lacks, which no human facilitator can be" (transmission dig 1, 2026-08-13, F5, marked [I]). *Fragments* chapter one is named in dig 1's readings-to-queue, with the question whether the condition bounds the facilitation model or destroys it.
