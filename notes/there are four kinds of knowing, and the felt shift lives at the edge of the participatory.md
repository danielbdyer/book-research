# there are four kinds of knowing, and the felt shift lives at the edge of the participatory

Four kinds of knowing are distinguished in the reframe conversation, drawn from John Vervaeke and treated as irreducible rather than as one knowing in degrees: propositional (knowing that), procedural (knowing how), perspectival (knowing what is salient here and now), and participatory (knowing through being involved in and co-identifying with an arena). The book's own verification standard, the felt shift, is proposed as an instance of the fourth kind — the bodily arrival of a not-yet-worded knowing is participatory knowing at its threshold, not a proposition accepted. The reading of Vervaeke confirms the taxonomy and its ordering but declines the identity: participatory knowing is a sub-personal, identity-level coupling, not a bodily register, so the felt shift is at most one experiential signature at the perspectival–participatory boundary rather than participatory knowing itself. Held as an isomorph, the note shows the four kinds from Vervaeke's own account and keeps the felt-shift placement as the vault's bridge rather than a certified equation.

## Substantiation

The four knowings, in the conversation's words: "He distinguishes four kinds of knowing: propositional - knowing that; procedural - knowing how; perspectival - knowing what is salient / relevant here and now; participatory - knowing through being involved in and co-identifying with an arena" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, line 2086). The conversation later names participatory knowing as the bridge to the book's instrument: "This is a natural bridge to Vervaeke and participatory knowing. There are different ways of knowing" (line 15352).

Verified at Vervaeke's own account (Grade B — the four-P taxonomy is in Vervaeke & Ferraro 2013 and the *Awakening from the Meaning Crisis* lectures, not in the 2012 relevance-realization paper): the four kinds are irreducible and ordered, "the propositional depends on the procedural, which then depends on the perspectival, which then depends on, is grounded in the participatory" (AMC Ep 30), participatory knowing being the deepest — a genuine agent-arena co-constitution ("reciprocal realization," "co-identification"), not a loose notion. So the propositional-versus-participatory boundary is a real, precisely-defined construct, and Vervaeke is an independent arrival at "you are constituted by the arena of knowing" from naturalized cognitive science.

The book's felt shift, however, only rhymes with participatory knowing rather than being it. Participatory knowing is a sub-personal, identity-level coupling exemplified by flow and conversion, and Vervaeke never identifies it with a somatic felt sense; the book's felt shift is the bodily countersignature that nothing enters the lattice unmarked ([[nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned]]) and the step that changes what can be said next ([[a genuine felt shift changes what can be said next]]). The felt shift is therefore at most one experiential signature at the perspectival–participatory boundary, and the note deliberately does not certify that it and participatory knowing are the same construct — asserting identity would collapse a narrow somatic marker into a broad structural layer.

One qualification the reading adds bears on the convergence's independence: Vervaeke roots participatory knowing in Heidegger ("from Heidegger... at the core of our being in the world... a foundational kind of caring," AMC Ep 32), so unlike von Foerster, Maturana and Varela, Garfinkel, or Wittgenstein he is not a witness free of debt to the book's founding pole. He is company for the participation law, not an arrival from outside the lineage.
