# there are four kinds of knowing, and the felt shift lives at the edge of the participatory

Four kinds of knowing are distinguished in the reframe conversation, drawn from John Vervaeke and treated as irreducible rather than as one knowing in degrees: propositional (knowing that), procedural (knowing how), perspectival (knowing what is salient here and now), and participatory (knowing through being involved in and co-identifying with an arena). The book's own verification standard, the felt shift, is proposed as an instance of the fourth kind — the bodily arrival of a not-yet-worded knowing is participatory knowing at its threshold, not a proposition accepted. The correspondence is asserted within the conversation rather than confirmed by a reading; Vervaeke's work is unread in this vault, so the claim is a seed. Held as an isomorph, it shows one side from the conversation and names the other from the book's existing felt-shift material.

## Substantiation

The four knowings, in the conversation's words: "He distinguishes four kinds of knowing: propositional - knowing that; procedural - knowing how; perspectival - knowing what is salient / relevant here and now; participatory - knowing through being involved in and co-identifying with an arena" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, line 2086). The conversation later names participatory knowing as the bridge to the book's instrument: "This is a natural bridge to Vervaeke and participatory knowing. There are different ways of knowing" (line 15352).

The book's side is named rather than quoted from Vervaeke, from the vault's own account of the felt shift: the standard that nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned, where an accurate word arrives as a bodily loosening rather than as an inference ([[nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned]]). The felt shift's defining test — a genuine shift is a step that changes what can be said next — is that participatory threshold stated clinically ([[a genuine felt shift changes what can be said next]]). The correspondence rests on the conversation's characterization of Vervaeke, whose primary work is unread here, so the note does not certify that the book's felt shift and Vervaeke's participatory knowing are the same construct.
