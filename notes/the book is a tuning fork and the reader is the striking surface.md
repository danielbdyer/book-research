# the book is a tuning fork and the reader is the striking surface

The book's image for itself is a tuning fork. The first-telling states it directly: "The book is a tuning fork. Silent on its own, forever — a tuning fork cannot play itself. It only sounds when struck against something. You are the striking surface" (first-telling: the honesty). The first-telling also records the placement and its stated purpose — the image is installed on page one "so that the ending lands as a promise kept rather than a twist" — and the image governs two practical questions: the ending (the final line is written to be answerable, an invitation to strike) and publication (the corpus attaches Lewis Hyde's gift economics, on which an instrument of this kind has its existence only while it is in use).

The corpus's image system pairs each image with a concept and an antecedent. This one carries Hopkins inside it: instress, the strike a particular thing's form makes on a beholder. Hopkins stands in the middle of a three-step lineage the corpus records for thisness, from Scotus to Deleuze, and the lineage is held at [[thisness is the grain of the container anatomy]].

## Substantiation

- Atlas §X.5h records the full pairing: the image, the book itself as its concept, and the Hopkins and Hyde connections.
- First-telling, "The honesty," states the image's function: deliberately humble, installed early, closing with the reader identified as the striking surface.
- The space-around survey identifies Hartmut Rosa's sociology of resonance as a completed external theory for this image, including the clause that resonance cannot be guaranteed.
- The same no-guarantee clause is stated by both of the book's own foundations, independently of each other and of Rosa: neither Heidegger nor the valley permits the epiphany to be engineered ([[both poles of the convergence refuse the engineered epiphany]]). That gives this image the strongest available warrant for its humility, written for it by the traditions the book stands on.
