# the tuesday is the temple

The working documents name this the Householder Axiom: the book asks for no renunciation, no retreat, no leaving of job or kitchen — ordinary life is the full site of the work. The corpus reports finding the axiom already implicit in the book's first image before it was articulated: the House with No Ceiling is open to the stars and also has a floor, walls, a door, and a kitchen.

The corpus cites a fourteenth-century precedent: Meister Eckhart's Sermon 86 ranks Martha, busy among the pots, above Mary rapt at the feet of Christ. It also keeps the axiom under pressure by housing its strongest opponent: Simone Weil's renunciate position is seated inside the book deliberately, and that arrangement has its own note, [[weil is seated as the opponent the plumbing deserves]].

## Substantiation

- Walked I states the axiom ("the Tuesday is the temple; no renunciation is required; the grid stays on") and records the first-image observation.
- First-telling, "The honesty," restates it for the reader: nothing in the book asks for the departure from job, kitchen, or life.
- Atlas §IX.8a records the Eckhart precedent, with the biographical note that he was tried for heresy and died before the verdict.
