# jakugo answers a passed koan by demonstrating with a quotation under a teacher's checking

The Rinzai school built a use of quotation that recruits enactment rather than collection. After a student passes a koan and its *sassho* — the teacher's checking questions — the student must find a *jakugo*, a capping phrase drawn from an approved phrase-book, that demonstrates the insight rather than explains it. The answer is given *with* a quotation, offered as proof of a state rather than as decoration or paraphrase, and a teacher who can reject it stands over the exchange. The move re-domesticates exactly the behavior an earlier ancestor of the tradition had treated as poison: collecting the capping phrases as literary performance was the danger named in the destruction of the Blue Cliff Record, and here the same quotation is put back to work as a supervised demonstration.

The device is the closest thing the margin traditions offer to a second voice the reader must answer by demonstrating. Its cost is precise and structural: it is load-bearing on the checker. The dig records that without the teacher who can reject the phrase, the *jakugo* decays into a memorized phrase-book answer — the collectible the whole apparatus was built to prevent. A book read alone has no checker, so the one margin device that recruits enactment reverts, ported to solitude, to the connoisseurship it was designed against.
