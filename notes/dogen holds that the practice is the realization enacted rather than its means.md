# dogen holds that the practice is the realization enacted rather than its means

The book's practice curriculum runs under one clause: a practice expresses a realization already present rather than causing it, so an exercise is not a means to somewhere ([[the practice is the expression of realization rather than a means to it]]). That clause currently rests on Dōgen alone, and the corpus states it in his voice — *shusho-itto*, practice and realization are one; zazen is not a means to enlightenment but its expression. This is structurally the situation the verification standard was in with Gendlin: the whole weight of a load-bearing commitment sits on one name that the vault has never read at his own work ([[the verification standard rests on one name and cannot be defended by company]]). The high-leverage move is the Gendlin move — read him at his primary text and let the author decide his seat. The reading is not done, so the claim is privated.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record, quoted in Dōgen's voice: "practice and realization are one; zazen is not a means to enlightenment but its expression… you do not do the practice to get somewhere; the doing is the somewhere" (walked IX), cross-referenced into the curriculum as a constraint on every exercise (atlas §IX.8d).
- Dōgen's own half is named, not shown: *shusho-itto* and the critique of "awaiting realization" (*taigo*) are in *Bendowa*, and the oneness of practice and verification is in *Genjokoan*. Both are to verify at the text; neither is quoted from memory. He holds a place in the reference-lineage roster with no interlocutor note, no load, and no reading — the seating stays the author's.
- The refusal is kept. Dōgen's *hongaku*, original enlightenment with nothing to attain, cuts against the book's arc-toward-a-peak and its promise of transformation, an internal contradiction the vault already tracks; the book's own lineage sharpens the same edge, since Zen's makyo instruction refuses even a genuine experience as self-certifying ([[zen's makyo instruction refuses the self-certifying experience]]).
