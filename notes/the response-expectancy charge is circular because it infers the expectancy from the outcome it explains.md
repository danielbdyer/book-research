# the response-expectancy charge is circular because it infers the expectancy from the outcome it explains

The strongest single mechanism in the compliance attack — Kirsch's response expectancy — carries its own methodological weakness, which is circularity. The explanatory move runs: the reader had a felt shift because the reader expected one. But the expectancy is typically inferred from the very outcome it is invoked to explain, since the anticipation is not independently measured before the shift and then shown to produce it. Where the expectancy is read off the result, the account can be fitted to any outcome and predicts none, which is the same over-reach that weakens Orne's construct from a different direction. The charge does real work as a candidate mechanism and none as a proof, until the expectancy is measured in advance of the experience it is said to generate.

The weakness is partial rather than total, and the note keeps the boundary. If a measured, prior expectancy is shown to produce a genuine physiological or behavioral change — which is what response-expectancy theory in its strong form claims — then two things follow at once: the mechanism is real, and "produced by expectancy" no longer entails "only an artifact," because the change it produces is genuine. So the circularity charge and the reality of the effect pull in opposite directions, and both are held.

This note carries the circularity charge as its subject.
- The cuts-both-ways half — that expectancy effects are real change — is homed to the counterfeit tension and is used here only as the boundary of the circularity charge, not re-argued ([[felt shift versus felt confirmation]]).
