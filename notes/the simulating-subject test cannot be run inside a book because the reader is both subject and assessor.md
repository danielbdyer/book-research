# the simulating-subject test cannot be run inside a book because the reader is both subject and assessor

Orne's signature quasi-control is the simulating-subject design: instructed fakers are passed before a blind assessor, and if the fake is indistinguishable from the real, the treatment is unproven. The device is a bar rather than a treatment — it does not produce an effect, it tests whether a reported effect can be told from a produced-on-demand imitation of one. Its structural requirement is a separation of roles: a subject who may be faking, and an assessor blind to which is which. That separation is exactly what a book read alone cannot supply, because the reader occupies both positions at once — the one undergoing the shift and the one judging whether it is real — so there is no blind party for whom the fake and the genuine article could be told apart. The finding is not that a book fails the test but that a book cannot run it; the portability of the simulating-subject design to a solitary reader is nil.

The consequence is directional. The device's absence does not clear the felt shift; it shows why the felt shift's discrimination cannot be internalized, and it points the same way the felt-shift audit points — toward a second person who can hold the assessor's role the reader cannot hold alone.

## Substantiation

- The dig records the device and its portability verdict in one line: the simulating-subject test passes "fakers past a blind assessor; indistinguishability = treatment unproven," with "portability to a book: nil — the reader is always both subject and assessor" (transmission dig 6: DEVICES). This note carries the mechanism and the verdict.
- The depth is thin and stated: the quasi-control sections of Orne 1962 were not read at the primary in dig 6, so the note is marked `nascent`. The queued reading asks whether demand produces altered *experience* or only compliant *behavior* (dig 6, READINGS TO QUEUE item 1); if only behavior, the felt shift needs Kirsch's mechanism and the two attacks are not interchangeable.
- The device belongs to the compliance case as its methodological bar: any felt-shift claim clears it before counting as evidence, and a book can never run its own blind control, which is why the case routes to an out-of-room check rather than to a better internal one.
- This is a claim about what a book cannot do, not a claim that a partnered reading would satisfy the bar; whether a designed second voice or a live partner can hold the assessor's role is the open half of the second-chair treatment.
