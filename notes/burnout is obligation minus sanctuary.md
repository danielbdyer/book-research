# burnout is obligation minus sanctuary

The book analyzes burnout as Obligation ⊖ Sanctuary, a privation: the obligation is intact, and what is missing is the place where the obligated person is held and restored. The formula reverses the standard prescription. If burnout were excess obligation, the cure would be less work; because it is unhoused obligation, the cure is the restoration of sanctuary, and reducing obligation without restoring sanctuary leaves the structure of the suffering intact.

The corpus scales the formula upward once: it cites Byung-Chul Han's diagnosis of the burnout society as this same privation at civilizational scale.

## Substantiation

- First-telling, "The offering": "Burnout is not 'too much work.' It's duty with the holding place removed... the cure isn't necessarily less obligation. It's the restoration of sanctuary."
- Atlas §IV lists the formula first among the canonical examples; §IX.8f records the Han connection.
- Sanctuary itself is one of the book's named molecules (atlas §IV lists it in the cluster of holding-postures). It does not yet have its own note; extracting the molecule catalogue is listed as pending work in `ops/queue/extractions.md`.
- The abridged edition decomposes both terms of the formula one level further, into the primitives: "Sanctuary = Contain + Maintain + Release. A space that holds you, lasts, and lets you let down," and "Obligation = Commit + Attend + Maintain. A duty you have taken on, are attending to, and are sustaining" (abridged ch6). Read together, the two compounds share Maintain, and what burnout removes is the pairing of Contain with Release — the capacity to hold and then to set down. The same chapter states the structural reason obligation cannot repair itself: "Obligation has a structural problem: it spends what it does not replenish."
