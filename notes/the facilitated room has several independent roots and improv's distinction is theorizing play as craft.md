# the facilitated room has several independent roots and improv's distinction is theorizing play as craft

The dig opens by flagging an overstatement it declines to validate: naming Spolin's improv the actual historical root of the facilitated room is too strong, because the room has roots of equal or earlier standing. J. L. Moreno's psychodrama and spontaneity training coined the terms spontaneity and warm-up before Spolin used them; Kurt Lewin's T-groups built the training group whose subject is itself; and Freirean adult education reached the facilitated room from a separate direction. Spolin-by-way-of-Boyd is one lineage among these, and its distinction is not primogeniture but the theorizing of play as a teachable craft with named devices.

The finding corrects a lineage claim the book might otherwise make and names the ancestor the play roster omitted. Moreno is, on the dig's reading, arguably the closest direct ancestor of the facilitated room and the coiner of its core term, and his absence means the region's own genealogy was returned incomplete. The corrective bears on how the book narrates where its facilitation craft comes from: the improv lineage is a real and richly worked source, but it is a source among several rather than the origin.

- Transmission dig 7 (2026-08-13), premise flag, records the several roots and states improv's distinction as theorizing play as craft rather than primogeniture; the "Absent stratum" note names Moreno as the omitted ancestor. The depth is [I]: the multiple-roots claim and the reading of Spolin's distinction are inference; Moreno's priority on the terms is stated from scholarly knowledge.
- A scoping read of Moreno's Who Shall Survive? and Psychodrama Vol. I is queued in `ops/reading queue.md` (dig 7, "Absent stratum") to test whether psychodrama is the direct ancestor and to recover the coinage.
- Two of the roots are now read at their primary texts. Lewin's T-group seed is in his own 1946 words — the "tremendous pedagogical effect which these evaluation meetings... had on the training process," the group examining its own recorded dynamics (Lewin 1946, p. 41) — though "the self-studying group was his invention" overstates it: Lewin convened and documented the 1946 New Britain workshop, and his associates (Bradford, Lippitt, Benne) carried the T-group forward as a formalized method and founded the National Training Laboratories after his death in February 1947. Johnstone's improv-as-craft distinction is read at *Impro* (status transactions, "be obvious," the offer-grammar — play theorized into teachable, named devices). The coinages "spontaneity" and "warm-up" stay attributed to Moreno, who remains the note's one unread root and, on the dig's reading, its closest direct ancestor.
