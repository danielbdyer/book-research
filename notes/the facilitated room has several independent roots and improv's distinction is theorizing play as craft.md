# the facilitated room has several independent roots and improv's distinction is theorizing play as craft

The dig opens by flagging an overstatement it declines to validate: naming Spolin's improv the actual historical root of the facilitated room is too strong, because the room has roots of equal or earlier standing. J. L. Moreno's psychodrama had Spontaneity Training and the warming-up process in print as named methods by 1934, before Spolin used the words; Kurt Lewin's T-groups built the training group whose subject is itself; and Freirean adult education reached the facilitated room from a separate direction. Spolin-by-way-of-Boyd is one lineage among these, and its distinction is not primogeniture but the theorizing of play as a teachable craft with named devices.

The finding corrects a lineage claim the book might otherwise make and names the ancestor the play roster omitted. Moreno is, on the dig's reading, arguably the closest direct ancestor of the facilitated room and the coiner of its core term, and his absence means the region's own genealogy was returned incomplete. The corrective bears on how the book narrates where its facilitation craft comes from: the improv lineage is a real and richly worked source, but it is a source among several rather than the origin.

## Substantiation

- Transmission dig 7 (2026-08-13), premise flag, records the several roots and states improv's distinction as theorizing play as craft rather than primogeniture; the "Absent stratum" note names Moreno as the omitted ancestor. The depth is [I]: the multiple-roots claim and the reading of Spolin's distinction are inference; Moreno's priority on the terms is stated from scholarly knowledge.
- **Moreno is read at two primary texts (2026-09-02), and the coinage clause corrects.** He did not coin "spontaneity" or "warm up," which are ordinary English; what he coined, and had in print by 1934, is the technical apparatus — "Spontaneity Training" as a chapter title and a named method ("To accomplish this aim we have developed techniques for training spontaneity," *Who Shall Survive?* 1934, pp. 321–22; "to train and develop man to the habit of spontaneity," p. 324) and the warming-up process as a mechanism with measured physiological indicators ("To act means to warm up to a state of feeling, to an 'Impromptu' state… altered breathing rate, gasping, crying, smiling, clenching the teeth," pp. 193–95). The priority over Spolin holds and is now verified. The famous definition — spontaneity "propels the individual towards an adequate response to a new situation or a new response to an old situation" — belongs to the 1953 third edition, absent from 1934, and is held at Grade B.
- **He is the ancestor on the enactment and not on the facilitator.** The enactment is the room's shape: "whereas conduct in a life situation is irrevocable, here every phase of performance is open to correction" (1934, p. 325); "the individual craves to embody far more roles than those he is allowed to act out in life" (p. 326). But his director is an active shaper and content-inserter — the auxiliary ego "provides the director with the opportunity to interpolate specific attitudes and behavior patterns" (symposium, 1945, pp. 458–59) — where the book's held container withholds, so on that axis Lewin's self-studying group and Johnstone's offer-grammar are nearer the book than Moreno is; only sociodrama is exempted from "a mighty dictator-director." And his 1945 statement of the group as therapeutic agent is the closest outside statement of the participation law from a named originator, a year before Lewin's T-group: "It is the group itself that becomes the therapeutic agent as a result of the interaction between the individuals who form the group" (pp. 288–89); "The role of the healer has changed from the owner and actor of therapy to its assigner and trustee" (pp. 316–17) ([[nothing transforms from the audience]]; [[transformation is enacted participation inside a held container]]).
- **What he would refuse is the book itself, by name, in his glossary.** "Cultural Conserve. The technical conservation of cultural values, as the book or the film, which substitutes and preserves man's creative expressions" (1934, Glossary); the drive toward "the illusion of the finished, perfected product whose assumed perfectibility was an excuse par excellence for forsaking its past" (pp. 363–64); and his chosen case, the Dionysian improvisations that "ended in the written drama; this was its natural fate… the creative process was conserved forever, and so then the unnecessary attempt at spontaneity could die out" (p. 338), documented from inside his own room, where improvisers left to themselves "ended in stereotypes" (p. 338). It is a refusal of the book's form rather than its content — a book is the genre of the thing refused — and it belongs beside the letter-genre finding and the kept counterface of the container where a word can be perfected forever.
- Two of the roots are now read at their primary texts. Lewin's T-group seed is in his own 1946 words — the "tremendous pedagogical effect which these evaluation meetings... had on the training process," the group examining its own recorded dynamics (Lewin 1946, p. 41) — though "the self-studying group was his invention" overstates it: Lewin convened and documented the 1946 New Britain workshop, and his associates (Bradford, Lippitt, Benne) carried the T-group forward as a formalized method and founded the National Training Laboratories after his death in February 1947. Johnstone's improv-as-craft distinction is read at *Impro* (status transactions, "be obvious," the offer-grammar — play theorized into teachable, named devices). Moreno's apparatus — Spontaneity Training and the warming-up process, not the ordinary words — is now read at its 1934 text, which completes the note's three roots.
