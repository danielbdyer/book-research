# the facilitated room has several independent roots and improv's distinction is theorizing play as craft

The dig opens by flagging an overstatement it declines to validate: naming Spolin's improv the actual historical root of the facilitated room is too strong, because the room has roots of equal or earlier standing. J. L. Moreno's psychodrama and spontaneity training coined the terms spontaneity and warm-up before Spolin used them; Kurt Lewin's T-groups built the training group whose subject is itself; and Freirean adult education reached the facilitated room from a separate direction. Spolin-by-way-of-Boyd is one lineage among these, and its distinction is not primogeniture but the theorizing of play as a teachable craft with named devices.

The finding corrects a lineage claim the book might otherwise make and names the ancestor the play roster omitted. Moreno is, on the dig's reading, arguably the closest direct ancestor of the facilitated room and the coiner of its core term, and his absence means the region's own genealogy was returned incomplete. The corrective bears on how the book narrates where its facilitation craft comes from: the improv lineage is a real and richly worked source, but it is a source among several rather than the origin.
