# knowing oneself is not identical to being in relationship with oneself

The reframe conversation distinguishes knowing oneself from being in relationship with oneself and denies that the two are the same. Knowing oneself is the possession of accurate information about oneself — one's patterns, history, and tendencies — held as an object of description. Being in relationship with oneself is an ongoing participatory contact, in which a person meets what is present in themselves rather than cataloguing it, and that contact can deepen or fail independently of how much information the person holds. The claim asserts that a person can know a great deal about themselves and be in almost no relationship with themselves, and that self-facilitation cultivates the relationship rather than the information. This continues the book's larger position that understanding which moves nothing is not the operative thing, recorded where the book opens from a surplus of insight rather than a lack of it at [[the book opens from a surplus of insight rather than a lack of it]], and it locates self-facilitation on the relational side of that divide.
