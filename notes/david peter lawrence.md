# david peter lawrence

David Peter Lawrence's *Rediscovering God with Transcendental Argument* (SUNY, 1999) carries the recognition philosophy into Western argumentative form, and his reading of the tradition supplies this book its strongest outside precedent: the Pratyabhijñā discourse is "both philosophical apologetics and spiritual exercise" — the treatise itself is a practice, argument as the vehicle of the recognition it argues for. The recognition school's own founding texts, on Lawrence's reading, already work the way this book means to work: the form enacts the content. The book's governing law has a millennium-old precedent in the very tradition beneath it.

The refusal kept: Lawrence's own project runs the crossover as proof — the system deployed to demonstrate the reality of God against relativism and deconstruction. That is an evidentiary ambition the book's stance explicitly does not share, and his work shows concretely what the crossover looks like when it is run as demonstration: the road the author considered and declined. Keeping him is keeping the fork visible.
