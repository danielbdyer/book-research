# delegation can deepen participation or destroy it

Delegation can deepen participation or destroy it depending on which part of an activity it takes, and the conversation locates it as the middle term between two poles. Consumption is the posture in which something happens to a person; participation is the posture in which the person is involved in what is happening. Delegation sits between them and resolves in either direction, since a tool that handles the mechanical part so the person can do the meaningful part increases participation, while a tool that does the meaningful part and leaves the person only to approve the result destroys it. The conversation marks the distinction as decisive for artificial intelligence, because the same act of handing work to a machine can increase or destroy a person's involvement according to which part is handed over.

## Substantiation

- The conversation defines the poles plainly: consumption "says: Something happens to me," participation "says: I am involved in what is happening," and "delegation sits somewhere between them" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: participation versus consumption).
- It gives delegation's two outcomes as a contrast in its own words, that delegation can increase participation — "This tool handles the mechanical part so I can do the meaningful part" — or destroy it — "The tool does the meaningful part and I merely approve the result" — and adds that "That distinction is enormous for AI."
