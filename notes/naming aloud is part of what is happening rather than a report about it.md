# naming aloud is part of what is happening rather than a report about it

The book's speech doctrine has a manuscript-scale form the vault already holds — a word spoken can be answered, a word withheld can only be perfected — and a session-scale form no note carried. The first telling states it inside the practice section: "Naming aloud isn't a report about what's happening. It's part of what's happening… A pattern that gets named becomes workable. An unnamed pattern runs the room" (first-telling: the practice). The outward survey calls it "the practice section's boldest-sounding line" and supplies its laboratory neighbor: Lieberman's affect-labeling result (naming a present feeling diminishes amygdala response — *Psychological Science* 18(5), 2007, verified at web depth 2026-08-06). One seam is drawn where this evidence lives twice: the same citation counters the formulation-as-resistance convergence, and the distinction that reconciles the two — labeling what is present now helps, the explanatory account of why can defend — is recorded at [[chairs that agree on nothing else agree that the formulation can be the resistance]].

The bridge matters because the two scales share one mechanism. The manuscript's unreleased state and the reader's unnamed pattern are the same structure at different sizes: an articulate interior that has not entered the field where it can be answered, and therefore has not yet done what speech does.
