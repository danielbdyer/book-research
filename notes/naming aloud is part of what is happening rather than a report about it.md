# naming aloud is part of what is happening rather than a report about it

The book's speech doctrine has a manuscript-scale form the vault already holds — a word spoken can be answered, a word withheld can only be perfected — and a session-scale form no note carried. The first telling states it inside the practice section: "Naming aloud isn't a report about what's happening. It's part of what's happening… A pattern that gets named becomes workable. An unnamed pattern runs the room" (first-telling: the practice). The outward survey calls it "the practice section's boldest-sounding line" and supplies its laboratory neighbor: Lieberman's affect-labeling studies, still unevaluated on the shelf.

The bridge matters because the two scales share one mechanism. The manuscript's unreleased state and the reader's unnamed pattern are the same structure at different sizes: an articulate interior that has not entered the field where it can be answered, and therefore has not yet done what speech does.
