# gendlin's focusing protocol already guards against the counterfeit shift

The book's verification standard borrows Gendlin's felt shift, and it turns out the counterfeit problem is not news to the source: the Focusing protocol carries explicit guards against it, written as instructions. The resonating step demands an actual re-contact rather than an endorsement: "To do this resonating, you must experience the felt sense again. You must touch it again as a feeling" — and if the feeling has vanished, "obviously you cannot check the words" (Focusing, fourth movement). The fifth movement rejects the counterfeit's signature tempo: "If you get a quick answer without a shift in the felt sense, just let that kind of answer go by," with the freshness requirement attached — "Make sure the quality is sensed again, freshly, vividly (not just remembered from before)." And the protocol extends verification across days: "check in with its felt edge, a number of times during the rest of the day, and perhaps for several days… 'Can I still feel that whole thing?… Ah, there it is. Anything new?'" (checking in). A fluency-shiver produced by a well-made sentence fails all three: it arrives fast, it cannot be re-contacted as a body-sense because it was never one, and it has nothing new when revisited.

The protocol even holds the doubt itself with instructions: "'Is this real? Maybe I'm fooling myself.'… They may be right, but they have to wait… It is time enough later to find out for sure if this step on the problem is real" (Focusing). Verification is deferred, not waived — the question is legitimate and its answer is downstream, in whether the step holds and carries further.

What this gives the writing: the book does not need to invent its defenses against its own fluency; it needs to *transmit its source's existing ones*. A reader taught the quick-answer rule, the freshness rule, and the checking-in habit has been armed against the book's own prose by the book's own lineage — which is the refusals-intact method applied to the instrument itself.

## Substantiation

- The resonating step's positive form: "Make sure they click precisely into place—a perfect fit. Ask (but don't answer): 'Is that right?' There should be a felt response, some deep breath inside, some felt release again," and the accompanying sentence that dissolves the check/change distinction: "The sense of rightness is not only a check of the handle. It is your body just now changing" (Focusing, fourth movement).
- A worked failed check exists in the source: "I waited for words and got 'out of place' and 'off,' but when I checked to see if they were right, they weren't—not quite" (Focusing, ch. 5) — the protocol returning a no, on the page.
- One honesty note: no explicit next-day re-resonance doctrine was found in the verified texts; the checking-in passages above, which extend over "several days," are the closest formulation, and the harder time-as-falsifier claim is held at that strength rather than pressed.
