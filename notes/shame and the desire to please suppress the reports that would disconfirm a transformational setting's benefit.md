# shame and the desire to please suppress the reports that would disconfirm a transformational setting's benefit

The compliance case's situation tier rests on a documented asymmetry: in settings that expect benefit, the report that would disconfirm the benefit is the report least likely to be spoken. A mindfulness sample found eighty-three percent reporting at least one side effect, while across roughly seven thousand studies fewer than one percent measured harms at all, and open-ended formats underestimate what harms occur — because participants stay hesitant to report negative reactions, out of shame or a desire to please the researcher or the instructor. The consequence for the book's verification is a logical one: where the setting suppresses the disconfirming report, the absence of such a report carries no evidential weight, so a reader who does not say the book failed is not thereby a reader for whom it worked. The felt-shift standard inherits this directly, because it asks the reader to report an inner result inside exactly the kind of high-demand frame the asymmetry describes — an invested reader, an admired author, cueing that a shift belongs here.

The base rate the asymmetry conceals is not trivial. High-demand transformational rooms carry a documented cost — roughly nineteen percent enduring casualties among encounter-group participants, extended to the large-group awareness trainings — which the suppression of negative report keeps out of view.

## Substantiation

- The dig records the field: Britton et al. found "83% of a mindfulness sample reported ≥1 side effect, yet across ~7,000 studies fewer than 1% measured harms," with participants "hesitant to report negative reactions… because of shame or a desire to please the researcher or instructor" (transmission dig 6: FINDINGS). The one short quotation this note carries is the shame-or-please clause, recorded at [S] with the primary unopened.
- The casualty base rate is the encounter-group finding of Lieberman, Yalom and Miles — sixteen enduring casualties among two hundred nine participants — carried at [S] from citation; it recurs in the remainder note as evidence demand cannot produce, and here as the cost the suppression hides.
- The depth is thin and stated: neither the Britton measurement study nor the LGAT record was read at the primary in dig 6, so the note is marked `nascent`. Whether reported harm rises as assessment demand falls is the queued reading (dig 6, READINGS TO QUEUE item 4; the taxonomy is queued at ops/reading queue.md item 17).
- The claim's logical core is one-directional and clean even before the readings land: a suppressed signal cannot be read as an absent one, so silence about failure is not evidence of success.
