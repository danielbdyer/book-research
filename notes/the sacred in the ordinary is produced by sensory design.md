# the sacred in the ordinary is produced by sensory design

The gesture asserts that the sacredness of ordinary life is produced rather than merely noticed, and that the production is sensory — light, texture, sound, the specific arrangement of a room. The vault holds the doctrine and not the production. The corpus's commitment to the ordinary is one of its strongest, and it is stated entirely as a matter of attention: the reader is to notice what is already there. This gesture claims the opposite direction of causation, that arrangement produces the noticing.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- The doctrine is recorded at [[the tuesday is the temple]] and rests on the corpus's householder axiom, transcendence with plumbing.
- The practice curriculum contains no material of this kind. The twelve kata are attentional and relational moves; none arranges an environment. Atlas §VII's four doors of practice include body, breath, posture, and effortful practice, and no door is the room.
- The one container-maker in the corpus's roster whose medium is physical space is Alexander, whose quality without a name is precisely a property of arrangement. The corpus uses him for the quality and not for the arranging.

## What would close it

Closing it means deciding whether the book asks the reader to notice their surroundings or to build them.
