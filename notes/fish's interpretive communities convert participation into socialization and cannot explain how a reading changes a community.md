# fish's interpretive communities convert participation into socialization and cannot explain how a reading changes a community

Fish holds that interpretive strategies precede and constitute the text, so meaning is produced by an interpretive community rather than by the text or the individual reader; it is the community that invests a text with meaning. Turned on the book's vocabulary, the thesis dissolves what the book relies on. Participation collapses into socialization into the community's prior strategies, since there is no text left to participate with independently; and the felt shift becomes a community artifact, a response the reader's formation produced rather than an event the reading occasioned. This is the compliance attack in sociological dress: the book's central safeguard is re-described as a group's shaping of what its members feel and find.

The position carries its own defeat, which is why the dig declines Fish and keeps him only as a reductio to refuse. If the community fully constitutes the text and the reader's responses, Fish cannot explain how a reading ever changes a community against its own strategies — how any genuinely new reading arises, or how transformation is possible at all. The thesis that would make the felt shift a mere artifact also makes change unaccountable, so it defeats the very possibility it is invoked to explain away.
