# every technology is evaluable by whether it increases or decreases capacity to participate

The claim proposes one evaluative question for any technology: does it increase or decrease the person's capacity to participate? The conversation immediately qualifies the test as not binary. A single technology may reduce friction at first, then increase capability, and eventually create dependency, so its effect on participation cannot be read at one moment; it must be read across the whole of a person's life and over time. The evaluation is therefore ecological and temporal rather than a one-time verdict. This test is the criterion the design ethic acts on, and the conversation applies it reflexively to the vault's own system, asking whether the system increases the author's capacity to participate in the work rather than whether the ontology is elegant. The claim is the conversation's model-assisted synthesis, held as candidate and kept nascent until the writing reaches for it.

## Substantiation

- The conversation states the test as a single question: "Every technology can be evaluated through a simple question" — "Does this increase my capacity to participate, or decrease it?" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: technology as a participation test).
- It qualifies the test in the same section: "This is not binary," because a technology may "initially reduce friction; then increase capability; but eventually create dependency," so "the question is ecological and temporal" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: technology as a participation test).
