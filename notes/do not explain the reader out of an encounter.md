# do not explain the reader out of an encounter

The reframe conversation places one constraint above the rest of the architecture: "Do not explain the reader out of an encounter." The rule holds that exposition can pre-empt the reader's own meeting with a thing, and that where an encounter is available it should come first — a concept understood only after long exposition may need an encounter before it; a tradition may be introduced through a lived question; a distinction may be experienced before it is named; a concept may be delayed until the reader has struggled with what makes it necessary. The purpose is to make the book's form consistent with its content: a book about facilitation should facilitate rather than describe, so the reader's encounter with the text is itself an occasion for the recognition the text is about.

The rule generalizes a collision the vault already holds at a single unit. At the peak, the law that the book performs what it describes could honor the shared refusal of the engineered epiphany only by withdrawing its apparatus. What was there a property of one chapter is here a constraint over every chapter: wherever explanation would stand in for the reader's own recognition, the explanation is withheld.

## Substantiation

- The constraint, stated as the most important one over both books: "Do not explain the reader out of an encounter."
- The four applications the conversation lists: if a concept needs twenty pages of exposition, "perhaps the reader needs an encounter first"; a tradition introduced "through a lived question"; a distinction "experienced before it is named"; a concept delayed until "the reader has struggled with something."
- The rationale: "This is how the form of the book becomes consistent with its philosophy," because the books "should not merely describe participation. They should facilitate it."
