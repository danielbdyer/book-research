# de jaegher and di paolo

Hanne De Jaegher and Ezequiel Di Paolo work in enactivist cognitive science, where cognition is treated as something a living system does in maintaining itself rather than as computation over representations. Their 2007 paper extends that to social encounters and makes a claim about what happens between two people that is unusually precise.

The interaction process itself, they argue, can "take on a form of autonomy" — it develops its own dynamics, resists disruption, and becomes "a level of organization not reducible, in general, to individual behaviours." Meaning is generated in the coordinated rhythm of the exchange rather than transmitted between two already-formed minds. Their spectrum runs from mere orientation of one person's sense-making by another, up to joint acts "that can only be completed socially" — handing someone an object being the plain example, since neither party performs it alone.

## Why the sort wants them

The book asserts a third thing: a field between two people that does work neither supplied ([[the field between two people does work]]). The vault has held that with a clinical bearer, and since 2026-08-05 with a proposed ontological one in Buber. It has had no mechanism. This is one, and it is stated in a register that can be argued with ([[the third thing has a mechanism in participatory sense-making]]).

They also pass the independence test that most of the company fails ([[the convergence's independence fails where three of its names share one teacher]]). Enactivism has phenomenological ancestry, so the independence is not absolute — Merleau-Ponty and Husserl are in its background, and Varela is already in the corpus's reference lineage. What it does not have is any route through Heidegger's account of disclosure or through the recognition tradition, which are the two poles the book's field is currently downstream of.

## What they would refuse

Derived rather than sourced; the 2007 paper has not been read in this vault.

They would refuse the third thing as an entity. Their irreducible level is a *process* — a pattern of coordination sustained in real time by two embodied agents — and it has no existence apart from the interacting. The book's language of a field that is present, that arrives, that does work, reads as a claim about something there; theirs is a claim about something happening. That is not a small difference and it is where a careless citation would sit.

They would refuse the extension to a reader alone with a book more sharply than any other name in the vault, because their account specifies the mechanism: coordination requires two coupled systems adjusting to each other in time. A text does not adjust. This is the fifth independent arrival at the same objection to the book's solitary form — after the Fourth Way, Sufi practice, collective trauma work, and Buber — and it is the only one that says exactly what is missing rather than that something is.

And they would resist the warmth. The synchrony literature the same argument draws on reports that coordination produces cooperation without positive emotion needing to be generated, which cuts against reading the relational field as fundamentally about feeling connected.

## What the evaluation still requires

The 2007 paper, with three questions: whether interactional autonomy is claimed as a strong ontological thesis or as a methodological level of description; how much of it survives asynchronous or mediated interaction, which is what a letter is; and whether their later work on linguistic bodies closes any of the gap between a real-time coupling account and a text that expects an answer.
