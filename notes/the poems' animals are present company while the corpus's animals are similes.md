# the poems' animals are present company while the corpus's animals are similes

The primary layer keeps animals as present company. Poem #8, at a grief: "my two cats... it does not matter whether they knew the same as me. but they did." Poem #15: "The moth didn't know the light until it bumped into it," and "We low like creatures. nothing need be known for us to bray our message." Poem #16: "i am still animal enough to want recurrence." Against this, the corpus's complete animal inventory — checked across all seven documents — is four similes: "like a cat" and "like a fox" (mythic-os), "like a shy animal" (first-telling), "like a moth looking for a window" (abridged). The creature register that populates the poems never entered the book except as comparison.

Two things follow for the image system. The grief doctrine's witness-inside-the-equation had, in the lived record, two cats — a witness the formula's clinical framing cannot see and the poems kept. And the moth koan — knowing by collision rather than instruction — is the engineered-epiphany refusal as an image, available from the primary layer if the book ever wants it.

- All poem passages verified at their numbers; the corpus inventory was gathered by searching the seven extractions for animal terms and confirming each hit's simile framing.
