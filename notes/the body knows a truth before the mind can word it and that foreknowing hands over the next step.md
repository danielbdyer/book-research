# the body knows a truth before the mind can word it and that foreknowing hands over the next step

Before a person can say what they mean, they can feel that they mean something — a whole sense, dense and wordless, already present at the edge. This is not a deficit waiting on language; it is knowledge in an earlier form, and it is generous. When the right word finally lands, the felt sense does not merely get labeled; it opens and hands over the next thing to say. A reader is never starting from nothing, because the body is already carrying the meaning forward, and the work is to listen for what it is reaching to become.

## Substantiation

- Two accounts hold that a whole meaning is present before its parts are spelled out. Gendlin's felt sense holds "more than is already there," and symbolizing it rightly carries the process forward into further steps rather than closing it (Experiencing and the Creation of Meaning, 1962; A Process Model; to verify). The Indic term *pratibhā* names the spontaneous flash by which a sentence's meaning is grasped as one, beneath and prior to the sequence of words (Bhartṛhari's Vākyapadīya; attributed, to verify). Company, cited by address.
- Refusal kept: Gendlin will not let this become mysticism on command — the felt sense is often murky, does not always give its word, and cannot be strong-armed. The foreknowing is real and also patient.
- Standing: a crossover from the 2026-08-21 harvest, held nascent. *Pratibhā*'s scope varies by tradition and is held as company rather than as identity with carrying-forward; the loci (a Vākyapadīya passage on pratibhā; Gendlin's own statement of "more than is already there") are queued.
