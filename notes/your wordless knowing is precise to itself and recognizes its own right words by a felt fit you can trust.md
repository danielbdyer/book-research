# your wordless knowing is precise to itself and recognizes its own right words by a felt fit you can trust

The reader gains an internal criterion for truth that is theirs and needs no external authority. The felt sense is fuzzy only from the side of concepts; to itself it is exact, and it "will not be fooled" — when a word does not fit, the body withholds its assent, and when the right word comes, there is a distinct give. So the reader is handed a compass, not a doctrine: they can tell, from the inside, whether a sentence is true *for them*, which is exactly the sovereignty the book means to protect. Its use to the writing is to ground the sovereign reader in a bodily faculty the book merely trusts rather than a permission it grants.

## Substantiation

- Gendlin (Focusing): the felt sense has "an exactness of its own," and the process proceeds by an ongoing checking in which a proposed word either brings a bodily easing (a felt shift) or does not; the body's yes is the criterion. This is meant as the ongoing compass that guides the unfolding, not the peak "whoa" of sudden recognition. Polanyi (The Tacit Dimension, 1966): "we know a person's face, and can recognize it among a thousand, indeed among a million, though we usually cannot tell how we recognize a face we know" — a knowing that judges correctly while unable to state its grounds.
- Company across a clinic and the philosophy of science: a knowing can be certain of fit while unable to state its rule.
