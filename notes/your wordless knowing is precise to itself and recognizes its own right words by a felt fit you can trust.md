# your wordless knowing is precise to itself and recognizes its own right words by a felt fit you can trust

The reader gains an internal criterion for truth that is theirs and needs no external authority. The felt sense is fuzzy only from the side of concepts; to itself it recognizes its own right word — when a word does not fit, the body withholds its assent, and when the right word comes, there is a distinct give. So the reader is handed a compass, not a doctrine: they can tell, from the inside, whether a sentence is true *for them*, which is exactly the sovereignty the book means to protect. Its use to the writing is to ground the sovereign reader in a bodily faculty the book merely trusts rather than a permission it grants.

## Substantiation

- Polanyi grounds his pole at the primary text: "We know a person's face, and can recognize it among a thousand, indeed among a million. Yet we usually cannot tell how we recognize a face we know" (The Tacit Dimension) — a knowing that judges correctly while unable to state its grounds, precise to its object without a stateable criterion. Gendlin grounds the second pole at the primary text: the felt sense recognizes its own right word by fit — a handle, when it lands, makes "the whole felt sense stir just slightly and ease a little … as if it said: 'This is right'" (Focusing, chs. 4–5) — and the process proceeds by an ongoing checking in which a proposed word either brings that bodily easing or does not. The precision is a fit-to-itself rather than an exactness: "the seeming fit will never be exact, for experience is richer than patterns" (Focusing, Appendix A).
- Company across the philosophy of science and a clinic: a knowing can be certain of fit while unable to state its rule.
- Standing: full — both poles read at the primary texts (Polanyi's face-recognition at The Tacit Dimension; Gendlin's felt fit at Focusing; records in `archive/`). One correction the reading forced: "an exactness of its own" is not verbatim in Focusing — the book's own language is that the word "fits … just right" while "the seeming fit will never be exact, for experience is richer than patterns," so the note is corrected to Gendlin's wording.
