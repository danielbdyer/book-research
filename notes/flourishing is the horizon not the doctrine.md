# flourishing is the horizon not the doctrine

The reframe conversation removes human flourishing from the position of the book's doctrine and reassigns it as the book's horizon. As doctrine, flourishing would be a defined end the book tells the reader to reach; as horizon, it is the direction the book's work tends toward without being specified. The conversation states the move plainly — flourishing "need not be the doctrine. It can be the horizon" — and draws the consequence for the book's address: rather than telling a reader "this is flourishing," the book can help the reader develop the capacity to recognize "this is what makes my participation in life more alive." The reassignment matters because the project's earlier organizing image, and a candidate cover title, had placed flourishing at the top; a horizon mistaken for a doctrine would be exactly the prescription the book's ethic refuses, the text declining to let its own title become its teaching.

## Substantiation

- The demotion, in the section titled the book's relationship to human flourishing: "Flourishing need not be the doctrine. It can be the **horizon**."
- The consequence for the book's address: the book "need not tell someone: 'This is flourishing'" but can help them "develop the capacity to recognize: 'This is what makes my participation in life more alive.'"
- The top-level placement the move reverses, from an adjacent section: human flourishing "is no longer necessarily the top-level object," becoming instead "an emergent consequence / horizon of the ecology."
