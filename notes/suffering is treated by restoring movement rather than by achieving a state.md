# suffering is treated by restoring movement rather than by achieving a state

If experience moves as a pulse of contraction and expansion, then a suffering is a pulse that has stalled — camped in a contraction that binds, or chasing an expansion that must be kept up. It follows that the book treats suffering not by reaching a good state but by restoring movement, and this rules out the two errors a state-focused account falls into. The repair error treats contraction as damage to be removed; the book denies it — nothing in the person is broken, something is folded. The escape error treats expansion as home to be secured; the engine denies it too — a system camped in expansion is no longer pulsing, and expansion held onto breeds the false peak. What remains is a movement therapy: the aim is to restore the oscillation, not to arrive anywhere. The book's Gatekeeper ethic is this performed in relation — the contraction met with a bow rather than a fight, honored as the protection it once was, so that it can move again rather than be forced open.

The claim is a first-principles derivation from the engine, held nascent and marked as inference; it states, as a consequence of the two-phase pulse, what the vault's suffering doctrine already implies but never says as a rule about treatment.

## Substantiation

- The two-phase premise, in the conversation: "a good system should permit both. It should know when to concentrate and when to open" (reframe conversation, 8224–8255, model-elaborated) — read here as a clinical sentence rather than a design one. The relational form is the corpus's Gatekeeper: "the protective part met with a bow, never a fight — saṅkoca honored as service… the contraction was always protection" (atlas Kata 4).
- The derivation: a stalled pulse is repaired by restoring its motion, not by fixing it into either phase; treating contraction as damage removes what was protection, and treating expansion as home stops the pulse and courts the false peak; so the therapeutic object is movement, not state. Strength: plausible.
- Standing: inferred from the engine; the Gatekeeper anchor is the corpus's, the two-phase premise is model-elaborated on the author's contraction/expansion seed (92, 156).
