# the invisibility that makes indirection succeed is identical to its unfalsifiability

The condition that makes indirect communication succeed is identical to the condition that makes it unfalsifiable. The method counts itself successful precisely when the result is invisible from outside — the reader having appropriated the truth inwardly rather than taken it over as a conclusion — and that same invisibility is what prevents anyone, the author included, from telling appropriation apart from mere situational compliance. Held against Martin Orne's account of demand characteristics, both the Socratic setting, an interlocutor cornered before an audience, and the Kierkegaardian one, a reader primed for edification, are high-demand situations where a felt recognition may be produced by the situation rather than by insight. The felt shift therefore cannot be defended against the demand-characteristics objection from inside the method; it can be defended only by durability outside the demand situation, which indirect communication declines to measure. The Orne reading is an inference in dig 1; the primary is queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.

## Substantiation

- Dig 1 states the identity: Kierkegaard's design "removes the verification that could distinguish appropriation from compliance — the success condition (invisibility of the result) is identical to its unfalsifiability" (transmission dig 1, 2026-08-13, felt-shift audit), marked [I].
- The dig locates the only remaining defense outside the room: the shift can be defended "only by durability *outside* the demand situation, which indirection declines to measure" (same). The demand-characteristics reading is inferential here and the Orne primary is queued.
