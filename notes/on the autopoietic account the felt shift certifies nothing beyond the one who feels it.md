# on the autopoietic account the felt shift certifies nothing beyond the one who feels it

The book asks a felt somatic confirmation to decide what is true, and the autopoietic account of cognition, taken as the book might cite it, quietly disqualifies that instrument. Maturana's governing claim is that every distinction is drawn by an observer inside a domain — "everything said is said by an observer" — so measurement itself is only the observer's description of recurrent structural congruence. Under that frame a felt shift is one more distinction-in-a-domain, an event certified by the observer who has it and warranting nothing that holds across observers. The standard the book installed to outrank mere assent is therefore demoted by the very science that would supply its relational mechanism: the shift reports the state of one system to itself and carries no trans-observer authority.

This is the cost of borrowing the biology whole. The same theory that grounds perturb-not-install ([[an interaction can trigger a change in a living system but cannot specify it]]) will not let the felt shift function as evidence, because it treats no first-person report as more than a distinction one observer draws.

- The finding compounds the standard's existing exposure rather than opening a new front: the vault already holds that the standard rests on one name and that its introspective premise is undefended, and this adds a frame in which the report certifies nothing beyond its owner even when accurate.
