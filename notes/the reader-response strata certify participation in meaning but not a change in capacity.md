# the reader-response strata certify participation in meaning but not a change in capacity

The reader-response theories the book might borrow from all theorize meaning, and the book's standard is a change in lived capacity, so the strata certify participation in meaning and nothing past it. Rosenblatt stops at the lived event of reading and re-files any consequence as efferent residue; Iser reaches the threshold of self-examination and declares the payload unknowable; Barthes has the change only as loss; Gadamer builds it partway as a hermeneutic gain whose only check is a felt bindingness that can be manufactured. The consequence for the book is that the premise it would most like to establish from this literature — that a text produces enactment — is not established here. To read Iser's participation-gap as enactment overstates it: it is self-examination at the level of the reader's preconceptions, not altered capacity.

This is a sharper limit than the familiar point that information transmits and realization does not, because it holds even where the reading is fully active. A reader can be made a participant, drawn into providing the connections and examining their own assumptions, and still arrive only at participation in meaning. The bridge from that to a changed capacity is absent across the region, and the record cannot rule that it exists.

## Substantiation

- The dig's remainder states the finding after working all four strata: they establish that texts produce participation in meaning and certify nothing beyond it (`transmission dig 2, 2026-08-13`, Remainder).
- Iser's 1972 essay, opened directly [P], is where the threshold is both reached and abandoned: he compels "a creative examination not only of the text, but also of ourselves" (295) and then concedes that "we do not know what happens to us." The concession is quoted at primary depth by the dig.
- Rosenblatt, Barthes, and Gadamer were worked at secondary depth [S]. Their primary readings remain undone and are registered among dig 2's readings to queue (`transmission dig 2, 2026-08-13`, "Readings to queue"), bound for `ops/reading queue.md`; the Rosenblatt entry asks specifically whether she anywhere names a reading whose product is altered capacity or explicitly closes that door.
