# curating beauty is a practice rather than only a hazard

The gesture asserts that choosing and arranging what is beautiful is itself a practice with spiritual content. The vault's position on beauty is currently one-sided and negative. Its only claim on the subject is that the book's prose beauty recruits the wrong posture, turning a practitioner into a connoisseur, and the survey supplies the philosophical genealogy for that worry rather than for its opposite. The corpus therefore has a theory of beauty's danger and none of its use.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- The hazard is recorded at [[beauty recruits the wrong posture]], from atlas §XI.1a: "the prose beauty recruits appreciation-posture: the reader becomes a connoisseur rather than a practitioner."
- Beauty and aesthetic together appear six times in the survey, and its entries are counterweights — Kierkegaard's aesthetic stage, a life of appreciation that never chooses, and Sontag against substituting commentary for contact. Both are on the pending-evaluation list in `ops/queue/candidates.md`.
- The one place the corpus treats an arrangement as doing work rather than as risking appreciation is its use of Alexander, whose quality without a name is a property of made things. The corpus takes the quality and not the making.

## What would close it

Closing it means deciding whether the book can commend an aesthetic practice while holding its own prose under suspicion.
