# modeling dissent in the verso column moves the refusal to the reader's side of the page

The resistance tension carries two treatments under trial — a refusal staged inside the text and a margin voice that argues back — and both keep the refusal on the book's side of the page. The book's own law holds that nothing transforms from the audience, so a refusal the book performs on the reader's behalf is a refusal the reader watched rather than one the reader made. The craft offering is to let the verso column, where a reader countersigns a practice with assent ("walked. still true." — atlas §X.6b), also model dissent — a countersign in the reader's own hand that reads, in the panel's proposed phrasing, "walked. not true for me." — so the destruction of the book's claim originates with the reader and the next page survives it without retaliating.

The offering lands as a third treatment candidate on the resistance tension, distinct from the two on trial because it moves the act of refusal off the page and into the reader. Its warrant is the object-usage account the tension already cites, which requires that the destruction originate with the subject; whether it does is what the queued Winnicott reading has to settle, and if it does, a book-staged refusal fails the warrant it cites while a reader-written one satisfies it.

## Substantiation

- The offering is recorded at the forest panel of 2026-08-08, panel three (the construction advisor), first entry.
- The verso column and its assenting countersign are specified at atlas §X.6b ("walked. still true."); the dissenting form ("walked. not true for me.") is the panel's proposed extension, not a corpus phrase.
- The decisive check is queued as the Winnicott object-usage reading in `ops/reading queue.md` (tier 6, entry 20): does the destruction in object usage have to originate with the subject, in which case only a reader-writable surface complies.
