# the book opens from a surplus of insight rather than a lack of it

The book's problem statement inverts the self-help premise. The first telling opens there: "We are not short on self-knowledge anymore… the narration doesn't seem to move anything. You can know exactly why you do the thing, and keep doing the thing… understanding has become easy to acquire, and easy to hide in" (first-telling: the problem). The outward survey treats "insight as a place to hide" as the book's own phrase and organizes its first section around "insight that doesn't move anything" (space-around).

This is the doorway to two of the vault's central concerns at once. The verification standard exists because understanding alone certifies nothing — the felt shift is the book's answer to exactly this surplus. And the author's defined second reader — fluent in integration vocabulary, using the fluency to avoid feeling — is this problem statement embodied: the surplus of insight, worn as armor.

## Substantiation

- First-telling, "The problem," carries the full statement, quoted above.
- Space-around's first section head quotes the book's phrase and surveys the field around it (Sass, Wegner, Vervaeke, Kegan — all still on the shelf in `ops/queue/candidates.md`).
- The claim is the front door to [[felt shift versus felt confirmation]]: a book against insight-as-hiding must verify by something insight cannot counterfeit, which is what makes the counterfeit-shift problem the book's own problem twice over.
