# the medicine sentence negates the familiar advice before naming the restoration

The diagnostic doctrine's prescriptions arrive in the executed draft through one repeated template: the culture's usual counsel is named and negated, then the structural restoration is named. "The medicine is not 'more discipline' or 'self-care' in the cosmetic sense. The medicine, precisely, is to restore Sanctuary" (abridged ch6). "The medicine is not to add more affection, which is what most couples try. The medicine is to restore difference" (abridged ch15).

The negation is what keeps each prescription from being read as one more piece of advice. More discipline, cosmetic self-care, and more affection are things the reader has already tried, and naming them first sets the prescription against them rather than beside them. What follows the negation is not another behavior to perform but a structure that is missing — Sanctuary in the burnout case, difference in the couples case — and the contrast with the familiar counsel is what makes that distinction visible inside a single sentence.

## Substantiation

- The exact phrase "The medicine is not" occurs four times in the extracted draft, with a fifth prescriptive instance in variant phrasing; the ch6 and ch15 specimens are quoted above.
- Anyone can check this by searching the draft for "The medicine is not": each hit names a familiar counsel before it names the structural prescription, and a prescription delivered without the negation is the exception to look for.
- In the book's notation, a molecule formula containing a minus sign names a suffering-state and the term after the minus is what must be supplied, while a formula built from plus signs alone only describes a condition ([[the minus sign is what makes a molecule formula prescriptive]]). This template is how that missing term reaches a reader as an ordinary sentence.
