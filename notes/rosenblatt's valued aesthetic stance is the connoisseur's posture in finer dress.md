# rosenblatt's valued aesthetic stance is the connoisseur's posture in finer dress

Rosenblatt's aesthetic stance, the one her polemic works to rescue from the schooled efferent default, is absorbed attention to one's own responses during reading, valued as an end in itself. That description is also a description of the connoisseur's appreciating posture, the very posture the book identifies as its wrong-way failure mode. The book cannot therefore borrow Rosenblatt's aesthetic reading as its own goal without importing the trouble it is trying to escape: an aesthetic reading raised to an end is a reader savouring their own responses, which is collection in finer dress rather than the enactment the book is after.

The point is a warning rather than a contradiction. Rosenblatt is right that schooling over-trains the efferent and that the lived event of reading deserves rescue; the book agrees the reader must live the reading rather than only analyse it. The caution is that the rescue stops one step short of the book's standard, at the savouring, and that the savouring is exactly the connoisseur's absorption the book means to move past.
