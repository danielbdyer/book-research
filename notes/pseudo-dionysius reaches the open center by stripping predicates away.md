# pseudo-dionysius reaches the open center by stripping predicates away

The book's open center is reached by removing a concealment rather than by adding a predicate ([[the open center is made possible by the three structures and identical with none of them]]; [[the book reads deep change as uncovering rather than addition]]). Christian apophatic theology reaches it by the same subtraction from an unrelated Neoplatonic lineage: a ladder of negations ascends into a "divine darkness," a cloud of unknowing, a knowing that exceeds understanding by un-knowing. The move is company for both the open center, which the darkness enters by negation, and the privative grammar, since union is approached by stripping away rather than by accretion. The reading is not done, so the claim is privated.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the three structures hold an open middle they are not, identified with *Lichtung* and *prakasa* (atlas §II).
- The apophatic half is named, not shown: the ascent by negation is at Pseudo-Dionysius, *The Mystical Theology*, ch. 1, and the closing cascade of pure negations at ch. 5. The seated Eckhart's own via negativa is his treatise *On Detachment* (*Von Abgeschiedenheit*), a face of him the vault has not mined (it holds his *Gelassenheit* via Caputo and his Martha-over-Mary householder move). Both are to verify at the text; neither is quoted from memory.
- The refusal is kept. The negations terminate in a God beyond being, and the stripping is meant to leave the self behind in union with what is above being, not to send the first person home to a house with no ceiling — the same shape as the recognition tradition's own recorded refusal, recognition without the recognized.
- The disposition is reopened on narrower terms. The apophatic lineage was semi-shelved 2026-08-07 on the ground that disciplined non-resolution is a vault practice rather than a book claim; that pass did not weigh the open-center and privative-grammar angles, which is why the candidate returns here.
