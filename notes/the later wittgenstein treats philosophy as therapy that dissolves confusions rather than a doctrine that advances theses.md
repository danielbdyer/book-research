# the later wittgenstein treats philosophy as therapy that dissolves confusions rather than a doctrine that advances theses

The later Wittgenstein offers a second model for a book that transmits nothing to store. In the region of the *Philosophical Investigations* around §§109–133 he treats philosophy as treatment — dissolving confusions rather than advancing theses — and holds that philosophy has not one method but many, which he compares to different therapies. The device blocks the craving for a doctrine to hold. Its cost is that no positive result survives the treatment: the author leaves the reader with a dissolved problem and nothing to carry away, nothing to admire, and nothing to cite. For a book whose failure mode is the reader collecting sentences instead of practicing, a method that leaves nothing collectible is a candidate discipline rather than a decoration. The finding rests on the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy; the primary passage is unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
