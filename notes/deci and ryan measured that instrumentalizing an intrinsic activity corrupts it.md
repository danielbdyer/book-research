# deci and ryan measured that instrumentalizing an intrinsic activity corrupts it

The book's practice clause holds that a practice expresses realization rather than being a means to it ([[the practice is the expression of realization rather than a means to it]]). Deci and Ryan's undermining effect is the strongest genuinely independent arrival at that clause, and the one that gives it an empirical arm: when an intrinsically motivated activity is done for an external reward, the intrinsic motivation is measurably degraded. A reading of their 1999 meta-analysis confirms it with numbers. It converts "the doing is the somewhere" from one Zen master's saying into a cross-tradition convergence with measured evidence — the caliber of company the convergence centerpiece is built from. With Dōgen and Aristotle it makes a three-tradition arrival: Zen, experimental psychology, and Greek philosophy on one clause.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the anti-instrumentality clause constrains every exercise in the curriculum (walked IX; [[the practice is the expression of realization rather than a means to it]]).
- Deci and Ryan's half is now shown, from the 1999 meta-analysis of 128 experiments (the reading record is `archive/reading — deci koestner and ryan, meta-analysis of rewards and intrinsic motivation (1999).md`): tangible contingent rewards significantly undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation — engagement-contingent d = −0.40, completion-contingent d = −0.36, performance-contingent d = −0.28 — while positive feedback enhanced it (d = 0.33). So it is reward experienced as controlling, the instrumentalizing frame, that degrades the activity, not reward as such — the book's own distinction between a practice expressing realization and one aimed at a payoff. Verified at the meta-analysis's reported abstract and effect sizes; the scanned paper would not text-extract in-container, so the mechanism detail is unconfirmed here.
- The refusal is kept. This is a theory of motivation rather than of realization, so it reaches the practical half of the clause (instrumentalizing corrupts) and not the metaphysical half (the doing *is* the realization); and it is not agenda-less, since it posits three needs — autonomy, competence, relatedness — as ends.
