# deci and ryan measured that instrumentalizing an intrinsic activity corrupts it

The book's practice clause holds that a practice expresses realization rather than being a means to it ([[the practice is the expression of realization rather than a means to it]]). Deci and Ryan's undermining effect is the strongest genuinely independent arrival at that clause, and the one that gives it an empirical arm: when an intrinsically motivated activity is done for an external reward, the intrinsic motivation is measurably degraded. It converts "the doing is the somewhere" from one Zen master's saying into a cross-tradition convergence with measured evidence — the caliber of company the convergence centerpiece is built from. With Dōgen and Aristotle it makes a three-tradition arrival: Zen, experimental psychology, and Greek philosophy on one clause. The reading is not done, so the claim is privated.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the anti-instrumentality clause constrains every exercise in the curriculum (walked IX; [[the practice is the expression of realization rather than a means to it]]).
- Deci and Ryan's half is named, not shown: the effect is in Deci 1971, "Effects of externally mediated rewards on intrinsic motivation," and the meta-analysis is Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999 in *Psychological Bulletin*. Both are to verify at the text; the entry already sits unevaluated on the candidate shelf.
- The refusal is kept. This is a theory of motivation rather than of realization, so it reaches the practical half of the clause (instrumentalizing corrupts) and not the metaphysical half (the doing *is* the realization); and it is not agenda-less, since it posits three needs — autonomy, competence, relatedness — as ends.
