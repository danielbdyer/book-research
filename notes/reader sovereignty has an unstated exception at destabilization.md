# reader sovereignty has an unstated exception at destabilization

The book states reader sovereignty without condition: the book is an instrument in service of a life that stays the reader's own, never an authority ([[the book can only be auxiliary to a sovereign reader]]). Its own safety findings expose a boundary the doctrine does not state. At destabilization — the peak the book stages, or a crisis the reading induces — the reader needs a watcher with the standing to overrule their own self-report, and the reading forbids exactly that while handing the judgment to the body, the instrument worst placed to make it ([[the book cannot tell dissolution from regression and neither can its reader]]). So sovereignty carries an exception the book has not written: at the edge of a crisis, the reader's own report is not the final authority, and something outside the reader has to be able to say so.

The tension is between two things the book holds, one of them silently. It states sovereignty as unconditional and never states the destabilization case that bounds it, so a reader in crisis meets a doctrine that forbids the one thing the crisis requires.

## Substantiation

- The unconditional side is on record: the book's honest maximum is auxiliary-not-authority, assembled from the letter's grammar, the resonance theory, and the facilitator's craft ([[the book can only be auxiliary to a sovereign reader]]), and it rests on the law that nothing transforms from the audience, so whatever transforms, the reader did ([[nothing transforms from the audience]]).
- The bounding case is also on record, in the sort's holes: the required second person splits into a judge who can overrule a self-report and a mirror who is forbidden to direct, and the sovereignty doctrine can supply only the mirror ([[the required second person splits into a judge and a mirror]], `mesh grid: tier 1`). The recorded safety failures — telling dissolution from regression, judging a state from inside it — are what the judge exists for.
- The two have never been put in one claim, so the exception stays unstated. Naming it does not resolve it: whether an outside authority can be admitted without breaking the sovereignty the whole book rests on is the author's to settle, and the vault holds the tension rather than resolving it.
- The treatment under trial is a division of labour rather than an authority the book installs over the reader: the mirror does the ordinary verification, and a pre-committed outside contact — chosen by the reader in advance, so the sovereignty is exercised rather than surrendered — holds the standing to overrule at the crisis points.
