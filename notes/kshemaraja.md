# kshemaraja

Kṣemarāja is an eleventh-century Kashmiri author, the disciple of Abhinavagupta, and the tradition's great condenser: his Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam — "the Heart of Recognition," twenty sūtras — is the corpus's chosen pocket summa of the recognition philosophy, the one Trika text on the book's recommended-reading list (atlas §IX.10). The corpus takes from him the formula it treats as the deepest practice sentence in its holdings: madhya-vikāsāt — in the corpus's gloss, "from the unfolding of the center comes the bliss of consciousness" (atlas §IX.6c) — which the book carries not as a citation but as a planted apparatus, half-erased at first appearance and completed chapters later ([[the unfolding of the center is planted half-erased and finished at a mirror]]).

He is seated twice. Beyond the source tradition, he is one of the book's margin hands — "the valley's own hand in the margins of a Western house" (atlas §IX.7) — which makes him the only figure who both supplies the book's deep formula and annotates the book that carries it.

## Substantiation

- Atlas §IX.6d records the text and its standing: "Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam (Kṣemarāja) — the Heart of Recognition, twenty sūtras"; walked IX calls it "the pocket-sized summa."
- Atlas §IX.6c records the formula and both of its sites in the book; atlas §IX.7 records the margin seat.
