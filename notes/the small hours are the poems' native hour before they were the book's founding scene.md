# the small hours are the poems' native hour before they were the book's founding scene

The one confirmed sighting of the book's promised event happened at 4am — a decade-old letter, read alone. The poems turn out to own that hour. Poem #7: "in crisp / 4 am bliss, all closed / in and crying – all / breathing and trying." Poem #12: "i don't care that it's 3am." Poem #16 timestamps itself: "current is 1:13 a.m." The seven corpus documents, checked whole, contain no small-hour timestamp at all: the corpus commits to the scale of the day — the Tuesday, the kettle — and never treats the hour, while the poems return to it three times across the collection's span.

The finding is provenance for the founding scene: when recognition arrived at 4am, it arrived at the hour the author's own record had already established as the hour of "crying… breathing and trying." The scene's design variables have been catalogued elsewhere; this claim adds that its hour was not incidental but native.

## Substantiation

- Poems #7, #12, and #16, quoted above, verified in the extracted text; the absence of small-hour timestamps in the seven corpus documents was checked by word-bounded search across the extractions.
- The 4am scene's record is the provenance reconstruction's fourth addendum (§II.2), held under the personal-provenance conditions; the scene's standing in the vault is at [[the book's native genre is the letter from a friend read alone]].
- The direction rule extends: the corpus's Tuesday-scale commitment ([[the tuesday is the temple]]) has a poem-scale complement it never absorbed.
