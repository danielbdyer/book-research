# the youngness is just beginning names a life lived on the season's own time that kairos and eternal return each named

This hands the reader the exact permission the author was handed as a boy: you are not late. The clock that says the young years are spent, the window closing, the schedule missed — that is chronos, and it is not the only time there is. There is also the almanac's time, the orchard's time, the time in which youngness is a thing that *begins* rather than ends. The reader who has ever felt, against all the counting, that they were somehow precisely on time has touched a form of time whole traditions were built to name, and the book can offer that felt rightness back as a practice rather than a lucky mood. Its use to the writing is to introduce the book's doctrine of time not as a teaching but as a permission the reader was perhaps once given too, and to distinguish the two clocks so cleanly a reader can feel which one they have been punishing themselves by.

## Substantiation

- On record: poem #5's ending, a bird readying for flight — "the youngness is just beginning. and he is soon ready to go" (poems #5); the father's lifelong "you're right on schedule" and the drafting line "the youngness — check the almanac — is just beginning," at [[the book's doctrine of time restates a sentence the author's father told him]]; poem #12's litany, "even leaving, beginning again; even older, beginning again" (poems #12).
- Inference, offered as a candidate: the Greek distinction between *chronos* (clock-time) and *kairos* (the right or ripe time) — "check the almanac" is a reading of kairos, with Ecclesiastes 3's "to everything a season" its scriptural face; and beneath the renewal figure, Nietzsche's *amor fati* and eternal return (Gay Science §341) name loving one's own time so fully one would will it back.
- Standing: plausible on kairos (grounded; the corpus already reaches for it), a reach on eternal return; touches the author's life through the father's sentence, held delicately — he confirms whether "the youngness is just beginning" was felt as renewal-that-returns or simply readiness-for-flight.
