# reading as remembering has a twenty-four-century ancestry in the west

The book's front-matter promise — you already know this, you've forgotten, reading is remembering — has a Western ancestry that runs twenty-four centuries. Plato argues learning-as-recollection in the Meno and Phaedo: birth imposes a forgetting, and inquiry undoes it. Augustine works memoria in Confessions X and finds within memory something beyond it. Heidegger's Parmenides lectures (1942–43) are the hinge where this lineage touches the book's central verb: there he reads lēthē as withdrawing concealment and grounds both remembering and forgetting in unconcealment. The Meno's slave boy is the oldest staging of the book's own method — a guide who inserts nothing and questions the knower toward what he already holds.

The lineage's honesty matters as much as its length: it disagrees with itself about what is remembered. Augustine kept Plato's structure and explicitly declined his mechanism — the soul's preexistence — and what Augustine finds (the God within) is not Plato's Forms. Even the West's own remembering-tradition is company rather than corroboration: the arrivals share the gesture and dispute the destination, which is the same shape the founding isomorph already has, and the same shape the valley's refusals keep on the other side.
