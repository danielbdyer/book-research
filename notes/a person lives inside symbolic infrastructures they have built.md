# a person lives inside symbolic infrastructures they have built

The gesture asserts that a person builds symbolic systems and then lives inside them, and that those systems can be read the way a text is read. This is the one open gesture the descriptive corpus has already acted on without naming. Both the atlas and the walked document record that the project's material has been read out of a running playlist, and the walked document reports what the reading found: that a production technique in the music turned out to carry one of the tradition's central concepts. That is the gesture performed once, on one artefact, and never generalised.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- The walked document records the instance and its finding: the project has "once read a running playlist, where sidechain compression turned out to be spanda" (walked X). The atlas records the same reading among the forms the material has taken — "book, graph, and once, without strain, a running playlist" (atlas §X).
- Spanda is the tradition's term for the pulse of consciousness, and it is one of the corpus's load-bearing concepts. The claim implied by that sentence is that a symbolic infrastructure can carry doctrine accurately enough to be read for it.
- The gesture's own scope is wider than the instance: "Dreams, Playlists, Sacred Code" (mythic-os: contents). Dreams appear three times in the earliest contents and once in the abridged edition; sacred code appears nowhere else in the corpus.

## What would close it

Closing it means deciding whether the playlist reading was an anecdote or a method, and if a method, stating it.
