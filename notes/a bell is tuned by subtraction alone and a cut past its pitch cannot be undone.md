# a bell is tuned by subtraction alone and a cut past its pitch cannot be undone

A bell is tuned after it is cast by removing bronze from its inner profile, bringing five partial tones into relation with one another. Metal can only be taken away, never added, and a bell cut past its intended pitch must be melted down and recast. The panel found two of the book's moves in this one craft. The true tone is found in what the casting already contains and change is a matter of removal, which is disclosure and privation together — the book's reading of deep change as uncovering rather than addition ([[aletheia and pratyabhijna converge on truth as un-forgetting]]; [[aristotle on privation]]). And because subtraction is irreversible, the discipline is knowing when to stop cutting, which touches the honest gap the book leaves open: the practice forms say when to begin and never how to tell one is finished ([[a practice round is finished when the body has refused at least one formulation]]).

This resonance accompanies the disclosure and privation doctrines rather than testing them: bell-founding is company for a change that proceeds by removal, not evidence that deep change is subtractive.
