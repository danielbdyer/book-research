# a device delivers a good while hiding the engagement it once required

Albert Borgmann's analysis separates a good into two parts and calls the arrangement that splits them a device. A device presents a commodity — the warmth, the meal, the music — while it conceals the machinery that produces the commodity — the wood, the hearth, the labor. The device delivers the commodity and withdraws the machinery from view, and Borgmann's finding is that the withdrawal is not free: the machinery was where the person's engagement lived, so an arrangement that disburdens also disengages. Central heating supplies warmth while the cutting, splitting, and tending of wood recede; the good arrives and the practice that once carried it is gone. Borgmann's positive pole is the focal thing — a hearth, a table, a run — which he holds to gather the relations of its setting and radiate into them, a center of orientation that engages rather than disburdens. The irony he records is that technology promised enrichment and instead removes the engagement that made the good worth having.

This bears on the book because the book hands the reader a good it calls recognition, and a book that delivered recognition as a commodity — a state procured, a payoff arriving on schedule — while concealing the practice that produces it would be, in Borgmann's terms, a device. The hazard is the one the book's own foundations already name: recognition supplied as a foregrounded good, with the enacting withdrawn, is the apparatus working rather than the world showing ([[both poles of the convergence refuse the engineered epiphany]]). The reading that would test the analysis against the book's own craft is queued: whether the concealed machinery can be another person's labor, and so whether facilitation is a focal practice or a device, is Borgmann's ch.9, which the dig reached only through Verbeek, held in `ops/reading queue.md`.

## Substantiation

- The dig opened Borgmann's ch.23, "Focal Things and Practices," at a reprint [P per dig 5]; the commodity/machinery split and the focal-thing account are drawn from there. The focal thing "gathers the relations of its context and radiates into its surroundings" (ch.23) — the sentence that states the engaging pole against which the device is measured.
- The honest edge is Verbeek's, reached [S per dig 5]: some devices amplify engagement rather than remove it, since recorded music widens musical life, so the commodity/machinery split is a lens rather than a law, and the word "engagement" can equivocate between exertion and meaning. The split is a diagnostic the reader can carry, not a verdict the book should promote to ontology.
- The note is held nascent because the vault holds no reading record for Borgmann, and the consequential extension — is the machinery of a helped transformation another person's care, and does that make facilitation a device? — is the queued ch.9 reading rather than a settled finding.
