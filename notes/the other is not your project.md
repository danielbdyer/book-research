# the other is not your project

The reframe conversation states as its first major ethical declaration that another person cannot become the facilitator's project. The participant is not the facilitator's evidence of effectiveness, validation, transformation project, client outcome, disciple, or proof that a theory works. The conversation grounds the declaration in three limits on what one person can do to another: interiority cannot be possessed, meaning cannot be authored from outside, and a becoming cannot be completed on someone's behalf. This is where the facilitation material introduces sovereignty — the participant remains other, and that otherness is the thing the ethic exists to protect.

## Substantiation

- The reframe conversation calls this "the first major ethical declaration" and lists what the other cannot be made into: "your evidence of effectiveness; your validation; your transformation project; your client outcome; your disciple; your proof that your theory works." It states the limits plainly: "The other remains other. Their interiority cannot be possessed. Their meaning cannot be authored from outside. Their becoming cannot be completed on their behalf," and marks that "This introduces sovereignty" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13: the other is not your project).
