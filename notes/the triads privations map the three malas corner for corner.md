# the triads privations map the three malas corner for corner

Each structure of the container anatomy has a characteristic failure, and the corpus finds the same three failures named by the Trika tradition a thousand years earlier. A failed Wall becomes deficiency — the walked document's image is a boundary "curdled into verdict," a sentence passed on oneself; Trika names this āṇava-mala, fullness contracted into a point that experiences itself as lack. A failed Door becomes separation — connection remembered but no longer operable, "doors painted shut"; Trika names this māyīya-mala, unity contracted into separateness. A failed Floor becomes residue — the past accumulating underneath rather than carrying, "gone to sediment"; Trika names this kārma-mala, action contracted into deeds done from lack and the residue they leave.

The corpus's conclusion, stated in the walked document: "Corner for corner, the map is the same map." The correspondence grounds the doctrine recorded at [[suffering is contraction never corruption]] and gives the anatomy its diagnostic use.

## Substantiation

- Walked II carries all three failure descriptions and the corner-for-corner sentence.
- Atlas §II.3 tabulates the correspondence with the privation notation (⊖) and cross-links it forward to the Privation Principle (§IV) as "the contraction doctrine in laboratory dress."
- The corner-for-corner fit reads differently once the tradition's standing is stated. The corpus does not treat this tradition as one influence among many; the book's foundation is built from its terms, and it is placed beneath the company rather than in it ([[the kashmir shaiva tradition underlies the book rather than joining its company]]). A correspondence between a framework and its own ground is a weaker surprise than a correspondence between strangers, and the dating keeps the direction honest: the structure was built first, and the tradition's diagnosis met it afterward ([[the triad predates the author's contact with the tradition named the triad]]).
- First-telling, "The offering," carries the reader-facing versions: the verdict, the painted-shut door, the sediment.
- The Trika half of the correspondence is carried here entirely by the corpus's account: every quotation above is the corpus's, and no primary text naming the three malas is cited in this note. The vault's nearest textual ground for the tradition is the cited apparatus at [[the kashmir shaiva tradition would decline four of the book's deepest moves]] — Torella's Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā, the Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam, Tantrāloka āhnika 13 — and none of it has been checked for the mala triad specifically. That check has now run (below), so the corner-for-corner match is established at the primary text for two corners and precisely qualified for the third, and the standing evaluation's question — whether the mapping is more than verbal ([[aristotle on privation]]) — is answered: more than verbal for two corners, an adjacent-construct match for the third.
- **The primary reading is now done (2026-08-28), at the Sanskrit.** Kṣemarāja's Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam was reached at primary — GRETIL's CC-licensed Sanskrit and Pradīpaka's Trika edition, with Jaideva Singh's translation — breaking the gate that blocked two prior attempts (the block was a URL-discovery problem and a user-agent bot-filter, not a policy wall). Sūtra 9, "cidvat tacchaktisaṅkocān malāvṛtaḥ saṃsārī," grounds the doctrine: the bound individual is Cit *covered by mala through the contraction (saṅkoca) of its powers*. The vṛtti's three definitions confirm the mapping for two corners at the malas' own level and locate the third precisely. **āṇavamala** is *apūrṇammanyatā*, "considering itself imperfect" (Singh p. 72) — the Wall's failure into deficiency. **māyīyamala** is *bhinnavedyaprathā*, "the apprehension of all objects as different" — the Door's failure into separation. But **kārmamala** is *śubhāśubhānuṣṭhānamaya*, "doing good or evil," whose core is limited *agency* (*kiñcitkartṛtva*), **not residue**: the Floor's "sediment" the book maps here is Singh's explanatory *vāsanā*-gloss and, structurally, the fourth of Kṣemarāja's five acts, *bīja-avasthāpana* ("settling of the seed," sūtra 11), where a withdrawn experience "acquires the state of saṃsāra in germ" — so the third corner corresponds to an adjacent construct rather than to kārmamala's own definition. What the reading confirms cleanly, and what most matters: all three malas are **contractions (*saṅkoca*) of consciousness's powers, "uncontracted powers appearing contracted," not added defects** — and sūtra 5, "citir eva … cetyasaṅkocinī cittam," is a second contraction-locus (universal Citi contracting into the individual citta) — which grounds [[suffering is contraction never corruption]] in the tradition's own words.
