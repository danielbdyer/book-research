# the triads privations map the three malas corner for corner

Each structure of the container anatomy has a characteristic failure, and the corpus finds the same three failures named by the Trika tradition a thousand years earlier. A failed Wall becomes deficiency — the walked document's image is a boundary "curdled into verdict," a sentence passed on oneself; Trika names this āṇava-mala, fullness contracted into a point that experiences itself as lack. A failed Door becomes separation — connection remembered but no longer operable, "doors painted shut"; Trika names this māyīya-mala, unity contracted into separateness. A failed Floor becomes residue — the past accumulating underneath rather than carrying, "gone to sediment"; Trika names this kārma-mala, action contracted into deeds done from lack and the residue they leave.

The corpus's conclusion, stated in the walked document: "Corner for corner, the map is the same map." The correspondence grounds the doctrine recorded at [[suffering is contraction never corruption]] and gives the anatomy its diagnostic use.

## Substantiation

- Walked II carries all three failure descriptions and the corner-for-corner sentence.
- Atlas §II.3 tabulates the correspondence with the privation notation (⊖) and cross-links it forward to the Privation Principle (§IV) as "the contraction doctrine in laboratory dress."
- The corner-for-corner fit reads differently once the tradition's standing is stated. The corpus does not treat this tradition as one influence among many; the book's foundation is built from its terms, and it is placed beneath the company rather than in it ([[the kashmir shaiva tradition underlies the book rather than joining its company]]). A correspondence between a framework and its own ground is a weaker surprise than a correspondence between strangers, and the dating keeps the direction honest: the structure was built first, and the tradition's diagnosis met it afterward ([[the triad predates the author's contact with the tradition named the triad]]).
- First-telling, "The offering," carries the reader-facing versions: the verdict, the painted-shut door, the sediment.
- The Trika half of the correspondence is carried here entirely by the corpus's account: every quotation above is the corpus's, and no primary text naming the three malas is cited in this note. The vault's nearest textual ground for the tradition is the cited apparatus at [[the kashmir shaiva tradition would decline four of the book's deepest moves]] — Torella's Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā, the Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam, Tantrāloka āhnika 13 — and none of it has been checked for the mala triad specifically. Until that check runs, the corner-for-corner match is the corpus's proposal rather than an established correspondence, and the standing evaluation asks exactly whether the mapping is more than verbal ([[aristotle on privation]]).
- **A preliminary secondary check (2026-08-09) found the mapping more than verbal but not clean corner-for-corner.** A genuine primary translation of the Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam was not reachable from this container — the scholarly Sanskrit-Trika edition refused automated retrieval, and the only full texts available were pirated copies of Jaideva Singh's copyright translation — so this rests on secondary scholarship (Torella, through a Pratyabhijñā study) and stops short of a primary reading. On that basis two of the three corners hold structurally: āṇavamala is the loss of consciousness's fullness, the sense of being imperfect, which matches the Wall's failure into deficiency; and māyīyamala is differentiated multiplicity, the non-dual "becoming many," which matches the Door's failure into separation. The third corner is looser: kārmamala's core is limited *agency* — a limited doer who "loses the knowledge of right and wrong" — which the book maps to the Floor's *residue* by way of karma's traces (the "cause of births and fruits") rather than by the mala's central meaning. So the corpus's "corner for corner" holds for two corners and rests on a downstream feature for the third. What the check does confirm cleanly is that all three malas are contractions (*saṅkoca*) of consciousness's powers rather than added defects, which grounds [[suffering is contraction never corruption]]. A primary reading of Kṣemarāja's own sūtra 9 and its *vṛtti* is still needed to settle the third corner; it waits on an author-supplied or library text.
