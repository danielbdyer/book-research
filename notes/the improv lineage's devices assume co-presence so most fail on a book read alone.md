# the improv lineage's devices assume co-presence so most fail on a book read alone

The dig's central remainder is that the theatre and improv lineage's devices are built on conditions a book cannot reproduce: co-presence, eye contact, a room, and a live other who responds in the moment and survives being tested. Struck honestly against a book read alone, most of the catalog does not cross. Side-coaching dies, because the page cannot deliver a live, timed intervention during the reader's activity. Status and accepting die as practice, because there is no partner on the page to transact status with or to make an offer the reader could accept. The point of concentration and approval-removal are maimed rather than killed, because they lose the holding field of co-present others also concentrating, so the reader's self-watching returns faster than it would in a room.

What crosses is thin and specifiable, and naming it is the dig's positive result. Two things survive the passage to the page. The first is the authorial posture in which the maker takes the failure, so the reader's not-getting-it is the book's fault rather than the reader's. The second, the deeper one, is Winnicott's reliable, non-impinging presence read as a design specification for what a book must be to be read alone. The remainder bites even here: Winnicott's other is alive, adapts to this particular reader, and survives being destroyed, and a book can do none of the three. The bridge from the room to the page is real as a target and false as an equivalence.
