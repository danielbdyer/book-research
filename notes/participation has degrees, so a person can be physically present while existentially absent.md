# participation has degrees, so a person can be physically present while existentially absent

The book defines participation as a composite rather than a switch. To participate is to bring attention, perception, interpretation, feeling, embodiment, relation, choice, action, reflection, and integration to bear, and because these can be present in varying measure, participation comes in degrees. The consequence the book draws is a diagnostic one: a person can satisfy the outward marks of being involved — bodily in the room, cognitively engaged, even highly productive — while being existentially absent from the very thing they are inside. Presence of the body is therefore not evidence of participation, and the gap between the two is where the book locates its central human criterion, the measure by which it asks whether a person is actually in their life or only attending it.

## Substantiation

This claim arose in the reframe conversation as the model's structured articulation, engaged by the author; it is recorded as a candidate proposition and has not been substantiated against a primary reading.

- The outline defines participation as the composite it later grades: participation "includes" attention, perception, interpretation, feeling, embodiment, relation, choice, action, reflection, and integration (reframe conversation, § participation).
- It states the degrees as three graded failures of presence: "A person may be physically present but not participating," "They may be cognitively engaged but existentially absent," and "They may be highly productive while deeply alienated" (reframe conversation, § participation).
- It names the stake: "This gives the project its central human criterion" (reframe conversation, § participation) — the measure of whether participation is happening rather than merely its outward signs.
- The claim is the individual-side complement of the practice lineages' shared law that the vault already holds: nothing transforms from the audience ([[nothing transforms from the audience]]). A person who is present but existentially absent is in the audience of their own experience, which is precisely the position that transforms nothing.
