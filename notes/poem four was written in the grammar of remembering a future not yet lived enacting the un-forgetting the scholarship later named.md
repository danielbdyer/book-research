# poem four was written in the grammar of remembering a future not yet lived enacting the un-forgetting the scholarship later named

This is the origin story performing itself in miniature, and it hands the reader the book's founding promise as a fact about their own mind rather than a doctrine to accept. The author wrote a whole poem from the future, narrating ages he had not reached as though already remembered, and revealed only at the end that none of it had happened yet — the front-matter promise, "you already know this," executed as a verb tense before it was ever a sentence. The reader who has ever known something before they could have learned it, recognized "the answer I had always had," has touched the oldest philosophy of East and West at once. Your moments of "I already knew that" are not naïveté; they are the thing the book is named for, arriving unbidden. Its use to the writing is the capstone of the origin story — the book's law that it performs what it describes was obeyed by a poem years before the law was written.
