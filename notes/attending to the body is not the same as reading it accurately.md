# attending to the body is not the same as reading it accurately

The interoception literature split body-listening into three measurable dimensions, and the split is load-bearing for a book whose verification standard is bodily. *Accuracy* is "performance on objective behavioural tests of heartbeat detection"; *sensibility* is "self-evaluated assessment of subjective interoception, gauged using interviews/questionnaires"; *awareness* is "metacognitive awareness of interoceptive accuracy, e.g. confidence–accuracy correspondence" (Garfinkel, Seth, Barrett, Suzuki & Critchley 2015). The finding: "all three dimensions were distinct and dissociable. Interoceptive accuracy was only partly predicted by interoceptive awareness and interoceptive sensibility" — and confidence tracked performance only in the participants with the greatest accuracy. A person can attend to their body constantly, rate themselves highly attuned, and read the signal inaccurately — that profile exists, dissociation is the norm rather than the exception, and self-report cannot detect it from inside.

This is the book's second reader given an empirical spine. The fluent reader who "has the entire vocabulary and uses that fluency to avoid feeling anything all the way through" is, in this literature's terms, high sensibility with unknown accuracy — rich subjective attunement that no amount of introspection can certify. The felt-shift standard's exposure is now precise: the readers most confident in their bodily reading are not thereby the readers reading well, and the correspondence between confidence and competence is itself the last thing to develop. The practical consequence runs the same direction as the corpus's own titration note: the standard needs external anchors — repetition, time, the relational field's second check — exactly because the internal signal cannot vouch for its own calibration.

## Substantiation

- Measurement honesty travels with the claim: the heartbeat-counting task behind many accuracy scores is contested — critics argue scores are contaminated by beliefs about resting heart rate (Zamariola et al. 2018), rebuttals followed, and newer discrimination-based methods are preferred. The three-dimension model stands; the accuracy *instruments* are unsettled, and the claim is held at the model's strength rather than any single task's.
- The anxiety connection is recorded as program-level rather than as a statistic: later work in the same program ties trait anxiety to the sensibility-over-accuracy discrepancy — overestimating one's bodily reading — but the exact figures live in the follow-up papers and were not verified at this depth.
- The standard sensibility instrument is the MAIA (Mehling et al. 2012); the corpus's own counterweight note already reported the signal "muted precisely where the folds are deepest," and this literature adds the sharper point that the mutedness can be invisible to its owner.
