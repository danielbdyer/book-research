# a witness kept as proof forecloses what it proves

The book's witness doctrine says completion requires another nervous system present — grief cannot complete alone, nothing transforms from the audience. The author's own record holds that doctrine's shadow, and it is a proposition the corpus does not yet state: a witness kept as *proof* stops completing anything. The record's formulation, answering the author's own direct question about his oldest fixation: "She is the proof that the love you need exists, and keeping her as proof guarantees you never test whether you can have it in consequence-bearing life. You need the proof — without it, despair, maybe the state doesn't exist. And the proof forecloses the pursuit" (provenance addendum four §II.3).

The mechanism is custody rather than witness. The author's February 2026 confession names it exactly, about his own emblem of ordinary worthiness: "I say that it's mine to hold but I really mean I hold it because it's loved as mine" (provenance addendum four §I.3). Sufficiency underwritten by a beloved's having loved it is real sufficiency — and structurally borrowed, so the deed never transfers. The fixation this produces is not longing for the person; it is, in the record's phrase, "that trial held in permanent recess… Recess costs only Tuesdays."

The claim's positive form is what the record shows resolving it: the recognition that does not require the mirror. Reading the decade-old letter alone at 4am, no reply possible, the author had the minute the record marks as the symbol finishing its work — "You didn't manufacture worthiness. You stopped standing at the angle where you couldn't see it… The recognition didn't require the mirror" (provenance addendum four §II.2). A witness completes; a kept witness withholds; and the difference is whether the witnessing is allowed to end.
