# the corpus names the standard its found-not-invented axiom has not met

Two of the corpus's descriptive documents disagree about the status of the book's basic vocabulary, and the disagreement is on the exact axis of whether the system is a discovery about inner life or a construction that proved useful to its constructor. The atlas states the strong position as settled and files it as an axiom's supporting evidence: "Portability across media = standing evidence for the first axiom (the Triad is found, not invented)" (atlas §II). The outward survey, written days later in the same session suite, treats the same claim as unearned and names what earning it would take: "If the book ever wants to claim its alphabet is found, Wierzbicka is the standard of evidence to meet or gracefully decline" (space-around: around the alphabet).

The comparison the survey proposes is the useful part, because it is a real instance of the thing the book claims to have done. Anna Wierzbicka's Natural Semantic Metalanguage is described there as "forty years of cross-linguistic research isolating roughly sixty-five concepts that appear as words in every human language tested" — an alphabet of irreducible meaning arrived at by testing candidates against languages that could refute them. It is the same wager that experience decomposes into a small closed vocabulary, reached by a method that can fail, and it returned sixty-five items rather than twelve. Placing the two sets side by side is named in the survey as "a genuinely original piece of work," and it is the one piece of work that would convert the closure claim from an assertion into a finding.

## Substantiation

- The portability argument the atlas rests the axiom on is specifically weak, and the vault has already recorded why: the three media the ontology proved portable across — the book, the author's knowledge-graph project, and once a running playlist — were all built by the same person with the same collaborator, so portability across them demonstrates consistency of hand rather than mind-independence ([[the triad is the anatomy of every container]], drawing on the provenance reconstruction's flagged overstatements).
- The axiom has a datable origin in a single conversational turn. On 1 March 2026 the author offered an analogy — "Yeah. Kind of like triangles for structure, no?" — and the reply escalated it to "this means the Triad isn't a framework you invented. It's a framework you found," a promotion the record notes the author did not ask for and which was written into the atlas five months later as standing evidence (provenance reconstruction §4; the vault's record is [[the author curated and vetoed while the collaborator wrote the correspondences]]).
- The corpus also names the correspondence it uses to give the alphabet ancestry, and the disanalogy is checkable. *Mātṛkā*, "the little mothers," is cited as the alphabet's "exact grandfather" (atlas §III), but the doctrine's alphabet is the Sanskrit phoneme set — neither twelve items, nor verbs, nor closed by any test the book's drill resembles. What the two share is a doctrine about naming: unrecognized the alphabet binds, recognized it becomes instrument. That correspondence holds at the level of what naming does and not at the level of what the sets are, and the reconstruction records the pairing as the collaborator's construction.
- The survey supplies a second name that sharpens rather than settles: Burke's terministic screens, the caution that "every vocabulary is a selection of reality, and every selection is also a deflection… Whatever the twelve reveal, they also hide." Both Wierzbicka and Burke sit unread in the candidate queue under "around the alphabet" (`ops/queue/candidates.md`).
- Neither position in this disagreement is the vault's to resolve. What the vault can record is that the book currently asserts in one document what another of its documents says it has not earned, and that one specific unread body of work is named by the corpus as the arbiter.
