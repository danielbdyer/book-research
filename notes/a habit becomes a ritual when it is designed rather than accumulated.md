# a habit becomes a ritual when it is designed rather than accumulated

The gesture asserts that the difference between a habit and a ritual is whether it was designed, and that daily life can be composed of small designed repetitions. The vault does not hold this, and the gap is larger than one gesture. Searching the four descriptive sources for habit returns nothing: the corpus has a practice curriculum of twelve kata, a stance to return to, and a metabolic arc, and it has no account of repetition itself — of what happens when a practice becomes automatic, or whether automaticity is the goal or the failure.

This note is a stub. Its state is `nascent`: the proposition is the gesture's, not the vault's, and it is recorded so the territory is reachable from the graph rather than only from the inventory in `ops/open corpus.md`. The gesture is a named unit of the earliest contents that no document develops; naming a territory is evidence the project has been to its edge, not evidence it belongs in the book.

## What the corpus supplies

- Habit appears three times in the abridged edition and once in the earliest contents, and zero times in the atlas, the walked document, the first telling, and the survey. The abridged uses are ordinary rather than technical, as in the integration material's "restructuring old habits" (abridged ch12).
- The kata material comes closest and points the other way: it holds that a form is practised "until the form is no longer practice; it is who one is" (abridged ch14), which asserts automaticity as the goal without examining it.
- The corpus's verification standard is a felt shift, which is by definition a non-automatic event. Whether a practice that has become automatic can still be verified is unasked.

## What would close it

Closing it means stating whether an automatic practice still counts under the book's own somatic test.
