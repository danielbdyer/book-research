# presence-language may radiate significance the sentence has not earned

The book's register leans on a vocabulary of presence — presence, participation, the felt, the lived, coming home to oneself — and Adorno's charge against exactly this vocabulary is that its words work as aura rather than as argument. On his account the auratic word radiates a gravity independent of the content of the sentence it stands in, so that pronouncing it *feels* like meaning it and the reader takes the pleasure of the prose for the truth of the claim. The mechanism has three moving parts the dig names: tone certifies content, so nothing falsifiable need be asserted; immediacy is sacralized, so the lived outranks the concept that would test it; and the whole is non-falsifiable, because a word determinate enough to be false is not being used. The tension for this book is that its foundations require this register — the felt shift is a somatic event that plain reportage cannot carry — while the register is the precise material the charge indicts, so the site is most exposed where the prose is most eloquent.

This note holds the attack as a live tension rather than a settled verdict, because the charge has a documented return edge recorded separately, and because the register earns a defense rather than only a wince. The tension is not that the book's prose is beautiful; it is that beauty of this specific kind may be doing the work an argument declines to do.

## Substantiation

- The dig reconstructs Adorno's mechanism from scholarship and turns it on the genre the commission names (a facilitator's presence-book promising return-to-self). It records the charge's sharpest form as a non-falsifiability that rereads objection as the objector's own inauthenticity — in the dig's paraphrase of Adorno, "a 'real' and a 'faked' authenticity cannot be distinguished" (transmission dig 6: FINDINGS; the phrase is Adorno's as recorded at [S], the primary unopened). Reproduced here as the one short quotation the note carries.
- The depth is honest and thin: dig 6 reached no clean primary read of Adorno, so this note stands on the SEP entry and one secondary reading and is marked `nascent`. Whether Adorno supplies any criterion separating legitimate presence-language from jargon is the reading queued at dig 6, READINGS TO QUEUE item 2; the answer decides whether the treatment below has a real gate or only a mood.
- The treatment under trial is the survivability catalog's first line: presence-language is keepable only where every auratic word sits beside a determinate claim it would be false without. This is a determinacy test the book can run on its own sentences, and it converges with the plain-prose pass already under trial for the adjacent posture problem.
- The charge has a return edge: it indicts its own author's prose and supplies no criterion exempting legitimate presence-language from jargon, which is why the tension stays `live` rather than resolving into a prohibition.
