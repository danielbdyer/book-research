# the huatou is an anti-page that reduces the whole case to one un-annotatable word

Dahui Zonggao (1089–1163), the heir of the Blue Cliff Record's commentator, met the layered book with a subtraction and a replacement. He destroyed available copies of his teacher's masterwork (the destruction is held at [[beauty recruits the wrong posture]]), and in its place he formulated *kanhua Chan*, the method of observing a single critical phrase. The whole ornate case collapses to one word — the *huatou*, most famously Zhaozhou's *wu*, "no" — and the practitioner sits with only that, uncommented. The most margin-rich book form ever built was answered by its own lineage with an object that carries no margin at all: a single word cannot be annotated, and that is the point of choosing it.

The finding is directional. Where the Blue Cliff Record multiplies voices around a case to keep it open, the huatou removes every voice and hands the practitioner one thing that admits no commentary. It names the opposite pole of the margin question — not how many voices a page can hold, but the un-annotatable object a page can hand instead. Its cost, the dig records, is that the object becomes mechanical, decaying into rote repetition of the single word.
