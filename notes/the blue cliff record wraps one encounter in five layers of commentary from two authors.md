# the blue cliff record wraps one encounter in five layers of commentary from two authors

The Blue Cliff Record surrounds a single teaching encounter with more commentary than any comparable book form. A case is wrapped in nested voices built by two authors across a century. Xuedou (980–1052) selected a hundred old cases and composed a verse for each, treating the encounters "as literature that has the power to awaken." Yuanwu (1063–1135) then added three further layers: a pointer that sets the reader's doubt before the case is read, a prose commentary on the case and on the verse, and interlinear capping phrases — one short, angled remark set into each line. The result is a stack — pointer, case, verse, prose, and the capping phrases threaded between — that a reader passes through in order.

The claim about the form is that its margin is not explanation but provocation multiplied. Yuanwu's capping phrases deepen the paradox rather than dissolve it; each is a jab that keeps the case open. Where the Talmud page runs two voices toward reconciliation, the Blue Cliff Record runs a chorus of voices whose office is to prevent the reader from settling the case discursively. It is the upper bound of the margin-rich book, and its own lineage later treated that richness as a danger.
