# the closing prompt changes genre where the book turns to praxis

Every unit of the executed draft but one ends with a short second-person instruction set off from the prose. Everywhere up to and including the Stance these instructions are uniform in kind: they direct attention to a condition already present in the reader and explicitly decline to ask for more. The Generative Triad ends "Just notice. You do not have to fix anything yet"; the Alphabet of the Soul, "Notice without changing anything. The noticing is the practice"; the Three Loops, "Don't try to fix anything. Just locate." Nothing is deferred and nothing is assigned.

At the Kata and the Engineer's Eye the genre changes. The Kata ends "Choose one. Try it today. Tomorrow, try another," and the Engineer's Eye ends "Tomorrow, try the Stack Scan once." These are tasks with a horizon, and they are the only two in the book. The switch coincides exactly with the point where the arc stops presenting architecture and begins issuing practice, which the treatment marks as the part where "invitations become instructions" (arc-gloss: part v).

The transferable point is that the book has a working rule it never states: an instruction may ask for attention anywhere in the arc, and may ask for an action only after the reader has been given something to do it with. A prompt that assigns a task before the practice material would be borrowing authority the arc has not yet earned. This is checkable on any new unit by reading its last three sentences and asking whether the reader could comply without leaving the page.

## Substantiation

- The full set of closing instructions, by named unit, in the executed order: the House with No Ceiling, "Pause. Let this settle. There is no hurry."; the Ink That Listens, "Think now of one person who, when you are with them, you become more yourself"; the Generative Triad, "Just notice"; the Alphabet of the Soul, "Notice without changing anything"; the Molecules of Experience, "Don't try to fix anything. Just see."; the Three Loops, "Don't try to fix anything. Just locate."; the Third Entity, "What does the Third need from you right now?"; Erotic-Spiritual Intelligence, "Where in your life are you trying to collapse a tension that wants to be held?"; the Way of Ripening, "What in your life is composting right now?"; the Psychedelic Moment, "Remain here. Let the chapter settle."; the Afterglow, "what did you do with it? What might you do differently, now, with the next one?"; the Stance, "Right now: thirty seconds. No agenda."; the Kata, "Choose one. Try it today."; the Engineer's Eye, "Tomorrow, try the Stack Scan once."
- The Geometry of Becoming is the single unit with no closing instruction. It ends on a transition into the architecture material instead — "We step back now to see the shape" — which is consistent with its recorded job as the hinge that hands the reader from felt experience to the named map.
- The prose measurements corroborate the same turn. Mean sentence length across the fifteen units is 12.4 words; the Kata is the shortest in the book at 7.8 words and the Stance the second shortest at 10.0. The two units that carry the instruction register are also the two whose sentences are most compressed, so the genre change is visible in the syntax as well as in the imperative.
- The atlas records a tension this pattern bears on directly: the book's forward momentum works against the stopping its practices require, treated with thresholds that require action before the text continues (atlas §XI.1b, carried at [[forward hunger defeats the pause]]). The executed draft does not use gate practices; its closing instructions are all skippable, which leaves that treatment untried.
