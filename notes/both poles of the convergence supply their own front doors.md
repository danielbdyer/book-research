# both poles of the convergence supply their own front doors

The author has named the design problem: the road into the valley, and a road to Heidegger, for a Western reader who may find neither comfortable. The useful finding is that both traditions built their own front doors, so the book's roads can begin on paths the traditions themselves paved.

**The valley's doors.** Kṣemarāja opens the Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam by saying whom it is for: those of tender understanding, who have not worked through the difficult śāstras of logic. The tradition's pocket summa is, by its own announcement, the beginner's text — and the corpus's reading list already chose it. The second door is the Vijñāna Bhairava, whose practices run through exactly the day this book cares about: the delight of seeing a friend after long absence (v. 71), the savor of food and drink (v. 72), absorption in song (v. 73), the edges of sneeze, terror, and hunger (v. 118). Faults to name at this door: the loveliest modern rendering, Lorin Roche's Radiance Sutras, is a poetic re-imagining rather than a translation and should be offered as such, with Jaideva Singh's edition as the sober companion; the word tantra now sells things in the West the valley would not recognize; and the extraction economy around Indian contemplative goods is real — the author's own stewardship line, that he does not wish to co-opt the ideas for his benefit, is recorded at the tension and belongs printed at this door.

**Heidegger's doors.** He wrote his own, twice over. The Memorial Address at Messkirch (1955) is Heidegger explaining releasement toward things and openness to mystery to the ordinary people of his hometown — no apparatus, one hour. "The Thing" (1950) and "Building Dwelling Thinking" (1951) do their philosophy with a jug, a bridge, and a farmhouse, which is the register this book already lives in. George Steiner's short study and Hubert Dreyfus's lectures are honest secondary escorts. Faults to name at this door: the prose fortress is real, and no general reader should be sent into Being and Time unescorted; the opposite risk is a Heidegger made soothing — Thomas Sheehan's deflationary reading stands as the guard against mysticizing him; and the stain is named at the door or the door is dishonest — the escorts for that, Celan, Arendt, and Adorno, are already seated in the book's own company ([[martin heidegger]]).

## Substantiation

- The Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam's opening states its intended reader in its own words; the fact that the tradition's most accessible text is the one already on the book's reading list means the corpus chose the front door before this note named it.
- The Vijñāna Bhairava verse numbers follow Singh's edition; the description governs where editions differ.
- Both doors serve the treatment under trial at [[the tradition the book owes most has no chair in its company]] — the road into the valley — and the Heidegger door serves the same road on the Western side.
