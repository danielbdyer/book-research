# internal family systems

Internal Family Systems (IFS) is Richard Schwartz's therapy model, currently among the most influential clinical frameworks in the book's likely readership. The space-around survey identifies it as the largest single near-neighbor not yet addressed anywhere in the project, and warns that its absence "will eventually look like an omission." It is not yet seated; this note is the candidate file.

The correspondence is close at every joint. IFS holds that the psyche is naturally multiple; that there are no bad parts; that the parts causing trouble are protectors running outdated jobs, guarding exiles who carry the original pain; and that beneath all parts is Self — calm, curious, compassionate presence that does not need to be built, only unblended. Mapped to the book: protectors correspond to the Gatekeeper (the book's image for the part that guards a contraction); no-bad-parts corresponds to the contraction doctrine; unblending corresponds to the book's practice of meeting the protective part with respect rather than force; and Self corresponds to the Stance, which the sources describe in nearly identical terms.

The survey's recommendation is positional rather than adoptive: the book does not need to incorporate IFS; it needs to know and state where it stands relative to it, and what it offers that IFS does not. The survey answers the second question itself: the book adds the container anatomy, the molecule formulas, the somatic verification standard, and a relational field treated as first-class rather than as technique.

## What the evaluation requires

Read Schwartz's model description in the primary texts; verify the four correspondences; draft the positioning statement (what the book adds; where the models genuinely differ); recommend whether IFS enters as an interlocutor seat, a stated relative in the book's text, or both. This work has not been done.
