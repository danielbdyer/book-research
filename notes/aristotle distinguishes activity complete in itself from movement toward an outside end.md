# aristotle distinguishes activity complete in itself from movement toward an outside end

The book's practice clause holds that a practice expresses realization rather than being a means to it ([[the practice is the expression of realization rather than a means to it]]). Aristotle draws the same line as a metaphysical distinction: some activities contain their end within themselves and are complete at every moment — seeing, living, flourishing — while others are mere movement toward an end outside them and are incomplete until finished, as building is. Practice of the first kind is not a means; it is already its own completion. With Dōgen's *shusho-itto* and Deci and Ryan's undermining effect, Aristotle makes the clause a three-tradition arrival — Zen, experimental psychology, and Greek philosophy — which is the caliber of company the convergence centerpiece is built from. The reading is not done, so the claim is privated.

## Substantiation

- The book's half is on record: the anti-instrumentality clause constrains every exercise in the curriculum (walked IX; [[the practice is the expression of realization rather than a means to it]]).
- Aristotle's half is named, not shown: the *energeia*/*kinesis* distinction is at *Metaphysics* Θ.6 (roughly 1048b18–36, "at the same time we are living well and have lived well"), and the *praxis*/*poiesis* distinction is at *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.4–5. Both are to verify at the text; neither is quoted from memory. Aristotle is in the vault for *steresis*, read at his own text on 2026-08-09, and for the recognition-scene; *energeia* is an unmined part of him.
- The refusal is kept, and it is the same shape the privation reading already found. Aristotle's *energeia* is actualization under a teleological metaphysics, which sits closer to the book's genuine-novelty fork than to recognition-as-un-forgetting, so he warrants the structure of the clause and not the recognition frame around it — as [[aristotle on privation]] warranted the absence-form and not the therapeutic step.
