# the encounter is the fundamental unit where ontology becomes lived

The conversation's data model settles on one entity as especially important: the encounter. The reasoning is a contrast with the other entities the system holds. A concept can exist without the person; a tradition can exist before the person; a source can exist independently of the person. The encounter is the one entity that asserts a relation between the person and reality — that "something happened between this person and this reality" — and it is therefore the point at which ontology stops being an inventory of what exists and becomes something lived. The design draws the consequence for transformation directly: the encounter is "the event from which transformation becomes possible," so a system built to support transformation must keep the encounter central rather than let it dissolve into concepts and metadata.

The claim carries a doctrinal load beyond the data model. It locates transformation not in the concept, the tradition, or the source — none of which requires the person's participation — but in the lived meeting, which is the same place the book locates recognition. The design's companion statement guards the entity from both sides: a concept without encounter "can become dead abstraction," and an encounter without concept "can disappear into memory," so the two are kept as a pair in which "encounter gives concepts ancestry."

## Substantiation

- The centrality and its reason, from the section the model titles encounter as central entity: "the encounter is where: ontology becomes experience. A concept can exist without you. A tradition can exist before you. A source can exist independently. But an encounter says: Something happened between this person and this reality. That is the event from which transformation becomes possible" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, line 4729).
- The paired guard, from the adjacent section on encounter as temporality: "A Concept without Encounter can become dead abstraction. An Encounter without Concept can disappear into memory... Encounter gives concepts ancestry."
- The title renders the model's "ontology becomes experience" as ontology becoming lived; the note holds the entity-design as a candidate reconstruction while recording the doctrinal claim it carries, that the lived meeting is where the abstract becomes participable.
