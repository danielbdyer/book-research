# dye holds by the cloth's mordant rather than by the brightness of the color

Most natural dyes wash out of cloth that has not been prepared. The fiber must first be treated with a mordant, a metallic salt that forms the chemical bond between dye and fiber, and a brilliantly dyed but unmordanted cloth simply loses its color in the wash. The panel read this as the book's reader-premise stated in a craft's plain terms: persistence tracks the preparation of the receiving structure, not the vividness of what is received, so the dyer's whole answer to "it didn't hold" is work done on the cloth rather than a stronger dye. This sits beside the book's mechanism for why an enormous experience does not last — the state is received by a structure it did not change ([[an enormous experience is interpreted by the structure it did not change]]) — and beside the book's shifted promise to a reader whose breakthrough already faded.

This resonance accompanies the reader-premise rather than testing it: dyeing chemistry is company for the claim that persistence is a property of the receiving structure, not evidence for it.
