# the fundamental unit of authorship is recognition, not the sentence

The conversation reduces the authorial problem to a single unit and names recognition, rather than the sentence, as that unit. A person does not become the author of the material by having typed every word of it; the person becomes the author because something passed through their participation and became seen by them, and the sentence is downstream of that seeing. The consequence for a book written with research tools and a language model is exact: fluent language a person did not recognize is not yet theirs to have authored, and a sentence a person recognized into being is authored even where the drafting was shared. What confers authorship is the moment of recognition, not the act of composition.

- The claim is stated in the conversation of 2026-08-13, in the section the model titles the true unit of authorship: "The fundamental unit of authorship is not the sentence. It is recognition."
- The mechanism, quoted from the same section: "You don't become the author because you typed every word. You become the author because something passed through your participation and became seen by you. The sentence is downstream."
