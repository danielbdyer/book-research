# acceptance is willingness to perceive what is actually here, not approval

The reframe conversation defines acceptance as an act of perception rather than an act of endorsement. Acceptance is the willingness to perceive what is actually present, and it is separated from approval, which would make perceiving conditional on agreeing. On this definition a person can accept a difficult state — see it clearly and admit that it is here — without approving of it, condoning it, or wanting it to remain. The distinction matters for self-facilitation because the practice opens from perceiving what is present, and an acceptance confused with approval would block perception exactly where a state is unwelcome, since a person who must approve before perceiving will refuse to perceive what they cannot approve. The claim places acceptance on the side of recognition, the book's central act of perceiving what is actually there, and it grounds the emotional practice in which a feeling is met and allowed to complete rather than repressed or endorsed, recorded at [[the affective practice completes a feeling's descent with consent between repression and venting]].
