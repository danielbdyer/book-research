# the third entity's failure mode is withdrawal and its core skill is staying

The vault's relational-field topic map already holds three things: that the field between two people is real and does work, that grief requires a witness in order to complete, and the book's image of intimacy as a tether. The executed draft adds the doctrine a drafting session would quote before any of them, and no note had recorded it. On the failure mode: "Most relationships break not at the moments of conflict but at the moments of withdrawal… The Third can survive most things; it cannot survive prolonged withdrawal" (abridged ch8). On the skill: "Most of the practical work of love is the practice of staying" (abridged ch8); the treatment's storyboard for the same chapter specifies "staying present installed as the core relational skill" (arc-gloss: ch8).

Both sentences are held under draft standing, which means they are quoted for what the drafts say and never credited for what they argue. What earns the claim its place is that the two states of the draft agree: the storyboard specified the skill and the executed chapter then wrote it. The vault's per-chapter comparison at `ops/chapter concordance.md` calls that relation between two states of one element "carried unchanged into", and here it holds at the level of doctrine rather than of a single passage.

## Substantiation

- Abridged ch8 carries both sentences, quoted above; arc-gloss ch8 carries the storyboard instruction.
- The failure mode states in the negative what the field's work requires, because whatever the field metabolizes it can only metabolize while both people remain inside it ([[the field between two people does work]], the claim that two people in genuine contact generate a third participant which digests what neither can digest alone).
- The staying skill is the relational form of the book's basic practice posture, which is defined as a point of return rather than a state, so that falling out of it and coming back counts as the practice; staying applies that definition to the space between two people rather than the space inside one ([[the basic practice posture is a point of return rather than a state]]).

**A boundary the doctrine needs was added on 2026-08-06.** The demand-withdraw research documents a pattern in which one partner's pursuit is the mechanism of the other's withdrawal, each move precipitating the other's, with worse outcomes measured across 74 studies (Christensen and Heavey, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 59(1), 1990; Schrodt, Witt and Shimkowski's meta-analysis, *Communication Monographs* 81(1), 2014, N = 14,255, r = .360). Staying that the other person experiences as demand therefore feeds the failure rather than repairing it, and the chapter's "practice of staying" has to be distinguished from pursuit or it becomes the pattern it was meant to prevent. Bowen's counter-frame is kept beside that finding: he reads distance and cutoff as symptoms of undifferentiated fusion, and prescribes a better-differentiated self remaining in contact rather than more pressure toward togetherness (*Family Therapy in Clinical Practice*, 1978). His prescription is close to the claim at [[the reader's working posture is solitude held inside relationship]], where the corpus asks its reader for solitude held inside relationship, and it gives the Minuchin-and-Bowen reading already listed in `ops/reading queue.md` a second question to answer. Both findings were verified at web depth, and the trace is `archive/research — counterpoint round background.md`.
