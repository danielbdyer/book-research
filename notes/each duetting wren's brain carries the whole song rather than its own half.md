# each duetting wren's brain carries the whole song rather than its own half

Andean plain-tailed wrens sing male-female duets so tightly interleaved that one bird's part sounds broken when it is heard alone. Recordings from the birds' premotor song circuits show that the neurons respond more strongly to the complete duet than to the bird's own contribution, so each brain carries the joint song rather than only its half. The panel offered this as a witness, from electrophysiology and outside the human record entirely, for the book's claim that the field between two people is a real participant doing work neither party can do alone ([[the field between two people does work]]). Its value is that it carries no stake any selection of agreeable traditions could have introduced: the birds were not gathered for the property they are then reported to share.

This resonance accompanies the field claim rather than testing it: an animal-model finding is company for the claim that a joint structure is carried in each participant, not evidence that a human relational field does work.
