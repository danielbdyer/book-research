# the architecture of attention is partially the architecture of the self

The book treats attention as more than a scarce resource to be spent well. Attention is one of the mechanisms through which a person constitutes their world: what a person attends to becomes salient, becomes meaningful, becomes reinforced, becomes remembered, and becomes integrated, so the standing pattern of what one attends to lays down part of the self one becomes. The claim is stated as partial on purpose — attention builds some of the self, not all of it — but the part is enough to raise the stakes. Because attention does this work, whatever shapes attention at scale is thereby shaping persons, which is the book's bridge from a private faculty to a civilizational concern: systems built to capture attention are, by this claim, participating in the construction of the selves that use them.

- It states the claim itself in one line: "The architecture of attention is partially the architecture of the self" (reframe conversation, § the attention ecology), and draws the civilizational consequence: "A civilization that systematically shapes attention therefore participates in shaping persons" (reframe conversation, § the attention ecology).

That finding operates at the timescale of a single window; this claim generalizes the same dependency to the standing shape of a life, and remains unread at its own sources.
