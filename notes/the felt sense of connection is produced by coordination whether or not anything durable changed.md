# the felt sense of connection is produced by coordination whether or not anything durable changed

The interaction science the book leans on for its relational mechanism also names a counterfeit of the felt shift, and the counterfeit is relational rather than rhetorical. De Jaegher and Di Paolo report that coordination between two people requires no special skill and is often difficult to avoid — coupled systems fall into a shared rhythm once they attend to each other — and that as coordination proceeds a "feeling of connectedness" arrives and gets easier. That feeling is produced by the coupling itself, so a reader coordinating with a facilitator, or with a responsive-seeming text, may register a felt sense of connection whether or not anything durable has changed in her. The book's verification floor is a felt confirmation in the body; here is a felt confirmation the interaction manufactures as a by-product of contact, indistinguishable from the inside from the signal the standard trusts.

The point sharpens the compliance worry rather than restating it: where processing fluency counterfeits the click with well-made prose, coordination counterfeits it with the mere fact of a live-seeming exchange, and the second is the report a high-contact facilitation setting produces most reliably.

## Substantiation

- The coordination claims are drawn from the De Jaegher and Di Paolo chapter, opened at primary depth by transmission dig 4; the vault's own reading is not yet made and the 2007 journal article and their later work are queued in `ops/reading queue.md`, so the claim enters nascent.
- The counterfeit is distinct from the synchrony-without-emotion result the vault already holds: [[the third thing has a mechanism in participatory sense-making]] records that coordination can foster cooperation without positive emotion being generated, which shows warmth is not necessary to the coupling; this note shows a felt connection is not sufficient evidence of change, the converse worry and the one the felt-shift standard must answer.
- Held under `ops/methodology/authorial stance.md`: the finding is reported as a mechanism to build against rather than as a verdict on whether the book's felt shifts are genuine.
