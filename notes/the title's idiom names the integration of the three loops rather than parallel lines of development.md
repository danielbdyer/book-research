# the title's idiom names the integration of the three loops rather than parallel lines of development

The book's title is the one unit every reader meets first, and the vault has held two accounts of what it asserts without noticing that they differ. The executed prose defines the idiom structurally: "'Firing on all cylinders' is the felt condition in which all three are functioning and integrated" (abridged ch7), the three being the body loop, the identity loop, and the continuity loop of the book's engine, and the atlas carries the same cylinder equation in its image system (atlas §X.5e). The Wilber chair reads the title otherwise: "lines of development — multiple semi-independent capacities maturing at different rates — is the premise the book's title asserts and never argues" ([[ken wilber]]). Those are two different schematics. The book's own definition is an integration of three functions running as loops — a parts-and-cycle structure — and it belongs to the layer the author demoted to scaffolding ([[the system is scaffolding rather than the centerpiece]]). Wilber's lines are parallel developmental capacities at different altitudes, a levels structure, and no note in the vault holds them.

So the title rests, by the book's own definition, on a demoted schematic, and the one chair that reads it as Wilber's has assigned the premise to a doctrine the vault has never read. The claim here is only that the two readings are different and that the book's own is the loops. Whether the idiom would also fit Wilber's lines, and whether the author means both, is a reading and a sounding.

## Substantiation

- The definition is verbatim at abridged ch7 and is recorded, with the atlas's cylinder equation, at [[the scheme's upper layers are one construction built on the container's three structures]], which also records the engine's theological face — the three cylinders as *icchā*, *jñāna*, *kriyā*, "a triadic theology in mechanic's clothing."
- The Wilber attribution is the mesh grid's ("addendum, Wilber properly seated") carried onto the chair; no primary Wilber has been read, and the chair says so.
- The two schematics are sorted in the survey of the vault's practice-models: the three loops as a loop-and-parts structure the book demotes ([[distress is a question about machinery not a verdict about character]]), Wilber's lines as a holarchy-family structure the vault does not carry (`ops/practice-model ecology.md`, section 3).
- What this does not establish: that the loops are the better reading, or that the demotion reaches the title. The scaffolding decision demotes the schemes as architecture; it does not say what a reader takes the title to promise.
