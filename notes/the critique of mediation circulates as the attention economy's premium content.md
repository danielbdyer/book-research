# the critique of mediation circulates as the attention economy's premium content

The steelman's sharpest turn against the why-now is that its own success refutes it. Han's aphorisms circulate virally, Odell's argument for refusal is a bestseller, and Rosa's case against acceleration is an eight-hundred-page commodity. The critique of mediation thrives as the attention economy's premium content, distributed by and profiting from the very circuits it condemns. The form Karataş gives the objection is that were the diagnosis true — were this mediation genuinely corrosive of attention and depth — the critique could not circulate so frictionlessly through it, so its fluency inside the medium is evidence against the medium's alleged toxicity.

The contradiction is the book's own. A book diagnosing commodified distraction is itself a commodity in the attention market, sold, ranked, and recommended by the systems it indicts. This is the media-specific form of the hazard the vault already holds as Andrea Jain's gestural subversion — commodified spirituality acknowledges the harms of the system it lives inside and subverts them in gesture only, so even the refusal is colonized ([[andrea jain]]) — aimed now at the critique-of-mediation genre rather than at the spirituality market. The honest consequence is that the book's commercial reach cannot count as evidence of its truth and may be read against it, which is exactly the posture the book's stance already requires: it postulates no efficacy of its own, so a wide readership and a true book stay different situations.
