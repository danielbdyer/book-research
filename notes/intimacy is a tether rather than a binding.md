# intimacy is a tether rather than a binding

The Tethered Intimacies material draws a distinction the rest of the corpus makes only negatively. A binding encloses: it holds two people by restricting what either can be. A tether connects across a distance it does not attempt to close, holding under tension without converting the tension into confinement. The proposition is that intimacy is structurally the second and not the first, so that a relation which begins to enclose has not intensified but changed kind.

The distinction supplies a name the chemistry lacks. The catalogue identifies the failure — a connection with the capacity to separate removed, which the executed draft calls enmeshment — and it identifies trust as a compound. It has no positive name for a connection that stays open, and the tether is that name. It also relocates the measure: closeness is assessed not by proximity but by how much undoing a person can risk in front of someone who stays.

The material is adjacent to two things the vault already holds rather than independent of them, and this note does not claim otherwise. The tether is what the trust compound describes from the inside, and it is the intact form of what enmeshment lacks. Its contribution is the positive statement and the change of measure, not a new component.

## Substantiation

- The distinction in the source's own words: "intimacy was never meant to be a binding, but a tether — something that connects without enclosing, that breathes between two bodies rather than over them. Something golden, supple, slightly taut, responsive to the weather of hearts" (mythic-os: page 13).
- The change of measure: the material bows to "the one that doesn't measure closeness by proximity, but by how safely you can be undone in front of someone who stays present" (mythic-os: page 13).
- The corresponding failure state in the executed draft is stated as a privation: "Connection − Sever-capacity gives you Enmeshment. The medicine is to restore the capacity to Sever, to set difference and boundary, without leaving the connection altogether" (abridged ch8). The tether is that medicine's result, described rather than prescribed.
- The same material holds that the tether's persistence does not depend on the relation continuing in its original form: connections that "grew sideways" toward old loves and sacred friendships are honoured rather than corrected, and are held to "loop back in time, root somewhere else, and still nourish your now" (mythic-os: page 13). This is a claim about severed relations continuing to do work, which the corpus otherwise makes only about grief.
- The material is written prose in the earliest document and appears in no descriptive source: one attestation.
