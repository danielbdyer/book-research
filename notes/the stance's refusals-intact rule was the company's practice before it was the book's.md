# the stance's refusals-intact rule was the company's practice before it was the book's

The rule of keeping every thinker's refusal on the record, so the thinker survives being cited, was a practice the company's own best members had already adopted before the book took it up. Read across all its members, the company keeps its own limits in print beside its findings. Heidegger withdrew the word truth from aletheia in his own voice in 1964, which has been verified here. Gendlin's founding footnote denies the stronger reading of his own standard (1964, fn. 9). Maslow's 1970 preface corrects his own peak toward the plateau. Caputo's evaluation records "a footstep and a guard in one person… a model, enacted at career scale, of keeping one's own refusal in print beside one's finding." Ueda's comparison of Eckhart and Zen "ends by recording a difference, declared by the comparativist with the most standing to declare identity." And James's discipline of two judgments separates what an experience certifies from what it proves.

So the rule about keeping refusals intact is not a courtesy the author extends to his company. It is how the strongest members of that company treated their own findings, which means the book joins an existing practice rather than inventing a scruple. That fact repeats the book's origin story at the level of method, because here too the noticing came first and the company was found afterwards, and it gives the book's note on sources a sentence with six instances standing behind it.

## Substantiation

- The Heidegger and Gendlin instances were verified at their evaluations, Heidegger in the crossover research of 2026-08-03 and Gendlin in the 1964 reading. Maslow's preface and the Caputo, Ueda, and James instances rest on works about those thinkers rather than on the thinkers' own texts, and this claim carries that mixed standing forward.
- The rule this claim generalizes is the authorial stance's, which holds that a thinker's refusal is kept so that the thinker survives being cited, recorded at `ops/methodology/authorial stance.md`.
- Caputo's evaluation already states the pattern for one person, and what is new here is that the same pattern holds across six of them.
