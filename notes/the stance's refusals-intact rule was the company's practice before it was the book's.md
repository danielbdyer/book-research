# the stance's refusals-intact rule was the company's practice before it was the book's

The vault keeps every thinker's refusal so the thinker survives being cited. Read across the company, that rule turns out to be the company's own documented practice. Heidegger withdrew the word truth from aletheia in his own voice (1964, verified). Gendlin's founding footnote denies the stronger reading of his own standard (1964, fn. 9). Maslow's 1970 preface corrects his own peak toward the plateau. Caputo's evaluation records "a footstep and a guard in one person… a model, enacted at career scale, of keeping one's own refusal in print beside one's finding." Ueda's comparison of Eckhart and Zen "ends by recording a difference, declared by the comparativist with the most standing to declare identity." James's two-judgments discipline separates what an experience certifies from what it proves.

So the refusals-intact rule is not a courtesy the author extends to his company; it is how the company's best members treated their own findings, and the book joins a practice rather than inventing a scruple. This performs the origin story at the level of method — noticing first, company after — and it gives the note on sources a sentence with six instances behind it.

## Substantiation

- The Heidegger and Gendlin instances are verified at their evaluations (crossover research 2026-08-03; the 1964 reading); Maslow's preface and the Caputo, Ueda, and James instances are carried on their evaluations from consulted works — a mixed standing the claim inherits.
- The rule this generalizes is the stance's: a thinker's refusal is kept so the thinker survives being cited (`ops/methodology/authorial stance.md`).
- The Caputo evaluation states the pattern for one person; the generalization across six is new.
