# the talmud page runs an explainer voice that clears obstacles and a challenger voice that manufactures them

The Talmud page carries two margin voices that do opposite jobs. Rashi's commentary supplies plain meaning and word-glosses; it removes the obstacle in front of the reader so that reading continues without friction. The Tosafot glosses do the reverse: they treat the whole Talmud as a single work, set a passage against a contradicting passage elsewhere, stop the reader on the contradiction, and only then reconcile the two by drawing a conceptual distinction. One voice clears the path and one voice blocks it. The dig states the failure of each voice alone: Rashi without Tosafot yields fluency with nothing to work against, and Tosafot without Rashi yields friction with no ground to stand on.

This two-job structure is the mechanism a single-voiced book lacks. An explainer keeps a reader moving; a challenger makes a reader stop and reconcile. A margin that only explains and a margin that only provokes are two different instruments with two different costs, and a book designing a second voice for its own pages is choosing between these jobs or dividing them.
