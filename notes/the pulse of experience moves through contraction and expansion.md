# the pulse of experience moves through contraction and expansion

The vault had named only one side of the pulse. Its doctrine of suffering runs on contraction — saṅkoca, fullness folded — and its per-chapter design instrument is identified with spanda, the tradition's word for the pulse of consciousness, yet the vault had never named the complementary movement. The reframe conversation supplies it: alongside saṅkoca it sets vikāsa, expansion, so that the pulse has two movements rather than one. Experience "continually moves through" narrowing and opening, identification and differentiation, integration and expression, withdrawal and re-engagement, and a system faithful to it "should permit both" — it "should know when to concentrate and when to open." The contraction the suffering doctrine describes is thereby one pole of a pulse whose other pole is expansion, and a full account of the movement needs the pole the vault had left unnamed.

## Substantiation

- reframe conversation, 2026-08-13 (transcript line 8222), the section pairing the two Śaiva terms as "another useful polarity": "contraction" and "expansion." It should know when to concentrate and when to open."
- The expansion pole is the reframe's addition to the vault, not a term the vault already carried. The vault's spanda material runs the pulse as an evocative-to-informative ratio at [[the per-chapter dial is a spanda ratio]] and named only the contracting side; this note records expansion (vikāsa) as the movement that completes it.
