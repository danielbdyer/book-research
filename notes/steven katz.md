# steven katz

Steven Katz's 1978 essay states its working hypothesis in one sentence: "There are NO pure (i.e. unmediated) experiences." On his constructivism, a tradition's concepts shape the experiences its practitioners have, so a Christian's union and a Buddhist's śūnyatā are two experiences, not one reality twice reported — and any claimed convergence between traditions is an artifact of description. He is the strongest scholarly opponent the core thesis has, and the scholarly form of a caution the vault already holds in its own words at [[the unanimity claim is an artifact of how the lineages were selected]].

What matters for the book is where his blade lands. Convergence held as proof falls to Katz. Convergence held as company — the stance's actual position, observation without evidentiary remarks — is not his target. His hypothesis forbids the strong reading of the founding isomorph (same experience, two vocabularies) and leaves the weak reading (same word-construction, same gesture) untouched, and the vault's phrasing of that isomorph already lives at the weak reading. The refusal kept is therefore double: he refuses the book the author declined to write, and his presence in the company keeps that book declined.

The reseating of 2026-08-06 aimed him at the verification standard rather than at the convergence, on the expectation that his hypothesis would deliver the deepest available form of the fluency objection: a body trained by the book's vocabulary producing the felt shift in response to the training rather than to anything beyond it. The essay was read on 2026-08-08 and does not deliver it. Katz withdraws evidential force from mystical experience and states on his second page that he is not arguing the experience did not happen, only that no grounds exist for deciding whether its claims are true; he reaches the act of discriminating in a single sentence, and that sentence argues the experiencer is active rather than that the experiencer is wrong ([[katz's objection reaches what a felt shift proves and not whether anything moved]]). The consequence for the sort is that the objection he was recruited to carry is carried better by two other names — the confabulation paper, which exempts present-tense reports of sensation, and Schwitzgebel, who builds the accuracy case on bodily experience by name.

His adversary load survives the reading on different ground than the one it was assigned for, and the new ground costs the book more. Katz holds that contemplative practice does not clear a practitioner's frame but substitutes one conditioned consciousness for another ([[a practice reconditions the frame rather than clearing it]]), which puts the book's practices inside the vocabulary the book installs; and he observes in passing that practices create expectations about what future states of consciousness will be like, calling the arrangement self-fulfilling ([[a practice that describes its destination manufactures the expectation it later confirms]]). Two things about the reading are limits rather than findings. The essay's comparative body, where his method is actually run against Jewish, Buddhist, Christian and Sufi material, was sampled rather than read, so a later test of the book's founding isomorph against his method still needs those pages. And his own refusal is kept: he declines to rank traditions and argues for reading each in its own terms, which is not the same as arguing that inner report is impossible.
