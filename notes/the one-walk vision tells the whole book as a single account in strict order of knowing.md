# the one-walk vision tells the whole book as a single account in strict order of knowing

The forest panel's third vision proposed a short continuous book with no chapters, only marked rests, telling one true sequence in strict order of knowing: the big experience that did not hold, the fluent years, the room in 2017 where the participation law was lived before it had a name, the noticing, and then, three-quarters of the way through, the discovery of company — so the founding convergence enters the book at the moment it entered the life. Its named model is the author's own, Augustine's Confessions, and its last line is the act of sending the book. The reader is asked to do nothing visible: the verification standard is demonstrated rather than delegated, which is this vision's answer to whether the standard can bear reader-side weight, since it declines to place any there.

This vision stays a recorded candidate beside the correspondence the author chose, its assumptions marked as losable bets. It settles the person fully to the first; it runs on a recognition-plot engine, so the reader turns pages to learn whether the writer actually changes; and the poems appear where they happened, dated. Its wager is the seam the author's own statement already names — the difference between "watch me change" and "this changes people" — with the fluent reader trying to collapse the two. What the vision makes urgent is the registered claim it leans on and the vault has not established: that the territory cannot be verified from outside itself.
