# the sixteenth poem returns the book's language to the life it came from

The author flags poem sixteen as the divergent one — the single poem in the collection that does not predate the book. What diverges is the direction of flow: where the corpus quotes the first fifteen, the sixteenth speaks the book's language back into the life, about the move across the country and the friendships the move thinned. It is the collection's one enactment record, and nearly every doctrine the book holds appears in it at the scale of a text message.

The correspondences are exact and dense. The caretaking structure the book renders as architecture appears as "a cathedral of care / so intricate / so considered / so acoustically beautiful / that my longing never had to arrive naked" — and the corpus's cathedral-making mind, which arrives too late at the cusp, is this cathedral. The naked sentence the book's witnessed round exists to meet is given three times over: "you matter to me / i miss you / i want to stay in the thread." The distinction between preservation and aliveness — the archive preserves, the orchard ripens — is run on love itself: "love will sit in a drawer and wait for you / love is patient / love is a saint / but current — / current is tuesday / current is game night / current is 1:13 a.m. / current is look at this bird." The somatic countersign files its report in a homecoming: "the home where people still say my name / like they mean it / and my body — when I heard my name said like that / my body filed a report and the report said / oh. oh that's what left. not love. / current." And the passage from the perfectible word to the answerable one — the corpus's blade — is lived rather than stated: "the courage now is the size of a text… without ceremony / without manuscript / without the careful architecture of preparation," followed by "the trembling that is the sound of the sentence leaving / the architecture."

The poem ends on the book's own ending, enacted: "and the wanting sentence will enter the room undressed and it will tremble / and it will tremble / and it will tremble and then — / it won't." The trembling before the answer, and its release — vaikharī, at the scale of a tuesday.

## Substantiation

- The bird is the tell that this is one continuous body of work rather than a borrowing: "current is look at this bird" reprises poem twelve's "we saw it because you saw it and you shared it to me" — the poet quoting his own earlier poem's finding about shared seeing, now with the book's vocabulary available to name it.
- "Without manuscript" is recorded with its full weight: the poem locates the courage below the scale of the manuscript entirely, which bears on the manuscript's own unreleased condition — the enactment the book asks for is shown happening at the size of a text, while the seventeen chapters rest ([[seventeen chapters rest at madhyama]]).
- The poem also carries the move itself — "i moved across the country a few years ago… i will not vanish to stay connected" — and the resolution the provenance record found live in the author's completion conversations of July 2026, which makes the poem the artifact of the arc recorded at [[the book was written across the author's move to seattle to live in earnest]].
