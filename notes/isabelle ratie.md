# isabelle ratie

Isabelle Ratié's *Le Soi et l'Autre* (Brill, 2011), reviewed as the most important study of the Pratyabhijñā to date, establishes the fact that matters most to this book's treatment of the tradition: the recognition system cannot be reduced to scriptural exegesis. It was built in live argument with Buddhist and Brahmanical opponents, concept by concept, in a specific century. For a book whose far pole has been staged as landscape rather than interlocutor, this is the scholarly warrant that the tradition argues — it earned its positions polemically and can argue back, which is exactly what a chair holds that a watershed cannot.

The refusal kept is the philologist's: the system's concepts were forged against specific opponents, and lifting them out of that argumentative context loses what makes them positions rather than poetry. Her caution aims at exactly the move already recorded in the valley's fourth refusal — the gesture where the tradition argues — and gives it a living scholar's voice.
