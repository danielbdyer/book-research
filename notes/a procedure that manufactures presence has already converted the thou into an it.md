# a procedure that manufactures presence has already converted the thou into an it

Martin Buber located the real not in the I and not in the Thou but in the between, das Zwischen, and held that the I-Thou relation is constitutively untransferable into method. The dig turns this into a blade against the whole play lineage and the book with it. The moment a relation becomes a technique for producing relation, the other has been made an object of a procedure — an It — so any device that manufactures presence has already converted the Thou it aimed at. Buber resisted method for this reason, and the dig notes that the encounter movements which tried to do I-Thou staged I-It performances of intimacy; the failure is structural rather than incidental.

The charge indicts every presence-device the dig catalogued and the book's own premise, since a book that sets out to produce recognition is running a procedure on its reader. The lineage's reply, which the dig records, is a division of labor: Spolin and Johnstone build the conditions for an event and refuse to command the event itself, which is the same refusal Rosa's uncontrollability requires. The residue the dig leaves open is whether condition-design is itself already an It — whether arranging the circumstances for presence is a subtler form of the instrumentalization Buber forbids, or the one honest thing a maker can do. The record cannot rule it.
