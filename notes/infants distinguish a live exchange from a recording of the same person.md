# infants distinguish a live exchange from a recording of the same person

The requirement that a partner respond in real time is detected before language, which raises the price of a medium that cannot respond. In Murray and Trevarthen's double-video paradigm, two-month-old infants sustain a live face-to-face exchange with the mother over video, and then, shown a non-contingent recording of the same mother behaving warmly but not to them, they become distressed and withdraw. The difference between the two conditions is contingency alone — the recorded mother is as present and as warm, but she does not answer this infant now — and the infant registers the loss of contingency as a rupture. De Jaegher and Di Paolo report the study alongside perceptual crossing as evidence that the discriminating work lives in the coupling rather than in either head.

The finding bears directly on the book's form. A book read alone is a non-contingent partner in the exact sense the recording is: attentive, even warm, but unable to answer this reader now — and the paradigm shows the absence of contingency is felt, not merely inferred, by the least sophisticated reader imaginable.
