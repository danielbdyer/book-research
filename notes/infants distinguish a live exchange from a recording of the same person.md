# infants distinguish a live exchange from a recording of the same person

The requirement that a partner respond in real time is detected before language, which raises the price of a medium that cannot respond. In Murray and Trevarthen's double-video paradigm, two-month-old infants sustain a live face-to-face exchange with the mother over video, and then, shown a non-contingent recording — "a recording of her behaviour earlier in the same interaction" — they become distressed and withdraw. The manipulated variable is contingency alone: the recorded mother is as present and as expressive, but she does not answer this infant now, and De Jaegher and Di Paolo underline that "it is not expressiveness that is lacking, but the ongoing engagement that has been unhinged" (2007, pp. 489–490). They report the study alongside perceptual crossing as evidence that the discriminating work lives in the coupling rather than in either head.

The finding bears directly on the book's form. A book read alone is a non-contingent partner in the exact sense the recording is: attentive, even warm, but unable to answer this reader now — and the paradigm shows the absence of contingency is felt, not merely inferred, by the least sophisticated reader imaginable.

## Substantiation

- The finding is verified at Grade A in both works that carry it into the vault — the 2007 journal article (pp. 489–490) and the 2008 chapter (p. 11), read in full 2026-08-30 — each reporting Murray and Trevarthen's 1985 study and drawing the same conclusion, that contingency, not warmth, is what the infant registers. The Murray and Trevarthen 1985 primary report itself is a further refinement owed only if a direct quotation from it is wanted; the claim as stated is grounded at the reporting texts.
- The evidence is distinct from the perceptual-crossing finding the registry homes to [[the third thing has a mechanism in participatory sense-making]]: perceptual crossing shows the dyad discriminating what the individual cannot, while the double-video shows contingency itself being detected and its absence distressing, which is the sharper analogue to a printed page.
- The extension from a two-month-old's distress to an adult reader is an inference the vault marks as its own; the study establishes that contingency is discriminable pre-verbally, not that a book fails for the same reason an infant withdraws.
