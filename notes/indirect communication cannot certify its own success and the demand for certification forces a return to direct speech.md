# indirect communication cannot certify its own success and the demand for certification forces a return to direct speech

Indirect communication cannot certify its own success, and the certification demand eventually breaks the method. Because double reflection makes the result invisible from outside, Kierkegaard could never confirm that any reader appropriated anything; his only available standard was theological — God, the knower of hearts, sees whether inwardness occurred — which is unavailable to a facilitator and untestable in principle. The deeper trap is that the verification he lacked could not simply be added: any human authority positioned to confirm that the reader had done the work would recruit the very deference the method exists to dissolve. *The Point of View* is the symptom — a signed, direct, retrospective book written to vouch for what a decade of disappearing had been doing — so the certification demand forces a return to direct speech. This is the book's own crisis, stated by the man who built the devices, and he did not solve it. The finding rests on secondary scholarship, the primaries unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.

## Substantiation

- The dig reads *The Point of View* as the return to direct speech, "a decade of disappearing, ended by a book written to reappear and explain" (Fa3); the primary is named in dig 1's readings-to-queue.
