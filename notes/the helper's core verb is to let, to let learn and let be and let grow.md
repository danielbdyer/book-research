# the helper's core verb is to let, to let learn and let be and let grow

The reader gains a single positive word for the act, so that non-doing stops being defined by what it withholds and becomes a thing one *does*: one lets. To let another learn, to let a person be what they are becoming, to let growth take its own course — each is transitive, an action with an object, chosen and enacted. The reader who has been handed only prohibitions ("do not impose," "do not rescue") is instead handed a verb they can practice. Its use to the writing is to turn the whole stance from prohibition into practice.

## Substantiation

- Heidegger, What Is Called Thinking?: "teaching is letting learn" — the teacher's fundamental act is not to transmit but to *let* the other's learning happen; and Gelassenheit is a "letting-be" (*Seinlassen*), releasing beings into their own presence. Rogers frees the client's own actualizing tendency: "individuals have within themselves vast resources for self-understanding… these resources can be tapped if a definable climate of facilitative psychological attitudes can be provided" (A Way of Being, 1980). Laozi: the sage lets things follow *ziran*, their own self-so course, and by not forcing leaves nothing undone.
- Refusals kept: Heidegger's letting is the hardest attentiveness, not permissiveness, and refuses to become a technique; Rogers' letting depends on conditions that are exacting to meet — the letting is not a shrug.
- Standing: nascent; the "letting learn" phrasing and the 1980 Rogers quotation are queued.
