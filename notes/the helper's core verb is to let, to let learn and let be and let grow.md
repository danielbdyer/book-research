# the helper's core verb is to let, to let learn and let be and let grow

The reader gains a single positive word for the act, so that non-doing stops being defined by what it withholds and becomes a thing one *does*: one lets. To let another learn, to let a person be what they are becoming, to let growth take its own course — each is transitive, an action with an object, chosen and enacted. The reader who has been handed only prohibitions ("do not impose," "do not rescue") is instead handed a verb they can practice. Its use to the writing is to turn the whole stance from prohibition into practice.

## Substantiation

- Laozi is the source, read at the primary text: the sage "manages affairs without doing anything, and conveys his instructions without the use of speech" (*wu-wei*, Daodejing ch. 2, Legge), letting things follow *ziran*, their own self-so course, "yet nothing is left undone" (ch. 37) — a fully-formed doctrine of letting, twenty-four centuries before Heidegger.
- Heidegger's "letting" is the inheritor, not a second independent arrival. *What Is Called Thinking?* makes the teacher's act "to let learn" — "what teaching calls for is this: to let learn," and "he has to learn to let them learn" (WICT, p. 15, verified via secondary; the vault's earlier "teaching is letting learn" was a compression, now corrected). His Gelassenheit is a letting-be, but it descends from two tributaries — Eckhart's *Gelâzenheit*, self-flagged, and the Daoist *wu-wei*, under-acknowledged — so Heidegger and Laozi are one lineage on this move rather than two witnesses ([[heidegger's letting and way descend from daoism so pairing him with laozi is lineage not convergence]]). (*Seinlassen*, "letting beings be," is a distinct term from "On the Essence of Truth," 1930, not the Gelassenheit texts; the vault's earlier conflation is corrected.)
- Rogers is the one genuinely independent witness, reached in the clinic with no contact with the Daoist source: the helper supplies a climate that frees the person's own actualizing tendency, the conditions doing the work rather than the direction (Rogers 1957, read; the exact "vast resources for self-understanding" phrasing is *A Way of Being*, 1980, a refinement owed).
- Refusals kept: Heidegger's letting is the hardest attentiveness, not permissiveness, and refuses to become a technique; Rogers' letting depends on conditions exacting to meet; the Daoist counterface stays, that its letting dissolves the striving self rather than restoring the reader's sovereign first person.
- Standing: full — Laozi read at primary, Heidegger's phrase verified and reframed as lineage, Rogers' conditions read; only the 1980 Rogers phrasing is a minor refinement owed.
