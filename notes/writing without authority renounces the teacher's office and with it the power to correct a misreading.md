# writing without authority renounces the teacher's office and with it the power to correct a misreading

Writing "without authority" (*uden Myndighed*) is Kierkegaard's formal renunciation of the office of apostle or teacher. He casts himself as a poet, a corrective, a witness who commands nothing, so the reader loses the option to obey or defer rather than to enact the thing himself. The device blocks deference at a cost that is exact and often unnoticed: having disclaimed the right to say "this is what I meant," the author also renounces the power to correct a misreading and cannot govern what the reader does with him. The device is the direct ancestor of the book's own posture, which earns authority through participation rather than asserting it and expects a word that can be answered rather than obeyed. The description rests on secondary commentary; *The Point of View* is unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
