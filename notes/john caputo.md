# john caputo

John Caputo's *The Mystical Element in Heidegger's Thought* (1978) established the fact this vault had carried on knowledge alone: Heidegger took the word Gelassenheit from Meister Eckhart, and both write of a releasement — Heidegger's thinking "without why," Eckhart's soul giving up its own will. The consequence for the book is direct: Eckhart is already seated in its company, so the book's chair for the fourteenth-century mystic already touches the twentieth-century philosopher's deepest borrowing. The bridge between the book's two Western deep sources exists in the scholarship and has a name.

The refusal kept is Caputo's own second act. In *Demythologizing Heidegger* (1993) the scholar who established the mystical element spent a book warning against it. The one figure who most successfully joined Heidegger to the mystics is also the literature's loudest caution about doing so — a footstep and a guard in one person, and a model, enacted at career scale, of keeping one's own refusal in print beside one's finding. That is the shape the book's company is built on, performed by a member of the field.
