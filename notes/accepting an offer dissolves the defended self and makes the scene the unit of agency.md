# accepting an offer dissolves the defended self and makes the scene the unit of agency

Johnstone named an offer any move a partner makes in a scene, and he sorted the responses to it into two: blocking, which negates the offer and kills the scene, and accepting — the move later shortened to yes-and — which yields to the offer and builds on it. The dig recovers the consequence beneath the craft rule. Accepting dissolves the defended self, because a player who takes and extends what is given has stopped protecting a position, and the scene rather than the ego becomes the unit of agency: what acts is the joint construction that neither player controls alone. The device has its own failure, a compulsive yes that yields a spineless scene, since accepting an offer is not the same as agreeing with it.

The finding bears on the book's participation law without collapsing into it. The corpus already holds that nothing transforms from the audience and that transformation is enacted participation inside a held container; Johnstone's offer-grammar supplies a concrete mechanism for how a defended self is set aside in favor of a shared field, arrived at in a rehearsal room with no philosophy underneath it. As a device it ports to a book only as a concept, because there is no live offer coming from the page for the reader to accept.
