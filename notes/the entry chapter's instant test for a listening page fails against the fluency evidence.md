# the entry chapter's instant test for a listening page fails against the fluency evidence

The entry movement's image of a page that listens carries a claim about verification, and the vault's own research into counterfeit felt truth contradicts that claim. The executed draft says: "You can tell, instantly, whether you are inside the field of an ink that listens or whether you are reading marketing copy" (abridged ch2). The vault's catalogue of documented ways a felt sense of truth can be counterfeited says the opposite about that judgment: fluent prose produces "a bodily-felt yes, produced by the vehicle rather than the cargo," marketing copy is the genre optimized for exactly that fluency, and "marginal apparatus is not exempt." The instant bodily discrimination the draft asserts is the very judgment the fluency studies show being fooled.

Two notes already in the vault supply the repair, and what this note adds is the statement that the draft and the research disagree, which is what makes those repairs findable from the entry chapter. The first replaces "you can tell instantly" with "you can tell by whether it refuses you," which asks the reader to notice a page refusing a wrong word rather than to judge it on contact. The second requires that a genuine shift change what can be said next, which makes the discrimination something checked across days rather than in an instant. A drafting session writing the entry movement inherits this exchange, and what it inherits is that the passage's claim survives if the instant judgment becomes a judgment made over time.

## Substantiation

- Abridged ch2 carries the instant-discrimination sentence, which is also quoted at [[the ink that listens is the relational field at the scale of a page]], the note holding the book's image of prose received by a patient listener rather than deposited by an author. The evidence on the other side is at [[the felt sense of truth has a documented counterfeit catalogue]], where the fluency, rhyme, decorative-photo and induced-insight studies are cited in place.
- The first repair is [[the felt sense rejects formulations and that friction is what separates it from fluency]], which holds that a felt sense refuses a wrong word and that the refusal is a cost mere fluency never pays. The second is [[a genuine felt shift changes what can be said next]], which holds that a real shift rearranges what a person can say while fluent assent leaves it unchanged.
- Neither of the two notes previously linked the other, and what this note adds is the statement that they disagree.
