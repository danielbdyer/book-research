# reading stance is a setting of selective attention rather than a property of the text

Rosenblatt holds that what a reader takes from a text is decided by a stance, a setting of selective attention, and not by a property of the text itself. In the efferent stance attention falls on what is carried away after reading, the referential and analytic residue; in the aesthetic stance attention falls on what is lived through during the reading, the qualitative and affective event. Because the stance belongs to the reader rather than to the marks, one and the same text admits either reading. The consequence is her third term: the "poem" is neither the reader nor the text but the lived event the transaction evokes, so a work has three terms where a transmission model has two.

The account matters to the book because it locates the reading event off the page, in the reader's attention, which is where the book's own doctrine locates whatever the book does. It also warns the book that its prose does not by itself determine the reader's stance; the same sentences can be read for residue or lived through, and which happens is a setting the reader brings.
