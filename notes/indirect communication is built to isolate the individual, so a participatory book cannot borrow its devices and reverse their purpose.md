# indirect communication is built to isolate the individual, so a participatory book cannot borrow its devices and reverse their purpose

The vector of Kierkegaard's indirection points away from the plural. The devices exist to isolate the single individual — to sever him from the crowd and stand him alone before the eternal — and his verdict that "the crowd is untruth" makes the plural the medium of illusion rather than the site of the real. A book about mutual participation runs the opposite motion, toward the relational and the shared, so it cannot simply borrow the indirect devices while reversing the purpose they were shaped for and assume they still work. The caution is structural rather than moral: a mechanism built to produce solitude before God is not guaranteed to produce anything when pointed at solidarity between readers. A participatory project that adopts the machinery owes an account of why it should function with its direction inverted. The finding rests on secondary scholarship, the primaries unopened here and queued in `ops/reading queue.md`.
