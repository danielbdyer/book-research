# an ecology is a bounded but permeable domain of multiple irreducible loci of participation, none sovereign

The reframe conversation offers "ecology" as its name for the structure the whole inquiry takes. An ecology is bounded, in that it has an inside and an edge; permeable, in that its several standpoints can cross into, challenge, and change one another; and populated by multiple irreducible loci of participation, where a locus is a standpoint from which some aspect of a phenomenon discloses that cannot be adequately disclosed from another. The defining constraint is that no one locus becomes sovereign over the others. The claim fixes the book's relation to the many traditions it draws on: the book is the field in which loci disclose, not a locus raised above the rest to adjudicate them, and each tradition it hosts keeps its own way of disclosing rather than being resolved into one master account.

The definition governs the vault as much as the book. The vault maps a plurality of thinkers and traditions, and this claim forbids the map from installing itself as the standpoint that ranks what the others see. Both the book and its vault remain fields that hold loci, answerable to a reality each locus discloses only in part.

## Substantiation

- The long form, in the conversation's words: "An ecology is a bounded but permeable domain of inquiry containing multiple irreducible loci of participation, each capable of disclosing aspects of a phenomenon that cannot be adequately disclosed from another locus, together with the encounters, practices, concepts, traditions, and transformations through which those disclosures become intelligible" (reframe conversation, 2026-08-13, line 4157).
- The short form, stated twice: "An ecology is a living field in which multiple loci can disclose, encounter, challenge, and transform one another without any single locus becoming sovereign" (line 4161; the same formulation recurs at line 2964).
- Standing: the definition is reached in the conversation's shared, model-assisted portion, prompted by the author's wariness of a coordinate system that would rank every perspective. It is held as candidate material and kept nascent until the writing reaches for it. Whether the book commits to this ecology in place of a nested metaphysical ontology is an open fork for the author (the reconciliation register's Fork 2), and this note records the ecology formulation without settling that choice.
