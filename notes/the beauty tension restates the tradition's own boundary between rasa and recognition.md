# the beauty tension restates the tradition's own boundary between rasa and recognition

The book's first assessment tension — its prose recruits an appreciating posture, so "the reader becomes a connoisseur rather than a practitioner" — turns out to be a line its own source tradition drew with a millennium of theory behind it. Abhinavagupta's aesthetics holds that the rasas exist only in the theater ("tena nāṭya eva rasāḥ, na loke"), that aesthetic tasting is "akin to, easier than, and not the yogin's permanent accomplishment," and that rasa "lasts exactly as long as the gustation." Relish is real, staged, and temporary; recognition is object-free and once-for-all. The connoisseur the tension fears is the tradition's sahṛdaya — the sensitive reader savoring inside the frame — and the tension is the tradition's own prediction of what happens when relish is never carried out of the theater.

This gives the beauty problem a doctrinal complement to its taste treatment: the fault is not ornament, which the tradition honors, but the reader remaining inside the staged frame — and it supplies the positive register the nascent territory on curated beauty currently lacks, since the same tradition holds that the aesthetic doorway can open onto recognition when it is walked through rather than lingered in.
