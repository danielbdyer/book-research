# the reader becomes author of what happens in their encounter with the text, not co-author of the text

The conversation draws a precise line on the reader's authorship. The reader does not become co-author of the text — the text remains the author's responsibility — but the reader does become the author of what happens in their own encounter with it. That distinction preserves both authorial responsibility and reader sovereignty at once: the author is answerable for what the book says, and the reader is the one who authors the recognition that occurs, or does not occur, in the reading. The book's ethical posture follows from it — the book will not tell the reader what they must recognize, but will attempt to create conditions in which the reader can encounter the question deeply enough to discover what is theirs to recognize.

## Substantiation

- The distinction is stated in the conversation of 2026-08-13, in the section the model titles the reader as co-author of experience: "The reader does not become co-author of the text. But they become: author of what happens in their own encounter with it."
- What the distinction protects, quoted from the same section: it "preserves both: authorial responsibility; reader sovereignty."
- The posture it yields, quoted from the adjacent passage on the book's ethical promise: "I will not tell you what you must recognize. I will instead attempt to create conditions in which you can encounter the question deeply enough to discover what is yours to recognize."
