# the two books differ in locus of practice not subject matter

The reframe conversation distinguishes the project's two books by the locus of practice rather than by their subject matter. Book I orients the practitioner's attention toward another person's process within a shared field, and its competencies are holding conditions, noticing, attuning, and preserving the other's sovereignty. Book II orients the same attention toward one's own process within the field of one's own life. Both books range across the same concerns — attention, awareness, embodiment, meaning, relationship, choice, practice, technology, uncertainty, transformation, and self-authorship — so the two are not two versions of one material but one material entered from two positions.

The conversation generated several competing architectures for the project on demand, so the vault holds the two-book split as a candidate structure rather than a settled scheme; what is durable in it is the claim that the difference between the books is a difference of locus, not of topic. The same conversation elsewhere cut the two books by audience, facilitators against a general reader, which is a different axis from locus, and the two axes are kept apart so that one numbering never silently carries both.

## Substantiation

- The section the conversation titles the two loci of practice states the distinction directly: "The deepest structural distinction between the books is not subject matter. It is **locus**."
- Book I's locus, quoted: the practitioner's attention is oriented toward "another person's process within a shared field," and the facilitator must learn "How do I participate without taking over?" Book II's locus: "their own process within the field of their life."
- The shared concerns are enumerated just before the split, and the conversation draws the consequence: "The two books are therefore **not redundant versions of the same material**. They occupy different positions within the same ecology."
