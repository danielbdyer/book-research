# sovereignty occupies the middle between everything happens to me and everything is my responsibility

The reframe conversation states the sovereignty motif as a question of where a person locates authorship, and it fails in two opposite directions. Located too far outside the person, authorship collapses into "everything happens to me," a passivity in which the person is only ever done to. Located too far inside, it inflates into "everything is my responsibility," an over-responsibility in which the person is answerable for what they cannot author. Sovereignty is the middle position, which the conversation states as the recognition that one cannot author everything that happens but can participate in how one meets what happens. The middle is therefore not a compromise between the two extremes but a different account of what authorship is for: not command over events, and not blame for them, but participation in one's own response to them.

The claim matters to the book because it gives the book's reader a workable stance toward their life without the two failures the book must avoid recommending — a resignation that abandons agency and a hyper-responsibility that mistakes everything for one's own doing. It also states, at the scale of a single person's relation to their circumstances, the same authorship-not-control distinction the sovereignty material draws elsewhere.
