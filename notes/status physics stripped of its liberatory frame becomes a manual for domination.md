# status physics stripped of its liberatory frame becomes a manual for domination

Johnstone's status work was built to free a scene by making the traffic of standing between people visible and adjustable, so that a player could raise or lower status deliberately in service of the play. The dig records its failure. The same physics, stripped of the liberatory frame that oriented it, becomes a manipulation manual: the status-games self-help genre takes his observable seesaw and turns it toward dominance, teaching a reader to win exchanges rather than to free a scene, which is the exact opposite of his intent. The dig also notes that his mask and trance work reportedly disturbed some students, brushing the danger zone where a technique for self-dissolution can injure.

The failure is the same shape the vault holds as a general rule: a technique detached from the ontology and ethics beneath it degrades into a way of working on a person. It bears directly on any borrowing the book might make, because the status material is the most portable analytic import from the play lineage and it arrives carrying this inversion as a live risk. A book that hands a reader status physics as a lens must also hand over the frame that keeps it a way of seeing rather than a way of dominating.
