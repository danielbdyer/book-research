# the arc is the instrument that holds both poles of a tension a page cannot hold at once

Several of the book's commitments require two things at once that a static page cannot deliver together: authority and its relinquishment, the transparency of the score and the absorption of the encounter, structure and surprise. A page presents one pole per moment; a reading occupies time, and a sequence can therefore hold both poles by placing them at different moments. The book's dramaturgy is, on this reading, tension-resolution in time — it front-loads one pole and spends toward the other — and this is the mechanism beneath the axiom that the medium should embody the message, because performance across time is the only embodiment print has.

The claim is a first-principles derivation from the conversation's premises rather than a statement the conversation makes, so it is held nascent and marked as inference. It re-derives, from below, both of the vault's recorded dramaturgy decisions: Book I front-loads authority and spends it toward the facilitator's disappearance, resolving the authority tension as sequence, and Book II alternates immersion and integration, resolving the transparency-and-absorption tension as alternation — which is the psychedelic arc's function, and the reason its peak resolves into a return rather than standing as the destination.

## Substantiation

- The non-co-maximizable pairs are named in the conversation. The authority pair: a book must "establish enough authority that the reader trusts the terrain" and "relinquish authority so the reader can discover their own relationship to it," and "those impulses pull against one another" (reframe conversation, 1371–1384, model-elaborated on the author's founding insight at 1358). The embodiment axiom: the medium should embody the message (521, the author ratifying at 523–526). The surprise pair, in the author's own voice: how much form holds just enough that surprise can still happen (1183–1190, "that is the question of my life").
- The derivation: because a page presents one pole at a time, two commitments that cannot both be maximized at a single moment can still both be honored if the reading occupies time; the arc is that occupation of time. Strength: strict, on the premise that the named pairs are genuinely non-co-maximizable.
- Standing: inferred from the premises, not asserted by the conversation; the reframe's model turns are works consulted, and the author-voice anchors (1358, 1183–1190) carry the premises the derivation leans on.
