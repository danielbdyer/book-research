# the executed draft stages a concept by cataloguing the reader's own remembered scenes

The design the corpus records promises that concepts are staged before they are named, and in the executed draft that staging turns out to be done by one specific sentence form. Before a naming, the prose runs a catalogue of short second-person perfect-tense sentences, each summoning one concrete scene from the reader's own past: "But Alexander knew the quality was real. So do you. You have felt it. You have walked into a friend's kitchen and the kitchen was a kitchen but it was also home" (abridged ch1). "You know this feeling. You know what it is to finally see a thing clearly that you had been seeing fuzzily" (abridged ch3). The hinge then converts the catalogue into the naming: "You have just felt three movements. Now we name them" (abridged ch4).

The cases from which the general structure is drawn are the reader's own remembered scenes, not scenes the book has invented for them. The draft narrates nothing here; it asks, and the reader's memory answers with a particular kitchen and a particular moment of seeing clearly. The name that follows therefore attaches to material the reader has already produced, which is how the front-matter promise — that the reader already knows this and reading is only remembering it — gets carried out one paragraph at a time.

## Substantiation

- Specimens at abridged ch1, ch3, and ch4 appear above as quotations from the extracted text, and five catalogue sites were verified across the draft, running to roughly fifteen catalogue sentences in total.
- Anyone can check this by looking at what follows each structural assertion in the draft, where a run of second-person perfect-tense sentences stands, each naming one rememberable scene. The catalogues rather than narrated scenes carry the work, and a staging done any other way is the exception to look for.
- The book's front matter promises that the reader already knows its contents and that reading is remembering them, and the descriptive documents record that promise as a design commitment kept structurally, with Part I inducing what Part II names ([[reading is remembering]]). These catalogues are how the executed prose keeps it at the scale of a paragraph.
