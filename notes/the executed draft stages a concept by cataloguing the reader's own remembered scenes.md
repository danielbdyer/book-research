# the executed draft stages a concept by cataloguing the reader's own remembered scenes

The recorded design promises that concepts are staged before they are named; the executed draft's staging instrument turns out to be a specific sentence form. Before a naming, the prose runs a catalogue of short second-person perfect-tense sentences, each summoning one concrete scene from the reader's own past: "But Alexander knew the quality was real. So do you. You have felt it. You have walked into a friend's kitchen and the kitchen was a kitchen but it was also home" (abridged ch1). "You know this feeling. You know what it is to finally see a thing clearly that you had been seeing fuzzily" (abridged ch3). The hinge then converts the catalogue into the naming: "You have just felt three movements. Now we name them" (abridged ch4).

The mechanism is that the reader's episodic memory does the induction: no scene is narrated to them; their own scenes are solicited, so the subsequent name arrives as a label for material already present — which is the front-matter doctrine executed at paragraph scale.
