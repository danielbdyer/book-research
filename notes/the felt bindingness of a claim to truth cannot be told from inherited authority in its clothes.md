# the felt bindingness of a claim to truth cannot be told from inherited authority in its clothes

Gadamer's participation model carries its own verification and its own defeat in one instrument. The check that a genuine understanding has occurred is the felt bindingness of a claim to truth — the reader being "pulled up short," addressed by a claim that will not be dismissed — which is the closest thing the reader-response strata hold to a felt shift with propositional content. Habermas's charge is that this same felt bindingness can be manufactured authority: Gadamer's rehabilitation of authority, tradition, and prejudice is blind to systematically distorted communication, power operating behind the back of the dialogue, so that a claim can feel binding because it is inherited and unquestioned rather than because it is true. The participation model and the warning that it launders authority therefore arise from a single source, and from inside the felt bindingness there is no test that separates the two.

Gadamer's reply is that no standpoint outside language and tradition exists from which to run such a test; critique is itself situated, and authority rightly understood rests on recognition of superior insight rather than on obedience. The exchange is unresolved, and the record cannot rule. The question bears directly on the book because the book leans on a felt bindingness of its own, and if that feeling cannot be told from inherited authority, the book's central safeguard is exactly where the manufactured-authority objection bites hardest.
