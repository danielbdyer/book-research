# sovereign intimacy names intimacy without surrender of authorship

The reframe conversation proposes the name "sovereign intimacy" for a relation the book cares about: closeness that does not require a person to give up authorship of their own experience. The concern the name marks is that intimacy is often assumed to entail surrender — that to be close is to be dissolved into the other or to hand over the point from which one participates — and the conversation denies the entailment. On this account the ideal of intimacy is neither isolation nor merger but contact in which each person keeps the authorship of their own side. The name is a candidate label for that concern and not the concern itself; stated plainly, the concern is intimacy that preserves each person's locus of participation.

The conversation notes that the same pattern reaches beyond closeness between persons — into facilitation, relation to artificial intelligence, to traditions, to books, and to systems — so that "sovereign intimacy" names one instance of the wider relation the conversation calls sovereignty within relationship.
