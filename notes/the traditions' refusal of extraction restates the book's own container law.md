# the traditions' refusal of extraction restates the book's own container law

Five unrelated traditions refuse the same operation, and the operation is one the book's method requires. Al-Qushayrī's evaluation states that the book "has taken a diagnostic tool out of the practice that gives it its content." The Hasidic evaluation says: "Strip that and what remains is mindful eating." The Ubuntu evaluation says that "the decontextualized version is a different thing wearing the same words." Ratié, writing for the Pratyabhijñā, says that "lifting them out of that argumentative context loses what makes them positions rather than poetry." Wasserstrom documents where the operation leads at scale, since the Eranos abstraction "produced a religion of its own while claiming only to describe." The Trika's own third refusal, that the teacher is the means, sits beneath all five.

What this note adds is the observation that the reason all five give is the book's own container doctrine, turned back on the book. If every container is constitutive of what it holds, then a practice is partly its container, and lifting a practice out changes the thing lifted, which means the critics are observing exactly what the book's own law predicts. The charge therefore cannot be rebutted without rebutting the container law itself, and the honest sentence available to the book's note on sources is that its own doctrine predicts what its critics observe.

## Substantiation

- The five refusals are quoted from their evaluations. The Trika's is anchored to the tradition's own texts at [[the kashmir shaiva tradition would decline four of the book's deepest moves]], where four refusals are each rested on a commitment the tradition states plainly. The other four refusals are marked derived, meaning the vault composed the position from the tradition's own commitments rather than quoting a sentence someone wrote, and this claim inherits that standing.
- The doctrine the five refusals restate is recorded at [[the triad is the anatomy of every container]], the book's claim that Wall, Door and Floor compose every container whatsoever, read here in its strong sense that the container constitutes what it holds.
- The book already answers the extraction charge in part, through the moves the corpus calls attribution, the flowers, and the road into the valley. Each of those addresses the charge as it appears on the surface. The reflexive half, that the book's own container law predicts what its critics observe, has not previously been stated.
