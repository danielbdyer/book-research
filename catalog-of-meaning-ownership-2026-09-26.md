# The catalog of meaning: how a word becomes someone's own

**What this document is.** This is one of six documents answering the author's request of 2026-09-26 to enumerate "the catalog of meaning which would be explored in the future" from what pull request #23 brings into scope. The first document, `catalog-of-meaning-2026-09-26.md`, names the whole space and explains how it is divided. This one divides the space by how a word becomes someone's own. It covers the stages a piece of material passes through on its way to belonging to someone, whose words are whose in the material the books draw on, how anyone knows that something happened, the making of the books, and sending and answering. Every entry is a session's proposal, at the level "proposed," and every quotation says whose words it is.

**Why this division belongs to the books and not only to the vault.** Pull request #23 proposed a field recording the author's own relation to each piece of material, set only by his decisions, and a mark on every quotation saying whose words it is. It proposed them as repairs to the vault's records. Read beside the books' subject, the same two things describe what the books are about: how something offered by someone else becomes a person's own without anyone taking the person's place. The first document's first finding states this; this document lists what follows from it.

## How this document is organized

1. The stages by which something offered becomes someone's own.
2. Whose words: the voices in and around the books.
3. How anyone knows that something happened.
4. The making of the books.
5. Sending and answering.

## 1. The stages by which something offered becomes someone's own

### The stages, as the design and the vault's reading name them

The design names six levels for the author's relation to a piece of material, from the transcript's own distinction between "what I encountered, inferred, believe, and will claim" (12415, the model):
- **proposed:** brought in by research, a conversation, or an AI, and not yet adopted;
- **experienced:** the person lived it;
- **inferred:** the person drew it from what they lived;
- **believed:** the person holds it;
- **claimed:** the person will state and defend it in public;
- **written:** it is in language the person drafted.

The vault's reading added two for a drafted passage: **sent** and **answered** (`system-and-vault-beyond-the-design-2026-09-25.md`, area 1). It also proposed a separate mark, **derived**, for a conclusion a session drew, so that the word "inferred" stays reserved for the person's own act. The vault holds one more scale in the author's own terms: his three tiers of confidence of 2026-08-04, which sort the book's material into what is settled, what is changing, and what is undecided (`ops/methodology/the book's current shape.md`). And the mesh grid, a sorting of the book's thinkers that the author brought into the vault on 2026-08-05, records a partial agreement the ordered levels cannot express: he cosigned it "gently… about 65–75%" (`ops/completed.md`, the entry for 2026-08-05).

### The same stages at three scales

The first document's first finding is that these stages describe three relationships, not one.

| Stage | The author with the vault | The reader with the book | The participant with the facilitator |
|---|---|---|---|
| proposed | a session, the corpus, or the model brings material | the book offers an invitation, an image, a question | the facilitator offers a prompt or a reflection |
| experienced | the author lives it | the reader tries the practice and something happens, or nothing does | the participant tries the exercise |
| inferred | the author draws from it | the reader draws a conclusion about their own life | the participant names what they noticed |
| believed | the author holds it | the reader holds it | the participant holds it |
| claimed | the author will state it in public | the reader says it aloud as their own | the participant says it to the group |
| written | the author drafts it | the reader writes an answer, as the first letter asks | the participant writes or speaks their own sentence |
| sent | the author sends it | the reader sends the answer | the participant says it to the person it concerns |
| answered | a reader replies | someone answers the reader | someone receives it |

At each scale the transcript states the same rule about who may move a thing from one stage to the next. For the vault: "Nothing becomes part of the author's philosophy merely because the AI said it" (14414, the model). For the book: "Create conditions for recognition without claiming sovereignty over what is recognized" (7113, the model). For the room: "The participant's recognition belongs to the participant" (1489, the model). The author's own line from the spoken part is older than all three: "the book can't verify that you've done the work" (266).

### Catalog: what can skip a stage, and what each skip looks like

This catalog lists the ways a piece of material can appear to reach a later stage without passing through the earlier ones. Each member is a session's reading of material the vault already holds.
- **Proposed read as experienced.** A sentence the model or a collaborator wrote is quoted as the author's record. Pull request #23 found at least seven instances in the vault, and section 2 below lists eleven. In a room, the equivalent is a facilitator's reflection that a participant repeats as their own insight.
- **Proposed read as believed.** The reader agrees because the prose is easy to read. The vault records that "statements shown in easier-to-read contrast are judged true above chance on presentation alone" (`notes/the felt sense of truth has a documented counterfeit catalogue.md`), and the transcript's test for it is "Have I encountered this, or have I merely recognized that it sounds true?" (14582, the model).
- **Experienced read as claimed.** A person who once had a large experience states it as a truth for everyone. Book II's reader as the vault defined it on 2026-08-04, someone "to whom it already happened once and did not last" (`notes/recognition.md`), is at this point.
- **Told in advance what the experience will feel like.** The brief for the verification standard's defense warns that "A reader taught this book's vocabulary and told what a shift feels like has been given a subculture's rules and its feedback" (`drafts/brief — the verification standard's defense.md`). The reader then reports the promised experience.
- **Adopted but not written.** The author took the model's question about form as "the question of my life" (1190). The words are the model's and the adoption is his. Pull request #23 proposes recording this as "the model's words, adopted by the author," which is a stage the six levels do not name.
- **Written but not sent.** The vault's concern with address depends on the finding that a word is not an act until it is sent. The vault's note on this open disagreement, `notes/seventeen chapters rest at madhyama.md`, recorded the whole corpus waiting at this stage until the pilot letter, the first letter of the planned correspondence, was sent.
- **Sent but not answered.** No reply to the pilot letter is recorded (`ops/correspondence program.md`, checked 2026-09-03).

### Where the books could go with this

- **A Book I passage on the stages.** A facilitator-reader could use the table above to see where a participant is, and what a facilitator may and may not do at each stage. The transcript's pair "expertise about process / expertise about another person" (15122–15130, the model) marks the line.
- **A Book II practice on the stages.** A reader could sort the book's own sentences by stage for themselves: which have I only been offered, which have I lived, which would I say aloud? The author's own phrase for the first step is "try this on" (176), and his fuller wording at 1190 is "to try it on and see whether it fits for me too."
- **The model's words adopted by the author, as a kind of its own.** The transcript holds several places where the author took the model's words as his own or recognized his own idea in them: "that is the question of my life" (1190), "that's my book" (934), "that's the precise idea I've arrived at too" (2509). A catalog of these adoptions, each with the model's wording and his response, would show the reader what adoption looks like when it is honest about its source.

## 2. Whose words: the voices in and around the books

### Catalog: the voices

Each member is a voice the books draw on or could draw on, with where its words are and how the vault marks them.
- **The author in his poems.** The collection holds sixteen poems, fifteen of which predate the book. For those fifteen, a phrase the corpus shares with a poem originates in the poem. The sixteenth "is exempted by the author's own flag and reads as the reverse flow — the book's language returning to the life" (`archive/source — the poems.md`).
- **The author speaking.** He speaks in 129 spoken turns and 13 typed turns of the transcript, which the vault's record of the conversation treats as primary material (`archive/encounter — the reframe conversation (2026-08-13).md`).
- **The author reflecting.** He wrote one reflection, `reflections/on recognition (2026-08-24).md`, whose words "are verbatim and inviolable — never edited, paraphrased, or condensed by a session."
- **The author deciding.** His decisions fill the 119 rows of the decisions register (`ops/decisions.md`, the table of settled questions), and notes quote his dated statements.
- **The letter from his first love.** Its words reach the vault through his transcription, marked [A] in the provenance record: "we each act as auxiliary for the other as we march through life with our own flags in hand" (`provenance/provenance fourth addendum.md`, lines 44–45). He summarized it in the conversation at 770.
- **His father.** Poem #1 quotes "danny, this is your daddy speaking," and the vault records that "the book's doctrine of time restates a sentence the author's father told him" (`notes/the book's doctrine of time restates a sentence the author's father told him.md`). The reflection adds his father reading *The Garden Behind the Moon* aloud.
- **The model in the reframe conversation.** The model wrote about 62,000 words across 142 exchanges, three quarters of them in seven outlines the author asked for (the design, Part 1).
- **The collaborator.** An AI wrote most of the seven corpus documents "to your specification" while the author chose, arranged, and vetoed (`notes/the author curated and vetoed while the collaborator wrote the correspondences.md`).
- **Sessions.** Sessions wrote the vault's notes, its files that read the whole graph by one principle, its drafting documents, and this catalog.
- **The thinkers.** The books quote traditions and people, each in their own vocabulary first.
- **The readers.** Two real readers received the pilot letter, and any reader may answer. Their words, if the author ever keeps them, are the readers' own material, and pull request #23 proposes keeping only a record that an answer happened, "and only if the author wants it kept."

10. This reading found two more, which pull request #23 does not list. The first: the relational-field topic map glosses the note `notes/the locus of control is better named the locus of inquiry.md` as "the author's recasting of the standpoint from a claim about who controls reality into a question about where one participates from." The recasting is the model's: "I'd actually sharpen that now to: locus of inquiry" (2695–2697). 11. The second: the vault's reading of the book's whole structure (`ops/scaffold.md`, line 220) names three phrases as "The author's own verification language." One is his: "the book can't verify that you've done the work" (266). The other two are sentences from the model's responses: "What survives movement?" (6872) and "Nothing becomes part of the author's philosophy merely because the AI said it" (14414). The author's own word behind the first is "durable perspective" (2975), and the criterion built on it is the model's wording.

The same pattern runs inside the transcript itself, before the vault existed to repeat it. At 2133 the model set a sentence of its own in quotation marks as a feeling the author had been having, "The corpus is getting smarter while I am becoming less involved," and at 2139–2141 it presented a formula of its own, "the scaffolding must return me to experience," as "your emerging principle." At 1461 it listed nine rules under the heading "Examples we've already discovered"; two of them restate its own earlier phrases: "The author is the first participant" repeats its "the author is the first participant" (887), and "Don't colonize the reader's experience" restates its "refuses to colonize the reader's experience" (725), which the author had praised at 730. In each case the model was restating the author's thinking back to him, as a facilitator does, and each restatement attributed a little more of its own wording to him. The model had described this risk in the same outline, in a list for facilitators: "Notice how your own interpretation can become a subtle form of colonization" (1408).

The reverse error also occurs. Reader F1 found that the design marks the practices as the model's alone, when the author "supplied the seed, the practice, the lineage choices, and every veto." So the catalog has two directions: the model's words credited to the author, and the author's work credited to the model.

## 3. How anyone knows that something happened

Pull request #23 found that most of the vault's felt-shift material is about verification rather than about inner life, and that the design has no place for it (`system-and-vault-beyond-the-design-2026-09-25.md`, area 3). This section lists what the space holds on the question.

### Catalog: signs that something happened

Reader F1's proposed record for a sign has four parts: the sign itself, who can check it, its false version and who catches it, and whether a printed page can carry it. The signs the vault holds, sorted by when they can be read:
- **At the moment.** The thing attended to moves (Gendlin's "referent movement"), the body says "yes," or the felt sense refuses a wording.
- **Over days.** A step leaves "the meanings and symbolizations one formulates are different" (Gendlin, as the vault quotes him).
- **By what follows.** The next thing a person can say shows it. The draft defending the verification standard offers "the test of a step ('can you now say something about your own situation that you could not say before this page')."
- **By conduct.** What the person does afterward shows it.
- **By the problem not coming back.** The difficulty does not return.
- **Through another person.** The draft's "test of a second person," the Zen private interview, the Ignatian director, and Gendlin's listener each put the check in another person.
- **By refusal.** The draft offers the "test of refusal ('can this thing say no to me')."
- **By lasting.** The durability criterion asks "What survives movement?" (6872, the model). The vault's reading of the book's structure calls this the author's own verification language (`ops/scaffold.md`, line 220); the phrase "durable perspective" is his (2975), and the criterion's wording is the model's (section 2, item 11).

### Catalog: false versions of the event

The vault holds a catalog of counterfeits for the felt shift (`notes/the felt sense of truth has a documented counterfeit catalogue.md`), and the reports found others scattered across the concerns. Gathered, they are these:
- the fluent "yes" that "arrives fast," "cannot be re-contacted as a body-sense," and "has nothing new when revisited" (`notes/gendlin's focusing protocol already guards against the counterfeit shift.md`);
- agreement produced by easy-to-read prose, and "artificially induced aha moments" that make unrelated statements feel true (reader F1);
- the false peak, which mimics recognition on the side of expansion (286; `notes/the pulse has a counterfeit at each phase, a false peak of expansion and a racket of forgotten contraction.md`);
- the racket, "a narrowing so habitual it no longer registers as a narrowing," on the side of contraction;
- recognition kept as a possession (`notes/recognition is irreversible as remembering but revocable when kept as a possession.md`);
- recognition taken as the ego's reward (`notes/recognition is the soul's reminder rather than the ego's reward.md`);
- the hollow interaction, "highly fluent and completely non-generative" (15918, the model; `notes/the hollow interaction is illumination that cannot recognize itself.md`);
- the self-certifying experience the Zen instruction on *makyō* refuses (`notes/zen's makyo instruction refuses the self-certifying experience.md`);
- glass taken for diamond (Kapleau, as reader O3 quotes him);
- in the author's own words, "the plateau-seeking enthusiastic bliss-mongering behavior routines" (poem #14).

### Catalog: what a page can carry and what it cannot

The vault has concluded that "a book read alone can state the whole of the restraint and none of the discrimination" (`notes/a solitary reader can run every guard that declines to evaluate and none that discriminates.md`). The limits the vault already records on what a book can do form a catalog of their own:
- a book cannot supply "the basic trust a reader who never had a reliable other lacks";
- "the maneuvers that change a person's frame require a live protected setting a book is not";
- "a text does not adjust" (De Jaegher and Di Paolo, as reader O3 quotes them);
- a book cannot certify: it "points at the external check it cannot administer, the finger rather than the moon";
- writing without authority "renounces the teacher's office and with it the power to correct a misreading";
- "the book must respect that it cannot verify the reader has done the work," which is the author's own line at 266;
- "the book's only honest promise is conditional testimony."

### Where the books could go with this

- **What a reader can check alone.** A catalog of what a reader can check alone, and what needs another person, could stand in Book II's promise to its reader. It would be the honest statement of limits that pull request #23 found Book II lacks.
- **Book I as the book of discernment.** The first document's fifth finding proposes that Book I teach what a reader alone cannot do: telling a real change from a false one, with another person.
- **The author's own standard.** Question B3 in `ops/practice-model ecology — candidate next steps.md` asks whether his standard is the felt shift or durability. His own line in the transcript, "the book can't verify that you've done the work" (266), bears on the question without settling it. Only he can answer.

## 4. The making of the books

### Catalog: the dated stages

The vault records the making as a sequence of dated events, and the direction of influence is settled: "the author said the plain thing first, in his own words, about his own life … and the scholarship arrived afterward and dressed it" (`CLAUDE.md`). The stages, with what each contributed:
- **The poems**, written across the author's life; fifteen predate the book.
- **Being read to as a child.** The reflection's earliest scene: "my dad read to me about David walking across the glimmering moonlit sea path to The Garden Behind the Moon (by Howard Pyle)."
- **The letter from his first love**, written about ten years before the conversation.
- **The Landmark Forum, 2017**, where "the author did the Landmark Forum in 2017, found it instrumental, and encountered Heidegger and est through it" (`notes/the central correspondence was lived nine years before it was assembled.md`).
- **Facilitation in Houston**, where he "trained as an Authentic Relating facilitator in Houston, held a facilitation community there before moving to Seattle" (`notes/interlocutors.md`).
- **The first images**, 9 April 2025, which the vault dates to "a season of burnout and container-building on every front" (`notes/the seed arrived in a season of burnout and container-building on every front.md`).
- **The triad**, 8 December 2025, worked out in another project, three months before his first formal contact with the tradition it is named for.
- **The retreat**, 19–22 March 2026, where he "took away this concept called spanda" (42).
- **A group he led**, 16 May 2026, with the prompt he chose: "Think of someone who loves you. Describe how they see you." (`provenance/provenance of the corpus round two.md`).
- **An outliner project**, 19 June 2026, which his vision brief asked to be built "like an instrument I'll play daily, not a database I'll administer."
- **The 4am rereading of the letter**, 1 July 2026, the one recorded occurrence of the event the book promises.
- **The corpus**, compiled in one month, July 2026, by the collaborator under the author's direction.
- **The reframe conversation**, 12 August 2026.
- **The vault**, from 1 August 2026, including pull request #23 and this catalog.

### Catalog: phrases that were first said somewhere else

The design proposes a field recording where each piece of material was first said, and a connection "first said in." Members the vault or this reading has found:

It loops. It folds.," where the poem would be (`abridged`, page 7).
- **Poem #5 and the corpus.** "the youngness is just beginning" is the poem's ending, not a book line the poem borrowed.
- **The rainbow thread and the gold thread.** The gold thread developed from a "rainbow thread" image the author once shared with a beloved friend, and later reclaimed as his own (`notes/the gold thread is the rainbow thread re-owned.md`).
- **The gatekeeper.** The image has "a lived referent in the author's own protective structure" (`notes/the gatekeeper has a lived referent in the author's own protective structure.md`).

- **"Nothing transforms from the audience."** The vault finds the phrase as the practice lineages' "one unanimous law" at atlas §IX.3, which the collaborator drafted; the author said "nothing transforms because of the audience" at 266 and added "I don't remember where this is from." Whether he coined it or was repeating the corpus is open (reader O3).

### Catalog: the making as a case of the books' subject

The first document's second finding proposes that the making be read as an instance of the books' subject. Its members:
- **The unmooring.** At 810 the author said that handing the research to others "unmoored me from the practice of experience around what it's like to write this book," and called it "a delegated practice that doesn't really scratch the itch." The vault holds this as his own encounter (`notes/the vault-and-AI architecture had unmoored the author from the practice of experiencing the book — a delegated practice that doesn't scratch the itch.md`).
- **The misattributions.** Section 2 above lists eleven places where one voice was presented as another.
- **The flagged prose.** On 2026-09-25 the author said of pull request #23, "I'm afraid I don't understand much of this writing," and gave rule 13 (`ops/register violations.md`).
- **The veto.** The collaborator wrote and the author chose, arranged, and vetoed (`notes/the author curated and vetoed while the collaborator wrote the correspondences.md`).
- **The container.** The vault records that "at every documented scale the material was container-built, the author curating and holding the frame while the writing became more fully itself inside it" (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **The exit.** The vault's proposal for its own ending, `ops/the vault's exit.md`, whose main sentence is "What decreases is the making of new structure; what stays is the presence of what was made and the willingness to be entered."

### Catalog: the conditions under which the author's own ideas arrived

The books' subject is the conditions under which a recognition becomes possible. The record of the making holds several occasions on which one arrived for the author, with the conditions around each:
- **Alone, at 4am, rereading a letter** a decade old, on 1 July 2026: the one recorded occurrence of the event the book promises.
- **In a season of burnout**, on 9 April 2025, when the first images of the house, the gold thread, and the gatekeeper came (`notes/the seed arrived in a season of burnout and container-building on every front.md`).
- **On a retreat**, 19–22 March 2026, from which, in his words, he "took away this concept called spanda" (42).
- **In a large-group seminar**, the Landmark Forum of 2017, which the vault records he found instrumental (`notes/the central correspondence was lived nine years before it was assembled.md`).
- **After a pause.** The idea of writing the first draft for facilitators alone came in the first typed turn after a 52-minute gap in a spoken conversation, which he opened with "I just had an interesting insight" (1356–1358, 9:57 PM; the previous spoken turn was at 9:05 PM, line 1338). What happened in the gap is not recorded.
- **In conversation**, when he recognized his earlier phrase in the model's account: "that's the precise idea I've arrived at too" (2509).

These are facts about when, and the record is silent on why. A drafting session could still use the list to check the books' statements about conditions against the author's own occasions, and the author could say which of them he would call a recognition.

### Where the books could go with this

- **Book I could tell the making as its case.** "The book teaches the facilitator to facilitate in the same way that the book itself was created" (9163, the model). A facilitator-reader would learn from an honest account of a collaboration in which the author stayed the author, including the places where he did not at first.
- **A chronology of when the author came to know each thing.** The author set a rule about order: "I can't reveal something early that I didn't know until much later without breaking the thing that makes it work" (`ops/methodology/the book's current shape.md`). A chronology built from the stages above would let a drafting session check a draft against that rule.
- **A table from each poem to the passages it became.** The poem #4 case shows the method. Tracing poems #1 to #15 into the named units would show which way material flowed and where the author's own lines were replaced.

## 5. Sending and answering

### Catalog: the acts after writing

- **Sending.** Poem #16 names it: "the courage now is not big / the courage now is the size of a text," and "the trembling that is the sound of the sentence leaving / the architecture."
- **The first sending.** The pilot letter, the first letter of the correspondence, was sent to two real readers before 2026-08-14. Its text is not in the vault.
- **Waiting for an answer.** No reply is recorded, and question S5 in the decision sitting (`ops/decision sitting — 2026-09-03.md`, the document gathering the questions only the author can answer) asks him whether either reader answered.
- **Answering.** The first letter asks the reader to finish a sentence before continuing: "something I can say now, that I could not have said an hour ago, is —" (reader O2).
- **The unanswered.** Poem #12 says: "i'm writing love poetry to nobody / in particular– just because it exists / and yet i'd be amiss if i said / it wasn't meant to mean something / to everybody i've ever loved."
- **The letter read alone.** The provenance reconstruction narrates the 4am rereading as "no mirror present, no reply possible" (`provenance/provenance fourth addendum.md`, line 139).

## 6. Plain statement

The first document's third finding reads the author's rule against mannered prose as the books' ethic at the scale of a sentence. It leads to the following catalog:
- **The sentence that performs.** Rule 13 says the mannered phrase exists "to display the writer, not to convey the idea."
- **The sentence that produces agreement by being easy to read.** Section 3 above lists it among the false versions of the event.
- **The sentence that explains the reader out of an encounter.** The model's rule against it is at 17197.
- **The sentence that can be refused.** A derived conclusion in the vault's file of derivations reads: "vagueness is a subtle colonization, so a claim stated crisply enough to be refused is a gift to the reader's sovereignty" (reader F2; `notes/a claim stated crisply enough to be refused is a gift to the reader's sovereignty.md`).
- **The sentence built to be answered.** A vault note states it: "a sentence built to be answered hands the reader the next move where one built to be believed closes over them" (`notes/a sentence built to be answered hands the reader the next move where one built to be believed closes over them.md`).
- **The naked sentence.** Poem #16 describes it: "the naked sentence is not hard / you matter to me / i miss you / i want to stay in the thread / three sentences a child could say." The vault records that "the circle is the form in which the naked sentence gets met" (`notes/the circle is the form in which the naked sentence gets met.md`).

## 7. What this division leaves out

This division lists how material comes to belong to someone. It does not decide that anything belongs to anyone. Every level above "proposed," for any piece of material, is the author's to set, and every reader's is the reader's.
