# The Loci — the first family, deepened

*A locus is a standpoint. A lens is a locus. This is the first family of `the-lenses.md`, built out as the worked example of what a deepened family looks like — not a closed description but a generative frame that grows by being questioned.*

## What a locus is

A locus is a bounded place to see from. Standing in one, some things become visible and others fall dark — and that is not a defect but the condition of seeing anything at all. The book borrows the Kashmir-Śaiva term *saṅkoca* for this contraction of awareness into a standpoint — but reads it as the *condition of seeing* rather than as bondage, which is the book's own re-valuation and a departure from classical Trika (where saṅkoca is predominantly bondage): recorded as a departure, never cited as convergence (`ops/decisions.md`, 2026-08-13). Held on its own relevance — *not* welded to saṅkoca in one circuit, since saṅkoca's own pair is *vikāsa*, expansion, and vimarśa is a distinct element (`ops/decisions.md`, 2026-08-20) — is *vimarśa*, the reflexive awareness of the standpoint one occupies, noticing *"this is where I'm seeing from, and here is what it hides."* So the book's method (read from many standpoints, let none rule) and these terms rhyme, each held for its own reason rather than fused into a single mechanism. That is why the Loci is the first family: it is the layer at which "a lens" stops being a metaphor and becomes the thing the book is actually about.

The one law over the whole family: **no locus becomes sovereign.** The moment a standpoint says "now I see how all the others fit," it has stopped being a locus and started colonizing. The polyphony exists to keep that from happening quietly.

**The structural form.** Every locus takes the same shape — a *named standpoint* with a *few attested members* (the thinkers, concepts, and traditions that occupy it) and an *open edge* (the members not yet named). The members are always partial. That partiality is the family's engine: each locus can always be asked what else belongs to it.

## The apparatus — the five questions you ask of any locus

These are the Five Great Questions the reframe conversation reached (`the-conversation-catalog.md`, Family B), turned from a reader's cycle into an explorer's engine. Run them against any locus — or against any named domain anywhere in the catalog — and the domain grows.

1. **Locus — *from where does this see?*** Name the standpoint precisely enough that its edge is visible.
2. **Encounter — *what is actually attested here?*** List the members already named — thinkers, concepts, traditions that genuinely occupy this standpoint (not ones that merely rhyme with it).
3. **Recognition — *what does standing here disclose, and what does it hide?*** Every locus has a gift and a blind spot; naming both is the *vimarśa* check that keeps it from over-reaching.
4. **Perspective — *what's around this that we haven't named yet?*** The generative frontier. What members are implied but unrecorded? What sits just outside the named set? This is the question that makes the layer self-growing.
5. **Practice — *what does this locus let the book do?*** Where it enters the manuscript, and what it makes possible that no other standpoint does.

The fourth question is the one you pointed at, and it is the whole reason this can be a substrate rather than a summary: a summary is finished when written; a frame built around question 4 is never finished, because it always names its own frontier.

## The register of loci

The standpoints the book actually sees from. The well-populated ones first, then the thin frontier where question 4 bites hardest. Each carries its attested members and its open edge; line numbers point into the transcript, `[[…]]` into the notes.

### 1 · The relevance locus — *"what matters here, and how did it become salient?"*
- **Discloses / hides:** how meaning and salience emerge from an unbounded field · hides the *what* of recognition (it explains the how), and the felt texture beneath the cognition.
- **Attested:** John Vervaeke; relevance realization; the four kinds of knowing (propositional / procedural / perspectival / participatory). *(2082–2145)*
- **Open edge:** who else stands here? — Gendlin's felt sense as *pre*-relevance; the acceleration theorists (Rosa, Han) as relevance-under-pressure; predictive-processing accounts; the attention-economy designers who engineer salience *against* the person.
- **Enters:** the rigorous diagnosis of why a knowledge graph can hollow its author — propositional knowing crowding out the other three.

### 2 · The recognition locus — *"what is already present that I've failed to recognize?"*
- **Discloses / hides:** recognition as un-forgetting of what never left; awareness recognizing itself (vimarśa); the pulse (spanda) · hides the cognitive mechanism, the social field, and empirical grounding.
- **Attested:** Utpaladeva, Kṣemarāja, Abhinavagupta; spanda, saṅkoca, vimarśa, pratyabhijñā. *(2986–3021, 3236–3292)*
- **Open edge:** its Western twin, Heidegger's *aletheia*, is a foundation but not yet seated *as a locus*; the neighbouring non-dualisms (Advaita's *neti neti*, Dzogchen *rigpa*) sit just outside; the four *upāyas* are sub-loci within this one.
- **Enters:** the book's foundation (aletheia × pratyabhijñā); the metaphysical machinery under "a lens is a locus."

### 3 · The phenomenological locus — *"what is actually occurring before I explain it?"*
- **Discloses / hides:** lived experience prior to any frame; embodiment; the felt · hides structure, system, and the collective.
- **Attested:** Merleau-Ponty; Gendlin (the felt shift); the contemplative report read as experience. *(2376–2419, root C)*
- **Open edge:** the micro-phenomenologists (Varela's neurophenomenology, Petitmengin's elicitation) and the somatic lineages (Levine, Ogden) — the *body* as a standpoint is gestured at but barely seated.
- **Enters:** the felt-shift verification floor; "knowing through" rather than "knowing about."

### 4 · The dialogical locus — *"what becomes possible between us that neither could produce alone?"*
- **Discloses / hides:** relational emergence; the third entity; meaning arising *between* · hides the individual interior and the solitary reader.
- **Attested:** `[[martin buber]]`, `[[de jaegher and di paolo]]`; Bohm and Heron & Reason are named but unseated (candidate reads — no note yet); Authentic Relating / Circling. *(1615–1791; root D)*
- **Open edge:** the group scale (Scharmer's collective presencing, Turner's communitas) sits above the dyad unseated; and the *machine-relational* — human↔AI as a genuine dialogical locus, not merely a tool — is named but not developed.
- **Enters:** the relational field (Book I's home); "nothing transforms from the audience."

### 5 · The systemic locus — *"what conditions make this participation more or less possible?"*
- **Discloses / hides:** incentives, the architecture of attention, institutions, the ecology of conditions · hides felt experience and the person.
- **Attested:** `[[gregory bateson]]`; Meadows is named but unseated (a candidate read — no note yet); the attention-economy critique; systems thinking. *(5374–5408; XIV 8133)*
- **Open edge:** the civilizational-condition theorists — Illich, Borgmann (the "device paradigm"), the platform/algorithm critics — who name how "technology participates in constructing the conditions under which we participate."
- **Enters:** the "why now" diagnosis; the container anatomy.

### 6 · The contemplative locus — *"what happens when attention becomes intimate with experience?"*
- **Discloses / hides:** presence, receptivity, the disciplines of attention · hides conceptual articulation and the social.
- **Attested:** the contemplative traditions — held (a key sort from the transcript) as *evidence-territories*, not method-forebears; Zen dokusan; meditation. *(1837–1860)*
- **Open edge:** `[[daniel ingram]]` and `[[jack kornfield]]` sit in the graph as interlocutors but are not yet related *into* this locus (a connection to make, not a reading to do); and the craft of *designed silence* — "where the author deliberately relinquishes control" — as a contemplative move in the prose itself is unwritten.
- **Enters:** the practice curriculum (Book II); the peak's withdrawal.

### 7 · The facilitative locus — *"what can I do without determining what another discovers?"*
- **Discloses / hides:** the levers a facilitator can touch — attention, pacing, constraint, question, safety — without owning the conclusion · hides the content of what's recognized and the metaphysics.
- **Attested:** `[[carl rogers]]`, `[[donald winnicott]]`; Grove (Clean Language), Schwarz (Mutual Learning), and Art of Hosting are named but unseated (candidate reads — no note yet); the author's own AR / Circling craft. *(1689–1771; 3586–3634)*
- **Open edge:** in the vault this seat is *currently unpopulated* — the function is scattered across reference-lineage and coaches with no clean seated set; and Moreno's psychodrama is named-but-unseated beside the seated `[[viola spolin]]`. This is the widest open edge in the register relative to the locus's importance.
- **Enters:** Book I entire; the facilitator-disappears arc.

### 8 · The four-quadrant grid — *a locus that generates loci*
Not one standpoint but a coverage instrument: **Interior/Individual** (experience, meaning) · **Exterior/Individual** (behaviour, embodiment) · **Interior/Collective** (culture, shared meaning) · **Exterior/Collective** (institutions, technology, civilization). *(2650–2691; XI–XIII 8049)*
- **Its gift:** run any concern through all four and the blind spot appears. The book's own blind spot, by this test: it over-weights *interior/individual* (felt experience, awareness) and under-weights *exterior/collective* (institutions, technology, civilization). Question 4, asked of the whole book, points here.

### The frontier — thin loci where "what's around this?" bites hardest

These are named but barely populated. They are the register's growing edge, and the clearest evidence that the layer is generative rather than finished.

- **9 · The poetic locus — *"what draws the reader in before any argument does?"*** Beauty as the felt lure; "the ecologies are rhythmic before they are conceptual" *(979–1007)*. Almost nothing is seated here — yet the chapter-patrons are poets (Rilke, Rumi, Eliot, Whyte, Oliver, Le Guin), occupying the book as epigraphs while the *poetic standpoint itself* goes unwritten. A whole unnamed chapter lives in this gap.
- **10 · The genealogical locus — *"where did this actually descend from?"*** Lineage and descent. Used in exactly one place (`[[the apparatus descends from the settlement the book positions itself against]]`) and nowhere else; the full descent-mapping of the book's own constructs is unnamed territory.
- **11 · The developmental locus — *"what stage of maturation is this?"*** Mezirow's transformative learning; state-vs-trait; the five metabolic states the vault already borrows for `state:`. Thinly seated as a *standpoint*, though the material is everywhere.
- **12 · The technological locus — *"who participates in forming this understanding?"*** The double meaning — external technology (AI, media, systems) and internal technology (attention, practice) — and whether the external can strengthen the internal rather than replace it *(14207–14260)*. Named as a concern; not yet worked as a standpoint one reads the whole book from.
- **13 · The margin / answerability locus — *"who is this said before, and can it be answered?"*** The letter genre; the word that expects a reply. The *material* is richly seated already — a whole `[[answerability]]` map and `[[the book's native genre is the letter from a friend read alone]]` — so what is missing is only the *standpoint framing* (reading the book from the addressed reader's chair), not the content; and this is the highest-leverage single seat in the polyphony, because the Axioms, the Paradoxes, and the Constellation each flag answerability as *their* open edge too, so drawing it once closes an edge in four families at once.

## How this family grows — the substrate loop

Here is the thing you were reaching for. This file is not finished when I stop writing it; it is finished each time question 4 comes up empty, and it never does for long. The loop:

1. **Ask Perspective of a locus** — "what's around this we haven't named?" — and get a list of unrecorded members (the open edges above).
2. **Send Encounter after them** — go read the named-but-unseated thinker, or the neighbouring tradition, at the primary text (this is what the reading queue already does).
3. **What the reading attests** becomes a new member of the locus; **what it discloses** may sharpen the locus's gift-and-blind-spot; **what it can't reconcile** goes to the clearing.
4. The register grows by one member, and its frontier moves.

Run that loop and the direction of flow inverts: instead of the research producing notes that later get summarized into a lens, **the lens's open edges tell the research what to go find, and the notes come back as attested members.** That is the "authoring substrate" pivot you named — the loci layer becomes the frame the research feeds *into*, not a surface downstream of it. The vault already has the machinery for step 2 (`ops/reading queue.md`) and step 3's overflow (`ops/clearing.md`); what this file adds is step 1, the generator, which the vault has never had.

This is held as a proposal, not a decision. What it would change: the reading queue would take its questions partly *from the loci's open edges*; a note, when minted, would record which locus it attests; and `the-lenses.md` would become the index over a set of growing family-files like this one, rather than a static list. None of that is done — it is what the pivot would mean, so you can weigh it.

The part of the mechanism that *is* now built is the checking. This file carries a content-hash receipt for each of its sections (the hidden markers below each block), and `scripts/queries/lens-check.sh` re-computes them to report which sections still rest on unmoved ground and which need re-reading — the same substrate the Axioms family runs on, described in full in `the-axioms.md`. So the register above cannot silently rot: when a source it was read from changes, the section that cited it says so.

## What I watched, building this

You asked me to watch what makes sense before we consider a workflow per lens. Three things became clear:

- **The apparatus generalizes cleanly.** The five questions are not specific to the Loci — they are the general form for interrogating *any* named-domain-with-members in the catalog. The Axioms family, the Constellation, the Concerns — each is a named domain with attested members and an open edge, and each would deepen under the identical five questions. So one method builds every family; the families differ only in their members, not in their shape.
- **The Perspective question is the whole engine, and also the whole risk.** It is what makes the layer generative — and it is *unbounded by design*. Run against the corpus, it will surface far more candidate members than the book can hold. So the readiness question for a workflow is not "can it generate?" (it obviously can) but **"is the admission discipline strong enough to hold the flood?"** The vault's guards — the via-positiva bar, the writing test, the clearing's inverted admission — are exactly that discipline, but they were built for a trickle of hand-minted notes, not a workflow's output. Before a per-lens workflow, we would want to have run this loop by hand enough to know what its output *looks like* and how much of it survives the guards.
- **The unit of the workflow is now obvious.** A family-deepening workflow would, per locus: run Encounter (gather attested members from notes + corpus), run Recognition (state gift/blind-spot), run Perspective (surface the open edge against the catalog + corpus), and return a structured register entry plus a reading-queue proposal for each open edge. That is a clean, bounded fan-out — one agent per locus, one synthesis pass — and it is genuinely workflow-shaped. But I'd hand-build one or two more families first (the Axioms is the natural second, being the other pure named-domain-with-members), so the template is stable and we've felt the volume before we let a workflow produce it.

My recommendation: **stay hands-on for one more family**, watch the output volume, confirm the guards hold — and if they do, the workflow-per-lens becomes the right tool, with the loci's open edges as its brief. The pivot to substrate is the larger decision underneath, and this file is meant to let you feel whether it earns the change.
