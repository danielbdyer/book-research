# The Arc — the sixth family, the book as a made object walked in order

*The book read as the thing it is: a made object that "performs what it describes," walked by a reader from preparation to integration. Where the Two Books family reads the book by its **locus of practice** and the Constellation reads it by **who else reached each finding**, this family reads it by **what the reader undergoes, in order** — the arc that is the map's one stable element — and by the devices, images, drafts, and making that carry that arc. It is authored from `notes/book-structure.md`, the graph's second-largest uncovered region, so it is the coverage pivot's third graph-authored harvest. Its disciplines are the book-structure map's own: the arc is stable but **the chapter scheme is not** (units are named, never numbered — `ops/methodology/reference by name.md`); the drafts are quoted for what they say, never credited for what they argue; two forks here are the author's to close, not the family's.*

## What this family is, and the apparatus

The book's one design law governs everything this family gathers: "the book performs what it describes" (`[[the book performs what it describes]]`). The reader consciously tours a framework and is carried, by the pacing itself, through the arc that framework describes — preparation, onset, deepening, peak, descent, integration. The five questions specialize to the object: **Locus** — which phase of the walk is this? **Encounter** — which named units, devices, and images build it? **Recognition** — what does the arc disclose as it turns, and where does the performing law bite hardest? **Perspective** — which drafts and which making-facts are settled, and which cells are nascent or the author's to decide? **Practice** — where the material enters the manuscript. The stable element is the arc; the assignment of named units to chapters is the working state, so this family refers to units by name and records the drafts' disagreements as differences rather than resolving them.
