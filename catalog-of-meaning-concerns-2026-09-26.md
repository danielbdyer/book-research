# The catalog of meaning: what is at stake

**What this document is.** This is one of six documents answering the author's request of 2026-09-26 to enumerate "the catalog of meaning which would be explored in the future" from what pull request #23 brings into scope. The first document, `catalog-of-meaning-2026-09-26.md`, names the whole space and explains how it is divided. This one divides it by what is at stake: the concerns and questions the books could take up. Every entry is a session's proposal, at the level "proposed," and every quotation says whose words it is. Numbers in parentheses are transcript lines of `reframe-conversation-full-2026-08-13.md`, and poems are cited by number.

**Where the concerns come from.** Five sources supply them, and the order is the order of standing in the vault.
1. **The author's poems.** The vault's six doctrine concerns all appear in his first fifteen poems (`notes/all six doctrine concerns appear in the first fifteen poems.md`), and a reading of the whole collection for this catalog found further concerns there.
2. **The author's own turns in the conversation, and his reflection.**
3. **The vault's lists.** These are the six doctrine concerns and their topic maps (a topic map is a page gathering the claims of one concern), `ops/clearing.md` (the list of what the book's central commitments cannot account for), and `ops/open corpus.md` (territories the corpus named and never developed).
4. **The design** in `reframe-map-and-system-2026-09-25.md`, whose fifteen topics come from the transcript, most with a question in the model's words.
5. **The model's outlines** in the transcript, whose chapter titles are a long list of candidate territories at the level "proposed."

**How each concern is described.** Each entry gives the concern's question in plain words, says whose words the question is in, and then names what the vault holds, what the author's own words hold, and where the books could go. Where the entry is short, the concern is new or thinly held.

## 1. The six concerns the poems already contain

### Recognition

**The question.** What happens when a person recognizes what was already there? The vault states the concern as a finding, "deep change is un-forgetting, not addition" (`the-concerns.md`), and the design's question for it is the model's: "How do we create conditions in which recognition becomes possible without prescribing what must be recognized?" (2197).

**What the author's own words hold.** His founding insight: "neither of those is actually looked at as being fundamentally less than the other" (156), and freedom as "popping out of that bubble, observing that that is a bubble that contains the two parts" (166). His reflection says: "a trueness or a clarity that was always already there, but just now opened-with," and his refusal of the word "hiding" in favor of "a fundamental clenching that didn't obscure the vision but just made it transfigured as not directly perceivable." Poem #4 says: "you see, I didn't need / to hear what I already knew— / but it was nice to be vulnerable / enough to recognize the answer / I had always had." Poem #10 ends "a crescendo clearing the mist / a recognition therewith."

**What the vault holds.** The topic map for recognition (the page gathering its claims), with 109 notes; the founding convergence of Heidegger's *aletheia* and the recognition school; the reflection and the one comparison the author made in it; and the recognition drafting document, which stops at a blank sentence for him (`drafts/recognition — a surface to write from.md`).

**Where the books could go.**
- **Opened-with, opened-by, opened-into.** The author's reflection sorts three ways a person can be opened, and the vault's survey of practices uses them. They are his own distinction, and Book I could hand them to facilitators as a way to see what their own moves do.
- **Recognition without the concept of it.** His reflection's "courage without needing to recognize it as courage" names recognition complete before anyone names it.
- **Book I and Book II.** The material for each book is thin: the transcript offers "Recognition Cannot Be Manufactured" (15455) for Book I and "You are already participating" (16084) for Book II, both the model's.

### Suffering: contraction, not corruption

**The question.** Which suffering comes from a contraction a person can learn to undo, which is a harm to be borne and never recast as the person's own doing, and which is a condition outside the person that has to be changed? The drafting document on suffering puts the first line in the corpus's image, as the line "between a fold a facilitator may help a person learn to open, and a wound or an injustice that it would be a violence to reframe as folded" (`drafts/contraction-not-corruption — a surface to write from.md`).

**What the author's own words hold.** Poem #4 says: "even uncertain, I am tethered; never broken." His reflection's "fundamental clenching." At 166: "play is, you know, writ very large here. We're talking about suffering," and "the whole fu- fundamental human condition is kind of wrapped around these sort of stories of disconnection or reaching towards connection, not having connection." At 156: "we don't have to judge the self-identifying parts of ourselves."

**What the vault holds.** 48 notes; the book's sentence "Nothing in you is broken. Something in you is folded" (first-telling); the four meanings of privation, among which the author's founding experience belongs to one, a fullness that has contracted (reader F1); five coaching theories of where suffering lives, read in their own words on 2026-09-02; and the drafting document's warning that the reading is "a mercy when it lifts a verdict off a person, and a cruelty when it is turned on a harm arranged outside them."

**Where the books could go.**
- **A person's own habit, or a harm done to them.** The warning above joins the question of power: some contraction is a person's own habit, and some is done to them. Book I needs the line between them, since reframing a harm "would be a violence."
- **Stories of disconnection.** The author's phrase at 166 describes the human condition as a story, which the book's practices can meet.
- **No practice.** The concern has no practice note, although the corpus has "Bowing to the Gatekeeper" (abridged ch14).

### Verification: how anyone knows that something happened

**The question.** How does a person, a reader, a facilitator, or a tradition know that something really happened, and who can tell a real change from a false one? The design has no place for this; pull request #23 found most of the felt-shift material is about it.

**What the author's own words hold.** "the book can't verify that you've done the work" (266); "nothing transforms because of the audience" (266); and the false peak (286). His own later question, B3 in `ops/practice-model ecology — candidate next steps.md`, asks whether his standard is the felt shift or durability. Poem #14 names his own chase of the counterfeit: "the plateau-seeking enthusiastic bliss-mongering behavior routines."

**What the vault holds.** 79 notes; Gendlin read in three works; the two strongest objections (Nisbett and Wilson; Schwitzgebel) read in full; the finding that "a book read alone can state the whole of the restraint and none of the discrimination"; and about 10,000 words of drafts defending the standard.

**Where the books could go.** See section 3 of `catalog-of-meaning-ownership-2026-09-26.md`, which lists the signs, the false versions, and what a page can and cannot carry.

### The container

**The question.** What makes a space one in which the hidden can appear? The design's question for this concern is the model's words, which the author adopted as "the question of my life" (1190): "how do we hold just enough form so that surprise can still happen?" (1183).

**What the author's own words hold.** "constraints are um in design are exactly what gives fluency... deprivation actually leads to recognition of that which stands essential" (1074); "emergent reality is the only way to really define it in a way that satisfies that core constraint" (1210); and, from the vision brief he dictated on 19 June 2026 and the provenance record marks as his own words, "I'm a poet, a systems thinker, and an Authentic Relating facilitator, and the thing running underneath all of it is the same: I build containers where things — people, ideas, a self — become more fully themselves" (`provenance/provenance of the corpus round two.md`, lines 13–16). Poem #2 describes a container chosen by two people, "a sanctuary for their love that is both passionate and chaste."

**What the vault holds.** 28 notes; the corpus's three structures of a container; the readings of the Daodejing, Pseudo-Dionysius, Luria on *tzimtzum*, the Philippians hymn, and Weil; and three readings owed: Goffman, "the strongest objection," on institutions that keep every structure of a container "and a dead center"; Scotus, Hopkins, and Deleuze; and the early Hasidic sources on worship through the body.

**Where the books could go.**
- **Institutions as containers.** Goffman's total institution is the container at the scale of a society: schools, clinics, workplaces, and platforms can contain a person without serving the person's own participation.
- **The container the author builds.** His sentence of June 2026 is his own view of this concern, in his own words. Two notes quote it as evidence about the making of the book; no note records it as his view of what a container is for.

### The relational field

**The question.** How does something happen between two people that neither makes alone, and how does each remain themselves within it? The design's question here is the model's: "How do I remain myself while genuinely allowing relationship to change me?" (18320).

**What the author's own words hold.** The letter at 770; "the book itself becomes me writing to the Third Thing in order to enact its outcomes through and with and upon myself" (his statement of 2026-08-02, `notes/the book is written to the relational field and enacted upon the author first.md`); his reflection's "the conviction of commutativity giving way to the third thing"; poem #16's "current"; poem #12's "we saw it because / you saw it and you shared it to me."

**What the vault holds.** 136 notes, far more than the roughly twenty-five a topic map may list before the vault splits it, with a planned division into sovereignty and facilitation; fifteen notes on facilitation craft in the model's words; and the survey of the second person.

**Where the books could go.** The relationships companion, `catalog-of-meaning-relationships-2026-09-26.md`, treats this concern by relationship. Its central opening is the author's own facilitation, which no note records.

### Address: the call and the answering

**The question.** What makes a word something a reader can answer rather than simply obey, and what does it become once it is sent? The question is reader O2's proposal, not the author's.

**What the author's own words hold.** Poem #16 says: "the courage now is not big / the courage now is the size of a text." Poem #12 says: "i'm writing love poetry to nobody / in particular– just because it exists / and yet i'd be amiss if i said / it wasn't meant to mean something / to everybody i've ever loved." Poem #15 opens "I dare you to kiss me. I dare you to know me for who I am."

**What the vault holds.** 58 notes; the correspondence as the chosen form; the pilot letter, sent; and the brief's rule that "a letter never answered has failed as a letter" (`drafts/brief — the first letter.md`).

**Where the books could go.** Section 5 of the ownership companion lists sending and answering; section 11 of the material companion lists forms and addressees.

## 2. Two further rows

### The company of thinkers

**The question.** What do traditions that reached the same finding independently show, and what survives their differences? "What survives the difference?" is the model's (3164).

**What the author's own words hold.** "it's really just like another set of fingers pointing to the moon" (224); "I don't claim a straight lineage there at all" (186); his decision of 2026-08-16 naming Kashmir Śaivism the tradition the book owes most; and, from his reflection, "it was never meant to be an all-encompassing constellation."

**Where the books could go.**
- **The ethics of borrowing in his own terms** (224), the catalog of refusals, and the book's own departures, which are listed in section 5 below.
- **Company, stated as company.** The model said of a finding reached from several traditions that "It is something that becomes increasingly trustworthy because you've encountered it from multiple independent loci" (3114). The vault's standing rule says the opposite: the traditions were gathered by one collector for the property they share, so "convergence is company, never corroboration" (`CLAUDE.md`). The model's own rule from the same outline agrees with the vault: "Never collapse a correspondence until the difference has been adequately understood" (3344). The books could state the difference openly, as part of how they hold their company.
- **How much the books show of what they owe.** The author said at 2975 that "one thing I’ve not yet revealed is how deep the Śaivism underpinning goes," and on 2026-08-16 he named Kashmir Śaivism the tradition the book owes most. The model named two ways to get this wrong: hiding how much the books owe the tradition, and writing "Here is Kashmir Śaivism translated into contemporary language" (3356). How openly the books show what they owe it, between those two, is a concern of its own.

### The return

**The question.** What happens after the high point, when ordinary life resumes? The design found Integration its thinnest topic, and the vault's reading of the book's structure found "Book II's return is the thinnest position in the whole graph" (`ops/scaffold.md`, §14).

**What the author's own words hold.** His decision of 2026-08-20, "peak resolves into return." Poem #7 says: "good endings aren't really endings / they're just ways of being sure / that what you're over is like / a bird flying over – yes, it's gone / but does it really cease to exist." Poem #12's "beginning again." Poem #4 says: "I am home — living, enjoying the weather."

**Where the books could go.** Home, beginning again, and what happens when nothing happens, all listed below.

## 4. Concerns the poems hold that no list names

The vault's list of what the book's center cannot account for (`ops/clearing.md`) read the poems once, on 2026-08-10, and asked three questions of them: whether the book wants death back as a fear rather than a phase (poem #15), whether the more-than-human witness to a grief belongs in the inner life the book describes (the cats of poem #8), and whether the book keeps "the raw second-person address, before it became a standard" (poem #15's "I dare you"). The concerns below are ones this reading found and no list names.

- **The carer's longing.** What does care cost the one who gives it? Poem #16's "cathedral of care... so acoustically beautiful / that my longing never had to arrive naked," and poem #8's "my burning desire to take care of the world around me." Poem #16 is the author's flagged exception, written after the corpus and in its language. The concern belongs to Book I, as the facilitator's own need.
- **Chasing the high.** What does the search for the next peak do to a life? Poem #14 says: "the plateau-seeking enthusiastic bliss-mongering behavior routines no longer wreak havoc on my life as they once did," and "my absolutism to love like a naturalized deity has brought me to my knees." This is the false peak in the author's words, and it is autobiographical rather than theoretical.
- **Presence that has to be renewed.** Poem #16 says: "i had been mistaking love preserved / for presence ongoing." The concern is what keeps a relationship current, as against merely kept.
- **Home.** Poem #1 ("the concept of home troubles my elderly father"), poem #4 ("I am home"), poem #7 ("home again, / what a relief, but is it?"), and poem #16 ("the place home before I knew the word home / the home where people still say my name / like they mean it"). The corpus ends on "welcome home," and the return is the vault's thinnest place.
- **Grief and reckoning.** Poem #7 opens "find me at the intersection of / grief and a reckoning." The vault's note `notes/grief cannot complete alone.md` records grief as needing a witness.
- **Surrender and effort.** Poem #7 says: "complete surrender / and trying one's very hardest." Poem #11 says: "we don't / need to try in order to receive it." Question SO-09 in the decision sitting (`ops/decision sitting — 2026-09-03.md`, the document gathering the questions only the author can answer) asks whether poem #7 holds these simultaneously by conviction or leaves them unresolved by choice.
- **Beginning again.** Poem #12 repeats "beginning again" through leaving, age, and crying: "even leaving, / beginning again, / even older, beginning again; even crying, / beginning again." It is a concern about recurrence, which the model's rule "support recurrence without requiring repetition" (10003) states abstractly.
- **Why now, in the author's words.** Poem #3 says: "the new paralysis of modernity / is dreaming so longingly about what could be / that we forget to live life in the now." It is the design's ninth function in his words, written before the conversation's account of the attention economy.
- **Power.** Poem #6 says: "i am not ready for someone in power of us who takes their power and uses it in feckless vain masking fearfulness with irresponsiblity and lies." On 2026-08-23 the vault deleted its question on power and the one open disagreement it had recorded about it.
- **Shared perception.** Poem #6 says: "i know the particulars of how sky becomes blue but could not demonstrate it for you so thank goodness we share that perception." This is the difference between knowing about something and sharing it, in the author's words.
- **Writing from the present.** Poem #11 says: "(only write from the now / right about now lest / you leave behind a part of / yourself in a place you cannot see)." It is a rule for writing the books, in the author's own voice.
- **Courage.** The reflection's "a deep essential moment of courage without needing to recognize it as courage," and poem #16's "the courage now is the size of a text."
- **Being alive.** Poem #10 says: "had I forgotten / what it means to be alive sometimes?" Poem #6 says: "i am still here." The model's question "what relationships reliably increase aliveness?" (981) is not in the vault.
- **Being known.** Poem #15 says: "I dare you to know me for who I am." Poem #16 says: "i will not translate myself into a shape / that can be loved / without being known."

## 5. Concerns the reports and this reading surfaced

- **When nothing happens.** No catalog holds the practice that produces nothing, the plateau, the dry season, or the question of whether anything happened at all. The members are gathered in the first document's sixth finding. The model's question belongs here: "how does a practice change when silence is the main teacher for a while?" (1079).
- **What arrives unbidden.** The book's rule that nothing transforms from the audience holds that "whatever transforms, the reader did it" (`CLAUDE.md`). The 4am rereading of the letter arrived from another person's hand, and the recognition school's teaching of grace (*śaktipāta*) and the second-person survey's role of "the giver" press on the rule. A concern on what arrives unbidden could keep the rule and name its limit (reader O2).
- **The authority hidden in restraint.** The model warned, in the outline that proposed Book I: "'I am creating conditions for your authentic experience' can itself become an extraordinarily subtle form of authority" (1805). Book I's promise is to teach facilitators to create conditions, so the risk lies inside Book I's own method: the stance the books recommend can become a form of the power they refuse. The model called the question that states it "a crucial shadow question" (1803): "When does facilitation become another form of colonization despite intending to liberate?" (1801).
- **Change without a claim that it works.** The model proposed that Book I teach "how to recognize when an encounter has actually changed something" (2451) and gave no criteria, and it proposed a test from Vervaeke: "Does this actually improve the agent's capacity to navigate reality?" (2316), adding "If not, perhaps we've generated another beautiful symbolic system" (2328). The author's stance makes no claim that the practices work (`ops/methodology/authorial stance.md`). Between these lies a concern of its own: how a person can notice that something changed for them without the books claiming that the practice causes change.
- **The rhetoric of manufactured recognition.** Fluent prose, induced moments of insight, and a trained vocabulary can each produce a report of recognition without the thing (reader F1). Book I could teach facilitators to notice it in their own work.
- **Offering.** The author described his opportunity as "offering the durable perspective I can acquire from it" (2975). What it means to offer a view, as a letter offers, rather than to teach it, is a concern of its own.
- **The book's own departures.** The places where the book deliberately differs from its company are what only this author says. The vault records them as departures and never as convergences (`ops/decisions.md`, 2026-08-13).
- **Forgetting.** The model said "The corpus should allow you to forget safely" (6440); the front matter says "You've just forgotten. Reading is remembering." The two senses of forgetting, as a design value and as the human condition, were never connected.
- **The body.** The author's first description of spanda was bodily ("you are about to sneeze or you've just sneezed, or you stub your toe," 52), and the model's list of what an insight must survive includes "embodied testing" (6880), which it never developed. The vault's list of what the center cannot account for names the embodied as a gap that readings of Merleau-Ponty have since begun to fill.
- **The center in the author's own words.** "Sufficiency" was the author's word before the model introduced "sovereignty" (446; 862), and "locus of control" was his phrase for where a person stands in their own life: "orient your locus control within" (214), and "this is essentially what I meant earlier by locus of control" (2509). The model renamed the phrase "locus of inquiry" (2697), and the author never used the new name. Both of his words are about a person's standing in their own life, and in psychology "locus of control" names whether a person believes that what happens in their life depends on their own actions or on forces outside them (Rotter's work, which the vault has not read). Whether the books' center is better named by his words is a question only he can answer.
- **Play.** The author said "play is, you know, writ very large here" (166), and the model listed "attention, trust, language, play" among what an ecology of participation holds (421). No note takes up play as a concern.
- **Trust and reciprocity.** The model's answer to "what remains" named trust, "the kind that lets someone stay with uncertainty long enough for recognition to emerge," and reciprocity, "the work shaped not just by what the reader receives but how it changes you, the writer" (981).
- **Wonder.** The vault holds `notes/wonder is not the novice's state to be outgrown but the native air of the awake.md`, and the author's reflection ends its list of words for the third thing on "a preternatural prehension of naturalness."
- **Belonging and solitude.** The model's list of the concerns of relation ends with intersubjectivity, belonging, and solitude (18300–18302), and the model never develops the last two. The poems develop both.
- **The workplace.** The author's "why now" comes from his work as a software engineering manager (862), and poem #6 from a month of not working. The workplace as a place where participation is enlarged or taken is a concern the model named once as "Organizational" (5390) and never developed.

## 6. Concerns the vault named and set aside

**The four gaps in what the book can account for** (`ops/clearing.md`): the singular (what only one person or one tradition ever reached, which has no company by definition), the embodied, the finite, and the non-human. Three have since moved into ordinary notes as readings found a second tradition reaching the same finding; the singular has not.

**The four territories the corpus named and never developed** (`ops/open corpus.md`): the design of lived time at the scale of a day; the symbolic and external systems a person builds and lives inside; ritual as a form; and a group of single gestures, namely metaphor, nostalgia, habit, the mapping of paradoxes, and aesthetic practice. On 2026-08-09 the vault declined twelve such subjects with reasons (`ops/queue/declines.md`), among them "ritual as a structure that holds a self," "a schedule aligned to the nervous system," "curating beauty as a practice," "nostalgia as continuity across a threshold," and "the morning as the day's coherence." The day meets the author's own words in poem #16: "current is tuesday / current is game night / current is 1:13 a.m.," and the vault's note `notes/the tuesday is the temple.md` states the doctrine without the practice. Whether any of the four becomes a concern of the books is the author's decision; `system-and-vault-beyond-the-design-2026-09-25.md` proposes them as rows at the level "proposed."

## 7. Five open questions from the transcript that the vault never recorded

Pull request #23 found these in the transcript and nowhere in the vault (`system-and-vault-left-to-populate-2026-09-25.md`, Part 7):
1. **The form of the work:** a book, a workbook, a workshop, or a retreat. The author floated "a workbook that is aligned with the actual workshop" (356) and said "I keep calling it a book, but you know what I mean?" (286).
2. **Consciousness:** whether it occurs within reality or is fundamental, which the project "should not resolve" (2340–2366, the model).
3. **Aliveness:** "what relationships reliably increase aliveness?" (981, the model), which the model said "could spiral through the next years without needing to close."
4. **What recognition is made of:** whether it is "cognitive, embodied, relational, or only comes into being through their interaction" (421, the model).
5. **The invariants:** "what are the invariants of transformative inquiry? What remains true across retreats, authentic relating, contemplative traditions, despite different metaphysics" (421, the model).

Two cautions go with the list. The model's Book II is a book of self-facilitation, not the arc of the psychedelic experience that the author gave Book II on 2026-08-16, so its titles are territories and not an order.
