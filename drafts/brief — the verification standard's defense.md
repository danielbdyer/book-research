## The finding that reorganises this section

The vault holds a claim that has shaped how this concern was understood: the verification standard "rests on one name and cannot be defended by company." Every other claim the book's argument depends on is held up by independent arrivals, and this one is Gendlin's alone. The counterpoint round of 2026-08-06 added that nobody ever looked for company — "No item anywhere on its search map requests an independent arrival at the felt-shift standard, so the claim the book's argument most depends on is left a lineage position by the grid's own definition."

The sweep found company. It is filed on other maps, it was never counted, and — this is the part that reorganises the section — **it agrees with the standard's critics rather than with the standard's current form.**

Four traditions answer the question *how does a person know disclosure has happened*, and not one of them answers with an in-the-moment solitary somatic signal.

- **Zen** instructs the sitter to dismiss the compelling experience outright: "Never be tempted into thinking that these phenomena are real or that the visions themselves have any meaning… Whenever makyo appear, simply ignore them and continue sitting wholeheartedly" (Kapleau, *The Three Pillars of Zen*, Yasutani's introductory lectures). What it installs instead is external and relational — a teacher, in dokusan, discriminates makyō from kenshō. And the warning does not stop at the threshold: "One who becomes attached to what he or she realizes through satori is also still lingering in the world of makyo."
- **Sufism** separates the state that descends as a gift from the station earned "by the sweat of the brow," and gives the reason a second person is required: "a person in a state is the worst-placed judge of what it means."
- **The recognition school** puts wonder at the definition of reflective awareness — Abhinavagupta's "pratyavamarśaḥ svātma-camatkāra-lakṣaṇa," and Kṣemarāja's gloss that vimarśana is "camatkārāpara-paryāya," that whose other name is wonder — but it holds rasa to the theater, "tena nāṭya eva rasāḥ, na loke," and keeps recognition object-free and once-for-all against a relish that "lasts exactly as long as the gustation."
- **Ignatius** is queued and unread, and the stake is already written: his discernment rules either certify "by course and aftermath rather than by felt quality in the moment," which would give the standard a 450-year-old independent arrival carrying an aftermath criterion, a counterfeit doctrine and a director requirement at once, or they certify by consolation as such and the resemblance is verbal.

Gendlin's own footnote 13 points the same way, and it is the standard's own author saying it: working alone, a person goes "round and round, feeling the same series of emotions and lacking any referent movement," while "often even just saying to the other person a little of what one has been feeling and thinking produces direct reference and referent movement." Solitary focusing is "always unsteady and difficult."

And both of the standard's sharpest published opponents arrive at the same place from the other side. Nisbett and Wilson head a section "Inadequate Feedback." Schwitzgebel writes: "Because no one ever scolds us for getting it wrong about our experience and we never see decisive evidence of error, we become cavalier. This lack of corrective feedback encourages a hypertrophy of confidence."

**So the honest shape of this section is not "here is a solitary somatic check and here is its defense."** It is: the check is real, it certifies less than the book has claimed, its solitary in-the-moment form is the weakest version of it, and every tradition and every critic that has thought hard about this says the same thing — that what settles it is time, aftermath, and another person. That is a stronger and more defensible position than the one the book currently occupies, and it is the position its own foundations already hold.

## 1. What the check is, and the three facts that make it hard

The standard, installed at the primitive level so the whole stack inherits it: "Nothing enters the lattice the body has not countersigned" (atlas §III). The reader-facing form and the genre claim that follows: the book "is handing you the means of its own falsification, sentence by sentence," which makes it "a different kind of object from a belief system" (first-telling: the instrument). The click is "unmistakable once you've felt it, and impossible to fake," and "Misses count as data."

The research sentence beneath it: "what is split off, not felt, remains the same; when it is felt, it changes" (walked III). Walked III's account of why it matters — none of the field's assumed candidates predicted change, and the predictor was attending to "a not-yet-worded bodily knowing." **Unverified:** that sentence is quoted from *Focusing* (1978), which is unread here.

**Three facts make this the hardest thing in the book to defend, and all three belong early rather than in a footnote.**

*It has one bearer.* "The form of support the book relies on everywhere else is not available here… Whatever defense it gets has to be built against the objections rather than out of arrivals." That claim now needs the narrowing above: the company exists for the *relational and aftermath* forms of the check, and not for the solitary somatic one.

*The author's own assessment is that it is underdefended*, and that "everything else runs downstream of it" (2026-08-04, settled in `ops/decisions.md`).

*The book is written against exactly the failure its own check is vulnerable to.* "We are not short on self-knowledge anymore… understanding has become easy to acquire, and easy to hide in" (first-telling: the problem). A book written against insight-as-hiding must verify by something insight cannot counterfeit, which makes a counterfeitable shift the book's own problem twice over.

## 2. The narrowing that the whole argument turns on

Gendlin does not claim what the book has claimed on his behalf. "'correctness' does not imply that a given set of symbols means what the felt referent alone means. Rather, 'correctness' refers to the experienced effect which certain symbols produce" (1964, fn. 9).

What the shift is: "not only a change but a directly experienced 'give' or 'movement' which feels right and welcome. Its tremendous importance lies in the fact that after such a referent movement (even very small), the implicit meanings are now different. The 'scenery,' as it were, which one confronts, changes" (def. 11).

The negative half, which is the usable instruction: "It is just this referent movement which is usually missing when one talks at oneself… From this lack of referent movement, one knows that nothing has really changed," and without it what is said is "'merely' talk, 'merely' intellectualization" (def. 11).

**The exposure this relocates rather than removes**, in the vault's own words: "a reader's shift at a sentence of the book establishes that the sentence moved them, not that it is true… it relocates the book's exposure from 'the body might misreport truth' to 'the book claims truth where its instrument certifies movement.'"

## 3. The attacks, and where each lands

**Nisbett and Wilson exempt the present sensation.** "he knows the focus of his attention at any given point in time; he knows what his current sensations are," among "private facts that can be known with near certainty" (1977, p. 255). Their target is "higher order, inference-based responses" (p. 233).

**And then attack change-detection directly**, in a section the vault did not know existed. "There is an important difference between awareness of the existence of an evaluation or motive state and awareness of a changed evaluation or motive state" (p. 235). Bem and McConnell: "subjects apparently changed their attitudes in the absence of any subjective experience of change" (p. 236). Goethals and Reckman, quoted in the paper: "No subject reported that the discussion had had any effect in changing or modifying his position" (p. 237). The failure is invisible from inside — the students did not feel uncertain, they felt certain they had not changed.

*The boundary that belongs with it:* both studies measure memory of a prior **attitude** across one to two weeks, and a felt shift compares a present bodily state against one held minutes earlier. The paper does not test that case.

**Schwitzgebel attacks the present bodily state, which is where the book stands.** His lead case is emotion and the features he says elude us are bodily: whether joy "is sometimes in the head, sometimes more visceral"; whether emotional consciousness "is simply the experience of one's bodily arousal"; whether it is "consistently located in space (for example, particular places in the interior of one's head and body)" (2008, p. 249). "Even the grossest features of emotional experience largely elude us." He names the exemption and rejects it: "We may concede the past to the skeptic, but not the present" is "blatantly unrealistic" (pp. 250–251). The only secure cases he grants are "really vivid, canonical pains and foveal colors," and "to use these cases only as one's inference base rigs the game" (p. 259).

*What the book should quote from him anyway:* introspection is "both (i) possible, important, necessary for a full life… and (ii) highly untrustworthy" (p. 246). And his question aimed at exactly this book: the meditative traditions "take comfort in optimism about introspection 'properly' conducted… Might they be right in their doubts but less so in their hopes?" (p. 246).

**The trained-vocabulary objection, with two independent bearers.** Katz holds that a body trained by the book's vocabulary produces the shift in response to the training. Nisbett and Wilson reached the same premise from the laboratory a year earlier: accuracy occurs "wherever the culture, or a subculture, specifies clearly what stimuli should produce which responses," and "such accuracy cannot be regarded as evidence of direct access… It is evidence for nothing more than the ability to describe the formal rules of evaluation" (p. 254). A reader taught this book's vocabulary and told what a shift feels like has been given a subculture's rules and its feedback.

**Attention to the body is not accuracy about it.** Interoceptive accuracy, sensibility and awareness "were distinct and dissociable," and confidence tracked performance only in the most accurate participants (Garfinkel et al. 2015). The fluent reader is high sensibility with unknown accuracy — which is the book's own second reader type. *Measurement honesty:* the heartbeat-counting instruments behind accuracy scores are contested; the three-dimension model stands, the instruments are unsettled.

**And the one judgment the check cannot make.** "The book's whole method hands the judgment to the reader's own body… and this is the one judgment the body is worst placed to make, because both conditions present as the interface failing. A standard that works by asking the reader what they feel cannot arbitrate a question about whether what they feel is trustworthy." That is the dissolution-versus-regression hole, still `unassigned`.

## 4. The counterfeit, measured

Fluency: statements in easier-to-read contrast are judged true above chance on presentation alone (Reber and Schwarz 1999); fluent stimuli are judged "more true, more likeable, more familiar, more confident" (Alter and Oppenheimer 2009). Rhyme: rhyming aphorisms are judged more accurate than meaning-identical non-rhyming twins (McGlone and Tofighbakhsh 2000) — **and the effect attenuates when readers are warned, which is the teachability datum.** Decoration: a non-probative photograph beside a claim raises the rate at which it is judged true, and the effect generalises to words, so "marginal apparatus is not exempt" (Newman et al. 2012). False insight: "thirty-seven percent of wrong solutions received aha ratings above the average for correct ones" (Danek and Wiley 2017), and induced aha moments make unrelated statements — including false ones — feel more true (Laukkonen et al. 2020). The inverse: needless complexity lowers judged intelligence and trust, so "plainness is not merely a stylistic virtue but the low-counterfeit register" (Oppenheimer 2006).

**Two details that decide how this is written.** The discernment is teachable, because warning worked. And the counterfeit is weaker on average than the genuine article — aha is reliably stronger for correct solutions — so the claim to write is "the aha does not *certify*," never "the aha means nothing."

*The honest edge:* these effect sizes are lab-scale, measured on trivia statements and aphorisms rather than on transformative prose, and the extension to a book like this one is the vault's inference.

**The corpus's own two counterweights**, which the book already holds: "The click can lie. Processing-fluency research shows that ease of processing feels like truth" and the signal "can be muted precisely where the folds are deepest" (space-around: around the instrument).

**And a collision inside the executed draft:** "You can tell, instantly, whether you are inside the field of an ink that listens or whether you are reading marketing copy" (abridged ch2) is the exact judgment the fluency studies show being fooled.

## 5. What survives, and none of it is a feeling

**Refusal.** "He may find that his previous formulation which felt correct must be replaced by another which now feels more correct," and "Not even the individual himself judges it but, we might say somewhat poetically, his direct referent does the judging" (1964, def. 8 and fn. 11). The generalised form is the friction test: accept a claim of this kind only where it pays a cost in the world, and "reject any version whose only evidence is that it feels deep." *Its own limit, stated in the note:* "The test raises the bar; it does not clear it," because the report of a refusal may itself be unreliable.

**The step.** After referent movement "the meanings and symbolizations one formulates are different. The relevant considerations are different." The checkable question: can the reader now say something about their own situation that they could not before the page, or only repeat the page? *Two boundary stones from the same source:* "A felt sense is not an emotion," and emotions can "skip" the implicit meanings "like an animal whose response to hunger is to bite itself in the leg" — so intensity is no evidence. And "Logically, you should feel worse. Yet you don't… your body feels better, more free, released" — which separates the shift from pleasure, the counterfeit's native register.

**The source's own guards, which are teachable instructions rather than assertions.** "If you get a quick answer without a shift in the felt sense, just let that kind of answer go by." "Make sure the quality is sensed again, freshly, vividly (not just remembered from before)." "check in with its felt edge, a number of times during the rest of the day, and perhaps for several days." And the doubt held with an instruction rather than dismissed: "'Is this real? Maybe I'm fooling myself.'… They may be right, but they have to wait." *The vault's own sentence:* "the book does not need to invent its defenses against its own fluency; it needs to transmit its source's existing ones."

**The second person**, which is where §0's convergence lands. Gendlin's fn. 13 above; the corpus's own statement of the wound, "the book's deepest claim concerns the relational field, yet the reading experience is solitary" (atlas §XI.1d); and both attackers' feedback diagnosis. *The caution to keep:* neither attacker proposes a second person as a remedy, and the inference to the Second Chair is the vault's own.

**The repair the opponent named.** "Perhaps the right kind of learning, practice, or care could largely shield us from error — an interesting possibility that merits exploration! — but I see as yet no robust scientific support for such hopes" (2008, p. 259). Dated 2008.

**The standard is not naked in the literature.** The construct was operationalised as the Experiencing Scale in 1969, and a meta-analysis of ten studies with 406 clients finds experiencing predicts symptom improvement at r = −.19 (Pascual-Leone and Yeryomenko 2017) — modest and correlational.

## 6. The disagreement that must not be papered over

The step test says check across days. Nisbett and Wilson put distance in time first among the causes of inaccurate self-report: "Perhaps chief among the circumstances that should decrease accuracy in self-report is a separation in time between the report and the actual occurrence of the process" (p. 251).

Checking now exposes the reader to the well-made sentence. Checking later exposes the reader to rebuilding the earlier state from the present one, which is what the busing students did.

*The candidate resolution, derived and untested:* the immediate comparison certifies only that something is moving now, and the later check asks what can be said or done now that could not be before — an outward difference rather than a remembered feeling.

## 7. What the book may not claim, quoted from the directives

"The intention I have is to not be an author that presupposes a righteousness but to gesture towards that which has its own lineage and the crossovers I see… So far I don't have any evidentiary remarks." A crossover is an observation, not an argument. "The vault does not postulate efficacy… What the author is claiming is narrower and stated plainly: there seems to be something here."

The refusal a designed apparatus inherits: both founding traditions hold that the breakthrough cannot be manufactured — Heidegger's Gestell and "only a god can save us," and śaktipāta "independent of the recipient's effort or merit." "An apparatus can prepare; it cannot cause." Applied to the check, this is a discriminator rather than a warning: a bodily yes produced by an apparatus is, in their terms, the apparatus working rather than the world showing.

The anti-instrumentality clause every verification condition has to survive: "practice and realization are one… you do not do the practice to get somewhere; the doing is the somewhere" (walked IX), and "an exercise that says what it is for has already broken the clause."

The honest maximum: "The book is a tuning fork. Silent on its own, forever… You are the striking surface" (first-telling: the honesty). And "a text that managed its reader into the felt shift would have destroyed the conditions for it."

## 8. What is missing, so the draft does not pretend otherwise

*Unread and load-bearing:* Gendlin's *A Process Model*, where the note says the philosophical weight sits; *Focusing* at book length, so the life-sentence is unverified against its book; Gendlin's own outcome research (1968, 1969), which the corpus's research story rests on and which the 1964 paper itself calls "tentative"; Katz at his own text on this question; *Perplexities of Consciousness*, whose chapter on constant tactile experience bears on the practices more directly than the paper that was read; Petitmengin 2013, the one candidate for the evidence Schwitzgebel asked for; Britton and the contemplative adverse-effects literature; Ignatius's discernment rules, which §0 says could supply an independent arrival.

*Never run, and cheap:* the per-practice verification-conditions pass, which asks of every practice what the corpus says would tell a practitioner it worked, and where the corpus says nothing that absence is the finding. The count of corpus pages where the shift is used as a truth-certificate rather than a movement-certificate. Whether any instructed practice induces one of Gendlin's four excluded attention-kinds. And the most dangerous unrun check in the inventory: **whether the false-insight studies record downstream generative effects or stop at feeling-ratings** — because if false ahas produce restructuring indistinguishable from genuine ones, the step test collapses empirically, and it has never been tested against the counterfeit evidence it is offered against.

*Where the book itself supplies no test:* for most of the twelve practice forms the draft "asserts an effect rather than naming a signal," the deferral is to a companion volume that does not exist, the thirty-second practice "asks the reader to notice a change without saying what change counts," and "none of the seven documents states what the reader does when the felt check returns nothing."

*And the one occurrence of the promised event has never had the instrument run over it.* The 4am scene of 1 July 2026 — "The recognition didn't require the mirror" — is the single dated case where the two centerpieces meet, and the proposed check asks whether it contains the movement-markers the narrowed certificate requires. Nobody has run it.
