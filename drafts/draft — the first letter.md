Dear friend,

You already went. That is the thing I know about you, and it is almost the only thing, and it is enough to write to you on. The retreat, or the ceremony, or the week when everything finally opened, or the one conversation that felt like the floor of your life moving — you had it. It was real. I am not going to spend one sentence of this letter doubting what happened to you, because I had mine too, and the doubting was never the problem.

The problem was the Tuesday after.

Not even the Tuesday after — the third Tuesday after, when you could still remember that it happened but not what it knew. You did the things a person does. You told someone, and heard how it sounded smaller out loud. You wrote it down, and the writing was accurate and dead. Maybe you got very good at describing it. I got very good at describing mine. I want to say that plainly, because it is the part I am least proud of and the part you will recognize fastest: there were years when my account of what had happened to me was the best furnished room in my life, and I lived in it, and nothing in it moved.

If you have picked up this book, somebody has probably already offered you the two available explanations. One of them is precise about you in a way that quietly assumes something in you is malfunctioning. The other is kind to you in a way that goes vague exactly where you need traction. Precision that costs you your dignity, or dignity that costs you precision — that is the menu, and I sat with it for a long time, ordering one and then the other.

I think there is a third thing, and I think you already suspect it, and that suspicion is why you are holding this. I am going to tell you what I think actually happened to you — why it was real, and why it did not hold, and why neither of those facts is bad news. But not in this letter. Partly because it took me years and I do not trust a version of it that fits in one page, and partly because there is something I need from you first, and I mean that literally.

Here is the something.

Before you read on, put this letter down for a moment. Not as an exercise — there is nothing to do and nothing to get right. Just set it down, and let the last few minutes be whatever they were. Take one breath you are not using for anything. Then come back.

If you have just done that, or even half done it, notice that I have no way of knowing. That is the honest shape of what this is: I am writing to you, and I cannot see you. Every page of this book has that limit built into it, and I would rather say so in the first letter than perform otherwise for three hundred pages. A book is the weakest possible form of what I want to do with you, which is sit down across a table. I chose it anyway, because it is the form that reaches the hour you are actually in — and because a letter, alone among the things a person can print, is built to be answered.

So answer it. I am asking for an actual act, the smallest one I can think of. Take anything to write with, or the notes application you complain about, or the back of this envelope if you have made one of this page, and finish one sentence in your own words: *something I can say now, that I could not have said an hour ago, is —.* Do not check whether it is deep. Deep is one of the rooms we are going to leave. If nothing comes, write that nothing came, and date it; you have my word that this is not a failure of the exercise, because it is not an exercise. It is the first half of a correspondence, and I have no idea what you are going to say — which is, if I am honest, the whole reason I am writing.

I will tell you one more thing before I stop, and then I will stop, because a friend who cannot stop talking is a lecture in a coat. I do not know whether this book will change anything for you. I have watched the people who promise that, and I noticed that the promising was doing something for them that it never did for the person promised to. <!-- flagged: this sentence and the one before it walk the no-efficacy edge by stating it; the alternative is silent obedience, and the choice is yours --> What I can promise is smaller and I can keep it: I will not tell you anything I did not walk, I will date every letter so you can see what I knew when, and I will never claim to know your life better than you do. You are the one holding the pen at your end. That is not a limitation of this book. That is its subject.

Write your sentence. Then turn the page.

Yours,
*[the signature is yours to set]*
