# The catalog of meaning: what a book can carry

**What this document is.** This is one of six documents answering the author's request of 2026-09-26 to enumerate "the catalog of meaning which would be explored in the future" from what pull request #23 brings into scope. The first document, `catalog-of-meaning-2026-09-26.md`, names the whole space and explains how it is divided. This one divides it by what a book can carry to its reader: pairs of ideas, practices, questions, images, motifs, writing techniques, promises, orders, endings, and forms. Every entry is a session's proposal, at the level "proposed," and every quotation says whose words it is. Numbers in parentheses are transcript lines of `reframe-conversation-full-2026-08-13.md`, and poems are cited by number.

**How it relates to the design.** The design in `reframe-map-and-system-2026-09-25.md` lists eleven kinds of material and fills them from the transcript in its Part 9, and `system-and-vault-beyond-the-design-2026-09-25.md` widens each kind with what the vault holds. This document does not repeat those lists. It adds three things they lack: the author's own material for each kind, the members the transcript's later outlines contain, and the kinds the design has no place for.

### Distinctions among more than two terms

The vault records sets of three or four terms that a pair cannot hold (reader O1), and the transcript adds more:
- **Four meanings of privation.** The vault distinguishes four ways of describing something by what it lacks: something added that must be removed; a fullness that has contracted; a capacity that never developed; something present rather than missing. The author's founding experience belongs to the second (reader F1).
- **Three accounts of why a big experience did not last.** The vault holds them apart "because they imply different books" (`notes/recognition.md`): the experience was interpreted by the structure it did not change; the window of plasticity reorganized only what attention was on; development was fractured.
- **Three ways a person can be opened.** The author's reflection distinguishes being opened with someone, by something, and into somewhere.
- **Kinds of remainder.** The model asked five questions of what is left over: "What remains?" "What remains unexplained?" "What remains alive?" "What remains resistant to synthesis?" "What remains mine?" (13986–13996).
- **Kinds of recognition.** The model listed seven: felt, conceptual, relational, embodied, existential, philosophical, and spiritual (5986–5992).
- **Seven ways of knowing something.** The model gave each its own question: information ("What has been said?"), interpretation ("What might it mean?"), experience ("What happens when it is lived?"), recognition ("What becomes apparent?"), integration ("How does it change the broader model?"), embodiment ("What becomes different in action?"), and transformation ("What kind of person becomes possible?") (9198–9224).
- **Degrees of presence.** The model distinguished being physically, cognitively, emotionally, relationally, and existentially present (18451–18455).

### False versions of each side

A polarity goes wrong on both sides. The vault records, for the founding polarity, "the false peak" on the side of expansion and "the racket" on the side of contraction (`notes/the pulse has a counterfeit at each phase, a false peak of expansion and a racket of forgotten contraction.md`). The model's list of eleven goods that go wrong without their partners ("pluralism without relativistic collapse, synthesis without homogenization, sovereignty without isolation, relationship without enmeshment," 10111–10121) is the fullest list of this kind in the transcript. The author's own poem #14 supplies a false version the model's list lacks: "plateau-seeking enthusiastic bliss-mongering."

## 2. Practices

Pull request #23 found practices the scarcest kind of material in the vault: nine notes, all in the felt-shift concern, and none recording the author doing the practice (`system-and-vault-left-to-populate-2026-09-25.md`, Part 1).

### Practices the author does or has done, in his own record

- **Meditation at a retreat**, from which he "took away this concept called spanda" (42), in March 2026.
- **Asking what remains.** "I always like to ask the question of like what what what remains here" (974).
- **Trying something on.** "the fundamental um ask I have of the reader is you know, try this on" (176), and of the model's answer at 1190, "to try it on and see whether it fits for me too."
- **Authentic Relating.** He described himself as "a trained authentic relating facilitator" (356). The May 2026 event ran on "a script of five agreements, trio rotations with a witness role, and a closing circle" (`notes/interlocutors.md`, line 249).
- **A prompt for a group.** He gave that group the prompt "Think of someone who loves you. Describe how they see you." (`provenance/provenance of the corpus round two.md`, line 100, in its retelling).
- **Crying, then sitting.** Poem #8 records it: "i cried, and i didn't know why i cried, so i meditated... and so i cried some more, but perhaps differently this time."
- **Writing without stopping.** His reflection says of itself: "just like my poems were spoken from the heart without stopping, this message/these messages/the current message I am authoring now was too."
- **Being read to.** His reflection records his father reading aloud to him, the earliest scene in his record.
- **Returning.** Poem #12 repeats "beginning again," and poem #16 says "i am building rooms with windows that open regularly / i am staying weather to the people i love."
- **Sending a small sentence.** Poem #16 gives the sentence itself: "the courage now is — hey. would you want this too?"

### Practices the transcript names that have no note

The transcript's practices for Book II are questions. Pull request #23 lists them as unrecorded: "Can I relate before I intervene?" (16298); the sequence for repair, which the model gives as notice, acknowledge, repair, and integrate (16472–16477); "What am I delegating?" "What am I losing through that delegation?" "What should remain mine?" (16567–16575); the questions a person asks as their own facilitator (13620–13632); noticing moments of contraction and expansion (1037); and "try this on" (176).

### Practices that could exist, by concern

- **Suffering.** A person could ask of a fold what shape it has, what it protected, and what would let it open (the vault's phrasing), or bow to the part that guards it, as in the corpus's "Bowing to the Gatekeeper" (abridged ch14).
- **The relational field.** The trio with a witness, the repair sequence, and the prompt about someone who loves you are practices already in the record.
- **Address.** A reader could finish the sentence "something I can say now, that I could not have said an hour ago, is —," which is the first letter's instruction, or send one small true sentence to one person.
- **The container.** A person could make one boundary on purpose for a set time, or keep a small record in the spirit of the author's own aim for his outliner: "Build it like an instrument I'll play daily, not a database I'll administer" (his vision brief of 19 June 2026, in `provenance/provenance of the corpus round two.md`).
- **Holding a disagreement open.** The vault holds ten untested moves, one for each of its open disagreements, awaiting the author's choice (reader F3). Their names include "forward hunger defeats the pause," "the reader's resistance has nowhere to land," and "dissolution versus regression."
- **Returning to life.** After a reading or a practice, a person could test what they found against their ordinary day, the step reader F4 called "the organ the text-only reading queue still lacks."
- **Leaving something unrecorded.** The model called it "equally valid" when an encounter leads to nothing (8834–8836). A practice of deliberately not writing something down belongs beside the practices of keeping records.

### What a practice record could say

Reader F1 proposed that a practice record say how one would know it worked: the sign, who can check it, its false version and who catches it, and whether a page can carry it. The author's own measure is smallness. The model's rule, "Practice should be small enough to remain alive" (16499), has no note, and only he can judge what is small enough.

## 3. Questions a reader can ask

The transcript is full of questions offered as practices. Gathered, they are a question bank a facilitator could use and a reader could use alone, of the same family as Clean Language's fixed questions. All are the model's unless marked.

- **The author's own questions.** "what what what remains here" (974); "Relationship to to what exactly" (830); "What is the ideal frame, if it's not a book" (286); and "what would that look like six months from now if you had to take a shot at it?" (1240).
- **The five questions.** "From where am I seeing?" "What is actually happening?" "What has become apparent?" "What remains when I move?" "What becomes possible now?" (7424–7442).
- **The inner facilitator's questions.** "What is happening?" "What wants attention?" "What conditions would allow something more truthful to emerge?" "What action preserves my participation?" (13620–13632).
- **Questions for a feeling.** "What is this feeling doing here?" "What conditions produced this response?" (16146; 16154).
- **The reader's questions of a framework.** "What do I see here? What don't I see? What does this framework make possible? What does it prevent? What happens when I try this? What remains after I stop using the framework?" (4944–4954).
- **Questions of a tradition.** "What human concern does this tradition notice?" and seven more, ending "What survives translation across contexts?" (13941–13948); and the three the model proposed to put to each forebear: "What did this person discover about the conditions under which recognition becomes possible?" "What did they mistake for the whole?" "What does their work make available that my emerging ecology doesn't yet know how to make available?" (2024–2032).
- **The four quadrant questions.** "What does this feel like?" "What does this look like behaviorally?" "What shared meanings sustain it?" "What structures produce it?" (18724–18738).
- **Questions for a tool.** "does it deepen attention or fragment it?"; "more connected to your experience or more dependent on external production?"; "do your capacities feel richer or thinner?" (867).
- **Questions of one's own standpoint.** "Who is noticing?" "Who is choosing?" "Who is interpreting?" "Who is integrating?" "Who is deciding what matters?" "Who is changed?" "Who bears responsibility for the resulting meaning?" (7792–7798).
- **The honesty question.** "Have I encountered this, or have I merely recognized that it sounds true?" (14582).
- **Questions for an intervention.** "What is actually preventing movement?" "What is the cost of intervening?" "What is the cost of not intervening?" (15625).
- **A facilitator's questions about a session already held.** "Why did you choose this invitation?" "What were you watching for?" "What could go wrong?" "What did you notice in yourself?" "What alternative interpretations were possible?" "Where did the intervention create possibility?" "Where might it have constrained possibility?" (1524–1530). The model proposed them as what Book I would show when it shows "the score to the musicians" (1516), meaning the reasoning behind each of the facilitator's moves. The author could answer them about the group he led in May 2026, and his answers would be the first record of his facilitation in the vault.
- **A reader's questions of this book.** "What does this book allow me to see?" "What does this book prevent me from seeing?" "What happens when I stop using this book's way of seeing?" (2608–2614). The last one asks the book to teach its own leaving. The vault holds the idea as `notes/a framework that teaches its own exit relates to authority differently than one seeking permanence.md`, supported by a reading of Wittgenstein's ladder at the end of the *Tractatus*.

## 6. Writing techniques

The vault once held eighteen notes on writing technique and the author removed them on 2026-08-09. Whether Book I's need reopens that decision is his (pull request #23, `system-and-vault-left-to-populate-2026-09-25.md`, Part 3). The catalog below lists what is available if he does, and much of it is his own.

- **The author's own rules for writing.** They include poem #11's "(only write from the now / right about now lest / you leave behind a part of / yourself in a place you cannot see)"; "ideally quoting rather than copy" (820); "one thing at a time" (1086); and rule 13 of `ops/methodology/prose register.md`, "The fix is to say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use it."
- **The poems' own technique.** The vault records that "the poems end by naming the next act and leaving it undone" (`notes/the poems end by naming the next act and leaving it undone.md`), and that poem #4 is written "in the grammar of remembering a future not yet lived."
- **The transcript's rules of form**, the model's: "Do not explain the reader out of an encounter," and, in its explanation, give an encounter before twenty pages of exposition, introduce a tradition through a lived question, let a distinction be experienced before it is named, and delay a concept until after the reader has struggled (17197–17205).
- **Two treatments from the vault's disagreements.** The vault's proposed ways of handling two of its open disagreements are writing techniques: "The Plain Water Pass," stretches of deliberately unornamented prose, and "the determinacy test," under which "every auratic word must sit beside a determinate claim it would be false without, or be cut" (reader F3). The first is rule 13's aim applied to a passage.
- **Sixteen writing constraints.** A session's document of 2026-08-20 derived sixteen constraints on the writing, among them "terms enter as distinctions, not definitions," "the reader's interior is off-limits in the declarative," and "meter is established so it can be broken" (reader F2, from `reframe-derivations-2026-08-20.md`).
- **The atlas's designed devices, never executed.** The atlas designed marginal quotations forming "a second book with its own plot"; a column where a reader signs "walked. still true."; a record following each early image to where it is completed; an escrow of promises; a ratio of evocative to informative prose; and an eight-question checklist. Of its devices only the interludes were executed ("Pause. Breathe. You have come a long way. We are about to go deeper.") (reader O4).
- **Showing the working method to facilitators.** The model's questions for Book I's reader would show a facilitator the working method behind a session: "Why did you choose this invitation?" "What were you watching for?" "What could go wrong?" "What did you notice in yourself?" (1524–1527). The brief and draft for the verification standard's defense are one worked example (`drafts/`).
- **Showing each sentence's standing.** The model listed what a person who holds a territory responsibly knows, among it "what is sourced," and "what is yours" (9180–9188). A book could let its reader see which sentences are the author's lived report, which are a tradition's, and which are inference.

## 7. Promises, and what the books are not

- **Book I's promise.** The model wrote it: "I will not give you a bag of techniques. I will invite you to examine what you think you are doing when you facilitate another human being" (17060).
- **Book II's promise.** The model wrote it too: "I will not tell you how to become a better version of yourself. I will invite you to develop a different relationship with the fact that you are already becoming" (17077).
- **The only honest promise.** In the vault's terms it is conditional testimony, "I helped create a field in which something became possible," never "I caused this transformation" (`notes/the book's only honest promise is conditional testimony.md`).
- **The author's own terms.** He offered "try this on" (176); "the book can't verify that you've done the work" (266); and "So it stays back and just sort of says, yeah, you know, here's this, here's how you would look into it further if you'd like to" (244), which is the promise of a referral.
- **What the books are not.** The model listed ten things the project "should not become": a giant ontology, a comparative-religion encyclopedia, a generic AI critique, a productivity system, a self-help manual, a purely academic work, a mystical manifesto, AI-written authorship, premature synthesis, and complete formalization (12247–12279).
- **The limits a promise must state.** The vault already records that a book cannot supply basic trust, cannot adjust to its reader, cannot certify, and cannot correct a misreading (section 3 of `catalog-of-meaning-ownership-2026-09-26.md`).

## 8. Orders

- **Book I's order.** By the author's decision of 2026-08-16 it follows the four movements, "Recognition → Relation → Participation → Stewardship of the field" (16970), with the model's ten steps from "I want to help." to "I become a steward of another person's conditions for becoming." (16818–16854).
- **Book II's order.** By the same decision it follows the arc of the psychedelic experience: preparation, onset, deepening, peak, descent, and integration, with the author's decision of 2026-08-20 that the "peak resolves into return."
- **The developmental sequence both share.** The vault places it at `notes/the learning arc.md`.
- **The model's arc of the reader's experience.** No one has compared it with the others: recognition ("Ah. I see this."), disorientation ("Wait. I don't know what I thought I knew."), experimentation ("What happens if I try this?"), encounter ("Something happened."), integration ("This changes how I understand the earlier material."), and agency ("I can work with this myself.") (10281–10325).
- **The survey of sequences.** It sorts about sixty sequences into fourteen types by what moves a reader on and what marks the end (`ops/arc ecology.md`).
- **The author's rule of order.** He said: "I can't reveal something early that I didn't know until much later without breaking the thing that makes it work" (`ops/methodology/the book's current shape.md`).
- **What moves a person on.** What moves a person from one phase to the next is something "nothing states" (`ops/arc ecology.md`), and the one criterion the abridged draft gives the reader is "If you finish quickly, you have not finished."

## 10. Forms

- **The correspondence.** The author chose it on 2026-08-08: twelve to twenty dated letters, written to the reader as "you."
- **A workbook aligned with a workshop.** The author floated it and was "still not like married to all the way" (356).
- **A retreat, a circle, or a journal.** The model offered these as alternatives (291).
- **A companion volume of drills.** The treatment says "full technical instruction belongs to the companion volume — the book keeps the vocabulary, the companion keeps the drills" (`arc-gloss` ch14, reader F1).
- **A printed record of a teacher's replies**, which Kapleau's book is: "a worked precedent for a book standing in for a teacher" (reader F1).
- **A score for facilitators.** The model's image of the book as a score (501), and its "show the score to the musicians" (1516), suggest a Book I that facilitators perform with groups.
- **A book read aloud.** The author's memory of his father reading to him suggests it.
- **Short first-person essays, one per concept.** The model voiced one such sentence for him, "Here is what Spanda has come to mean to me." (9074), which must never be cited as his; a genre of them would have to be his own.
- **A handoff.** The author asked the model twice to recast the whole conversation "as a context handoff to another agent" (10694). The form in which material passes from one reader to the next is a form he already uses.
- **A series of conversations with participants.** The model proposed it: "Could even start simply as a series of conversations with a few participants. Exploring one principle at a time" (361).

## 11. Addressees and grammatical person

- **The first reader of Book I** is a facilitator, by the author's own insight at 1358.
- **Book II's reader**, by the vault's dated definitions, is someone to whom a big experience "already happened once and did not last" (2026-08-04), widened to anyone "already on their way" (2026-08-10).
- **An addressee who is not a reader.** The author named one on 2026-08-02: "The book itself becomes me writing to the Third Thing."
- **Readers the model named for Book I.** It listed managers, coaches, teachers, parents, leaders, therapists, mentors, AI designers, and community builders (14718–14729).
- **Readers Book I should not try to serve at once.** The model named them: "expert facilitator + curious general reader + spiritual seeker + manager + self-help consumer" (17130).
- **Grammatical person.** The first person addressing the reader as "you" was chosen for the correspondence on 2026-08-08. Book I's person and form are open (item 15 of the decision sitting).
- **A genre at the right warmth.** The vault's question "what professional genre runs at love's temperature" (reader F2) asks for a form that a facilitator can use at work and that still reads like a letter from a friend.
