## 1. The instrument — the felt shift's defense may already exist in Gendlin's own philosophy

- Almost nobody reads past *Focusing* into *Experiencing and the Creation of Meaning* and *A Process Model*. Gendlin's **carrying forward** is the candidate defense: a genuine felt shift is not assent but a step — the situation changes and something new becomes sayable. Fluent prose produces assent with no step. That is a checkable difference, and it converts the counterfeit problem into a diagnostic.
- The counterfeit catalogue could be teachable rather than merely defensive: processing fluency (Reber, Schwarz, Oppenheimer), false insights (Danek — convinced "aha" feelings for wrong answers), emotional contagion. The book could train shift-versus-shiver discernment, which is exactly what the fluent second reader needs.
- Garfinkel's interoception model splits body-listening into accuracy, sensibility, and awareness. High sensibility with low accuracy is the second reader given an empirical spine.
- Time as falsifier, from Gendlin himself: a real shift survives re-contact the next day; a fluency-shiver does not.
- For the author rather than the reader: Gendlin's Thinking at the Edge is the compositional method that matches the standard — writing from the felt sense.
