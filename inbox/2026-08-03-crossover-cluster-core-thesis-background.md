## Key Findings

### The one new load-bearing fact: Heidegger retracted "truth" for aletheia in his own voice

In "The End of Philosophy and the Task of Thinking" (1964), Heidegger withdrew the equation his earlier work had made: "aletheia, unconcealment in the sense of the opening, may not be equated with truth. Rather, aletheia, unconcealment thought as opening, first grants the possibility of truth." Scholarly discussion of the essay renders the self-correction more bluntly: "it was inadequate and misleading to call aletheia, in the sense of opening, truth." In the same essay he conceded the philological objection that had been raised against him — that alethes in Homer already appears only in verba dicendi, in the sense of correctness and reliability, not unconcealment. Three consequences for the vault:

- The founding isomorph at [[aletheia and pratyabhijna converge on truth as un-forgetting]] survives, because it is established at the level of word-construction and gesture: a- + lēthē remains a privative undoing of concealment, and unconcealment remains the gesture. What acquires a footnote is the word "truth" in the English slogan — the near pole's author spent his last period saying unconcealment is the *ground* of truth rather than truth itself.
- The refusal is quotable and honors the book's method: the thinker kept his own correction, in print, against his own most famous reading. A drafting session that quotes aletheia can quote the retraction in the same breath, which is exactly the refusals-intact form the company is built on.
- The retraction narrows rather than breaks the crossover: "unconcealment first grants the possibility of truth" is structurally closer to the Pratyabhijñā's position (recognition does not add knowledge; it removes the concealment under which knowing already operated) than the retracted slogan was.

### The footsteps

**Henry Corbin.** Verified: Corbin was Heidegger's first French translator — his *Qu'est-ce que la métaphysique?* appeared in 1939 — and he translated two treatises of Suhrawardi in 1933–35, after Louis Massignon gave him a lithograph of the *Ḥikmat al-ishrāq*. The pointing, from the Radio France-Culture interview with Philippe Nemo (2 June 1976) published as "From Heidegger to Suhravardi": Corbin said Heidegger's great merit was "having centered the act of philosophizing in hermeneutics," and described that hermeneutics as a key — "the principal tool equipping the mental laboratory of phenomenology" — which he then used to open "other levels of being" than the ones Heidegger opened. He rendered "phenomenology" for Persian-speaking students as *kashf al-mahjub*, the rending of the veil to reveal the hidden — unveiling carried whole into another tradition's vocabulary. The refusal worth keeping: the passage was one-way and Corbin said so; using the key did not require sharing Heidegger's worldview, and he did not stay. The instructive difference the survey named is confirmed in outline: Corbin's destination was esoteric — the imaginal realm, Shi'ite gnosis — where this book's destination is a householder's Tuesday.

**J. L. Mehta.** Verified: 1912–1988, Banaras Hindu University, then Harvard's Center for the Study of World Religions and the University of Hawaii; *The Philosophy of Martin Heidegger* (1967), revised as *Martin Heidegger: The Way and the Vision* (University Press of Hawaii, 1976); the essay "Heidegger and Vedanta" carries the encounter directly. The pointing is his famous dictum on the Europeanization of the earth, quoted through Wilhelm Halbfass's *India and Europe*: "there is no other way open, to us in the East, but to go along with this Europeanization and to go through it. Only through this voyage into the foreign and the strange can we win back our own selfhood; here as elsewhere, the way to what is closest to us is the longest way back." (Wording to be checked against the printed Halbfass before manuscript use.) The sentence is the book's road-into-the-valley responsibility in mirror image — Mehta walks an Indian reader through the West and home, the author proposes to walk a Western reader through the West's own philosopher into the valley — and its closing clause is the doctrine of the long way back said fifty years early. The refusal worth keeping: Mehta is the standing refusal of the shortcut. The founding India–Heidegger scholar, in full command of both sides, held that the encounter cannot be skipped to; fusion talk that spares the reader the voyage is what his life's work declines.

**John Caputo.** Verified: *The Mystical Element in Heidegger's Thought* (Ohio University Press, 1978; revised Fordham edition, 1986) establishes that Heidegger took the word Gelassenheit from Meister Eckhart and that both write of a releasement — Heidegger's thinking "without why," Eckhart's letting the soul give up its own will. This confirms the survey's claim that the book's Eckhart seat already touches Heidegger's deepest borrowing. The refusal worth keeping is Caputo's own second act: *Demythologizing Heidegger* (Indiana University Press, 1993), in which the scholar who established the mystical element spent a book warning against it. The one figure who most successfully joined Heidegger to the mystics is also the literature's loudest caution about doing so — a footstep and a guard in one person, and a model of keeping one's own refusal that the book's method could cite as such.

**The Kyoto School beyond Nishida.** Verified: Nishitani Keiji attended Heidegger's lectures at Freiburg for two years (the Nietzsche lectures, late 1930s) and while there lectured on Zarathustra and Meister Eckhart; he trained in Zen at Shōkoku-ji for twenty-four years; *Religion and Nothingness* works śūnyatā against Western nihilism. The survey's claim that Nishitani studied under Heidegger himself holds. The refusal worth keeping belongs to Ueda Shizuteru, Nishitani's student and the tradition's Eckhart specialist: after the closest Eckhart–Zen comparison on record, Ueda concludes — as the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's Kyoto School entry summarizes him — that Eckhart's nothingness, like negative theology's generally, "still points to an inexpressibly higher being," and so is not Zen's absolute nothingness. The most sustained comparison in the whole crossover field ends by recording a difference. That is the pattern the book's stance predicts, arrived at independently by the comparativist with the most standing to declare identity.

### The developers

**Isabelle Ratié.** Verified: *Le Soi et l'Autre: Identité, différence et altérité dans la philosophie de la Pratyabhijñā* (Brill, Jerusalem Studies in Religion and Culture 13, 2011), reviewed as "the most important study of the... Pratyabhijñā to be published to date"; Utpaladeva fl. c. 925–975, Abhinavagupta fl. c. 975–1025, the *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* as "Stanzas on the Recognition of the Lord" with Abhinavagupta's two commentaries. The pointing: Ratié's study shows the recognition system "cannot be reduced to a mere scriptural exegesis" — it was built in live argument with Buddhist and Brahmanical opponents, concept by concept. For a book whose far pole is sometimes staged as landscape rather than interlocutor, this is the scholarly warrant that the tradition argues: it earned its positions polemically and can argue back. The refusal worth keeping is the philologist's: the system's concepts were forged against specific opponents in a specific century, and lifting them out of that argumentative context loses what makes them positions rather than poetry — a caution aimed at exactly the gesture-where-the-tradition-argues move already recorded in [[the kashmir shaiva tradition would decline four of the book's deepest moves]].

**David Peter Lawrence.** Verified: *Rediscovering God with Transcendental Argument: A Contemporary Interpretation of Monistic Kashmiri Śaiva Philosophy* (SUNY Press, 1999). The pointing: Lawrence reads the Pratyabhijñā discourse as "both philosophical apologetics and spiritual exercise" — the treatise itself is a practice, argument as the vehicle of the recognition it argues for. That is the strongest outside precedent found this round for a book whose form enacts its content: the recognition school's own founding texts already work that way on Lawrence's reading. The refusal worth keeping: Lawrence carries the system into Western argumentative form to *demonstrate* the reality of God against relativism and deconstruction — an evidentiary ambition the book's stance explicitly does not share. He is the developer who shows what the crossover looks like when it is run as proof, which is the road the author has declined.

**Loriliai Biernacki.** Verified: *The Matter of Wonder: Abhinavagupta's Panentheism and the New Materialism* (Oxford University Press, 2023); Biernacki is at the University of Colorado Boulder. The pointing: she takes Abhinavagupta's late commentary (the *Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vivṛti-vimarśinī*) as a live philosophical resource for the present-day consciousness-and-matter debate — on her reading, everything has an "innate capacity for sentience" even where nothing is actually sentient — which makes her the proof that the crossover is a current scholarly project and not a closed historical episode. Wonder is her operative term, from Abhinavagupta's camatkāra. The refusal worth keeping: her destination is metaphysics — a model for New Materialism and environmental thought — not practice; the living developer develops the crossover toward theory, which leaves the book's practice-verified lane unoccupied even here.

**Michel Henry.** Verified as to the philosophy: *The Essence of Manifestation* (French original 1963) argues that the origin of all manifestation is a self-revelation that refers to nothing but itself — auto-affection, prior to any intentionality, invisible to the world's light; "life" in Henry's sense is exclusively phenomenological, and manifestation's self-feeling is a pathos, a pure undergoing of itself. The structural nearness to vimarśa — consciousness touching itself without taking itself as object — is evident on juxtaposition. **The survey's further claim did not verify:** the search found no established comparative literature pairing Henry with vimarśa or the Pratyabhijñā; results on each side are rich, results joining them are absent at this depth. The survey note's "comparative work beginning to exist" should be held as unconfirmed until a scholarly-database pass says otherwise — and if the absence is real, this specific pairing is open territory adjacent to the author's own. The refusal worth keeping: Henry's whole polemic is *against* the world's appearing — he indicts the assumption (Heidegger's included, by name, as "ontological monism") that all manifestation is worldly and ek-static, and his late work roots life's self-revelation in a specifically Christian frame. The phenomenologist nearest to vimarśa refuses both the near pole's clearing and the householder's daylight; he is company for the interior half of the thesis only.

### The against

**Steven Katz.** Verified: "Language, Epistemology, and Mysticism," in *Mysticism and Philosophical Analysis* (Oxford University Press, 1978). The pointing is the sentence itself, his stated working hypothesis: "There are NO pure (i.e. unmediated) experiences." On Katz's constructivism, a tradition's concepts shape the experiences its practitioners have, so a Christian's union and a Buddhist's śūnyatā are two experiences, not one reality twice reported — and any claimed convergence is an artifact of description. Bearing on the core thesis: Katz is the scholarly name for a caution the vault already holds in its own form at [[the unanimity claim is an artifact of how the lineages were selected]], and he breaks only the book the author has already declined to write. Convergence held as proof falls to Katz; convergence held as company — the stance's actual position, observation without evidentiary remarks — is not his target. The refusal worth keeping cuts both ways: his hypothesis forbids the strong reading of the founding isomorph (same experience, two vocabularies) while leaving the weak reading (same word-construction, same gesture) untouched, and the vault's phrasing of that isomorph already lives at the weak reading.

**Thomas Sheehan.** Verified: *Making Sense of Heidegger: A Paradigm Shift* (2015), Stanford. The pointing: Sheehan's deflation — Heidegger's lifelong topic was not a cosmic mystery called Being but the finite clearing in which things become intelligible at all; Heidegger renders aletheia with sobrieties like "disclosedness" and "unconcealedness," and the mystical register is the reader's addition. Sheehan is the standing guard against exactly the move an enthusiastic drafting session would make with aletheia, and the 1964 retraction recorded above is the primary-text anchor his reading rests on. The refusal worth keeping: on Sheehan's account the deflationary reading is not hostile to the book's use — a clearing in which the concealed becomes visible is still the structure — but it strips the word of any claim to name a mystical event, which disciplines the slogan without touching the practice.

**Andrea Jain.** Verified: *Selling Yoga: From Counterculture to Pop Culture* (Oxford, 2014) and *Peace Love Yoga: The Politics of Global Spirituality* (Oxford, 2020); Indiana University Indianapolis. The pointing: her named mechanism, "gestural subversion" — commodified spirituality acknowledges the harms of the system it lives in and subverts them in gesture only, so that subversion itself is colonized; her analysis targets therapeutic spirituality that concentrates on (as the discussion of the book puts it) healing the broken person rather than undermining the system that broke that person. Bearing on the book: this is the sharpest available form of the challenge the author's stewardship line must answer, and it is structural rather than personal — sincerity is not a defense, since gestural subversion is what sincere actors do inside the economy she describes. The vault already holds the beginning of an answer in the seat found for Hartmut Rosa (the position that some suffering lives in economic and institutional arrangement), and Jain is the test of whether that answer is load-bearing or gestural. The refusal worth keeping: she would refuse the framing of Indian contemplative goods as apolitical inner technology at all; any book that walks a Western reader into the valley is, on her account, operating inside the appropriation economy and owes its answer from there.

### The deep ancestry

**Plato's anamnesis and Augustine's memoria.** Verified: Plato argues learning-as-recollection in the *Meno* and *Phaedo* and invokes it in the *Phaedrus*; birth induces a forgetting (lēthē) that embodiment imposes and inquiry undoes. Augustine works memoria in *Confessions* Book X — and, decisively, revises the doctrine he inherits: recollection of a soul's past knowledge becomes illumination by an eternal presence found within memory yet beyond it. Heidegger's Parmenides lectures (Freiburg, winter semester 1942–43) are confirmed as the hinge the survey named: there he reads lēthē as withdrawing concealment and grounds both remembering and forgetting in unconcealment — the West's remembering-doctrines and the aletheia reading touch in one text. The pointing for a drafting session: the *Meno*'s slave boy is the oldest staging of the book's own method — the guide who inserts nothing and questions the knower to what he already holds — and "reading is remembering" has a twenty-four-century ancestry that runs Meno → Confessions X → the Parmenides lectures. The refusal worth keeping: the lineage disagrees with itself about what is remembered. Plato's mechanism (the soul's preexistence) is the part Augustine explicitly declined while keeping the structure, and Augustine's what-is-found (the God within) is not Plato's Forms. Even the West's own remembering-tradition is company rather than corroboration — the arrivals share the gesture and dispute the destination, which is the same shape the founding isomorph already has.
